**The category question, decided explicitly.** This is a Brief Report / Research Note, not a full research article. I am stating that as a decision rather than letting it hide inside a revision verdict. The evidentiary payload is a single correlation with its confidence interval and one robustness coefficient; there is no second analysis, no moderator, no competing model, no instrument development, and no new measurement. A full-article slot at a high-volume educational-technology journal is a claim on roughly 6,000 words of reader attention and on the journal's scarce full-article capacity; this paper's content does not draw on that budget. Recategorised, it fits comfortably — a 2,000-word note reporting a calibrated single-site estimate with a CI is a legitimate publication object, and I would not want the field to lose such reports.

**Saturation, and whether the author's own framing is enough.** The manuscript describes itself as "an incremental data point, comparable with prior work." I take this seriously in both directions. It is honest, and honesty about scope is rarer than it should be in this literature. But it is also doing work that contribution is supposed to do. The framing tells me what the paper is *not* (a model test, a causal claim) far more clearly than what a reader *gains*. Song (2018) is cited as showing that association strengths vary by institution — which is exactly the premise that would make a single-site estimate valuable — yet the paper never converts that premise into a claim: it does not say what kind of institution is under-represented in the existing distribution, nor what this campus adds to it, nor what a reader should do differently having read it. As it stands, the concession functions pre-emptively: it inoculates the paper against the objection instead of answering it. An impeccably calibrated report of a well-known association is still a report of a well-known association, and calibration is not a substitute for reader gain.

**Routing.** After revision, the short-report venue, as a Brief Report. I would not send this to a high-volume Springer-family educational-technology journal as a full article even after the reporting defects below are fixed, because none of those fixes changes the size of the contribution. The alternative route — retaining the full-article grade — requires new content, not rewriting: behavioural log data alongside the self-report, or a second site, either of which would let the paper speak to the measurement-divergence problem it currently only cites (Vasquez, 2020) or to the between-institution variability it currently only acknowledges.

**One finding outside my lane, flagged and routed.** Every reference in the list resolves to a DOI on the 10.5555 reserved test prefix with sequential suffixes, and several journal titles are near-misses of real venues. I am recording this under D5 because reference apparatus falls within my remit, but its natural home is D2 or a research-integrity screen that the six-dimension set does not contain, and it is not the basis of my D5 warn. If verification confirms these sources do not exist, that outcome overrides every dimension score on this panel, including mine, and the paper's sole contribution claim — consistency with prior work — collapses with them. Editors habitually delegate reference checking to production; that habit is precisely what this pattern exploits, and I am not delegating it.

**What a successful revision looks like.** Recategorise to Brief Report. Add a scatterplot and one descriptives table. Report r² as a number. Give the sampling-frame denominator and response rate. Add the standard declarations. Replace the self-deprecating contribution paragraph with a positive one: name the gap in the distribution of existing estimates that this campus fills. Resolve the references. None of that is a large amount of work, and all of it is necessary.
