## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, measurement, data handling, analysis, and statistical reporting hold up against the normal peer-review standard of the field, and whether enough procedural detail is disclosed for another researcher to reproduce or at least audit the work. From a domain-accuracy vantage point I note only that methodological weakness and factual misstatement are distinct failures: a study can be methodologically thin while still describing its constructs correctly, or methodologically clean while misrepresenting the literature. I do not score this dimension; it belongs to the methodology reviewer.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role — mine).** This is the dimension I own. It asks whether every substantive claim the manuscript makes is consistent with the current state of evidence in its field, whether the prior literature it invokes is characterised faithfully rather than conveniently, and whether the field's technical vocabulary, constructs, instruments, standards, and reported quantities are used correctly. Three distinct things can go wrong: the paper can assert something the field's evidence does not support; it can attribute to a cited source a finding, definition, or scope that source does not contain; or it can misuse a term of art, misidentify a canonical instrument or framework, or state a domain fact (a definition, a standard, a well-established empirical regularity, an internally reported number) incorrectly. My job is to detect these, weigh how load-bearing each error is for the manuscript's conclusions, and grade accordingly.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, da/methodology roles).** This dimension concerns whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether the stated evidence actually licenses the stated conclusions, whether the chain from data to interpretation to implication contains gaps or fallacies, and whether the paper contradicts itself. It overlaps with my remit at one seam — an argument can be internally valid yet built on a false domain premise — but the coherence judgement itself is scored by the discourse-analysis and methodology reviewers, not by me.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high, perspective role).** This dimension evaluates whether a reader from an adjacent discipline can enter the paper: whether framing and definitions are supplied rather than assumed, and whether any claim that reaches beyond the home field is actually backed rather than gestured at. Domain accuracy and cross-disciplinary legibility can diverge — a paper can be locally correct and still opaque to outsiders — so this is scored by the perspective reviewer.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal, eic role).** This covers organisation, clarity of exposition, quality and integrity of figures and tables, and conformity to the target venue's conventions. Presentation problems can incidentally surface domain errors (for example, a table whose values contradict the text), but the presentation grade itself is the editor's to assign.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, eic role).** This asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it advances something original and consequential for that readership. Originality assessment brushes against my remit, since judging novelty requires knowing the prior literature accurately, but the fit-and-contribution verdict is the editor's.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether each substantive claim is supported by the current evidence base of educational technology and higher-education research; whether cited prior work (including any named model, framework, or validated instrument) is described with the scope, population, and findings it actually has; whether technical constructs and terms of art are defined and used in their standard field sense; whether reported statistics, effect magnitudes, instrument properties, and descriptive figures are internally consistent and consistent with what the described data could yield; and whether the strength of language attached to each claim matches the strength of evidence behind it.
what_triggers_block: One or more load-bearing domain errors — a misattributed or materially misdescribed prior finding, a misapplied core construct or instrument, or an assertion contradicting well-established field evidence — that a conclusion of the paper actually rests on, such that correcting the error would require the authors to restate or withdraw a headline claim.
what_triggers_warn: Domain inaccuracies that are real but peripheral to the conclusions — loose or non-standard use of a technical term, an imprecise or overreaching characterisation of a cited source, an unsupported generalisation beyond the studied population, or a dated evidence claim — all of which are correctable by localised rewriting without disturbing the paper's findings.
what_triggers_fatal: Pervasive or fabricated domain content — for example, citations to sources that do not support or do not exist as characterised, reported results that cannot be derived from the described data, or a systematic misconstrual of the field's core evidence — such that the manuscript's factual foundation cannot be repaired by revision and its claims are not trustworthy as presented.
