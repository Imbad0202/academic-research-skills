## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, my ownership).** From a methodology standpoint, this dimension asks whether the study's design can actually bear the weight of the inferences drawn from it. I read it as covering: whether the design (here, whatever sampling and measurement structure the authors chose) is fit for the question asked; whether the data pipeline is described well enough that another researcher could reconstruct it — population definition, recruitment route, response and attrition accounting, instrument provenance, item wording, scale construction; whether the analysis is reported to the field's standard, meaning effect estimates with uncertainty rather than bare p-values, stated assumptions, handling of missing data, and no undisclosed multiplicity; and whether reproducibility affordances exist at all (instrument in an appendix, data or code availability statement, ethics/consent statement). It also covers the fit between what was measured and what is claimed to have been measured — construct validity, reliability evidence, and whether self-report or single-source measurement is acknowledged as a limit rather than silently treated as objective fact. Failures here are not stylistic; they determine whether any conclusion is licensed.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role).** This dimension is about factual and literature-level correctness within the substantive field: are the constructs used as the field uses them, is prior work represented accurately rather than conveniently, and are there outright errors in domain terminology, established findings, or cited results. I am not scoring this dimension, and I will not substitute my own judgement for the domain reviewer's. Where a methodological defect has a domain-accuracy shadow (for example, a measure described using a name that belongs to a different established instrument), I will note it as methodology evidence and leave the domain adjudication to the owner.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the discourse-analysis role, eligible to me).** As a methodologist I read coherence narrowly and concretely: does the chain from data to claim hold, link by link. That means checking whether the conclusion is the conclusion the design can support, whether correlational evidence is quietly upgraded to causal or directional language, whether the abstract and discussion make claims broader in population, time, or mechanism than the sample and measurement permit, whether stated limitations are contradicted by later assertions, and whether any inference depends on an unstated premise that the reported evidence does not establish. Internal inconsistency in reported numbers between sections also falls here for me, since it breaks the evidential chain even when each individual passage reads sensibly.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high, perspective role).** This concerns whether a reader from an adjacent field can enter the paper: are constructs defined rather than assumed, is jargon unpacked, and are any claims that reach across disciplinary boundaries actually backed rather than asserted by analogy. I am not eligible to score it and will not plan a score for it.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal, editor-in-chief role).** This covers organisation, expositional clarity, quality and self-sufficiency of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's formatting and reporting conventions. Not mine to score; if a table is unreadable in a way that blocks methodological verification, I will record that as D1 evidence rather than trespass into D5.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, editor-in-chief role).** This asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it advances knowledge in a way that readership would consider original and significant. Judgements about novelty and scope sit with the editorial role; I will not score it, though my D1 findings may be an input the editor uses.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor

dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: Explicit population and sampling frame; recruitment route and response/attrition accounting; provenance and full wording of the instrument plus reliability and validity evidence for each constructed scale; pre-specified analysis with effect estimates, uncertainty intervals, and stated assumptions; missing-data handling; multiplicity disclosure; ethics/consent statement; and reproducibility affordances such as instrument appendix, data availability, or analysis code.
what_triggers_block: A load-bearing methodological element is absent or unverifiable such that the reported results cannot be reconstructed or evaluated at all — for example no sample-size or response accounting, no description of how scales were formed, or inferential statistics reported with no test specification, denominators, or uncertainty.
what_triggers_warn: The core design and analysis are recoverable but rigour is incomplete in ways that weaken confidence without voiding it — for example missing reliability coefficients, unreported missing-data treatment, no ethics statement, undisclosed multiple comparisons, or no data/instrument availability statement.
what_triggers_fatal: The reported methodology is internally impossible or invalidating rather than merely underspecified — for example numbers that cannot arise from the stated design, an analysis structurally inapplicable to the data type collected, contradictory sample sizes across sections that cannot be reconciled, or evidence that results were selected after inspecting outcomes.

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: A traceable chain from measurement to claim; alignment between the strength of language in abstract, results, and discussion and the strength of evidence actually produced; explicit acknowledgement of design-imposed limits on causality, generalisation, and self-report; internally consistent numbers and consistent construct definitions across sections; and no conclusion resting on an unstated premise the paper never evidences.
what_triggers_block: The central conclusion is not supported by the reported evidence — a causal, predictive, or mechanistic claim is asserted on a design that can only support association, or the headline claim generalises to a population or setting the study never sampled.
what_triggers_warn: The main argument holds but contains local slippage — occasional causal verbs in otherwise correlational discussion, limitations that are listed then ignored in the implications, or claims stated more confidently in the abstract than the results justify.
what_triggers_fatal: The argument is self-defeating: the paper's own reported evidence contradicts its stated conclusion, or two central claims are mutually exclusive with no reconciliation, so no coherent thesis can be extracted.
