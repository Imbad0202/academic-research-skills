## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** As I read it, this dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data handling, statistical reporting, and the affordances it offers for replication would survive scrutiny by a competent methodologist in the manuscript's own field. From where I sit as the cross-disciplinary reader, my stake in this dimension is indirect but real: a reader from an adjacent field cannot audit a method they cannot reconstruct, so methodological under-specification is also an accessibility failure. I will not score this dimension — it is not in my eligible roles — and I will defer to the methodology reviewer's verdict rather than shadow-judging design quality.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by the domain reviewer).** This dimension covers whether the manuscript's claims are consistent with the current state of evidence in its home domain, whether prior work is characterised faithfully, and whether domain-specific constructs, instruments, and reported results are used without factual error. My cross-disciplinary interest here concerns the boundary between a term's technical meaning inside the field and the looser meaning an outside reader would import; but adjudicating whether the field's literature is correctly represented belongs to the domain specialist, and I will not score it.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the discourse-analysis reviewer).** Here the question is whether the central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether the stated evidence actually licenses the stated conclusions, whether inferential steps are valid, and whether any fallacy or equivocation hollows out the core argument. This overlaps with my own concern, because arguments that slide between constructs are often the same arguments that mislead adjacent-field readers. Still, ownership sits with the discourse-analysis role and secondary eligibility with methodology, so I will raise coherence observations only as context inside my own dimension and will not issue a D3 score.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by me).** This is my dimension. It asks whether the manuscript's framing, its definitions of key constructs, and its stated implications are intelligible and usable to readers in fields adjacent to the paper's home discipline, and whether any claim that reaches beyond that home discipline is actually substantiated rather than asserted. Two failure directions matter symmetrically: under-translation, where the paper is locked behind unexplained in-field jargon, unexplained instruments, or unstated assumptions so that an adjacent reader cannot tell what was measured or why it matters; and over-reach, where the paper exports its findings to neighbouring domains, to policy, to practice, or to populations that its evidence base cannot reach. I will judge scope claims against the actual design the manuscript reports, treating the gap between what the evidence can support and what the discussion invites readers to conclude as the central measurable quantity.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension concerns organisation, expository clarity, the quality and self-sufficiency of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's conventions. It is adjacent to my dimension without being identical: prose can be clean and well-ordered while still assuming a reader already inside the field, and prose can be rough while remaining perfectly legible across fields. I will keep these separate and will not score D5.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it advances something original and consequential for that venue's readership. My dimension can inform it — a paper whose interdisciplinary reach is oversold may have a narrower real contribution than claimed — but the fit-and-significance judgement is the editor's, and I will not score it.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether key constructs, instruments, and outcome measures are defined in terms an adjacent-field reader can grasp without prior domain training; whether the abstract and introduction state what was measured, in whom, and under what design before technical terminology arrives; whether every implication offered to neighbouring fields, to practice, or to policy is traceable to evidence the study actually reports; and whether the stated population, setting, and inference scope are held consistent from title through conclusions.
what_triggers_block: The manuscript exports its findings to audiences, populations, settings, or causal questions its reported design cannot reach — for example generalising to institutions, disciplines, or learner groups never sampled, or advancing practice/policy prescriptions that require evidence the paper does not present — and does so in the abstract or conclusions without any scope qualification, such that an adjacent-field reader would be actively misled about what is known.
what_triggers_warn: Core constructs are used without operational definition, or definitions drift between sections, or interdisciplinary implications are stated with hedges but without an explicit limitations passage tying scope back to the sample and design, leaving an adjacent-field reader able to reconstruct the study only with effort and at risk of over-reading a single result.
