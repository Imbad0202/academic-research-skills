## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data collection, handling, and statistical reporting would survive scrutiny by a competent peer reviewer in the paper's field, and whether enough procedural detail is given that another team could reproduce the work. From a domain-accuracy standpoint I read this as the "how was it done" axis: instrument construction, analysis choices, and disclosure of materials. It is owned and scored by the methodology reviewer, not by me.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role).** This is my dimension. It asks whether the substantive claims the manuscript makes are consistent with what the field currently knows, whether the literature it cites is characterised faithfully rather than stretched or misattributed, and whether the specialised vocabulary, constructs, instruments, and reported quantities are used correctly and consistently. It covers both errors of commission (a wrong definition, a misstated prior finding, an inflated causal reading of a correlational result, a construct given a meaning the field does not grant it) and errors of representation (ignoring or misrepresenting an established body of evidence that bears directly on the claim). Numeric or terminological internal inconsistency inside the manuscript also falls here when it signals a factual defect rather than a mere typographical slip.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, da/methodology roles).** This dimension asks whether the paper's central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether the stated evidence actually licenses the conclusions drawn, whether intermediate steps follow, and whether any reasoning defect is severe enough to undermine the main argument rather than merely a side remark. It overlaps my concerns where a claim is both domain-false and logically unsupported, but scoring authority sits with the devil's-advocate reviewer.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high, perspective role).** This dimension asks whether a reader from an adjacent field can enter the paper: whether framing and definitions are supplied rather than assumed, and whether any claim that reaches beyond the home discipline is actually backed rather than asserted. It is scored by the perspective reviewer.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal, eic role).** This dimension covers manuscript craft: section organisation, clarity of exposition, quality and legibility of figures and tables, and conformity to the target venue's presentational conventions. It is scored by the editor-in-chief role.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, eic role).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue at all and whether it advances something original and consequential for that venue's readership, as opposed to restating settled material. It is scored by the editor-in-chief role.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether constructs, instruments, and technical terms are defined and used as the field defines them; whether cited prior work is described faithfully and supports what it is cited for; whether reported results, effect directions, and magnitudes are internally consistent and consistent with established evidence; whether the strength of the causal or generalisability language matches what the stated design and data can support.
what_triggers_block: One or more substantive domain errors that a knowledgeable reader would have to correct before trusting the paper — for example a core construct or instrument defined incorrectly, a cited source misrepresented in a way that props up a central claim, or a headline conclusion that contradicts the paper's own reported evidence — such that a key claim cannot stand as written.
what_triggers_warn: Localised or peripheral inaccuracies that do not topple a central claim — imprecise or drifting terminology, an over-broad characterisation of the prior literature, a missing acknowledgement of well-known contrary evidence, or unhedged causal phrasing around a secondary finding — all correctable by revision without new data.
what_triggers_fatal: Pervasive or unrecoverable factual failure in the domain layer — the central claim is contradicted by settled evidence in the field, reported findings appear fabricated or irreconcilable with the described data, or the paper is built on a misunderstanding of its core construct so fundamental that no revision short of a new study could repair it.
