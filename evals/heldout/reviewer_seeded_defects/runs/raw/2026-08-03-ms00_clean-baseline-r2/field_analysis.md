## Recommended Target Journals (Top 3)

1. **Research in Learning Technology** (ALT, open access) — Best fit. Accepts smaller-scale, single-institution empirical work and has an established shorter-format research-note track. At ~1,900 words the manuscript is closer to a research note than a full article, and this venue can accommodate that honestly rather than forcing padding.
2. **Contemporary Educational Technology** — Q2/Q3, publishes replication-adjacent and single-site survey work in educational technology; would expect the theoretical genealogy (Davis, Venkatesh) to be repaired and the finding positioned against existing meta-analytic estimates.
3. **Journal of Applied Research in Higher Education** (Emerald) — Q2/Q3. Practice-facing framing gives the onboarding implication a legitimate home, but this venue will demand that the practical recommendation be made proportionate to an r of .42 rather than gestured at.

*Stretch tier, currently out of reach*: **International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education** or **AJET** would require log-linked behavioural data, multi-institution sampling, or a longitudinal wave — i.e. a different study, not a revision.

## Reviewer Configuration Cards

### Reviewer Configuration Card #1

**Role**: EIC
**Display role**: Journal-Fit Reviewer
**Identity Description**: Senior Editor of *Research in Learning Technology*, background in higher-education learning-technology evaluation; handles roughly 200 submissions a year and led the journal's introduction of a short empirical research-note format. Routinely triages single-site survey submissions and has written editorially on the difference between a correct finding and a publishable one.
**Review Focus**:
1. **Format and scope fit.** One bivariate coefficient in ~1,900 words: is this an Original Research article or a Research Note? Determine what a learning-technology readership obtains here that it cannot already obtain from existing acceptance-research syntheses at higher precision.
2. **Source verifiability and reporting-standards screening.** All six references share the DOI prefix **10.5555**, a reserved test/demonstration prefix rather than a live registrant, and none of the six journal titles corresponds to an indexed periodical. The study's central methodological warrant — that perceived usefulness was measured with a "previously validated instrument" (Costa & Wren, 2019) — therefore rests on a source that cannot be checked. Separately, screen the missing sampling frame and response rate against survey reporting requirements.
3. **Contribution statement.** "An incremental data point" is an honest description of scale but is not itself a contribution claim. Require an explicit statement of what a reader or institution can now do differently, and judge whether that statement survives contact with the effect size.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether epistemic humility is functioning as a substitute for contribution. A manuscript can be entirely accurate about its own limits and still have nothing a journal needs to publish; conversely, correct calibration at a modest scale is exactly what this journal's note format exists for. The editorial decision is which of those two this is.
**Possible blind spots**: Likely to treat measurement problems as fixable in revision where Reviewer 1 sees them as capping the maximum defensible claim. May also under-credit how unusually clean the statistical reporting is for this tier — the CI, sensitivity statement, and robustness check are better than most submissions at this level, and that deserves to survive into the synthesis.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #2

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 1
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 1
**Identity Description**: Quantitative survey methodologist and psychometrician specialising in self-report measurement quality; substantive interest in common-method variance and single-item measures in technology-use research. Teaches JARS-Quant reporting to doctoral cohorts and reviews method sections for two educational-measurement journals.
**Review Focus**:
1. **The single-item ordinal dependent variable.** Reliability is unknown and unknowable for a one-item measure, so attenuation cannot be estimated; five coarse categories further truncate the observable correlation. The Spearman check addresses monotonicity but not coarseness — a polychoric or polyserial estimate would speak to the latent association. The reported r = .42 is simultaneously a *lower* bound on the latent relationship (attenuation) and potentially an *upper* bound on the substantive one (see focus 2). That two-sided distortion needs discussing, not one robustness coefficient.
2. **Common-method variance — the largest undisclosed threat.** Both variables come from the same instrument, the same respondent, the same occasion, and share response-style variance. There is no marker variable, no Harman single-factor test, no procedural separation, and — critically — no mention of this among the four limitations, which cover generalisability, self-report accuracy, causality, and non-response. Part of the .42 is method, and the paper does not say so.
3. **Sample and data provenance.** 233 responses reduced to 214 with no sampling frame, no eligible-population denominator, and therefore no response rate. There is a logical tension in §3.3–3.1: "no identifying information was collected" and "responses could not be linked back to individual students," yet "5 duplicate entries were removed" — by what mechanism were duplicates detected under those conditions? Additionally, α = .88 on the adapted 6-item scale establishes internal consistency but not unidimensionality; no factor structure is reported for *this* sample despite the instrument being adapted rather than used intact. Clarify whether the power statement is a priori or post hoc sensitivity.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether a coefficient reported to two decimals with a confidence interval is defensible when its dependent variable is one coarse ordinal item collected in the same instrument as its predictor.
**Possible blind spots**: May review the paper as though it claimed to measure behaviour, ignoring that the authors explicitly and repeatedly reframe the outcome as *perceived* use — a legitimate narrowing that changes what the critique should demand. Likely to have nothing to say about journal fit, theoretical positioning, or practical value; will not notice that the same-instrument problem also has a design remedy that costs the authors nothing to acknowledge.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #3

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 2
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 2
**Identity Description**: Technology-acceptance researcher in higher education working in the TAM/UTAUT lineage; has published meta-analytic syntheses of acceptance research in educational settings and studies mandatory- versus voluntary-use boundary conditions.
**Review Focus**:
1. **Missing theoretical genealogy.** Perceived usefulness is Davis's construct; the extension line runs through Venkatesh & Davis and the UTAUT formulations, with Bhattacherjee for continuance. The manuscript deploys the construct while citing none of the primary sources, leaving "perceived usefulness" defined only by an unverifiable secondary instrument paper. Relatedly, the modelled path PU → use omits behavioural intention, the mediator the parent theory itself specifies; the paper should either invoke the intention step or state why it is bypassed.
2. **No anchor for the incremental claim.** If the stated contribution is "one point in a distribution," the distribution must be named. The paper needs the pooled PU–use estimate from the existing education-sector meta-analyses and an explicit statement of where .42 falls relative to it — inside the interval, at the low tail, wherever. Without that anchor, "comparable with prior work" is an assertion and the incremental framing collapses.
3. **Boundary condition: mandatory use.** LMS engagement in contemporary undergraduate programmes is largely compelled by assessment submission, attendance, and announcements. In mandatory-use contexts, acceptance constructs are known to weaken as predictors. §4 concedes this in a single trailing clause ("including course requirements and assessment schedules") and then proceeds as if the frame were intact. The reviewer should press whether compulsion *bounds* the finding or *invalidates the theoretical frame* — those call for different revisions.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether the study adds anything the field does not already know at greater precision, and whether the construct is theoretically live in a setting where use is not optional.
**Possible blind spots**: Strong risk of recommending scope creep — demanding a full acceptance model, mediators, or additional constructs that the existing 214-response dataset cannot support, which would push the authors toward claims their design cannot carry (the opposite of this manuscript's main virtue). Weak on statistical detail; will likely accept r = .42 at face value.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #4

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 3
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 3
**Identity Description**: Head of learning analytics and institutional research at a public university, information-science background; runs the LMS event-log pipeline into the institutional data warehouse and advises the pro-vice-chancellor on digital onboarding and digital-equity programmes. Reads this literature as a buyer of its conclusions, not a producer of them.
**Review Focus**:
1. **The institution already owns the behavioural data.** Canvas/Moodle/Blackboard event logs sit in the warehouse of the very university surveyed here. The manuscript treats the absence of log data as an inherent limitation (§6) rather than as a choice. Require the authors to state whether log access was sought and refused — if it was refused on governance or ethics grounds, that is itself a reportable finding about institutional data access, and it converts a soft limitation into a hard contribution.
2. **"Accessed the LMS" is not a defined unit.** In practice this bundles background mobile-app sync, a push-notification tap, a single deep link from an email, an instructor-forced quiz window, and a genuine study session. Students answering a five-point frequency stem are each counting something different. Undefined at this level, the outcome cannot be mapped onto anything an institution can manage or move.
3. **Proportionality of the practical implication, and who is missing from the sample.** An r of .42 cannot underwrite an onboarding-investment recommendation; specify what an institution would change on Monday and at what cost, or withdraw the implication to a research agenda. Then ask who occupies the low-perceived-usefulness tail — commuters, part-time, first-generation, students on poor connectivity. Voluntary recruitment through the course-announcement channel structurally excludes precisely the students least engaged with institutional channels. §6 notes this as an overrepresentation problem for the estimate; the sharper point is that it removes the population the recommendation is aimed at.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether §5's onboarding paragraph is proportionate to the evidence behind it, and whether the students an institution most needs to understand were systematically absent from the sample.
**Possible blind spots**: May demand log-linked data the authors are contractually or ethically barred from obtaining, and may undervalue single-site confirmatory evidence as a legitimate genre — practitioners tend to dismiss incremental replication that the field actually needs.

## Review Strategy Recommendations

**1. Resolve reference integrity before any substantive literature verdict.** All six DOIs use the reserved 10.5555 test prefix and no journal title resolves to an indexed periodical. This is upstream of two other reviewers' work: Reviewer 1's assessment of instrument validity depends entirely on Costa & Wren (2019) being a real validation study, and Reviewer 2's positioning critique depends on knowing what the cited literature actually says. If the sources are unverifiable, the correct action is an author query, **not** a substantive verdict on the literature base. Assign ownership to the Journal-Fit Reviewer; forbid the other three from silently assuming the citations are sound.

**2. Separate three distinct questions — the manuscript performs differently on each.** (a) What claims can this design support? (b) What claims does the paper actually make? (c) What claims will a reader infer? Alignment between (a) and (b) is genuinely good and unusually disciplined — correlational language is held throughout, causality is refused twice, the outcome is reframed as *perceived* use. The exposure is at (c), in the §5 onboarding paragraph, which is hedged sentence-by-sentence yet is the only actionable statement in the paper and will be the only thing quoted from it. Reviewers should critique (c) without punishing the paper for the discipline it shows on (a)–(b).

**3. Disclosure is not remediation — but it does change the remedy.** The paper pre-empts four of its limitations. That earns credit for calibration; it does not neutralise the underlying design constraints. Instruct reviewers to state, for each disclosed limitation, whether the appropriate remedy is *reframing* (retier, reformat as a research note, soften the implication) or *new data* (logs, second site, second wave). Conflating those two produces unactionable reviews.

**4. Enforce the R1/R3 boundary on self-report versus logs — the one real overlap risk.** Reviewer 1 owns the *psychometric consequence* (attenuation, common-method variance, what the coefficient can mean). Reviewer 3 owns the *data-availability and construct-definition* question (why logs were not used, what counts as "use," whether the result is actionable). If both drift into the other's lane, the synthesis will double-count a single issue and inflate its apparent severity.

**5. Watch the additive-rejection failure mode.** Predicted spread: R1 major revision or reject-and-resubmit on method variance; R2 major revision on theoretical anchoring; R3 major revision plus reframing; Journal-Fit likely retier or reformat as a research note. Four "major revisions" on four non-overlapping axes sum to a de facto rejection that no individual reviewer stated or intended. The synthesiser must decide explicitly whether the union of requested changes is achievable with the existing dataset — if it is not, say so directly rather than letting arithmetic deliver the verdict.

**6. Register: developmental. Verdict: evidence-based.** The split maturity (pre-submission form, first-draft contribution) means the wording should be constructive and the authors should be told what is working, because a fair amount is. That governs tone only. The recommendation itself must be judged against the criteria, not softened to match the register.

**7. Minor items no card owns — assign to the synthesiser's editorial pass.** The abstract reports r and p but drops the confidence interval that the body supplies. §4 describes "the proportion of variance shared by the two measures" as "modest" without ever giving the number (r² ≈ .18); reviewers will ask, and stating it strengthens rather than weakens the paper. §3.1's "spanned all four year levels" is asserted with no demographic table, so the sample cannot be compared to any other. None of these is decisive; all are cheap to fix and their omission slightly undercuts the transparency the manuscript is otherwise trading on.
