## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical reporting, and reproducibility affordances clear the ordinary peer-review bar for the field. Read from my seat as the cross-disciplinary reader, methodology matters because readers from adjacent fields cannot re-derive a result they cannot see the machinery of; but I do not own this dimension and will not score it. My interest is limited to noting whether methodological choices are described in terms an outside-field reader could evaluate, and I will leave the substantive adequacy judgement to the owning role.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by the domain reviewer).** This dimension checks that claims track current domain evidence, that prior work is represented faithfully, and that domain-specific terminology and reported results contain no factual errors. From a cross-disciplinary angle, terminology accuracy and correct attribution are exactly what an adjacent-field reader is least able to verify independently, so errors here propagate silently outward. I nonetheless do not score this dimension; the domain specialist holds it, and I will defer to that role's verdict rather than substituting my own reading of the literature.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the devil's-advocate role, also open to methodology).** This dimension asks whether the central thesis holds together internally, whether the evidence actually bears the weight of the claims placed on it, and whether any fallacy undermines the core argument. Cross-disciplinary accessibility and argumentative coherence are neighbours but not the same thing: a paper can be beautifully legible to outsiders while resting on a non-sequitur, or airtight while impenetrable. I am not an eligible scorer here and will not encroach on it.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by me as the perspective reviewer).** This is my dimension. It asks whether the framing, the operational definitions, and the stated implications are genuinely usable by a reader trained in an adjacent field rather than only by insiders, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplinary boundaries is actually substantiated rather than merely asserted. Concretely, I am judging two things at once: legibility (are constructs, instruments, acronyms, and background assumptions explained well enough that a competent outsider can follow the reasoning and see where the boundaries of the claim lie?) and warrant for reach (when the paper says its findings speak to some other domain, population, or practice, is that bridge supported by what was actually measured?). Overclaiming outward is the characteristic failure mode I am looking for, and it is distinct from being merely obscure.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension covers manuscript organisation, clarity of exposition, quality of figures and tables, and conformity to venue conventions. It overlaps my concern with legibility but is not identical: prose can be clean and conventionally formatted while still assuming insider knowledge, and conversely a poorly organised manuscript can still define its terms hospitably. Scoring this belongs to the EiC role, so I will not produce a score for it and will avoid double-penalising presentation issues under my own dimension.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it makes an original, significant contribution for that readership. Cross-disciplinary reach is one input to significance, but fit and novelty are editorial judgements about the venue's scope and the state of the field, not about outside-field accessibility. I do not score it and will not let my reading of interdisciplinary breadth stand in for an originality verdict.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether central constructs, instruments, and acronyms are defined on first substantive use; whether the population, setting, and measurement basis are stated precisely enough for an adjacent-field reader to locate the claim's scope; whether background assumptions imported from the home field are made explicit rather than presumed; and whether every implication reaching beyond the studied population, discipline, or practice context is tied to something the study actually measured, with limits on generalisation stated in the paper's own voice.
what_triggers_block: An interdisciplinary or practice-facing claim is asserted with no substantiating link to the reported evidence — for example, implications addressed to a different population, discipline, setting, or causal question than the one measured, with no acknowledged limitation — or core constructs are used throughout as if self-evident so that an adjacent-field reader cannot determine what was actually measured.
what_triggers_warn: Definitions and scope are recoverable but require effort from an outside-field reader — undefined jargon or acronyms in a small number of places, implications stated somewhat more broadly than the design supports yet hedged or bounded elsewhere, or an interdisciplinary framing that is plausible but thinly supported rather than unsupported.
