## Review Body

This manuscript is unusually well-defended against the objections a devil's advocate normally reaches for first. It does not launder a correlation into a causal claim; it names the reverse pathway explicitly and attributes the caution to a cited source; it reports the coefficient with a confidence interval, a rank-order robustness check, a sensitivity statement, and the sample size; and its Limitations section anticipates single-site generalisation, self-report validity, temporality, and voluntary response. I want to record that plainly, because it constrains how far my objections can go. I looked for the failure modes that would justify a fatal or block reading on this dimension — a headline claim contradicted by the paper's own numbers, a mechanistic inference smuggled in behind associational language, a circular thesis — and I did not find them. Nothing here alone reaches rejection level, which is why my CRITICAL band is empty rather than populated for form's sake.

What I did find is a pattern the paper's own text sets up and then does not honour. In §2 the authors commit to a specific epistemic boundary: the outcome variable is an indicator of *perceived* use, "rather than a behavioral count." That commitment is load-bearing, because Vasquez (2020) is invoked precisely to establish that self-report and behaviour diverge. Yet the Abstract's terminal sentence and the Discussion both revert to "engagement" — a behavioural construct — as the thing perceived usefulness "tracks with." Either the §2 boundary is real, in which case the finding concerns two perceptions and the word "engagement" must be struck from the Abstract and Discussion; or the boundary is decorative. The manuscript cannot have it both ways in the two most-read sections.

That slippage exposes the objection the paper never confronts. If the outcome is a perception rather than a behaviour, then both variables in the headline correlation are self-reports elicited from the same respondent in the same instrument at the same sitting. A shared evaluative disposition — students who feel positively about the platform report both higher usefulness and higher frequency — is a rival account of the *magnitude* of r = .42, not merely of its direction. The paper cites Vasquez only as a limit on generalising to behaviour, and treats the divergence as noise pushing the estimate around; it never asks whether common method inflates it. This is, in my judgement, the strongest available objection to the paper's own reasoning, and the manuscript neither raises nor answers it.

The second structural problem is that the paper's only claimed contribution is comparability, and comparability is never performed. §2 positions the work as "an incremental data point, comparable with prior work"; §5 and §7 assert the result is "consistent with prior work." No prior effect size appears anywhere in the manuscript. Worse, §2 itself states that effect sizes "vary across samples and instruments" and that Song (2018) found strengths varying by institution. Under the paper's own premises, "consistent with prior research" is unfalsifiable: any positive coefficient would satisfy it. The terminal conclusion therefore rests on a comparison the reader cannot check.

Third, the Discussion contains a genuine internal tension rather than a merely soft hedge. The authors state that the reverse pathway is "equally consistent with the data," and then recommend onboarding designed to raise perceived usefulness. If direction is indeterminate, an intervention on the predictor has *no* support from these data — not weak support. The hedge "suggested by, not proven by" softens the assertion without repairing the inference, and citing a practitioner account (Whitfield, 2019) imports outside warrant for a step this design forbids. Either drop the implication or explain how it survives the indeterminacy the same paragraph concedes.

Fourth, and this bears directly on what population the headline number describes: §1 states the LMS hosts "announcements," and §3.1 states the survey was distributed through "the institution's course-announcement channel." The manuscript never discloses whether these are the same system. If they are, recruitment was conditioned on the dependent variable, and students in the lowest use categories were structurally unlikely to be reached. Limitation four concedes only that "students who engage more with institutional channels may be overrepresented," which understates a selection-on-the-outcome problem and treats it as affecting levels rather than the correlation. The reader cannot bound this because §4 reports only a median category and never the distribution across the five response options. Absent that distribution and a clear statement of the recruitment channel, the inference from this sample to "undergraduates at one university" is not licensed by the design.

Fifth, §3.1 reports that "5 duplicate entries were removed" while §3.3 states that no identifying information was collected and responses "could not be linked back to individual students." Duplicate detection requires some persistent identifier or fingerprint. As written these two subsections contradict each other, and the contradiction touches a claim made to an ethics committee, so it needs resolution rather than trimming.

Two further items I record without elevating them. §4 paraphrases shared variance in words ("accordingly modest") where the corresponding value from the reported coefficient is roughly eighteen percent; giving the number would be more candid than the adjective, and adjectives are where over-reading starts. And §3.1 reports 233 responses against an eligibility frame of all enrolled undergraduates with no denominator, so the response rate — the quantity that would let a reader size the selection concern — is absent, while "spanned all four year levels" is offered where a distribution is needed.

Taken together, these are repairable by reframing, additional descriptive reporting, and one re-analysis; none forces a different study or a different thesis. The core finding — a moderate positive association between two self-reported measures in one truncatable sample — survives. That is why this dimension warns rather than blocks.
