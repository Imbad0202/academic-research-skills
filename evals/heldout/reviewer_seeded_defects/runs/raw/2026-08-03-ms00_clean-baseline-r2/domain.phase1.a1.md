## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role):** This dimension asks whether the study's design and execution would survive scrutiny by a competent methodologist in the manuscript's field: was the sampling and data-collection strategy appropriate to the question, were the data handled and cleaned transparently, are statistical procedures reported with the detail a reader needs (estimates, uncertainty, assumptions, model specification), and has the author supplied enough material — instruments, analysis scripts, data availability statements — that another team could in principle repeat the work. It is a mandatory dimension, so a serious defect here can sink the manuscript on its own. As the domain reviewer I do not score it, though domain knowledge may inform what I flag narratively.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role):** This is my owned dimension. It asks whether every substantive claim the manuscript makes about its subject matter is consistent with the current state of evidence in that field, whether the cited and paraphrased prior literature is represented faithfully rather than bent to fit the author's narrative, and whether field-specific constructs, instruments, definitions, and reported quantities are used with their accepted technical meanings. Errors of this kind include misattributing a finding to a source that does not contain it, treating a contested or superseded position as settled consensus, silently redefining an established construct, misnaming or misapplying a standard instrument or theoretical model, and internally inconsistent or arithmetically impossible domain quantities. Because it is mandatory, a fatal-level failure implies rejection.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, discourse-analysis role):** This dimension concerns whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together logically from introduction to conclusion: whether the evidence actually presented supports the strength of the conclusions drawn, whether inferential leaps (for example from association to causation, or from a narrow sample to a general population) are marked and justified, and whether any reasoning defects are severe enough to undermine the main argument rather than merely a side remark. It is owned by the discourse-analysis reviewer with methodology as an alternate; I do not score it.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high, perspective role):** This dimension checks whether readers from neighbouring fields can enter the paper: are terms defined at first use, is the framing intelligible without insider tacit knowledge, and where the manuscript claims relevance or implications for another discipline, is that claim actually substantiated rather than asserted decoratively. It is high priority but not mandatory, and it is scored by the perspective reviewer, not by me.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal, editor role):** This dimension covers craft and presentation: whether the sections are organised so the argument unfolds in a usable order, whether exposition is clear and economical, whether figures and tables are legible, correctly labelled, and self-sufficient, and whether the manuscript observes the target venue's formatting, length, and reporting conventions. It carries normal priority and belongs to the editor-in-chief role.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, editor role):** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue at all and whether it advances knowledge enough to warrant that venue's pages: is the topic within scope for the readership, is the contribution genuinely novel relative to existing published work, and is its significance proportionate to the venue's selectivity. It is mandatory but scored by the editor-in-chief role, so I do not assign it a score.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether each domain claim matches current field evidence, whether cited prior work is characterised as that work actually reports it, whether field constructs, instruments, theoretical models, and reported quantities are named and used with their established technical meanings, and whether domain numbers are mutually consistent across text, tables, and abstract.
what_triggers_block: One or more clearly demonstrable domain errors that materially affect a headline claim — for example a construct or standard instrument misdefined or misapplied in a way that changes what the results mean, a cited source represented as supporting a conclusion it does not support, or a contested position asserted as settled consensus with no acknowledgement of the counter-evidence — where correction would require substantive rewriting of the claims but the study itself remains salvageable.
what_triggers_warn: Localised domain inaccuracies or imprecisions that do not overturn any headline claim — for example loose or non-standard terminology, an outdated or thin representation of the relevant literature, a missing distinction between related constructs, or a citation that is only tangentially apt — all correctable by targeted revision of wording and references.
what_triggers_fatal: Pervasive or foundational domain error such that the manuscript's central subject-matter premise is wrong — for example the core construct or measurement being fundamentally misconceived, results reported that are internally impossible or contradict the paper's own data, or prior literature systematically misrepresented or fabricated — so that no revision short of a different study could rescue the contribution.
