## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical reporting, and reproducibility supports would survive scrutiny from a competent peer reviewer in the paper's home field. From my cross-disciplinary vantage point, the relevance is that methodological choices are the main thing an adjacent-field reader has to take on trust: if the sampling frame, measurement instruments, model specification, and analysis code are not described in terms a reader outside the immediate subfield can follow and re-execute, the work's credibility does not travel beyond its home community. I do not score this dimension, but I read it as the baseline that makes any interdisciplinary claim inspectable rather than merely assertable.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role).** This dimension covers whether claims match the current state of evidence in the paper's own field, whether prior work is characterised fairly, and whether domain terminology and reported results are factually correct. Read through a cross-disciplinary lens, accuracy inside the home domain is the precondition for accuracy outside it: a term used loosely at home becomes a false friend when an adjacent-field reader imports it under their own definition. The specialist reviewer owns this judgement; my interest is only that the vocabulary is stable enough that borrowing it elsewhere does not silently change its meaning.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, da/methodology roles).** This dimension asks whether the central thesis holds together, whether the evidence presented actually supports the claims made, and whether any reasoning gaps or fallacies undercut the main argument. For a reader coming from a neighbouring discipline, coherence is the load-bearing element, because such a reader lacks the tacit background that lets insiders bridge unstated inferential steps. An argument that leans on shared assumptions never made explicit may look sound to specialists and simply broken to everyone else. Scoring belongs to the discourse-analysis and methodology roles; I treat coherence as context for judging whether the framing genuinely communicates.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, perspective role — mine).** This is the dimension I own. It asks whether the manuscript's framing, definitions, and stated implications are usable by readers in adjacent fields, and whether any claim the paper makes about relevance beyond its own subfield is actually substantiated rather than gestured at. Two failure modes matter symmetrically: hermetic writing that locks out competent neighbouring readers through unglossed jargon, undeclared measurement conventions, or unexplained venue-specific shorthand; and overreach, where the paper asserts significance for other disciplines, for practice, or for policy without evidence that the result transfers under those fields' own standards. I look for explicit scope conditions, defined constructs, honest statements of what does and does not generalise, and implications sized to the evidence actually presented.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, eic role).** This dimension concerns organisation, clarity of exposition, the quality and legibility of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's conventions. Its cross-disciplinary bearing is direct but distinct from mine: structural clarity determines how much effort an outside reader must spend locating the claim, the evidence, and the caveats, while my dimension concerns whether those elements, once located, are intelligible and appropriately bounded. The editor-in-chief role scores this; I do not.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, eic role).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it offers an original, meaningful contribution to that venue's readership. It intersects with cross-disciplinary relevance without duplicating it: a paper may be perfectly accessible to adjacent fields yet contribute nothing new to its target readership, or may be a genuine contribution that is nonetheless written so that only insiders can extract it. The editorial role adjudicates fit and novelty; my scope stops at whether reach claims are substantiated and whether the exposition serves readers beyond the core audience.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Explicit definitions of key constructs and outcome measures at first use, stated scope and boundary conditions for the findings, transparent description of setting and population that lets an adjacent-field reader judge transferability, glossed field-specific jargon and abbreviations, and implications for other disciplines, practice, or policy that are each traceable to evidence actually reported in the manuscript.
what_triggers_block: Central interdisciplinary or practical claims are asserted with no supporting evidence or mechanism, or core constructs remain undefined and operationally opaque to the point that an adjacent-field reader cannot determine what was measured or whether the conclusion applies outside the single studied setting.
what_triggers_warn: Framing is broadly followable but weakened by localised problems such as a handful of unglossed terms, scope conditions that are implied rather than stated, or implications for adjacent fields that outrun the evidence in emphasis while still resting on some reported result.
