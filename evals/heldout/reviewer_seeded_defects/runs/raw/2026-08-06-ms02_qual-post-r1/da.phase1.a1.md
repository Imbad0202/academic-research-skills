## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** The contract demands that design, sampling, data handling, statistical or analytic reporting, and reproducibility affordances clear the ordinary peer-review bar for the field. Read adversarially, this dimension asks whether a sceptic given only the manuscript could tell what was actually done, to whom, and with what analytic decisions — and could in principle repeat it. Absent instrumentation, undisclosed sampling logic, unreported analytic procedure, or claims outrunning the evidentiary design are the failure modes this dimension exists to catch. I am not eligible to score it, so my obligation is to avoid poaching its territory while noticing when a methodological hole is really an argument-structure hole in disguise.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by the domain reviewer).** This dimension requires that domain claims track current evidence, that prior work is represented as its authors would recognise it, and that field-specific terminology and reported results contain no factual errors. The adversarial reading is that citation-as-decoration, straw-man renderings of prior positions, and terminology used in a locally invented sense all count as inaccuracy even when they read smoothly. Scoring belongs to the domain reviewer; my discipline is to flag only where an apparent domain error is load-bearing for the central argument, and to leave the verdict to the owner.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, my dimension).** The contract obliges me to test whether the core thesis holds together on its own terms: whether the stated claims are the claims actually defended, whether the evidence adduced is the kind of evidence that could support those claims, and whether any fallacy or gap severs the chain from data to conclusion. Adversarially, I am hunting for thesis drift between abstract and conclusion, conclusions whose strength exceeds the warrant offered, circularity where a framework is both assumption and finding, undefended causal language attached to non-causal evidence, unfalsifiable framing, and contradictions between sections that a reader following the argument linearly would trip over. Coherence is not the same as fluency; a well-written manuscript can be incoherent, and a rough one can be tight. My job is to score the argument's skeleton, not its prose.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high, owned by the perspective reviewer).** The requirement is that framing, definitions, and implications be legible to adjacent-field readers, and that any interdisciplinary claim be substantiated rather than asserted by analogy. The adversarial reading is that unglossed jargon and borrowed constructs used without their originating field's constraints both degrade relevance. Not mine to score; I will only note where a definitional ambiguity is simultaneously an argumentative equivocation.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal, owned by the EiC).** This covers organisation, clarity of exposition, quality and necessity of figures and tables, and conformity to venue conventions. Adversarially, structure failures include sections that do not do the work their headings promise, and exhibits that are decorative rather than evidentiary. I do not score it, and I must resist letting stylistic irritation contaminate a coherence judgement.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the EiC).** The contract requires genuine fit with the configured venue plus an original, significant contribution for that readership. The adversarial question is whether anything here is new, and whether the novelty claimed is novelty delivered rather than a restatement of settled knowledge in fresh vocabulary. Scoring rests with the EiC; my contribution is limited to noting when a novelty claim is argumentatively unsupported, which is a D3 matter, without pronouncing on fit.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: A single identifiable thesis that is stated, defended, and restated consistently across abstract, body, and conclusion; explicit linkage from each presented item of evidence to the specific claim it is meant to license; conclusion strength calibrated to the strength and type of evidence offered; internally consistent use of key constructs; acknowledged limits that actually constrain the claims made; and absence of circular, equivocal, or causally overreaching reasoning.
what_triggers_block: The central conclusion does not follow from the evidence presented — for example, causal or generalising claims are asserted on evidence structurally incapable of supporting them, the thesis in the conclusion differs materially from the thesis defended in the body, or a key construct shifts meaning mid-argument so that the argument only appears to close.
what_triggers_warn: The main argumentative chain survives scrutiny but has localised defects — one or more secondary claims are asserted without supporting evidence, hedging is inconsistent so some passages overclaim relative to the paper's own stated limits, or a section's evidence is presented without explicit articulation of what it is meant to establish.
what_triggers_fatal: There is no recoverable argument to evaluate — the manuscript contains mutually contradictory core claims with no reconciliation, or its thesis is framed so that no evidence could count against it, such that the reasoning cannot be repaired by revision and the conclusions must be withdrawn rather than qualified.
