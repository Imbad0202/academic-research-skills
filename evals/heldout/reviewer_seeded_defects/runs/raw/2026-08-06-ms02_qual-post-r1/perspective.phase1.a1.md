## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data handling, statistical or qualitative analysis, and reproducibility affordances clear the bar that a peer reviewer in this field would apply. Read through a cross-disciplinary lens, the question I care about is whether the methods are described in terms that a reader trained in an adjacent field — policy analysis, organisational sociology, evaluation science — could recognise, audit, and in principle repeat, rather than in terms whose meaning is only stable inside one sub-community. I do not score this dimension, but its outcome constrains how much interpretive weight any cross-field implication can carry.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by the domain reviewer).** Here the test is whether the manuscript's claims match current evidence in its home domain, whether prior work is represented faithfully rather than strawmanned or over-credited, and whether domain-specific terminology and reported results are factually correct. From my angle, domain accuracy is the precondition for any borrowing across fields: if a term of art is used loosely at home, an adjacent-field reader will import the looseness without noticing it. I do not score this dimension, but I will treat its findings as authoritative when judging whether definitions travel safely.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the discourse-analysis reviewer, also eligible for methodology).** This dimension checks that the central thesis holds together internally, that the evidence actually supports the claims made from it, and that no fallacy or non-sequitur hollows out the main argument. My cross-disciplinary concern overlaps here without duplicating it: an argument can be internally coherent for insiders yet rest on premises left tacit because everyone in the field shares them, which is exactly what breaks when the paper is read from outside. I do not score D3; I confine myself to whether the argument is legible beyond its home audience.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by me).** This is my dimension. It asks whether the framing, the definitions, and the stated implications are accessible to readers in adjacent fields, and whether any claim the paper makes about relevance beyond its own discipline is actually substantiated rather than asserted. Two distinct failure modes sit inside it: inaccessibility, where the paper is intelligible only to specialists because key constructs, contexts, or institutional arrangements go undefined; and overreach, where the paper asserts import for other fields, other national systems, or other organisational settings without evidence that would license the transfer. A good result on this dimension means an informed outsider can state what was claimed, on what basis, and where the boundary of the claim lies.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This covers organisation, clarity of exposition, quality of figures and tables, and conformity with venue conventions. It touches my concern because poor structure and unclear exposition are a common proximate cause of inaccessibility to adjacent-field readers, but the two are analytically separate: a well-organised paper can still be conceptually closed to outsiders, and an awkwardly written one can still define its terms fully. I do not score D5 and will not smuggle prose-quality complaints into my own dimension.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension judges whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it offers an original, significant contribution suited to that readership. It is adjacent to mine — a venue with an interdisciplinary readership raises the practical stakes of accessibility — but the judgement of fit and originality belongs to the editorial role, not to me. I do not score D6 and will not convert a cross-disciplinary accessibility finding into a verdict about novelty or venue suitability.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether core constructs (quality culture, quality assurance, institutional/administrator roles) are explicitly defined rather than assumed; whether the governance, regulatory, and national/system context is specified well enough for an outsider to locate the study; whether any claim of relevance to adjacent fields, other sectors, or other systems is backed by cited evidence or explicit boundary conditions; and whether implications are stated at a level of generality the evidence actually licenses.
what_triggers_block: Central constructs or the institutional/system context are left undefined or used inconsistently such that an adjacent-field reader cannot determine what was studied, or the paper asserts transferable implications for other fields, sectors, or national systems with no supporting evidence and no stated scope limits.
what_triggers_warn: Constructs and context are recoverable but require insider knowledge in places — undefined field jargon, unglossed acronyms, or implicit institutional assumptions — or interdisciplinary implications are gestured at with thin or one-sided support while the core claims remain scoped and legible.
