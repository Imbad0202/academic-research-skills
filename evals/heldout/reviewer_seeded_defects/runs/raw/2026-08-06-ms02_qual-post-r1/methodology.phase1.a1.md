## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, my ownership).** From a methodology standpoint, this dimension asks whether the study's design actually licenses the inferences drawn from it: is the research question matched to an appropriate design; are sampling frame, recruitment, and unit of analysis specified; is data collection instrumentation documented; are analytic procedures (coding scheme, inter-rater agreement, statistical model, assumption checks) reported at a level that a competent reader in higher education policy research could scrutinise or repeat? It also asks about reproducibility affordances — availability of instruments, codebooks, anonymised data, or at minimum a transparent audit trail — and about honest handling of missingness, non-response, and limitations. The bar is the field's ordinary peer-review bar, not an idealised one, but the burden of demonstrating rigor sits with the manuscript, not with the reviewer's charity.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by the domain reviewer).** This concerns whether substantive claims about quality assurance policy, accreditation regimes, and prior scholarship are factually correct and current, whether cited work is represented as its authors would recognise it, and whether domain-specific terminology and reported results are used accurately. I am not scoring this dimension; where methodological weakness could masquerade as a domain error (for example, a mis-specified construct being read as a mis-stated fact), I will flag it under my own dimensions and leave the domain judgement to the owner.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the discourse-analysis reviewer; I am also eligible).** Methodologically, coherence means the chain from data to warrant to conclusion holds without gaps: the evidence presented is of the type and strength that the stated conclusion requires; the thesis does not shift between sections; qualifiers used in the results are not dropped in the discussion; and no inferential fallacy — correlation read as cause, sample-specific findings generalised without justification, absence of evidence read as evidence of absence — carries the central argument. My contribution here is specifically the evidence-to-claim fit rather than rhetorical structure.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high, owned by the perspective reviewer).** This asks whether framing, definitions, and stated implications are intelligible and useful to readers in adjacent fields, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplines is actually substantiated rather than asserted. I am not scoring it. If an interdisciplinary claim rests on a method the manuscript did not run, that is a methodology or coherence problem and I will record it there.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This covers organisation, exposition clarity, quality and self-sufficiency of figures and tables, and conformity to venue conventions. I am not scoring it. Note the boundary: a table that is merely ugly is D5, whereas a table whose numbers do not reconcile with the reported analysis is evidence under D1.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and offers an original, significant contribution to its readership. I am not scoring it. Methodological adequacy is a necessary but not sufficient input to that judgement, and I will not substitute my own view of importance for the owner's.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor

dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: Explicit design statement and rationale; described sampling frame, recruitment route, participant count and characteristics; documented instruments or interview protocol; stated analytic procedure with coding/reliability or statistical model and assumption checks; reported handling of non-response and missing data; ethics/consent approval; reproducibility affordances such as available protocol, codebook, or anonymised data; a limitations section whose stated scope matches the actual design; and internal arithmetic consistency between text, tables, and reported totals.
what_triggers_block: A load-bearing methodological element is absent or unreconstructable — for example no stated sample size or selection procedure, no described analytic procedure for how the reported findings were derived, quantitative claims with no test statistics or dispersion, or numbers that contradict each other — such that the reported results cannot be evaluated or reproduced as written, yet the deficit is in principle repairable by supplying the missing information from work already done.
what_triggers_warn: The design and analysis are recoverable and broadly appropriate, but rigor falls short of the field bar in bounded ways — thin reporting of coder training or agreement, no reflexivity or positionality statement where the design requires one, single-source self-report treated as sufficient without triangulation, absent effect sizes alongside significance tests, or reproducibility materials described as available but not actually specified.
what_triggers_fatal: The design cannot in principle support the study's central inference and no additional reporting could rescue it — for example the stated conclusion requires comparison, change over time, or causal identification that the data collection never included; findings appear to be asserted without any traceable data collection at all; or there are signs of fabricated, duplicated, or ethically improper data that would require the study to be re-run rather than re-written.

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: A stable, explicitly stated thesis; conclusions whose strength matches the evidence type actually collected; qualifiers and scope conditions carried consistently from results into discussion, abstract, and title; explicit treatment of disconfirming or discrepant cases; causal language used only where the design identifies causes; recommendations and policy implications traceable to specific reported findings rather than to background literature or the authors' priors.
what_triggers_block: A central claim is not supported by the evidence presented — the abstract, discussion, or recommendations assert generality, causality, or effectiveness that the reported data do not establish — or a substantive inconsistency exists between sections (results say one thing, discussion another) that a reader cannot resolve, while the underlying study could still support a properly narrowed argument after rewriting.
what_triggers_warn: The core argument holds but its articulation leaks in localised ways — occasional over-reach in wording, uneven acknowledgement of alternative explanations, findings presented without engaging plausible counter-interpretations, or one or two recommendations that outrun the specific evidence cited for them without destabilising the overall thesis.
what_triggers_fatal: The paper's central argument is self-defeating or circular — the conclusion is presupposed by the framing and the analysis cannot in principle bear on it, or two claims essential to the thesis are mutually contradictory — so that no revision short of a different argument built on different evidence could repair it.
