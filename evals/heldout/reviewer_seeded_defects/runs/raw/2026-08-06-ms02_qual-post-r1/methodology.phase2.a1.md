## Review Body

This manuscript addresses a question worth asking — how the people who operate QA machinery construct meaning around it — and the interview material it does report is vivid. My difficulty is that, as written, I cannot establish how many people were interviewed, how their accounts were analysed, what the survey measured, or what test produced the one inferential claim in the paper. Those are not stylistic gaps; they are the reporting on which any assessment of trustworthiness depends.

Taking the qualitative strand first. §3.4 tells me transcripts were "analyzed thematically" with iterative coding "until a stable structure was reached." That sentence is compatible with Braun and Clarke's reflexive thematic analysis, with a codebook/framework approach, and with content analysis — three traditions with incompatible criteria for what counts as a defensible theme and for whether coder agreement is even a meaningful concept. Without knowing which tradition is claimed, I have no standard against which to judge the analysis, and neither will a reader. Nothing is reported about who coded, whether more than one person did, what if any codebook or audit trail exists, or how the authors positioned themselves relative to institutional QA offices that also acted as their recruitment gatekeepers. "A stable structure was reached" is a restatement of the conclusion that saturation occurred, not evidence for it.

Against that already thin backdrop, §4.3 discloses that participants who dissented from the value of the QA enterprise were dropped because they "fell outside the three-theme structure." Two sentences earlier the same paragraph claims the analysis captured "the full range of administrator views." In every qualitative tradition I know, disconfirming cases are the strongest available test of a theme structure; removing them because they do not fit is the operation that theme structures are supposed to survive, not the one that produces them. This single move undoes the inductive warrant behind the word "emerged" and falsifies the coverage claim made in the same paragraph. It is the most serious defect in the paper.

I classify D1 as a repairable rather than a fatal block, and I want the basis to be visible. The interviews happened, they were recorded and transcribed, and the transcripts presumably still exist, so re-analysing them with the dissenting accounts included, reporting the analytic procedure, and supplying the survey instrument are all acts of re-analysis and re-reporting from work already done. That is what separates this from a design that could never support its inference. The exception I would flag for the panel is the confidentiality problem in W7: repairing it may require returning to participants rather than only editing text, and that judgement is not mine alone.

On the quantitative strand, the paper reports M=3.9, SD=0.6 for "institutional quality culture" and a p<.05 subgroup difference. I attempted the four bounded recomputations documented in the Arithmetic Receipts section below and completed none of them, because the paper supplies no item count, no response-scale anchors, no test family, no test statistic, and no degrees of freedom. This matters beyond consistency-checking: without anchors I cannot tell whether 3.9 is "moderately positive" or the exact midpoint of a seven-point scale, so the abstract's characterisation is an interpretation of a number whose scale is undisclosed. The construct was scored, not measured. Note separately that 9 + 11 accounts for 20 of the 48 respondents with the third institution's cell never reported; that reconciliation failure is a reporting-consistency finding (W6), not a bounded recompute, and I have kept it out of the receipts accordingly.

Finally, on integration. §3.1 declares strand priority, which is more than many submissions do, but declaring priority is not integrating. There is no joint display, no stated point of interface, and no analytic operation anywhere in §4.4 or §5 that links a specific survey result to a specific theme. The survey's relation to the interviews is asserted in two directions at once — "corroborated," then contradicted expectations — within one paragraph. As it stands this is a qualitative study with a descriptive survey appended, and the "mixed-methods" label in the title and abstract is not yet earned.

My D3 score reflects the evidence-to-claim fit rather than rhetoric. Three institutions in one national system are read in §5 as "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture"; §4.1 uses protocol fixity to rule out site-specific artefact, which it cannot do; and §5's use of Delacroix directly reverses what §2 and the paper's own reference annotation say that author argues. A reader cannot resolve these from the text. I flag the Delacroix reversal as an internal inconsistency and leave the question of whether the source is also misrepresented to the domain seat.

### S1: Purposive multi-site sampling across contrasting institutional types
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.2 "one large public research university, one mid-sized public teaching-focused university, and one private university"

Sampling three institutions of deliberately contrasting mission and control within one regulatory system is the right structural choice for a question about how external requirement is translated locally, and it is what makes the eventual institutional-type contrast in §4.4 conceptually interesting even though it is not yet statistically reportable.

### S2: Strand priority is stated explicitly rather than left implicit
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.1 "The qualitative component was primary; the survey served to contextualize the interview findings"

Many submissions with a dominant qualitative strand never declare dominance, leaving readers to infer it. Declaring it up front is a genuine, if minimal, mixed-methods design commitment and gives reviewers a stated criterion against which to judge the survey's role.

### S3: Interview conduct is documented at a usable level of detail
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.3 "lasting between 45 and 70 minutes, and were audio-recorded and transcribed"

Duration range, modality, recording, and transcription are all specified. This is the one part of the data-collection account that a reader could evaluate or replicate as written.

### S4: Recruitment route and role composition of the interview sample are named
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.2 "quality directors, associate deans, and registry heads" recruited through "institutional QA offices"

Naming the role types establishes that the sample sits at the intended organisational hinge, and naming the recruitment channel is honest reporting even though it also introduces the gatekeeper-selection risk that the paper does not yet discuss.

### W1: Disconfirming accounts were removed to preserve the theme structure while full coverage was claimed
**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "capturing the full range of administrator views on QA implementation"; "these were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure that organized our analysis"
**Confidence**: 5 — I teach negative-case analysis and trustworthiness criteria in a doctoral mixed-methods seminar.

Excluding participants because their accounts fell outside the structure that the analysis was supposed to derive from the accounts inverts the direction of inference. The stated reason ("for space") does not survive the stated criterion ("fell outside the three-theme structure"), which is a substantive analytic exclusion, not an editorial one. Two consequences follow. First, the claim that themes "emerged" is no longer supportable, because the only cases that could have disconfirmed them were removed. Second, the coverage claim in the same paragraph is false as stated. I band this Critical on its own decision impact and not by association with W2: uncorrected, it invalidates the paper's central empirical result — the three-theme structure — regardless of every other finding here. The remedy is re-analysis of the retained transcripts with the dissenting accounts included and reported, including what they contest and whether the structure survives them.

### W2: The interview sample size is reported as two different numbers
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth" vs §3.2 "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct textual comparison, no domain judgement required.

The abstract reports fourteen interviewees and §3.2 reports twelve. One of these is wrong, or two participants were interviewed and then dropped. Because §4.3 discloses that dissenting participants were excluded, the natural reading — that the missing two are the excluded dissenters — is available to any reader and is nowhere addressed. I band this Major rather than Critical because the defect is a reporting failure that correction resolves and the core account can survive it; I do not inherit W1's band merely because the two findings may share a cause. The authors should state the number interviewed, the number analysed, and the disposition of any difference.

### W3: The qualitative analytic procedure is not specified at a reconstructable level
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.4 "Coding proceeded iteratively, moving between transcripts and an emerging set of themes until a stable structure was reached"
**Confidence**: 5 — this is the standard reporting bar in the journals I review for.

The tradition is unnamed, the number of coders is unstated, no codebook or coding tree is described, there is no statement about agreement or about why agreement is or is not an appropriate criterion under the chosen tradition, no audit trail or memoing practice is mentioned, and no positionality statement appears despite recruitment through the QA offices whose work the study evaluates. "Until a stable structure was reached" asserts saturation rather than evidencing it; state instead when new codes ceased to appear, over how many transcripts, and against what stopping rule. This is repairable by reporting, provided the procedures actually existed at the time.

### W4: "Quality culture" is scored by an undocumented instrument with no scale anchors
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.3 survey instrumentation — expected item wording or an appended instrument, the number of items, the response-scale anchors, and any reliability or validity evidence for the quality-culture score; checked §3.3 Data collection, §3.4 Analysis, §4.4 Survey findings, the Abstract, and the reference list for an instrument source
**Confidence**: 4 — applied measurement reporting is within my competence; I cannot rule out an instrument existing but unreported.

Nothing in the manuscript tells me what respondents were asked, how many items there were, what the endpoints of the response scale meant, whether items were aggregated and how, or whether the composite has any internal-consistency or validity evidence. Consequently M=3.9 cannot be interpreted: on a five-point scale it is above the midpoint, on a seven-point scale it is essentially at it, and the abstract's "moderately positive" reading is licensed by neither until the scale is disclosed. This is Major rather than Critical because the paper's core claim is carried by the qualitative strand and would survive the survey's removal; it nonetheless invalidates a claim that currently appears in the abstract.

### W5: A significance claim is made with no test, no statistic, no effect size, and no assumption checks
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "Comparing staff at the research university (n=9) with staff at the teaching-focused university (n=11), we found a statistically significant difference (p<.05)"
**Confidence**: 5 — applied inferential statistics; the omission is unambiguous.

The test family is unnamed, no test statistic or degrees of freedom is given, no group means or SDs are reported, no effect size accompanies the p value, and no distributional or variance assumptions are examined. I could therefore complete no recomputation of this claim (see AR3). At n=9 versus n=11 the authors additionally owe the reader a defensible position on whether any inferential test is warranted rather than a descriptive contrast with an interval estimate: at these cell sizes the result is highly sensitive to one or two respondents, and a bare p<.05 conveys almost nothing about magnitude. Supplying the test, statistic, df, effect size with a confidence interval, and assumption checks is the minimum; reframing the contrast descriptively is the alternative I would find more defensible.

### W6: Sample composition and survey response are not reportable as given
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.2 and §3.3 sample composition — expected the survey population denominator with response rate, subgroup counts by institution and role summing to 48, and interview participant counts by institution and role; checked §3.2 Participants and setting, §3.3 Data collection, §3.4 Analysis, §4.4 Survey findings, and the Abstract
**Confidence**: 5 — routine sampling-reporting check.

The 48 respondents have no stated population denominator and no response rate, so non-response bias cannot be assessed at all. The two subgroups compared in §4.4 account for 20 of the 48; the third institution's cell is never reported, and no role breakdown is given despite §3.3 saying role information was collected and §3.4 saying subgroup comparisons were "examined where role categories permitted." The interview sample is likewise never broken down by institution or role, so claims about patterns holding "across institutions" cannot be checked against how many participants each institution contributed. All of this is repairable from records the study must already hold.

### W7: The anonymisation statement is contradicted by re-identifiable quote attributions
**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified", §4.1 "the quality director of the largest private university in the region"
**Confidence**: 4 — data-handling and confidentiality practice; the consent wording itself is unavailable to me.

§3.5 gives an unqualified assurance that no individual could be identified in reported findings. §4.1 then attributes quotations to the quality director of the largest private university in the region and to the associate dean for quality at the public research university. Given a sample of twelve people drawn from three institutions whose type, size, and sector are all stated, these are single-post roles and the attributions are effectively identifying — and the quoted content is critical of the participants' own institutions. I treat this as squarely methodological rather than as an ethics-committee matter: it is data handling, and it means the manuscript's own account of how the data were protected is unreliable. I band it Critical because, uncorrected, publication is not defensible, and because the repair may not be a matter of editing text alone — if consent did not cover attributed, re-identifiable quotation, the authors may need to return to participants. That possibility is what distinguishes it from the reporting failures above.

### W8: No integration mechanism, so the design is two parallel studies rather than a mixed-methods one
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.1 and §4.4 mixed-methods integration — expected a stated point of interface with a joint display or narrative weaving that links specific survey results to specific themes; checked §3.1 Design, §3.4 Analysis, §4.4 Survey findings, and §5 Discussion
**Confidence**: 5 — integration is the specific literature I teach and review in.

The paper declares strand priority but never specifies where mixing occurs: whether the survey informed the protocol, whether the interviews informed the instrument, whether the strands were analysed concurrently, or how divergence between them would have been handled. §4.4 places qualitative and quantitative material side by side and §5 comments on each in turn; neither performs an integrative operation. A joint display mapping the three themes against the survey dimensions, plus an explicit statement of sequence and interface, is the standard remedy. Until then the title's and abstract's "mixed-methods" claim describes data types rather than a design.

### W9: Findings from three institutions are generalised to the sector
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "these findings demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment rather than a managerial output"; "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture"
**Confidence**: 5 — evidence-to-claim scope is the core of this judgement.

"Demonstrate," "across the sector," and "the higher education sector as a whole" are not supportable by twelve (or fourteen) purposively recruited administrators at three institutions in one national system, particularly after the dissenting accounts were removed. The second sentence explicitly authorises the reader to make the leap ("can be read as"), which compounds rather than hedges the problem. Transferability in this tradition is established by thick description that lets readers judge fit to their own settings, not by assertion of generality; the fix is to narrow the claim to these three institutions and to supply the contextual detail that would let a reader assess transfer.

### W10: The survey is said to corroborate and to contradict the qualitative account in the same paragraph
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "The survey corroborated the qualitative picture"; "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture"
**Confidence**: 5 — internal-consistency reading, no external knowledge required.

Corroboration and disconfirmation are different results with different implications, and the paper claims both about the same comparison within four sentences. If the survey diverged from what the interviews implied, that divergence is the interesting finding and should be handled as such — divergence in mixed-methods work warrants explicit reconciliation, not relabelling as agreement. As written, a reader cannot tell what the authors take the survey to have shown, which is exactly the ambiguity that a joint display (W8) would have forced them to resolve.

### W11: A structured/semi-structured contradiction supports an inference that protocol fixity cannot license
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.1 "semi-structured interviews"; §4.1 "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol"; §4.1 "not an artifact of any single institution's local circumstances"
**Confidence**: 5 — both the contradiction and the invalid inference are on the page.

The methods describe a semi-structured protocol and the findings describe a structured one; these are different instruments with different implications for how far responses were shaped by the interviewer. More consequentially, the passage treats protocol fixity as grounds for ruling out site-specific artefact. It is not: asking the same questions everywhere controls the stimulus, not the local circumstances that shape the answers, and a uniform protocol applied at three sites can just as easily produce a uniform artefact. Ruling out site effects would require analysis by site — which W6 shows is not reported — and even then the sample would not support a strong claim. The word "emerged" also sits awkwardly beside both a fixed protocol and the exclusion documented in W1.

### W12: The discussion attributes to Delacroix the opposite of what the literature review and reference annotation state
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual"; §5 "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture"
**Confidence**: 4 — internal contradiction is certain; whether the source is also misrepresented is for the domain seat.

§2 presents Delacroix (2018) as arguing against treating consultation as sufficient evidence of quality culture, and the reference-list annotation says the same. §5 then aligns the paper's design recommendation with Delacroix as a proponent of consultation as "the engine of internalization." The paper therefore contradicts itself about the source that carries its central practical recommendation, and a reader cannot determine which reading the authors hold. I raise this under evidence-to-claim coherence rather than as an attribution judgement, and defer the latter to the domain reviewer.

### W13: An unsupported priority claim that the paper's own exclusion contradicts
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §6 "This paper has offered the first comprehensive account of how university administrators experience and enact QA implementation"
**Confidence**: 4 — the internal contradiction is clear; the priority claim itself is a domain matter.

No evidence is offered that this is the first such account, and §2 cites Silva and Tan (2021), Pettersen (2022), and Rahman (2020) on closely adjacent ground. "Comprehensive" is separately falsified by §4.3's exclusion of dissenting participants. Deleting the claim costs the paper nothing substantive, which is why I band this Minor rather than allowing it to inherit W1's or W9's severity.

### W14: Moderation language from a cross-sectional two-cell contrast
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "points to institutional type as a possible moderator of quality culture"
**Confidence**: 5 — standard modelling vocabulary.

"Moderator" names a tested interaction between a predictor and an outcome; what is reported is a two-group difference in a single cross-sectional score with n=9 and n=11 and no model, no interaction term, and no outcome variable distinct from the score itself. The hedge "possible" keeps this local and it is fixable by rewording to a descriptive between-site difference worth examining in future work, which is why it is Minor rather than Major. It should not be repaired by fitting a moderation model to these cells.
