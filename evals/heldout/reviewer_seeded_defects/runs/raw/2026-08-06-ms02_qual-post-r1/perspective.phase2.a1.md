## Review Body

I read higher education QA as one audited sector among several, and I read this manuscript twice: once for what an adjacent-field reader can actually extract from it, and once for what it claims to travel. On the first reading, the paper is more legible than most QA writing — the participation-versus-performance puzzle in §2 is stated cleanly enough that a hospital accreditation officer or a financial compliance manager would recognise the problem immediately. On the second reading, the paper fails, and it fails in the specific way my dimension is designed to catch: it asserts reach beyond its three cases while withholding the information an outsider would need to assess any transfer at all.

The central problem is that the paper's three findings are not discoveries; they are three well-established organizational phenomena renamed. "Ritual compliance" is institutional decoupling and ceremonial conformity. "Distributed leadership" as described here — administrators spending their energy on persuasion and coalition-building across constituencies with different logics — is a standard account of brokering between institutional logics. "Identity work" is a mature literature on how professionals sustain vocational commitment inside contradictory demands, and the paper's best sentence (§4.3, on holding the gap open) is a near-restatement of its core finding. None of that literature is named, cited, or argued with. This is not a citation-completeness complaint. It has a direct analytic consequence: because the paper never engages the generic theory, it cannot say what is *specific* to higher education QA as against any audited profession, and specificity is exactly what §5–§6 claim to deliver. The honest test is the one I would put to the authors directly: what in these findings would surprise a hospital accreditation officer or a bank compliance officer? On the current text, nothing would. That is a theoretical contribution claim resting on the reader's unfamiliarity with a neighbouring field.

Compounding this, the case itself is unlocatable. "Three universities in a single national system" never becomes a named system; no external body, no review cycle, no consequence structure, no legal or funding stakes are given. For an adjacent-field reader, that is the difference between a low-stakes documentary exercise and a licensure regime — and it determines whether decoupling here is cheap talk or costly ritual. The paper then leaps from this unlocatable case to "universities across the sector" and "administrators everywhere" (§5), and to "the first comprehensive account" (§6). The §6 sentence about future research across wider institutional types and national systems does not repair this; it is a future-work gesture that the §5 claims flatly contradict, and there is no limitations section anywhere. This combination — unspecified regime plus sector-wide and cross-system assertion with no supporting evidence and no operative scope limits — is why D4 blocks rather than warns. The repair is available and not especially painful: name the system and regime, rescope the claims to the three cases, and use the generic organizational literature to state explicitly which mechanisms should and should not be expected to travel.

Two further matters sit outside my scoring dimension but bear on whether this account can be published at all, and I flag them because I chair a research ethics committee and have published on confidentiality risk in small-N elite interviewing. First, §3.5's anonymization assurance is contradicted on the next page. Naming a speaker as the quality director of the largest private university in the region, in a study containing exactly one private university, is an identification, not a descriptor; the same holds for the associate dean for quality at the public research university. The quotes so attributed are sharply critical of the participants' own employers. That is a concrete employment risk to identifiable individuals and a breach of the consent basis the paper asserts. Second, and this is the finding I would most want the authors to sit with: §4.3 claims balanced representation capturing the full range of administrator views in the same paragraph in which it reports excluding the dissenters, and §4.4 then recruits a favourable survey mean as corroboration. A paper that critiques institutions for producing tidy documentation that flatters the panel has here produced a tidy three-theme narrative that flatters its own thesis, and has reported it in the language of completeness. Whether or not that irony is intended, an outside reader will see it, and it bears on the credibility of the whole account rather than on any single theme.

On practice: the recommendation that QA processes "create room for meaning-making" cannot be executed by anyone. It names no process, cycle, instrument, committee, or document. A quality director reading §6 on Monday morning has nothing to change. Given that the paper's stated implication is about QA design, this gap is worth closing with two or three concrete design moves the interviews actually support.

### S1: The participation-versus-performance tension is framed in terms an outsider can use

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2, "This tension — between QA as participatory cultivation and QA as sophisticated performance — animates the present study."

The §2 staging of Delacroix against Ferreira & Lund and Nakamura sets up a genuine, non-trivial puzzle without requiring insider knowledge of QA field debates. This is the part of the manuscript that would survive translation to another regulated sector intact.

### S2: The refusal to resolve the compliance/meaning tension is analytically productive

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3, "they held it open, treating the gap itself as the space in which their professional identity was located"

Treating the unresolved gap as the site of professional identity, rather than as a problem the administrators solve, is the manuscript's most interesting observation and the one with the clearest purchase on the identity-work literature the paper does not cite.

### S3: The level-differentiated reading of quality culture is stated rather than smoothed over

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5, "quality culture may be experienced quite differently at different levels of the institution"

The discussion declines to force the administrator accounts and the staff survey into agreement at this point, and names the divergence as a finding in its own right. That is the right instinct and worth building on.

### W1: Institution-linked role descriptors deanonymize participants and contradict the stated consent basis

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 and §4.1, "no individual could be identified", "the quality director of the largest private university in the region", "It's theater, and everyone knows it's theater"
**Confidence**: 5 (chairs a university research ethics committee, has published on confidentiality risk in small-N elite interviewing)

With one private university in the sample and a single such post in most institutions, these descriptors identify individuals whose attributed quotes disparage their own employers. Remove institution-linked role descriptors, and add a statement of how quote-level disclosure risk was assessed and what the consent form actually promised regarding attributed criticism.

### W2: The paper claims full-range representation in the same paragraph that reports excluding dissenting participants

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3, "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views on QA implementation", "these were excluded for space"
**Confidence**: 4 (organizational sociologist, routine assessment of disconfirming-case handling in qualitative institutional research)

Disconfirming views were removed because they fell outside the three-theme structure, and the structure is then presented as the full range. Uncorrected, the reported dominance of the three themes cannot be credited, and the manuscript enacts the performativity it critiques. Reinstate the dissenting accounts and let them test the theme structure.

### W3: Sector-wide and cross-system claims with no supporting evidence and no operative scope limits

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5, "universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment rather than a managerial output", "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension"
**Confidence**: 5 (reads higher education as one regulated sector among several, routinely assesses transfer claims across sectors)

Three institutions in one unnamed system cannot license a general account of the sector, and the §6 future-research sentence does not function as a scope limit on these assertions. Rescope to the cases, add an explicit limitations statement, and state the boundary conditions under which the mechanisms would be expected to hold elsewhere.

### W4: The three findings restate generic organizational theory without naming it, so no higher-education-specific contribution is established

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §2 and reference list — expected named engagement with institutional decoupling and ceremonial conformity, identity-work, and distributed-leadership theory, plus boundary conditions separating higher education QA from other audited professions; checked §2 three strands, §4 theme labels, §5 discussion, §6 conclusion, and all twelve references
**Confidence**: 5 (organizational sociologist working on decoupling, institutional logics, and professional identity work)

All twelve references are higher education sources. Without the generic literature the paper cannot distinguish its findings from what is known about any audited profession, which is the contribution §5–§6 assert. Engage that work and state explicitly what is field-specific.

### W5: The regulatory regime and national system are never specified, so transferability cannot be assessed

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.2 setting description — expected identification of the national QA system, the external body conducting review, and the stakes attached to the review cycle; checked §1 framing, §3.2 participants and setting, §3.3 data collection, and §4 findings
**Confidence**: 4 (comparative reader of regulated-sector accreditation regimes)

Whether decoupling is cheap or costly depends entirely on the consequence structure of the regime, which is withheld. An adjacent-field reader cannot judge which findings are artefacts of a low-stakes documentary cycle. Name the system and regime, or state why anonymity requires withholding it and what is lost thereby.

### W6: A three-party negotiation claim is evidenced from one party's testimony

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §6 and §3.2, "an ongoing negotiation among administrators, academics, and external bodies", "recruited through institutional QA offices"
**Confidence**: 4 (organizational sociologist, standard assessment of evidentiary sufficiency for multi-actor claims)

Only QA officeholders were interviewed; academics appear solely as objects of persuasion and external bodies not at all. Either narrow the claim to how administrators construe the negotiation, or add academic and external-body accounts. As written, one party's self-description carries a claim about three parties.

### W7: The discussion inverts the cited position of Delacroix (2018) that the literature review states correctly

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 and §5, "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual", "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture"
**Confidence**: 5 (based on the manuscript's own §2 characterisation and reference-list annotation)

§5 recruits Delacroix as support for the exact position §2 reports him arguing against, and the recommendation that consultation is "the engine of internalization" is precisely the participation ritual he criticises. An outsider reading only §5 would import an inverted claim. The design argument in §5 needs rebuilding on a defensible reading.

### W8: The reported number of interview participants is inconsistent between abstract and methods

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract and §3.2, "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth", "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 5 (direct textual comparison)

Fourteen in the abstract, twelve in §3.2. Reconcile, and if two interviews were excluded, say so and on what grounds — which also connects to the disconfirming-case question in W2.

### W9: The design recommendation is not operationalizable at the level of any QA process, cycle, or instrument

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §6, "processes that create room for meaning-making rather than merely demanding documentation"
**Confidence**: 4 (works with quality assurance and accreditation practitioners across regulated sectors)

No process, cycle, instrument, or committee is named, so no quality director could act on this. Given that the abstract promises implications for QA design, specify two or three concrete changes the interview data support — to what artefact, at what point in the cycle, replacing what.
