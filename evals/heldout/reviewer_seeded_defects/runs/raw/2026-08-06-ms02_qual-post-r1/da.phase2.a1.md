## Review Body

I read this manuscript looking only for the skeleton of its argument: what is claimed, what is offered as warrant, and whether the two are joined. The prose is fluent and the thematic material is plausible on its face. That is precisely the danger here, because the argumentative chain breaks in four independent places, and in three of them the manuscript contradicts itself in adjacent or near-adjacent text rather than merely overreaching.

The most damaging break is the treatment of Delacroix (2018). Section 2 builds the paper's animating puzzle out of Delacroix's position, correctly rendered: participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual, and consultation is *not* sufficient evidence of a healthy quality culture. The reference list reinforces this with an explicit annotation. Section 5 then recruits the same source in support of the opposite recommendation, describing Delacroix as recommending consultation as "the central mechanism" for building quality culture, and asserting that the paper's findings "align" with him. This is not a citation slip at the margin. The third of three headline discussion findings — the design recommendation that carries into the Conclusion's practical claim — rests on the inverted reading, and the inversion dissolves the very tension the paper announced as its reason for existing. Whichever reading the authors intend, one of Section 2 or Section 5 must be withdrawn, and with it either the paper's framing or its recommendation. The domain reviewer owns the accuracy verdict on the source itself; I record it here as a load-bearing internal contradiction.

The second break is scope. Section 5's second finding states that the results "demonstrate that universities across the sector" treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment, and invites the reader to take a three-institution study as "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture," with administrators "everywhere" facing the same tension. Twelve (or fourteen — see below) purposively recruited administrators in three institutions within one national system cannot license a sector-wide or universal claim; no sampling logic reported in Section 3.2 makes those institutions representative of anything beyond themselves. The manuscript knows this: the Conclusion asks that "future research should test these dynamics across a wider range of institutional types and national systems," which concedes the generalisation is untested at the moment it is asserted as demonstrated. The Conclusion's own thesis statement ("we have shown that quality culture is neither a spontaneous emergence nor a manageable output") inherits the inflated scope. This is the defect that anchors my block: the conclusion's strength exceeds the warrant that the design can produce.

The third break is circular selection. Section 4.3 claims in one sentence that the study "achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views," and in the next reports that dissenting participants were excluded "as they fell outside the three-theme structure that organized our analysis." The first sentence is falsified by the second. Worse, the exclusion criterion is the finding: cases that would disconfirm the three-theme structure are removed on the ground that they do not fit it, which insulates the theme structure from the only evidence capable of testing it. Every downstream prevalence claim — "the first and most pervasive theme," "recurred across every interview," "administrators consistently described" — is compromised by this, because the population over which prevalence is asserted has been trimmed to the conclusion's shape. Section 4.1's assurance that the themes "emerged systematically from the structured protocol" compounds rather than repairs the problem: Section 3.1 describes semi-structured interviews and Section 3.4 an inductive iterative coding process, so the protocol is simultaneously described as structured and semi-structured, and as generating themes deductively and inductively. The inference drawn from it — that protocol structure gives confidence the pattern is not an artifact of a single institution's circumstances — does not follow at all, since instrument design cannot establish cross-institutional robustness.

The fourth break is the contradiction between the ethics protocol and the reporting practice. Section 3.5 states that all data were fully anonymised so that no individual could be identified. Section 4.1 then attributes quotations to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and "the associate dean for quality at the public research university" — in a three-institution design containing exactly one private and one research university, these are unique identifiers of named post-holders. I raise this under coherence because the manuscript asserts a property of its data that its own findings section disproves, but the consequence is practical: the consent basis described cannot be reconciled with the text as written.

Beyond these, the evidentiary base is not stably specified. The abstract reports fourteen administrators interviewed; Section 3.2 reports twelve, redundantly marked "(n=12)." A reader cannot tell how much interview evidence exists. The survey fares no better as a support structure: Section 4.4 announces that the survey "corroborated the qualitative picture" and then, two sentences later, that the interview accounts "had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture," while Section 5 recharacterises the same result as "a note of complication." A finding cannot corroborate and complicate the same expectation; the abstract propagates only the corroborative reading. The subgroup inference is weaker still. A comparison of nine against eleven respondents is offered as pointing to "institutional type as a possible moderator," but with one institution per type, type and institution are perfectly confounded, so the design cannot in principle distinguish the two — and 9 + 11 leaves 28 of the 48 respondents unaccounted for, with no test statistic, effect size, or test name given. The statistical adequacy question belongs to the methodology reviewer; the inferential leap from that contrast to a moderator claim is mine. Finally, the Conclusion's priority claim — "the first comprehensive account" — is contradicted by the paper's own Section 2, which identifies Pettersen (2022) and Rahman (2020) as "closest in spirit" and Silva and Tan (2021) as already describing the intermediary position, and by the Introduction's own gentler framing that "comparatively little" is known. "Comprehensive" is also unavailable to a study that has just told the reader it discarded dissenting cases. The venue-fit and contribution verdict is the EiC's; I flag only that the novelty claim is argumentatively unsupported by the manuscript's own literature review.

I record the block as repairable rather than fatal deliberately. A defensible thesis survives underneath: that quality culture in these three institutions is negotiated rather than delivered, held open by administrators as identity work. Reaching it requires withdrawing the sector-wide generalisation and the priority claim, resolving the Delacroix reading in one direction and rewriting whichever section loses, re-analysing with the excluded dissenting cases restored, redacting the identifying attributions, and characterising the survey as divergent rather than corroborative. That is a substantial rebuild of Sections 4 and 5, but the argument is not unfalsifiable in principle and the conclusions can be qualified rather than abandoned. Lesser matters I note without pressing: Section 4.2's "recurred across every interview" is a universal claim stated without any indication of how it was checked, and the abstract's three-theme summary carries no signal that the theme set was closed by exclusion.
