## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data collection and handling, analytic procedures, and statistical or qualitative reporting reach the standard that peer reviewers in this field would demand, and whether enough detail is given for another researcher to reproduce or audit the work. From my vantage point as the domain-accuracy reviewer, I read this dimension as adjacent to mine but not mine to score: where a design choice is defensible within the discipline's conventions yet described inaccurately in domain terms, that is my concern, while the adequacy of the design itself belongs to the methodology reviewer.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by me).** This is my dimension. It asks whether the manuscript's substantive claims are consistent with what the higher education policy and quality assurance literature currently supports; whether prior work, frameworks, agencies, standards, and named instruments are represented faithfully rather than distorted or over-attributed; and whether the technical vocabulary of the field is used with its established meaning. It also covers factual correctness of reported domain facts, including regulatory arrangements, accreditation or evaluation regimes, definitions of quality culture and its constituent constructs, and any results that are described as findings from other studies. Misuse of a term of art, a citation that does not say what the manuscript claims it says, or a confident empirical assertion that contradicts the settled evidence base all fall squarely inside this dimension.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the discourse-analysis reviewer).** This dimension concerns whether the central thesis holds together internally, whether the evidence actually presented is capable of supporting the conclusions drawn from it, and whether any logical defect is severe enough to undermine the paper's core argument. My role does not score it. I note only that the boundary I respect is this: a claim that is logically well-connected to its evidence but factually wrong about the domain is my finding; a claim that is factually accurate but does not follow from the evidence offered is theirs.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by the perspective reviewer).** This dimension evaluates whether the framing, key definitions, and stated implications can be followed and used by readers from adjacent fields such as organisational studies, public administration, or education management, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplinary boundaries is properly substantiated. I do not score it. Where a paper borrows a construct from a neighbouring discipline and describes that construct incorrectly, I would treat the factual misdescription as a domain-accuracy issue and leave the question of accessibility to the perspective reviewer.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension covers manuscript organisation, clarity of exposition, the quality and legibility of figures and tables, and conformity to the conventions of the configured venue. I do not score it. Expository problems only enter my findings when the wording makes a domain claim factually wrong rather than merely unclear.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it offers an original and significant contribution to that venue's readership. I do not score it, though my assessment of how faithfully the paper positions itself against existing work is intended to be usable evidence for whoever does.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether quality assurance terms of art (quality culture, internal/external QA, accreditation versus programme review, compliance versus enhancement, stakeholder and administrator roles) are used with their established field meanings; whether cited frameworks, agencies, standards documents and prior empirical studies are attributed and characterised accurately; whether stated regulatory, institutional or national-system facts are correct; whether claims about what the literature has found are consistent with the current evidence base and are not overstated beyond what that evidence supports; and whether the paper's account of administrator perspectives is framed against prior work rather than presented as if the domain had no antecedents.
what_triggers_block: One or more substantive domain errors that a reader would carry away as false, such as a core construct redefined incorrectly and then relied upon throughout, a misattributed or materially misrepresented framework or prior study that the argument depends on, or an incorrect factual claim about a quality assurance regime that changes how the findings should be read — errors that require the authors to revise substance rather than wording.
what_triggers_warn: Localised or non-load-bearing domain inaccuracies, such as imprecise or inconsistent use of a technical term that the surrounding text still disambiguates, a thin or dated engagement with the relevant literature, a claim stated more strongly than the cited source warrants, or an omission of a well-established competing account that should have been acknowledged but whose absence does not falsify the paper's conclusions.
what_triggers_fatal: Pervasive misrepresentation of the domain such that the manuscript's evidential base cannot be trusted — for example fabricated, non-existent or systematically wrong citations, invented standards or agency positions, or empirical assertions that directly contradict well-documented facts of the field and cannot be repaired by revision because the paper's substantive premises are themselves false.
