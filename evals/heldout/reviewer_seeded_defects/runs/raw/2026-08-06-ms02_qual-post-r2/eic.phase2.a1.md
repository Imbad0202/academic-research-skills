### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution

score: block
block_class: repairable
trigger: "a contribution claim not distinguished from clearly antecedent work"

Topical fit is not in doubt: administrator enactment of QA and the formation of institutional quality culture is core scope for this readership, and I would be glad to have a strong version of this paper. The problem is the contribution claim. §6 asserts "the first comprehensive account of how university administrators experience and enact QA implementation" while the established quality-culture canon this journal has itself carried — the EUA Quality Culture Project reports, Harvey and Stensaker's typology, Ehlers, Sursock, Vettori, Cardoso and colleagues, Loukkola and Zhang — is absent from the manuscript entirely. Worse, the three themes offered as the finding map one-to-one onto three sources the manuscript already cites: ritual compliance onto Okonkwo (2018), distributed leadership onto Pettersen (2022), identity and vocation onto Rahman (2020). The paper therefore states a priority claim it has not positioned against work it is itself aware of, which is the block condition as I framed it in Phase 1.

I record this as repairable rather than fatal deliberately. The manuscript is inside scope, and there is a residual empirical contribution — grounded administrator voice where Silva and Tan (2021) is described as remaining conceptual. That contribution does not dissolve on inspection; it is smaller and older than the manuscript claims. Recovering it requires the authors to drop the first-ness claim, engage the canon, disclose the national system, and either substantially deepen the evidence base or reposition the piece as a research note. That is major revision work, not a rejection on scope.

## Review Body

I am handling this submission as the seat responsible for fit, originality, and publication-ethics queries, so I will take the gate items first and the editorial judgement second.

The gate item is the reference list, and it does not survive inspection. Twelve of twelve DOIs sit on the `10.5555/` reserved test prefix with sequential suffixes. Whatever the explanation — a citation manager populated from a template, a placeholder set never replaced, or something more serious — the effect at the editorial desk is identical: no reader, and no reviewer, can resolve a single source the manuscript relies on. The malformed Delacroix entry, carrying an editorial gloss inside the reference list, points the same way about how this list was assembled. I cannot in good conscience treat the literature review as verified evidence until the authors supply resolvable identifiers for every entry, and I would ask the handling editor to put that request to the authors before further reviewer time is spent.

The second ethics item is smaller but concrete. §3.5 states that all data were anonymised "so that no individual could be identified in reported findings." §4.1 then attributes a quotation to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and another to "the associate dean for quality at the public research university" — in a three-institution sample containing exactly one private and one research university. Those descriptors are deductive identifiers, and they contradict the assurance the participants were presumably given. This is a straightforward redaction fix, but it must be made.

On editorial judgement: the manuscript is well written at sentence level and the interview material is genuinely good. What it is not, on present evidence, is a full research article for this journal. Roughly 1,850 body words, twelve interviews, three themes each carried by one or two quotations, one descriptive mean, and no table or figure of any kind. Set against that, §6 claims a first comprehensive account and §5 claims that findings from three institutions in one unnamed system "demonstrate that universities across the sector" behave in a given way and "can be read as a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture." That inferential leap is the devil's-advocate seat's to adjudicate under D3, and I flag it rather than score it, but from the fit seat it matters because the significance being claimed is what the paper is asking the journal's space for, and the delivered content does not reach it.

I also draw the domain seat's attention to Delacroix. §2 and the reference annotation both present Delacroix (2018) as arguing *against* stakeholder consultation as evidence of quality culture; §5 aligns the paper's findings with Delacroix as someone "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism." The manuscript contradicts itself about a source in a way that reverses the source's position. The accuracy verdict belongs to D2, but I have recorded it below because the discussion's positioning against the record is my dimension's business.

Two further matters I note for the seats that own them rather than resolve here: the p<.05 subgroup claim on n=9 versus n=11 (methodology), and §4.3's exclusion of dissenting cases (methodology and devil's advocate). My own reading of the exclusion is recorded below as a reporting contradiction; whether it is an analytic-validity failure is not my call.
