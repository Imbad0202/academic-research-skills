## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data handling, analysis, and reporting would survive scrutiny from a competent peer in the field, and whether enough procedural detail is given that another researcher could repeat the work. Read through a cross-disciplinary lens, the same question becomes: would a reader trained in an adjacent discipline — say, organisational sociology, public administration, or evaluation science — be able to recognise and audit the method, or is it described in terms so local that outsiders must take it on trust? I do not score this dimension; it belongs to the methodology reviewer, and my role is to avoid trespassing on it even when method description affects accessibility.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by the domain reviewer).** This covers whether the manuscript's substantive claims track current evidence in its own field, whether prior work is represented faithfully rather than caricatured, and whether field-specific terminology and reported results are factually correct. From my vantage point, domain accuracy is the substrate on which any interdisciplinary claim rests: a bridge to another field cannot be sound if the near bank is misdescribed. Nonetheless, adjudicating what the domain literature actually says is the domain reviewer's job, not mine, and I will not issue a score here.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the discourse-analysis reviewer).** This dimension concerns internal consistency: whether the central thesis holds together, whether the evidence presented actually supports the claims made from it, and whether any reasoning defect is serious enough to undermine the paper's core argument. Cross-disciplinary readers are often the first to notice incoherence, because they cannot patch gaps with tacit field knowledge; still, the formal adjudication of argument structure sits with the DA reviewer and secondarily the methodology reviewer, and I do not score it.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by me).** This is my dimension. It asks whether the framing, the operative definitions, and the stated implications are usable by readers from adjacent fields, and whether any claim the paper makes about reaching beyond its home discipline is actually substantiated rather than asserted. Concretely I am judging three things: (a) accessibility — are the constructs, jargon, and institutional context explained well enough that a reader from a neighbouring field can follow the argument without insider knowledge; (b) portability — are the implications drawn at a level of generality that other settings, systems, or disciplines could genuinely act on; and (c) substantiation — where the paper claims relevance to, borrowing from, or contribution to another field, is that claim backed by engagement with that field's actual literature and concepts rather than a decorative citation. Because this dimension is high priority rather than mandatory, a block here escalates the editorial decision but cannot by itself trigger rejection.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension addresses organisation, clarity of exposition, quality of figures and tables, and conformity to venue conventions. It overlaps with mine at the edges — an unclear paper is also an inaccessible one — but the distinction I will hold is that D5 is about craft and presentation for the paper's own readership, whereas D4 is about whether meaning survives transport to a different readership. Scoring D5 belongs to the EIC role.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it offers something original and significant enough to matter to that venue's readers. Interdisciplinary breadth can be mistaken for contribution, and I want to be careful not to launder my own dimension into a fitness judgement: a paper can be admirably legible to outsiders and still make no new contribution, or be narrow yet genuinely novel. The EIC role scores this one.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether key constructs, institutional context, and abbreviations are defined on first use for a non-specialist; whether the framing situates the problem in terms an adjacent-field reader (organisational studies, public policy, evaluation, management) would recognise; whether stated implications are portable beyond the specific systems studied; and whether every claim of relevance to or borrowing from another discipline is backed by actual engagement with that discipline's literature and concepts.
what_triggers_block: Central constructs or context are presented as self-evident insider knowledge such that an adjacent-field reader cannot reconstruct what is being claimed, and/or the paper asserts cross-disciplinary contribution or draws on another field's theory without substantiating engagement, so the interdisciplinary claim is unsupported as written.
what_triggers_warn: The argument remains followable for an outside reader, but accessibility or portability is uneven — for example scattered undefined jargon, context assumed for one national or institutional system without orientation, or implications stated so locally that transfer to adjacent settings is left entirely to the reader.
