### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
score: block
trigger: "the paper asserts cross-disciplinary contribution or draws on another field's theory without substantiating engagement, so the interdisciplinary claim is unsupported as written"

The block rests on three independent substantiation failures rather than on accessibility. Accessibility is in fact adequate: the prose is clean, QA and quality culture are defined on first use, and an adjacent-field reader can follow the argument. What that reader cannot do is accept the paper's cross-field claims as supported. (a) The manuscript's entire analytic vocabulary — ritual, theater, decoupling, calendar-bound audit cycles, identity work — is imported from neo-institutional sociology and the audit-society literature, and none of that literature is engaged or cited. (b) The single explicit theoretical alignment claim in the Discussion attributes to Delacroix (2018) the opposite of the position the paper's own §2 and reference annotation assign to him, and that misattribution is the sole citational support for the paper's only practical recommendation. (c) The portability claim runs the wrong way: rather than under-claiming transfer, §5 asserts that three institutions in an unnamed national system yield "a general account" of the sector, while withholding the regulatory and institutional context an outside reader would need to judge transfer at all. As a high-priority dimension this block escalates the editorial decision but cannot by itself compel rejection.

## Review Body

I read this manuscript from organizational sociology of accountability regimes, and secondarily as someone who chairs an ethics committee for organizational and elite-informant fieldwork. Two things follow from that vantage point.

The first is that this is a decoupling study that does not appear to know it is one. "We produce beautiful evidence portfolios that nobody reads except the panel. It's theater, and everyone knows it's theater" is not a novel finding; it is ceremonial conformity, described in essentially these terms since Meyer and Rowan (1977) and formalized since as policy–practice decoupling and means–ends decoupling. The "whole season of the year given over to feeding the machine" is the audit ritual as Power described it. §4.3's identity work is a documented response to reactivity, well theorized in the measurement-effects literature. The Discussion even uses the word "decouple" — an imported technical term — without a single citation to the tradition that supplies it. This is not a demand that the authors convert to organization theory. It is that the paper's contribution claim cannot be assessed until the authors state what remains new once that tradition is on the table. My honest read is that engaging it would sharpen rather than dissolve the contribution, because the genuinely interesting finding here is not the ritualism but the recovery: administrators who label the ritual theater and then actively reconstruct meaning from it. That is a mechanism, it speaks directly to the decoupling literature's weakest point (what agents do inside the gap), and it currently receives one paragraph.

I should name my own bias. Administrator-level accounts in higher education QA genuinely are thinner than academic-level accounts, and the Journal-Fit and domain seats are better placed than I am to judge how far toward organization theory this paper should be pushed before it stops belonging to its own readership. I am not asking for a different paper's disciplinary home; I am asking that borrowed constructs be attributed and that a sector-wide claim be earned.

The second thing is not a disciplinary preference but a gate. §3.5 asserts full anonymization such that no individual could be identified. §4.1 then attributes critical quotations to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and "the associate dean for quality at the public research university" in a three-site design containing exactly one private university and one public research university. Both are singular role-holders. Both are, for any reader inside that national system, named. Both quotes disparage their own institutions' core compliance work. I cannot see how the anonymization claim and these attributions coexist. Before this can proceed, the authors need to state whether consent covered attributable role-level quotation, whether participants reviewed and approved their quotes for publication, and what the approving committee was told. Remediation is straightforward on the page — de-specify roles and institutions, or obtain documented quote-level consent — but it cannot be waved through.

Third, on triangulation: the design makes administrators the only witnesses to the value of work that exists because of the ritual they critique. There is an obvious rival reading the manuscript never tests — that vocational framing is occupational self-legitimation rather than a coping resource — and no academic, student, or accreditor data are present to adjudicate it. The 48-respondent staff survey is positioned as corroboration, not as triangulation, and then immediately reports divergence from the interview accounts. The paper's central interpretive claim is therefore unfalsifiable as designed. This does not require abandoning the design; it requires stating the limitation and reframing the claim as administrators' accounts rather than as an account of quality culture.

Fourth, usability. Stripped of the misread Delacroix support, §5's recommendation reduces to favoring "processes that maximize genuine participation." A QA director reading this cannot act on it Monday morning, and Delacroix's actual argument — that consultation is routinely co-opted into compliance performance — would have generated the sharper and more useful recommendation the authors' own data support.

Finally, I note two internal reporting contradictions that any outside reader will hit immediately: the sample is fourteen in the Abstract and twelve in Methods, and §4.1 credits a "structured protocol" for thematic emergence in a study described as semi-structured with iterative coding. The methodology and DA seats own the scoring of these; I record them because they materially affect whether an adjacent-field reader can reconstruct what was done.

### S1: The gap statement is real and precisely located
The manuscript identifies a genuine hinge position — actors who are neither imposers nor resisters of standards — and locates it against a specifically conceptual prior treatment (Silva & Tan, 2021). For an adjacent-field reader this is a legible, well-posed empirical opening, and it is the strongest sentence in the paper.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 closing paragraph, "Missing from this body of work is sustained attention to the administrators who occupy the hinge between external requirement and internal practice"

### S2: The meaning-recovery observation is a genuine analytic contribution in embryo
The claim that administrators hold the compliance/meaning tension open rather than resolving it, and locate professional identity in the gap itself, is portable and non-obvious. It is exactly what the decoupling literature under-specifies, and it would travel to public administration, healthcare accreditation, and audit settings without translation.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3, "they held it open, treating the gap itself as the space in which their professional identity was located"

### S3: Definitional hygiene supports outside readership
Core constructs are glossed on first use rather than assumed, abbreviations are expanded, and jargon density is low. Accessibility, as distinct from substantiation, is not the problem with this manuscript.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §1, "the shared values, practices, and commitments through which an institution takes collective ownership of its own standards"

### S4: The literature review states Delacroix's position correctly
Because §2 renders Delacroix's contrarian argument accurately, the Discussion's inversion is a localized error rather than a sustained misreading, which makes it cheap to repair and makes the authors' actual grasp of the source visible.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2, "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual"

### W1: Attributed role-level quotes defeat the stated anonymization guarantee
In a three-site design with exactly one private university and one public research university, "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and "the associate dean for quality at the public research university" are singular role-holders and are effectively identified. Both are quoted disparaging their own institutions' compliance work. This is irreconcilable with §3.5's guarantee. The authors must document whether consent covered attributable role-level quotation, whether participants approved their quotes, and what the ethics committee approved; remediation requires either de-specification of role and institution or documented quote-level consent. Uncorrected, this makes publication impossible under standard organizational-fieldwork ethics frameworks regardless of the paper's other merits, which is why it is Critical on its own.
**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 with §4.1, "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified"; "the quality director of the largest private university in the region"
**Confidence**: 5 — I chair an ethics committee for organizational and elite-informant fieldwork and assess deductive-disclosure risk in small-N designs routinely.

### W2: Core analytic vocabulary is borrowed from neo-institutional sociology without engaging it
Ritual compliance, theater, calendar-bound audit cycles, identity work, and the Discussion's own use of "decouple" are all constructs from the decoupling and audit-society traditions. None of that literature appears anywhere. The consequence is not stylistic: the contribution claim in §6 cannot be evaluated, because the reader cannot tell which part of the finding is new to the site and which part is a forty-year-old model rediscovered. Repair requires substantial repositioning of §2 and §5 — stating what the administrator hinge adds to decoupling theory, most plausibly the recovery mechanism in §4.3 — but the empirical material survives it.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §2 literature review and the 12-item reference list — expected at least one citation to the neo-institutional decoupling or audit-society literature that already models ceremonial compliance and reactivity to measurement; checked all 12 references, §2 paragraphs 1-4, §5 paragraph 2
**Confidence**: 5 — this is my primary research literature.

### W3: The Discussion attributes to Delacroix the reverse of his cited position
§2 and the reference annotation both state that Delacroix argues *against* treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of quality culture. §5 then claims alignment with Delacroix as recommending consultation as "the central mechanism." The paper's only cited theoretical support for its only practical recommendation is therefore a reversal of the source. This invalidates §5's third finding and the recommendation built on it; the paper's central negotiated-accomplishment thesis survives, so this is Major rather than Critical. Repair is not merely a citation swap: Delacroix's actual argument cuts against the recommendation, so the recommendation itself must be rebuilt.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 third finding, "our findings align with Delacroix (2018), who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture"
**Confidence**: 5 — the contradiction is internal to the manuscript and verifiable without external sources.

### W4: The only witnesses are the interested parties, and no triangulation is attempted
Administrators whose posts exist because of the QA apparatus are the sole source on whether that apparatus matters. The rival explanation — that vocational framing is occupational self-legitimation — is never stated, let alone tested, and no academic, student, or accreditor data are collected. The staff survey is positioned as corroboration rather than as an independent perspective. As designed, the interpretive claim that administrators genuinely "recover meaning" cannot be distinguished from a self-serving account, which materially weakens the paper's central finding and requires either additional data or a substantially narrowed claim.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.2 sampling frame and §4 findings — expected at least one academic, student, or external reviewer data source capable of testing administrators' self-accounts; checked §3.2 participants, §3.3 data collection, §4.1 through §4.4, §5 discussion
**Confidence**: 4 — design inference from the reported sampling frame; possible that unreported data exist, in which case the omission is itself a reporting defect.

### W5: Dissenting participants were excluded while the paper claims to capture the full range of views
§4.3 asserts balanced representation and full range of administrator views, then in the next sentence discloses that participants sceptical of the entire QA enterprise were excluded because they fell outside the three-theme structure. This inverts the logic of thematic analysis: the theme structure was insulated from the cases that would have tested it, and the claim of representativeness is contradicted in the same paragraph that makes it. Uncorrected, this invalidates the findings section's core claim about what administrators think, and it alone would justify a block on methodological rigor.
**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 final paragraph, "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views"; "A minority of participants expressed more skeptical or dissenting views about the value of the entire QA enterprise; these were excluded for space"
**Confidence**: 5 — the contradiction is explicit on the page; the methodology and DA seats own the scoring consequence.

### W6: Sector-wide generalization is asserted from three sites in an unnamed system
§5 escalates from three institutions to "a general account" of the higher education sector and to administrators "everywhere." Simultaneously the manuscript withholds the material an outside reader needs to judge any transfer: the national system is unnamed, the QA regime type (accreditation, audit, or assessment) is unspecified, and the regulatory stakes attached to review outcomes are not described. The portability problem is thus double — claims too broad, context too thin — and both the Discussion and Conclusion require rewriting to a defensible scope.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 second finding, "can be read as a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture"; "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension between external requirement and internal meaning"
**Confidence**: 5 — the claims and the missing context are both on the page.

### W7: The paper's most novel mechanism is described but never analyzed
Meaning recovery from a ritual the actor himself labels theater is the finding that would justify the paper to an adjacent-field readership. It receives one descriptive paragraph and one sentence in the Discussion, with no account of how the conversion works, when it fails, or what institutional conditions sustain it. Developing it would raise the paper's contribution substantially without new data, but the core claims do not change if it remains undeveloped.
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §5 treatment of the meaning-recovery mechanism — expected analytic specification of how administrators convert rituals they call theater into vocational commitment, and under what conditions it fails; checked §4.3, §5 paragraph 2, §6
**Confidence**: 4 — judgement about analytic development rather than a factual defect.

### W8: The practical recommendation is unactionable as stated
Once the Delacroix support is removed, the recommendation is an exhortation. "Genuine" participation is undefined and undistinguished from the consultation-as-artifact the paper's own §4.1 documents, so a quality director learns nothing about what to change. This limits practitioner usability without altering the paper's claims.
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 third finding, "processes that maximize genuine participation should be favored"
**Confidence**: 4 — usability judgement, informed by working with QA offices.

### W9: The interview sample size is reported inconsistently
The Abstract reports fourteen interviewees; §3.2 reports twelve, with the figure repeated numerically. The manuscript as submitted therefore has an indeterminate empirical base, and per-institution coverage across three sites — the quantity any outside reader needs to assess transferability — cannot be computed. If this is a typographical error the fix is trivial, but a reviewer cannot assume that, and every count derived from the interviews must be restated and verified.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract with §3.2, "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth"; "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct textual contradiction.

### W10: The claim that themes emerged from a "structured protocol" misdescribes the stated design
§3.1 and §3.3 describe semi-structured interviews and §3.4 describes iterative, emergent coding. §4.1 then credits a structured protocol with systematic thematic emergence and treats this as warranting confidence that the pattern is not a local artifact. The inference does not follow from either design, and the design descriptions conflict. Clarity repair rather than claim revision.
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.1 with §3.1, "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol"; "semi-structured interviews"
**Confidence**: 4 — internal inconsistency is clear; the methodology seat owns the inferential consequence.

### W11: The survey is labelled corroboration in the sentence before it is reported as divergence
§4.4 opens by stating that the survey corroborated the qualitative picture, then reports that the interview accounts had led the authors to expect something more skeptical than the survey delivered. Divergence between components is a legitimate and interesting mixed-methods result, but it is not corroboration, and an adjacent-field reader in evaluation science will read the framing as an unintegrated design. Rewriting the paragraph to own the divergence would strengthen it.
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4, "The survey corroborated the qualitative picture"; "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture"
**Confidence**: 4 — framing judgement; the statistical adequacy of the subgroup comparison belongs to the methodology seat.
