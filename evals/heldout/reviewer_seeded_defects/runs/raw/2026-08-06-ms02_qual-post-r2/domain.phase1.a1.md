## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design and execution would survive scrutiny by a competent peer reviewer in the field: whether the sampling, instruments, and data-collection procedures fit the stated research question; whether analytic choices are reported with enough detail (including any statistics, coding procedures, or qualitative trustworthiness checks) that a reader can judge their soundness; and whether the manuscript supplies enough procedural transparency — protocols, instruments, coding schemes, availability statements — for another researcher to reproduce or at least audit the work. From my seat as the domain reader I note that methodological weakness often surfaces as domain-implausible results, but the formal judgement here belongs to the methodology reviewer, not to me.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role).** This is my dimension. It asks whether every substantive claim the manuscript makes about higher education policy and quality assurance is consistent with the current state of evidence and practice in that field; whether prior scholarship, frameworks, and policy instruments are described as they actually are rather than as a convenient caricature; and whether field-specific vocabulary — terms such as quality culture, internal versus external quality assurance, accreditation versus audit versus evaluation, programme versus institutional review, compliance versus enhancement orientations — is used with its accepted technical meaning rather than loosely or interchangeably. It also covers factual accuracy about named systems, agencies, standards, and reported results: dates, jurisdictional arrangements, standard titles, and the substance of cited findings must be right, and the manuscript's own reported results must be stated consistently wherever they recur. Misattribution of a concept to the wrong author or tradition, silent redefinition of an established construct, or a claim presented as settled fact when the field treats it as contested all fall inside this dimension.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, devil's-advocate and methodology roles).** This dimension concerns whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether premises actually entail the conclusions drawn, whether the evidence presented is of the right kind and strength to license the claims made, and whether the argument avoids reasoning defects — circularity, equivocation, unwarranted causal leaps from associational or perceptual data, selective use of confirming cases — that would hollow out the main contribution. Coherence is distinct from accuracy: an argument can be built entirely from true statements and still fail to support its conclusion, and it is that structural question this dimension targets.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high, perspective role).** This dimension evaluates whether a reader from an adjacent field — organisational studies, public administration, general education research, evaluation science — can enter the manuscript without insider knowledge: whether framing establishes why the problem matters beyond its immediate community, whether specialised terms and institutional arrangements are defined at first use, and whether any claim that reaches into another discipline's territory is backed by that discipline's own evidence rather than asserted by analogy. Overclaiming generality, or borrowing a construct from another literature without engaging how that literature actually uses it, is the characteristic failure mode.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal, editor-in-chief role).** This dimension covers craft and presentation: whether the manuscript is organised so that the reader can follow the path from problem to method to evidence to interpretation; whether prose is clear and appropriately economical; whether tables and figures are legible, correctly labelled, self-explanatory, and consistent with the text they support; and whether the submission observes the target venue's conventions for structure, referencing, and length. These are remediable matters in most cases, but they bear directly on whether the substantive content can be assessed at all.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, editor-in-chief role).** This dimension asks two linked questions: does the manuscript belong in the configured venue given that venue's scope and readership, and does it add something genuinely new and consequential to what is already known? Contribution is judged against the existing literature, so a competent, clean study that restates a well-established finding without extending it, or a manuscript whose substance sits squarely in a different field's remit, can fail here even when its internal execution is unobjectionable. The judgement is the editor's, though it depends on an accurate picture of the prior literature, which is where my dimension feeds into it.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Correct and consistent use of quality assurance terminology (quality culture, internal/external QA, accreditation vs. audit vs. evaluation, programme vs. institutional review, compliance vs. enhancement orientation); accurate characterisation of the frameworks, standards, agencies, and national systems invoked; prior work described as its authors actually argued it, with contested positions marked as contested rather than settled; correct dates, titles, and jurisdictional facts for any named policy instrument or body; internal consistency of the manuscript's own reported figures and findings across abstract, results, and discussion; and claims about administrator behaviour or institutional effects that are proportionate to what the cited evidence in this field actually supports.
what_to_look_for_supplement: Attention also to whether the concept of quality culture is used in a way traceable to the established literature rather than as an undefined honorific.
what_triggers_block: One or more substantive domain errors that would mislead a specialist reader and that materially support the manuscript's conclusions — for example a core construct redefined or conflated with a different one throughout, a cited source's position materially misrepresented in a way the argument leans on, or a named standard, agency remit, or national QA arrangement described incorrectly where that description carries argumentative weight.
what_triggers_warn: Localised or peripheral inaccuracies that a specialist would flag but that do not carry the conclusions — imprecise or drifting use of a technical term in a single passage, an outdated framework version or superseded citation where the current one exists, thin or one-sided engagement with a relevant strand of the field's literature, or an overstated generalisation that is correctable by rewording without disturbing the findings.
what_triggers_fatal: Pervasive domain misrepresentation such that the manuscript's factual basis in higher education quality assurance cannot be salvaged by revision — for example results or citations that cannot be reconciled with the sources or systems they claim to describe, fabricated or unverifiable attributions to named frameworks or agencies, or a central claim that directly contradicts well-established evidence in the field with no acknowledgement or defence.
