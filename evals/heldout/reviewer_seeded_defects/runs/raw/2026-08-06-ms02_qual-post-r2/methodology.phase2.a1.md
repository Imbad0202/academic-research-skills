## Scoring Plan Dissent

dimension_id: D1
rationale: My Phase 1 fatal trigger listed "data or participants that cannot be reconciled across sections of the manuscript" as sufficient for fatality, and the manuscript exhibits exactly that (Abstract "Fourteen administrators" versus §3.2 "Twelve senior administrators (n=12)"). On reading the paper I judge that clause over-broad as drafted: an unreconciled participant count is fatal only when the discrepancy demonstrably propagates into the reported analysis in a way the authors' own records cannot repair. Here I can show the contradiction but cannot establish propagation (the arithmetic 14 minus the two excluded dissenting cases equalling 12 is my suspicion, not a demonstrated fact), and the correct value is knowable to the authors. I therefore narrow the fatal trigger to irreconcilability that no correction could resolve, and score D1 as a repairable block rather than fatal. The remaining fatal clauses ("results that could not have been produced by the described procedure"; absence of ethical clearance) stand unchanged and are not met: ethics approval and consent are asserted (§3.5), and nothing I found shows the reported themes could not have been produced — only that they cannot be verified.

The primary strand of this study is qualitative, and §3.4 devotes three sentences to its analysis. ## Review Body

This manuscript studies a genuinely under-examined population — the administrators who sit between accreditor and department — with a design that is, in outline, well matched to that question. My difficulty is not with the design in outline but with what the manuscript allows a reader to verify. Across the Methods and Findings sections, almost every load-bearing procedural fact is either absent, asserted, or contradicted elsewhere in the same text. Three problems are structural rather than presentational, and I want to be explicit that I am not filing them as writing complaints: the sample size differs between the abstract and §3.2; the analytic procedure that produced the three themes is not recoverable; and the study's own text reports that disconfirming cases were removed to preserve that three-theme structure, immediately after claiming to capture "the full range of administrator views." The last of these is negative-case elimination in defence of a coding scheme, and it is the single finding that most damages the paper, because it makes the headline result partly an artefact of the analytic decision rather than a description of the corpus.

The qualitative claims are also not traceable at the level of individual assertions. "Most pervasive" (§4.1) and "recurred across every interview" (§4.2) are quantified claims about a corpus, but no counts, no per-site distribution, and no extract table are supplied, so a reader cannot distinguish a cross-institutional pattern from three articulate speakers. §4.1 makes this worse by attributing the themes to a "structured protocol" — a design the paper does not have, per §3.1 — and then inferring from that mischaracterisation that the pattern is "not an artifact of any single institution's local circumstances." Nothing in the reported procedure supports that inference; theme prevalence across sites is an empirical question the paper could answer with a simple per-site table and currently does not address at all.

On the survey strand, my judgement is that no inferential claim in §4.4 is currently defensible and the wording must be reduced to descriptive contrast unless full reporting is supplied. M=3.9 with SD=0.6 has no scale range, no anchors, no item set, no provenance, no reliability coefficient, and no dimensionality evidence, so "moderately positive" is an interpretation of a number whose metric is unknown. The "statistically significant difference (p<.05)" names no test, reports no statistic, no degrees of freedom, no effect size, no confidence interval, and no correction, on groups of nine and eleven. I attempted the bounded recomputations documented in my Arithmetic Receipts; none could be completed, and the reason in every case is that the manuscript withholds the inputs the procedure needs, which is itself the finding. Separately, the arithmetic of the sample does not close: 48 respondents are recruited, 20 appear in the only reported comparison, the third institution never appears in the survey results, and no response rate or per-site denominator is given. I would require a participant table with per-site n, role, tenure, and response rate for both strands before any of this could be assessed.

Finally, on integration: §3.1 assigns the survey a contextualising role, but the paper never states a point of integration, never presents a joint display, and in §4.4 says the survey "corroborated" the qualitative picture in one sentence and reports that it contradicted the authors' expectations in the next. As it stands, what the design mixes is unclear; two strands are reported adjacently and the tension between them is noted rather than analysed. I also note, though the substantive verdict belongs to the domain seat, that §5 attributes to Delacroix (2018) a recommendation that is the direct inverse of the position the manuscript's own reference annotation records. I raise it here only as an internal contradiction between two parts of the same submission.

I have not attempted a judgement on theoretical novelty (the themes' relation to decoupling and audit-culture literatures) or on venue contribution; those lie with other seats. My scope limit, offered as a fact for the EiC rather than an opinion on contribution, is that this design can support a local, descriptive, multi-site account and nothing broader.

### S1: Research question is answerable by the chosen design

The stated question asks how administrators experience and enact QA and how their accounts illuminate quality-culture formation. That is an interpretive question about meaning-making, properly matched to semi-structured interviewing, and the paper deserves credit for not framing it as an effect-estimation question the design could not address.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §1 "How do senior university administrators experience and enact QA implementation, and how do their accounts illuminate the formation of institutional quality culture?"
**Confidence**: 5 — routine design-question matching judgement.

### S2: Purposive multi-site sampling with a stated role criterion and recruitment route

Three contrasting institutional types, an explicit inclusion criterion (direct QA responsibility, with named post types), and a stated recruitment channel (institutional QA offices) give the sampling logic real shape. This is more than many interview studies report, and it is the foundation on which the missing participant table could be built.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.2 "one large public research university, one mid-sized public teaching-focused university, and one private university"
**Confidence**: 4 — recruitment route is stated but its selection consequences are not discussed.

### S3: Interview administration parameters are reported

Mode, duration range, recording, and transcription are all specified. These are the elements that let a reader gauge data richness, and their presence is a genuine, if modest, methodological credit.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.3 "lasting between 45 and 70 minutes, and were audio-recorded and transcribed"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct reading of reported parameters.

### S4: Ethical approval and consent are explicitly asserted

The paper states institutional approval and informed consent rather than leaving them to inference, which is the correct baseline for human-participant research and is frequently omitted in this literature. I credit the approval and consent statement on its own terms; the anonymisation claim in the same section is a separate matter treated below.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 "The study received institutional ethical approval. All participants gave informed consent"
**Confidence**: 4 — the assertion is present, though unverifiable without an approval reference number.

### W1: Interview sample size is irreconcilable between abstract and Methods

The abstract reports fourteen interviewees; §3.2 reports twelve, redundantly marked "(n=12)". Nothing in the text reconciles them, and no attrition, exclusion, or pilot account appears. The consequence is not cosmetic: N is the entire denominator for every qualitative claim in §4, so a reader cannot evaluate "most pervasive" or "across every interview" against any known base. I also cannot rule out that the two-participant gap is the same two cases §4.3 says were excluded, which would mean the reported N silently encodes the exclusion; I raise that as an unresolved possibility, not a demonstrated fact, and it is not the basis of the band. Uncorrected, a manuscript reporting two different sample sizes for the same interview study cannot be accepted.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth" and §3.2 "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct textual comparison, no inference required.

### W2: Disconfirming cases were excluded to preserve the three-theme structure, falsifying the adjacent representativeness claim

§4.3 claims balanced representation and the full range of administrator views, then states in the next sentence that skeptical or dissenting participants were dropped because they fell outside the three-theme structure. Those two sentences cannot both stand. Worse, the stated ground for exclusion is the coding scheme itself, which inverts the relationship between data and analysis: in any trustworthiness framework, cases that do not fit the structure are the evidence that tests the structure. "For space" is not an admissible reason to remove the only cases capable of disconfirming the paper's central finding. Uncorrected, the three-theme result is a product of the exclusion and the abstract's characterisation of it is unsupported; repair requires re-analysis with the dissenting cases restored and either integrated or explicitly reported as a negative-case analysis.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views" and "these were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure"
**Confidence**: 5 — the manuscript states the exclusion and its rationale itself.

### W3: The thematic analysis is not an auditable procedure

§3.4 describes iterative movement between transcripts and "an emerging set of themes until a stable structure was reached." That is a restatement of the outcome, not a method. Absent are the analytic tradition being followed, the coding framework or codebook, how many analysts coded and how disagreements were resolved, what "stable" meant operationally, any saturation evidence, any audit trail, any member checking, and any reflexivity statement about the authors' relation to the QA offices that recruited their participants. For a study whose primary strand is qualitative, this means the central findings are unverifiable in principle: a second analyst could not attempt the analysis, and a reader cannot test whether the three themes are the corpus's structure or the authors'. This defect independently justifies a block on D1.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.4 Analysis — expected a named coding framework, coder count, agreement or consensus procedure, saturation evidence, audit trail, and reflexivity statement; checked §3.1 Design, §3.3 Data collection, §3.4 Analysis, §4 Findings, and end matter for appendices or supplementary files
**Confidence**: 5 — exhaustive check of every methods and findings surface in the submission.

### W4: The anonymisation guarantee is contradicted by identifying descriptors in the Findings

§3.5 states that data were fully anonymised so that no individual could be identified in reported findings. §4.1 then attributes a quotation to the quality director of the largest private university in the region, and another to the associate dean for quality at the public research university. Within a three-institution design whose institutions are described by type, these are effectively unique identifiers: each names a single post at a single identifiable institution. The consent basis reported in §3.5 is therefore not the basis on which the findings are written, and the quoted material is critical of the participants' own institutions. Uncorrected, publishing this text would breach the confidentiality assurance the paper says participants were given, which makes acceptance impossible independently of every other defect. I treat this as a human-participants integrity problem, not a copy-editing matter.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified" and §4.1 "the quality director of the largest private university in the region"
**Confidence**: 5 — identifiability follows directly from the design's three named institution types.

### W5: Survey denominators do not close and the third institution is absent from the results

Forty-eight respondents are reported in the abstract and §3.2, yet the only analysis in §4.4 involves nine plus eleven cases. Twenty-eight respondents and one entire institution never appear in the results, and no per-site n, role distribution, tenure distribution, or response rate is reported anywhere. Consequently the composite M=3.9 cannot be located — it may be all 48, the 20 compared, or some other subset — and §5's suggestion that institutional type moderates quality culture rests on a comparison that omits a third of the design's institutions. The core qualitative claim survives this, but the quantitative strand and the moderator suggestion require re-reporting from the ground up.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "Comparing staff at the research university (n=9) with staff at the teaching-focused university (n=11)"
**Confidence**: 5 — arithmetic comparison of stated denominators.

### W6: The significance claim is unreportable as stated and not defensible as inference

"A statistically significant difference (p<.05)" names no test, gives no test statistic, no degrees of freedom, no group means or dispersions, no effect size, no confidence interval, and no adjustment for the multiple subgroup comparisons that §3.4 says were "examined where role categories permitted." With nine versus eleven cases, the choice of test and tail is decision-relevant, and no reader can reconstruct or contest the result. I attempted p recomputation and could not complete it for exactly this reason (AR2). My recommendation is unambiguous: either report the full test with an effect size and interval and justify the test choice at these group sizes, or delete the inferential language and present the contrast descriptively with both group means and dispersions. As stated, this cannot stand in either form.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §4.4 survey findings — expected the test name, test statistic, degrees of freedom, effect size, confidence interval, and any multiplicity correction for the reported p<.05; checked §3.3 Data collection, §3.4 Analysis, §4.4, and end matter for supplementary tables
**Confidence**: 5 — standard quantitative reporting requirements, checked against every reporting surface.

### W7: The survey instrument is undocumented, so M=3.9 and SD=0.6 have no interpretable metric

No scale range, no response anchors, no item wording, no item count, no provenance (adapted from a published instrument or newly written), no internal-consistency coefficient, and no dimensionality evidence are given for a score that the paper treats as a unitary measure of "institutional quality culture." Without a range, "moderately positive" is an assertion rather than a reading; without dimensionality evidence, treating quality culture as one summable construct is an untested assumption, and the literature the paper itself reviews (§2) describes quality culture as multi-faceted. This is also what blocked the GRIM and GRIMMER checks in AR1 and AR4.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.3 Data collection — expected the scale range and response anchors, item wording or published source, internal-consistency coefficient, and dimensionality evidence for the quality-culture score; checked §3.1 Design, §3.3, §3.4 Analysis, §4.4 Survey findings, and the reference list for an instrument citation
**Confidence**: 5 — construct-measurement reporting is squarely within my competence.

### W8: §4.1 mischaracterises the design and draws an inference the procedure cannot license

The claim that themes "emerged systematically from the structured protocol" conflicts with §3.1, which specifies semi-structured interviewing; the two cannot both describe the same data collection. The sentence then treats protocol structure as warrant for the conclusion that the pattern is not an artefact of any single institution's circumstances. That does not follow: instrument standardisation says nothing about how observations distribute across sites, which is an empirical matter the paper never reports. The claim of cross-institutional robustness is therefore asserted on a premise that is both factually wrong about the design and logically inapt.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.1 "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol" and §3.1 "semi-structured interviews with a supplementary staff survey"
**Confidence**: 5 — internal contradiction plus a straightforward non sequitur.

### W9: Prevalence claims are quantified in language but unquantified in evidence

"Recurred across every interview" is a universal claim over the corpus; "most pervasive" (§4.1) is a comparative ranking of theme prevalence; "consistently" and "recurred repeatedly" appear similarly. None is supported by counts, by a per-site or per-participant distribution, or by an extract table. The themes are illustrated by roughly six quotations in total, several attributed to the same role types. A reader therefore cannot tell whether a theme is present in twelve accounts or in three vivid ones, and cannot check whether it holds in the private institution as well as the public ones. This is repairable with a theme-by-participant matrix, but until then the prevalence language overstates what is shown.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.2 "This relational, distributed understanding of leadership recurred across every interview"
**Confidence**: 5 — the absence of counts is verifiable by inspection.

### W10: The Discussion generalises from three institutions to the sector, and the Conclusion claims priority

§5 states that the findings "demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment" and that the study "can be read as a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture," adding that administrators "everywhere" face the same tension. Twelve purposively recruited post-holders in three institutions of one national system, recruited through the QA offices whose work they describe, cannot warrant a demonstrative sector-level claim; the design supports an interpretive account of these sites. §6's "first comprehensive account" adds an unsubstantiated priority claim on top, which the §2 review of Pettersen (2022) and Rahman (2020) does not obviously support. The core thesis survives at local scope, so this is Major rather than Critical, but the escalation is systematic across abstract, discussion, and conclusion rather than a single loose sentence.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "these findings demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment" and §6 "the first comprehensive account"
**Confidence**: 5 — sampling-to-inference mismatch is the core of my expertise.

### W11: The mixed-methods design has no stated point of integration, and §4.4 both corroborates and contradicts

§3.1 assigns the survey a contextualising role, but no integration procedure is described: no joint display, no matrix relating themes to survey items, no statement of whether integration occurs at design, analysis, or interpretation stage. §4.4 then says the survey "corroborated the qualitative picture" and immediately reports that the interview accounts "had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture," which is a divergence, not a corroboration. Divergence is a legitimate and often valuable mixed-methods result, but it must be analysed as such rather than labelled corroboration in one sentence and expectation-violation in the next. As submitted, this reads as two adjacently reported studies rather than an integrated design, and the label "mixed methods" in the title and abstract is not yet earned.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "The survey corroborated the qualitative picture" and "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture"
**Confidence**: 4 — integration-quality judgement, with the textual contradiction itself certain.

### W12: §5 attributes to a cited source the inverse of the position recorded in the manuscript's own reference list

The Discussion aligns the paper's recommendation with Delacroix (2018) as recommending broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building quality culture. The reference entry for the same work, supplied by the authors, states that Delacroix argues against relying on stakeholder consultation as evidence of quality culture, and §2 describes the same position correctly. The paper's third practical recommendation is thus supported by a source the paper elsewhere says holds the opposite view, and the recommendation loses its warrant. I flag this as an internal inconsistency within the submission and as a defect in the evidence-to-claim chain; whether Delacroix's actual position is characterised correctly is for the domain seat to adjudicate.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture" and References "Delacroix argues against relying on stakeholder consultation as evidence of quality culture"
**Confidence**: 4 — the internal contradiction is certain; the external verdict is the domain reviewer's.

### W13: No limitations statement anywhere in the manuscript

Neither §5 nor §6 nor Methods contains a limitations discussion. §6 gestures at future research across wider institutional types, but that is a research agenda, not an acknowledgement of the present study's inferential ceiling. A study with roughly a dozen interviews, gatekeeper recruitment, one national system, self-report data, an undocumented instrument, and subgroup comparisons on nine and eleven cases has a great deal to declare. The absence is consequential because it is precisely what allows §5's sector-level claim to pass unchallenged inside the paper's own text; a limitations paragraph would have caught it.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §5 Discussion and §6 Conclusion — expected an explicit limitations statement naming sample size, single national system, gatekeeper recruitment, self-report data, and the non-representative survey subgroups; checked §3 Methods, §5 Discussion, §6 Conclusion, and the abstract
**Confidence**: 5 — full-text check for any limitations discussion.

### W14: No reproducibility affordances are supplied

There is no interview guide or topic list, no survey item set, and no data availability or sharing statement (including no statement of why anonymised transcript extracts cannot be shared, which would be a legitimate answer). None of this is fatal on its own, and I recognise the constraints on sharing interview data, but a study whose claims rest on unreported instruments should at minimum deposit or append the protocol and the survey items. This is the cheapest of my requests to satisfy and would materially improve the paper's auditability.

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: end matter — expected an interview guide, the survey item set, and a data availability or sharing statement; checked §3.3 Data collection, §3.4 Analysis, §6 Conclusion, and the reference list for a deposited-protocol citation
**Confidence**: 5 — inspection of the submission's end matter.
