## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** This dimension asks whether the study was actually built to support the kind of inference it attempts: is the design appropriate to the question, is the data provenance and handling described well enough to be trusted, are statistical procedures reported completely rather than selectively, and could a competent colleague reconstruct what was done from the manuscript and its supporting materials? As editor I treat this as a load-bearing dimension — if the methodology reviewer finds it broken at the fatal level, no amount of good writing or topical relevance rescues the submission. I will not score it myself; it belongs to the methodology role.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by the domain reviewer).** Here the question is whether the manuscript's substantive claims are consistent with what the field currently knows, whether prior literature is characterised fairly rather than strawmanned or misattributed, and whether domain-specific terminology, constructs, instruments, and reported results are used correctly. Factual error in the substance of the field is disqualifying in a way that stylistic weakness is not. This dimension sits with the domain specialist; my role is to weigh their verdict in the aggregate decision, not to duplicate it.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the devil's-advocate reviewer, with methodology also eligible).** This concerns the internal logic of the paper as an argument: does the central thesis hold together across sections, does the evidence presented actually license the conclusions drawn, and are there inferential leaps, equivocations, or fallacies that would collapse the argument if pressed? A paper can be methodologically clean and factually accurate and still fail here by over-claiming. It is a mandatory dimension, so a fatal finding drives rejection, but the assessment is not mine to make.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by the perspective reviewer).** This asks whether a reader from an adjacent field could enter the paper without insider decoding — whether framing, definitions, and stated implications are legible outside the immediate specialty, and whether any claims that reach across disciplinary boundaries are actually backed rather than asserted. It is high priority rather than mandatory: a block here forces major revision but cannot by itself trigger rejection. The perspective reviewer owns it.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by me as editor).** This is my dimension. I assess whether the manuscript is organised so that the argument is findable, whether exposition is clear at sentence and section level, whether figures and tables are legible, correctly captioned, and doing genuine work rather than decoration, and whether the submission observes the conventions the venue expects — abstract, structure, citation practice, length discipline. It carries normal priority, so weaknesses here shape revision requests rather than acceptance in principle, but a manuscript that cannot be read cannot be evaluated.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by me as editor).** Also mine. I judge whether this manuscript belongs in the configured venue at all — does it speak to that readership, at that level, on that subject — and whether it advances something original and consequential rather than restating known results or reporting a purely local implementation with no transferable claim. Because it is mandatory, a fatal finding here means rejection regardless of technical quality: a rigorous, accurate, coherent paper in the wrong venue or with no contribution is still a reject.

## Scoring Plan

### D5: writing_and_structure

dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: Whether the manuscript has a navigable structure with a stated question, method, results, and interpretation in identifiable places; whether prose is unambiguous at the sentence level; whether every figure and table is numbered, captioned, referenced in text, and independently interpretable; and whether abstract, citation style, and length conform to venue convention.
what_triggers_block: Organisation or exposition is broken badly enough that a competent reader cannot reliably determine what was done or what is being claimed — for example missing or unlabelled results presentation, figures/tables with no captions or no textual referent, or sections whose content does not match their headings.
what_triggers_warn: The manuscript is followable but has localised defects — verbose or imprecise passages, inconsistent terminology, a weak or non-informative abstract, figures that are legible but poorly labelled, or citation/formatting deviations from venue convention that a copy-edit pass would resolve.
what_triggers_fatal: NOT_APPLICABLE

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution

dimension_id: D6
what_to_look_for: An explicit statement of what is new relative to existing work, evidence that the claimed contribution is generalisable beyond a single local deployment, alignment of topic and depth with the configured venue's scope and readership, and adequate positioning against the prior literature the venue's readers would expect to see engaged.
what_triggers_block: The contribution is stated but thin or under-substantiated for the venue — for instance an incremental replication or single-site implementation report whose transferable claim is asserted rather than demonstrated, or positioning against prior work that is too shallow to establish novelty.
what_triggers_warn: The contribution is real and venue-appropriate but under-articulated — novelty is implicit rather than stated, the readership implications are gestured at without development, or scope is slightly off-centre for the venue in a way an author could correct by reframing.
what_triggers_fatal: The manuscript has no discernible original contribution, or is categorically outside the configured venue's scope and readership, such that no revision within the current study could make it publishable here.
