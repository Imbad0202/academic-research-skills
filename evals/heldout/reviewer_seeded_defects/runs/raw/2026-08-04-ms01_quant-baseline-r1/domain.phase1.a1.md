## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** This dimension asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical reporting, and reproducibility supports would survive scrutiny by competent referees in the manuscript's own field. From my seat as the domain-accuracy reviewer I read this as the "could this study have produced the numbers it reports" test: sampling and assignment, treatment of missing or attritted cases, choice and reporting of estimators, uncertainty quantification, and enough procedural detail that another group could rerun the analysis. It is a mandatory dimension owned by the methodology reviewer, so I do not score it, but I note that a claim can be domain-plausible and still methodologically unsupported, and vice versa.

**D2 — domain_accuracy.** This is my dimension. It asks whether every substantive assertion the manuscript makes about its subject matter is consistent with what the field currently knows, whether the literature it cites is characterised faithfully rather than conveniently, and whether the specialised vocabulary, constructs, instruments, metrics, and reported results are used in the sense the field actually uses them. Three distinct failure surfaces live here: factual error about the world (a stated empirical regularity that the evidence base contradicts), misrepresentation of prior work (attributing to a cited source a finding, scope, or effect direction it does not have; ignoring a well-established contrary literature), and terminological or construct error (using a field term to denote something other than its accepted referent, conflating operationally distinct constructs, or reporting derived quantities that are internally inconsistent with the definitions given). It is mandatory, so a fatal defect here drives outright rejection.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** This dimension concerns the internal logic of the manuscript: whether the central thesis is stated consistently across abstract, body, and conclusions; whether the evidence actually presented licenses the inferences drawn from it; and whether the argument avoids fallacies serious enough to hollow out its main claim. It overlaps with mine at the seam where a domain misstatement becomes a load-bearing premise, but the dimension itself is owned by the devil's-advocate reviewer and shared with methodology, so I do not score it.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** This dimension asks whether a competent reader from a neighbouring discipline can enter the paper: are framings and definitions supplied rather than assumed, are implications drawn in terms that travel beyond the immediate subfield, and are any claims about relevance to other disciplines actually backed by evidence rather than asserted. It is a high-priority dimension owned by the perspective reviewer; I do not score it, though I recognise that unexplained jargon and misused jargon are different failures and only the latter is mine.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** This dimension covers organisation, expositional clarity, quality and legibility of figures and tables, and conformity to the target venue's conventions of formatting, length, and section structure. It is a normal-priority dimension owned by the editor-in-chief role. I do not score it; a paper can be beautifully written and factually wrong, and my job is the second half of that sentence.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution.** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it advances something original and substantial enough to matter to that venue's readership. Judging originality requires knowing what already exists, which touches my territory, but the dimension is mandatory and owned by the editor-in-chief, so my contribution is limited to supplying accurate statements about the prior literature rather than scoring fit myself.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether every domain claim, cited prior finding, technical term, construct definition, instrument description, and reported quantity is faithful to the current evidence base of the paper's field — checking cited works for correct attribution of findings, scope, population, and effect direction; checking that specialised terminology denotes what the field takes it to denote and that operationally distinct constructs are not conflated; checking that reported statistics, units, and derived values are internally consistent with the definitions and data the paper itself supplies; and checking that well-established contrary evidence is engaged rather than silently omitted.
what_triggers_block: A load-bearing domain error that the paper's own conclusions rest on — for example, a central cited source materially misrepresented (wrong finding, reversed effect direction, or scope inflated beyond what the source supports), a core construct defined or operationalised in a way the field does not accept and then used as the basis for the headline claim, or a stated empirical regularity that is contradicted by the established literature and is not merely incidental to the argument.
what_triggers_warn: Domain inaccuracies that are real but not load-bearing — imprecise or dated use of a technical term, a peripheral citation whose characterisation is loose or over-generalised, an omitted body of relevant prior work that would sharpen but not overturn the contribution, or a minor numerical or unit inconsistency that does not change the reported direction or substantive interpretation of a result.
what_triggers_fatal: Domain misrepresentation of a kind that cannot be repaired by revision because the paper's factual foundation is invalid — for example, fabricated, non-existent, or systematically mis-attributed references; results that are arithmetically or definitionally impossible given the stated data and construct definitions; or a pattern of misstatement across multiple central claims sufficient to make the manuscript unreliable as a report of what is known in the field.
