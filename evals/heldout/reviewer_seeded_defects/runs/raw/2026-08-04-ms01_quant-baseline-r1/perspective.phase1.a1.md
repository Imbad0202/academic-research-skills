## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** This dimension asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical reporting, and reproducibility supports would survive scrutiny from a competent methodologist in the paper's own field. It is owned by the methodology reviewer, and I am not scoring it. From my cross-disciplinary vantage point, what matters about D1 is that methodological choices are described in language that an adjacent-field reader could evaluate rather than merely trust; when a design decision is only legible to specialists holding tacit conventions, that is a relevance problem for me even though the rigor judgement itself belongs elsewhere.

**D2 — domain_accuracy.** Here the question is whether the manuscript's claims match the current state of evidence in its own domain, whether prior work is represented faithfully, and whether domain terminology and reported results are factually correct. This is the domain reviewer's mandate. My interest in it is adjacent: terminology that is accurate inside the field can still be used equivocally when the paper reaches across to neighbouring literatures, and borrowed constructs from other disciplines can be cited accurately yet applied outside the conditions under which they were established.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** This dimension covers whether the central thesis holds together internally, whether the evidence actually bears on the claims made, and whether any reasoning defects undermine the core argument. It is owned by the discourse-analysis reviewer with the methodology reviewer also eligible. I am not scoring it, but coherence and cross-disciplinary accessibility interact: an argument whose chain of inference is only reconstructable by insiders may read as coherent to specialists and as a gap to outsiders, and I will note such cases as relevance findings rather than restating them as coherence verdicts.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** This is my dimension. It asks whether the framing, the definitions of key constructs, and the stated implications are genuinely accessible to readers from adjacent fields, and whether any claim that reaches beyond the paper's home discipline is actually substantiated rather than asserted. Two failure modes sit inside it. The first is accessibility: unexplained jargon, undefined constructs, missing motivation for why the problem matters outside its niche, or implications stated at a level of generality the reader cannot locate. The second is overreach: implications drawn for populations, settings, disciplines, or policy arenas that the study's own evidence base does not license. It carries `high` priority, which means a `block` here triggers a major-revision failure condition even though the dimension is not itself mandatory.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** This dimension concerns organisation, clarity of exposition, quality of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's conventions, and it is owned by the editor-in-chief role. I am not scoring it. I do need to keep my own findings distinct from it: prose that is merely awkward or a table that is merely cluttered is a writing issue, whereas prose that leaves an adjacent-field reader unable to determine what a construct refers to is a relevance issue. When the two overlap I will describe the relevance consequence and leave the stylistic remedy to the owner.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution.** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it makes an original contribution of appropriate significance for that readership. It is mandatory and owned by the editor-in-chief. My dimension is adjacent but not identical: fit concerns whether the right readers will encounter the work, while my concern is whether readers slightly outside the core audience can use it once they do. I will not render a fit or novelty verdict, and I will avoid letting a judgement about breadth of interest substitute for the accessibility and substantiation tests that D4 actually specifies.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Explicit definitions of central constructs (including any engagement, retention, and analytics-deployment measures) at first use in terms an adjacent-field reader could apply; a motivation section that states why the problem matters beyond its immediate subfield; scope statements that name the population, setting, and platform the findings pertain to; implications and generalisation claims whose reach is tied to specific reported evidence; and citation or explanation whenever a construct, theory, or method is imported from a neighbouring discipline.
what_triggers_block: Central constructs are never operationally defined for a non-specialist, or the paper's headline implications extend to populations, institutions, disciplines, or policy decisions well outside the studied setting with no evidence or hedging offered to support that extension, so that an adjacent-field reader would be misled about what the work establishes.
what_triggers_warn: Constructs are defined but only loosely or late, motivation for outside readers is thin or assumed, borrowed cross-disciplinary terminology is used without explanation, or a subset of implications overreaches the evidence while the main claims stay properly scoped — all repairable by adding definitions, scope qualifiers, and framing text without new data.
