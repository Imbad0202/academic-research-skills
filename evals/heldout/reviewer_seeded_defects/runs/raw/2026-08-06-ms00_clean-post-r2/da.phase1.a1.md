## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** The contract asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical reporting, and reproducibility affordances would survive scrutiny by a competent peer reviewer in this field. As an adversary I read this as a demand for receipts: is the sampling frame specified and defensible, are response rates and attrition disclosed, are the measures validated or ad hoc, are inferential statistics reported with the quantities needed to check them (effect sizes, intervals, exact tests, df), and could an independent party re-run the analysis from what is given? Absence of these is not a stylistic gap but a rigor deficit. Ownership sits with the methodology reviewer, so my job is not to score it but to avoid letting a rigor complaint masquerade as a coherence complaint in my own reasoning.

**D2 — domain_accuracy.** This dimension demands that domain-specific claims track the current evidence base, that prior work is characterised as it actually is rather than as a convenient foil, and that terminology and reported results contain no factual errors. The adversarial reading is that a manuscript can be internally tidy and still be wrong about the field: mislabelled constructs, borrowed theoretical vocabulary used loosely, or a literature summary that overstates consensus or novelty. This belongs to the domain reviewer; I must not score it, and I must be careful not to convert my suspicion about domain claims into a coherence verdict.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** This is my dimension. The contract requires that the central thesis be internally consistent, that the evidence actually adduced supports the claims made, and that no fallacy undermines the core argument. Adversarially, coherence is not the same as readability: a manuscript can be lucid, well-signposted, and still commit the decisive errors — inferring causation or influence from cross-sectional association, letting a construct silently change definition between hypothesis and discussion, treating one measure as a proxy for another without argument, concluding more strongly in the abstract than the results licence, or generalising beyond the population the evidence reaches. My task is to trace each headline claim back to the specific evidence offered and mark every step where the inference does not carry, including the case where limitations are acknowledged in one section but the conclusions elsewhere proceed as if they did not exist.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** The contract asks whether framing, definitions, and implications are legible to adjacent-field readers and whether interdisciplinary claims are substantiated rather than gestured at. The hostile reading is that borrowing a construct or framework from a neighbouring discipline creates a debt: the borrowing must be justified and the imported assumptions must hold in the new setting. This is the perspective reviewer's dimension; I do not score it.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** This concerns organisation, clarity of exposition, quality of figures and tables, and conformity to venue conventions. The adversarial angle is that presentation defects can conceal substantive ones — tables that do not report what the text claims they report, or structure that separates a claim from its evidence so the reader cannot check the link. Ownership is the editor-in-chief's, so I record such observations only where they bear on whether an argument can be verified at all, and I do not assign a score here.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution.** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it makes an original, significant contribution for that readership. The adversarial reading is that confirming an already well-established relationship, or reporting a single-site descriptive survey, may be sound and still contribute little; conversely, a genuine contribution may be undersold. This is the editor-in-chief's call, not mine, and I will not let a judgement about contribution size leak into my coherence score.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether each headline claim in the abstract, results, discussion, and conclusion can be traced to specific evidence actually presented, with inference steps that hold — consistent construct definitions across sections, design-appropriate causal language, no substitution of one measure for another without argument, no conclusion stronger or broader than the reported evidence and stated population support, and acknowledged limitations genuinely constraining the conclusions rather than sitting inert.
what_triggers_block: The central thesis rests on an inference the evidence cannot bear — for example causal, directional, or effectiveness conclusions drawn from associational or cross-sectional data, or a key construct redefined mid-argument — such that the paper's main claim would not stand even if all reported numbers are correct.
what_triggers_warn: The core argument survives but specific secondary claims overreach or are unsupported — abstract or conclusion phrasing outrunning the results, generalisation beyond the sampled population, limitations acknowledged yet not reflected in the strength of adjacent claims — all repairable by rewording and scoping without new data or a new analysis.
what_triggers_fatal: The manuscript is argumentatively self-defeating: two or more load-bearing claims are mutually contradictory, or the stated conclusion is contradicted by the paper's own reported evidence, or the thesis is circular or unfalsifiable as written, so no revision short of rebuilding the argument could rescue it.
