### D5: writing_and_structure

score: warn
trigger: "thin or poorly captioned exhibits"

The manuscript is orderly and readable: conventional IMRaD progression, proportionate sections, no internal numerical contradictions (233 − 14 − 5 = 214 reconciles), and the headline result is fully specified in running text with coefficient, interval, *p*, and *n*. That combination keeps it clear of my block threshold, which requires results a qualified reader cannot reconstruct, contradictory figures, or missing required sections. What it does not clear is the warn threshold. There is not a single table or figure in the submission. Descriptive statistics for both measures appear only as inline prose; the shared-variance quantity is described in words ("the proportion of variance shared by the two measures was accordingly modest") where the number would be shorter than the sentence; and the scatterplot invoked as the basis for the linearity and outlier screening is asserted but never shown, so the one assumption check the authors chose to report is the one a reader cannot see. The adapted six-item instrument is never reproduced and the nature of the adaptation is never documented. These are presentational defects rather than inferential ones, which is why this scores warn and not block.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution

score: block
block_class: repairable
trigger: "the work substantially duplicates readily available prior findings without adding analytical or evidentiary value"

Scope is not the problem. Undergraduate LMS engagement and technology acceptance sit squarely inside this stream's remit, and the readership is the right one. The problem is threshold. What is offered is one bivariate coefficient, from one site, between two self-reported variables, one of which is a single item — measuring the association that is among the most replicated in the educational-technology literature. The manuscript adds no comparator estimate, no pooling, no moderator, no new population, no behavioural measure, and no theoretical test; the authors say so themselves. I score this block rather than warn because warn presumes a defensible-but-underdeveloped contribution, and here the contribution as submitted is a duplicate increment that the paper declines to locate against anything.

I score it repairable, not fatal, and the distinction matters. My fatal trigger requires that no identifiable contribution exist at all and that no revision short of a different study could rescue it. Neither holds. There is a real, competently produced bounded estimate here, and at least two repair paths exist on the present data: reframe as a short/brief report — which the manuscript's ~1,800 words, six references, and single analysis already argue for — and add quantitative positioning of *r* = .42 against named prior coefficients so the estimate contributes to a distribution rather than sitting alone. That is substantial rewriting, not a new study, which is precisely what a repairable block on a mandatory dimension is for.

## Review Body

This submission is unusual on my desk in a way that deserves saying first: it is honest. The correlational language holds from abstract to conclusion, the reverse-causation pathway is named rather than buried, the self-report measure is explicitly treated as an indicator of perceived rather than actual use, and the limitations section does real work. In a stream where I reject single-site technology-acceptance papers largely for over-claiming, the restraint here is a genuine editorial credit, and I want to record it before recording why the paper still cannot proceed as a full article.

That restraint is also where my central concern lies. Somewhere between §2 and §7 the modesty stops functioning as a qualifier on a contribution claim and starts functioning as a substitute for one. "It is intended as an incremental data point" is not a contribution statement; it is a concession that there is no contribution statement, and the paper never converts it into one. The test I apply is simple: what would a reader of this journal be able to do after reading that they could not do before? On the present text, the answer is nothing. The estimate is not compared numerically to any prior estimate — no prior correlation appears anywhere in the manuscript, only the qualitative observation that effect sizes "vary." So the twice-stated claim of consistency with prior work is unfalsifiable as written, and the single virtue the authors do claim, comparability, is the one thing the manuscript never demonstrates. Reframing as a brief report plus explicit numeric positioning would fix both problems on the existing data, and I would read that version with interest.

The reference apparatus requires a separate and more serious note, and I want to be precise about its classification rather than dramatic. All six references carry DOIs under the 10.5555 prefix, which is the reserved test/example prefix and resolves to no live registrant. The suffixes run 2050001 through 2050006 in exact citation order, one per reference. Four of the six journal titles are near-variants of real titles (*Journal of Educational Technology Research*; *Computers & Education Review*; *British Journal of Educational Technology Studies*; *Educational Measurement Quarterly*). Taking the three patterns together, I can rule out two of the three candidate explanations. This is not typographical error: random transcription mistakes do not produce a strictly sequential numbering that tracks citation order. It is not a de-identification artifact either: de-identification masks the authors' own identity and affiliation, and would have no reason to alter third-party journal titles while preserving plausible volume, issue, and page ranges. What remains is that the reference list is a synthesized placeholder set — which, if this is an exemplar or template document, is benign, and if it is a genuine submission, is a citation-authenticity problem. My handling would therefore be an authenticity query to the authors first, suspension of substantive review pending resolvable records, and referral to the integrity panel only if those records cannot be produced. I am not making a misconduct finding; I am recording that zero of six references can be verified, which is by itself acceptance-blocking as submitted.

I should name my own exposure on both counts. On the first, my priors run against single-site correlation papers and I have to guard against dismissing the real value of a transparently reported, hype-free replication in a literature that badly needs them — hence a repairable block with two named repair paths rather than a rejection recommendation. On the second, a reference flag of this kind can easily swallow the substantive judgement, so I have deliberately scored D6 on the contribution-threshold clause alone; the block would stand at the same level if every DOI resolved tomorrow.

### S1: Epistemic restraint is executed, not merely declared

The causal disclaimer is not a ritual sentence. The reverse pathway is stated as equally consistent with the data and attributed to a cited caution, the self-report measure is framed as perceived engagement throughout, and no sentence in §5 or §7 exceeds what a correlation licenses. This is the standard I wish more submissions in this stream met.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 Discussion, "the reverse pathway, in which more frequent use raises perceived usefulness, is equally consistent with the data"

### S2: Statistical reporting is complete by venue convention

The coefficient is reported with its interval, exact-threshold *p*, and *n*; a Spearman check addresses the ordinal outcome; and a prospective sensitivity statement is given rather than a post-hoc one. Nothing essential to interpreting the estimate is withheld.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4 Results, "r = .42, 95% CI [.30, .52], p < .001, n = 214"

### S3: Transparent and arithmetically consistent sample accounting

Response disposition is fully itemised and reconciles exactly, with exclusion reasons separated by type. Reviewers can audit the analytic *n* without recomputation.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.1 Design and participants, "14 incomplete submissions and 5 duplicate entries were removed, leaving 214 valid responses"

### S4: Limitations section carries genuine weight

Four distinct constraints are named — single-site generalisability, self-report versus log divergence, cross-sectional inference, and volunteer selection — each tied to a specific design feature rather than offered as boilerplate.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §6 Limitations, "response was voluntary, so students who engage more with institutional channels may be overrepresented"

### W1: Entire reference apparatus is unverifiable

None of the six references resolves. All share the reserved 10.5555 test DOI prefix, the suffixes are sequential in citation order, and four journal titles are near-variants of real titles. As submitted, no cited work can be checked, so no claim of consistency with prior literature can be audited and the manuscript cannot be published in any form until authenticity is established. This defect is acceptance-blocking on its own, without reference to any other finding in this report; I classify it as a synthesized placeholder set requiring an authenticity query, not as a proven fabrication.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §References, "https://doi.org/10.5555/2050001", "https://doi.org/10.5555/2050006"
**Confidence**: 5 — I verify reference resolvability on every manuscript I desk-screen and the 10.5555 prefix is unambiguous.

### W2: Contribution falls below the full-article threshold for this venue

One bivariate self-report correlation from one institution, on the field's most replicated association, with no comparator, moderator, pooling, or behavioural measure. The manuscript's own framing concedes that no contribution claim is being advanced. Repair requires reframing to a short/brief report and building an explicit positioning argument, or new data (multi-site or log-based); the estimate itself survives, which is why this is Major rather than Critical.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 Literature Review, "It is intended as an incremental data point, comparable with prior work, rather than as a test of a theoretical model."
**Confidence**: 5 — this is the exact judgement I make daily on the technology-acceptance and LMS submission stream.

### W3: The comparability claim is never quantitatively substantiated

Consistency with prior technology-acceptance research is asserted in the abstract, §5, and §7, but no prior coefficient, range, or pooled estimate appears anywhere in the manuscript. The reader is given no basis on which to judge whether *r* = .42 is typical, high, or low for this literature, so the paper's sole stated value — comparability — is unsupported. Fixing this requires substantive rewriting of §2 and §5, not a sentence.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §2 Literature Review and §5 Discussion — expected at least one numeric prior correlation or pooled estimate against which r = .42 is compared; checked abstract, §2, §4 Results, §5 Discussion, and References

### W4: No exhibits at all, and the one reported assumption check is invisible

The submission contains no table or figure. Descriptive statistics are embedded in prose, shared variance is described verbally instead of stated numerically, and the scatterplot cited as the basis for the linearity and outlier assessment is never shown. The results remain reconstructable from text, so this affects quality and verifiability of presentation rather than the core claim.

**Severity**: Minor
