### ⚠ Verification flag routed to the review team

All six references share DOI prefix **10.5555** with sequential suffixes **2050001–2050006**. The 10.5555 prefix is a widely used placeholder/test prefix and does not correspond to a registered publisher; the sequential suffixes are consistent with placeholder generation rather than independent registration. Two journal titles are also plausible-but-unverifiable near-variants of real journals (*British Journal of Educational Technology **Studies*** vs. the actual *British Journal of Educational Technology*; *Computers & Education **Review*** vs. *Computers & Education*).

This is flagged as **a verification task, not a finding**. It may indicate a de-identified or synthetic manuscript, a citation-manager artifact, or fabricated references — the review team must check, not assume. Primary ownership: Journal-Fit Reviewer (editorial screening). Secondary: Peer Reviewer 2 (literature coverage).

## Recommended Target Journals (Top 3)

1. **Education and Information Technologies** (Springer, Q1/Q2 by metric but high-volume and receptive to incremental TAM/LMS survey work) — the most realistic home *if* the authors expand the theoretical framing and reference base. Currently under-powered on literature, not on statistics.
2. **Research in Learning Technology** (ALT, open access, Q2/Q3) — explicitly welcomes single-institution, practitioner-relevant studies and short-form empirical reports. The paper's calibrated modesty is a fit rather than a liability here.
3. **Journal of Applied Research in Higher Education** (Emerald, Q3) — applied/institutional orientation, tolerant of single-site descriptive findings framed for practice.

*Alternative if the authors want the fastest clean route*: reframe as a **brief/short report** (e.g., *Contemporary Educational Technology*, or a short-report track). This matches the paper's actual scale and reduces the novelty burden. The Journal-Fit Reviewer should treat "is this an article or a short report?" as a live question, not a formality.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #2

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 1
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 1
**Identity Description**: Quantitative survey methodologist and psychometrician in an educational measurement department; specializes in measurement error in self-report instruments and in the consequences of single-item outcome measures; regularly reviews for *Educational and Psychological Measurement* and teaches a graduate seminar on why reliability attenuates observed correlations.

**Review Focus**:
1. **Single-item ordinal DV and attenuation**: The outcome is one five-point frequency item with no reliability estimate. Assess how much of the "moderate" r = .42 is bounded by unreliability in the DV, and whether a disattenuation discussion or a multi-item use measure is required before the coefficient can be compared to prior estimates at all.
2. **Sampling and response-rate reporting**: There is no sampling frame size and no response rate — 233 responses arrived from an unstated eligible population. There are no demographics beyond the assertion that the sample "spanned all four year levels." Determine whether nonresponse bias can be assessed at all, and whether the voluntary-response limitation in §6 is a fix or a shield.
3. **Internal consistency of the data-handling account**: §3.3 states "no identifying information was collected" and that responses "could not be linked back to individual students," yet §3.1 reports removing 5 duplicate entries. Establish how duplicates were detected under strict anonymity, and whether the deduplication procedure is compatible with the stated ethics protocol.

**Also verify** (secondary, deterministic checks): the CI–point-estimate consistency (Fisher-z on r = .42, n = 214 yields approximately [.30, .53] — the reported [.30, .52] is consistent, and the reviewer should confirm this rather than assume it); the α = .88 report absent any dimensionality evidence for a six-item adapted scale; and whether the power statement is genuinely a priori or a post hoc sensitivity calculation presented as design justification.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether the reported precision of the statistics creates an unearned impression of rigor that the measurement layer does not support — clean inference on top of a one-item outcome.
**Possible blind spots**: May treat measurement critique as sufficient grounds for rejection without weighing whether the paper's own limitations section has already conceded the point honestly; may under-attend to whether any of the requested fixes are feasible within this design.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #3

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 2
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 2
**Identity Description**: Senior higher-education researcher in educational technology whose own work synthesizes TAM/UTAUT applications in post-secondary settings; author of a meta-analysis of perceived-usefulness–use associations across LMS studies; knows the canonical literature (Davis 1989; Venkatesh et al. 2003/2012; Šumak et al. 2011; Scherer et al. 2019) and the pooled effect sizes by heart.

**Review Focus**:
1. **Literature base adequacy and canonical omission**: Six references, none of them the foundational TAM sources, no UTAUT, no meta-analysis, no post-2021 work. Assess whether the paper can position itself in a literature it has not engaged, and specify which omissions are disqualifying versus merely thin.
2. **The "incremental data point" claim requires a benchmark**: The paper asks to be read as one point in a distribution (echoing Song, 2018) but never states what that distribution is. Determine whether r = .42 sits near, above, or below the pooled meta-analytic estimate — and note that without this comparison the central contribution claim is unevaluable, not merely modest.
3. **Construct specification of "use"**: The field distinguishes access frequency, session duration, depth of feature use, and assessment-driven compliance use. Evaluate whether "how often did you access the LMS in a typical week" measures a construct the prior literature would recognize as comparable, and whether the Discussion's comparison to Costa & Wren and Ibarra & Poll is therefore apples-to-apples.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether a genuinely saturated literature makes an unbenchmarked single-site replication redundant — and whether the authors know the literature well enough to know that.
**Possible blind spots**: May default to "add more citations" as a remedy when the deeper problem is contribution positioning; may also miss the reference-verification issue by reading the six sources as merely insufficient rather than as unverifiable.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #4

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 3
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 3
**Identity Description**: Director of learning analytics and institutional research at a public university system; owns the LMS data warehouse, chairs the institution's student-data governance committee, and has published on the divergence between survey-reported and log-recorded platform engagement. Approaches every LMS study first by asking what the institution would actually do differently on Monday morning.

**Review Focus**:
1. **The unused-data problem**: The institution owns the LMS and therefore owns the logs. The paper cites Vasquez (2020) on self-report/log divergence, lists it as Limitation 2, and then does nothing about it. Establish whether log data was unavailable (data-governance restriction, IRB scope, no linkage key under anonymity) or simply not pursued — and require the authors to say which. A stated constraint is a limitation; an unexplained omission is a design choice that needs defending.
2. **Actionability of the practical implication**: §5 suggests onboarding that "helps students see concrete usefulness" may warrant institutional attention. From a resource-allocation standpoint, evaluate whether a moderate cross-sectional correlation of ambiguous direction can support any onboarding investment decision, and whether the hedging ("suggested by, not proven by") makes the recommendation safe or makes it empty.
3. **Data-governance and consent architecture**: Assess the anonymity-versus-deduplication tension from a data-governance rather than a statistical angle, and whether the consent language on the landing page would have permitted subsequent log linkage in a future study. This matters for the paper's own "future research using behavioral log data" recommendation — the current consent design may have foreclosed it.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether this study, conducted inside an institution holding the exact behavioral data that would answer its question better, has produced a finding that any institution could act on.
**Possible blind spots**: May undervalue perceived usefulness as a legitimate psychological construct worth measuring in its own right, treating self-report as merely a degraded proxy for logs; may apply an institutional-decision standard that is stricter than a research journal's.

## Review Strategy Recommendations

**1. The defining characteristic of this manuscript is calibrated restraint, and that is a trap for reviewers in both directions.** The authors pre-empt nearly every standard criticism: they name the reverse-causation pathway, cite the self-report/log divergence, acknowledge single-site limits, and refuse causal language throughout. Reviewers must not "discover" limitations the authors already stated as though they were new — that produces a review that looks rigorous and says nothing. The productive question in every case is narrower: **is the acknowledgment a fix or a shield?** Naming a limitation does not neutralize it. R1 should apply this to the single-item DV, R3 to the unused log data, and the Journal-Fit Reviewer to the contribution claim itself.

**2. Manufactured tension is expected between R1 and R3, and it is deliberate.** R1 will want better measurement within the survey paradigm; R3 will argue the survey paradigm was the wrong instrument when logs were available. These are not compatible fixes — one improves the study, the other replaces it. The synthesizer must not average them. Whichever is chosen determines whether the recommendation is major revision or reject-and-resubmit-as-new-study.

**3. Complementarity on the two hard structural questions.** *Contribution*: the Journal-Fit Reviewer judges it against a journal threshold, R2 against the meta-analytic literature; both must land before a verdict. *Anonymity vs. deduplication*: R1 owns the statistical/procedural framing, R3 the governance framing; the two should converge on a single request for clarification rather than issuing duplicate comments.

**4. Do not let the reference flag substitute for substantive review.** If the 10.5555 DOIs turn out to be placeholders or fabrications, that is decisive and short-circuits everything. But the review must be complete on the substance regardless, so that a legitimate explanation (de-identification for review, citation-manager corruption) does not leave the manuscript unreviewed. Run both tracks in parallel.

**5. Register recommendation.** This is a competently executed, honestly reported, under-contributing paper — not a bad paper. Reviewers should adopt a **developmental register**: precise, non-punitive wording. The register governs tone only. The verdict remains whatever the evidence against the criteria supports, and none of the four reviewers should soften a recommendation because the authors were candid.
