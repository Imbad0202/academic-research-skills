## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role):** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data collection, handling, and statistical reporting would survive scrutiny by competent peer reviewers in the manuscript's field, and whether enough procedural detail is disclosed for another researcher to reproduce or at least audit the work. From where I sit as the domain reader, this is the dimension that judges *how* the evidence was made, not *whether* the resulting claims match what the field already knows. I do not score it, but I note that domain-level factual errors and methodological weaknesses often co-occur, so my findings may inform the methodology reviewer without displacing their judgement.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role — mine):** This is my dimension. It asks whether every substantive claim in the manuscript is consistent with the current state of evidence in educational technology and higher education research; whether prior work — theories, models, named instruments, landmark studies — is described as its authors actually described it, rather than paraphrased into something the source does not support; and whether field-specific terminology, constructs, measures, and reported results are used correctly and without internal contradiction. It covers misattribution, mischaracterised constructs, outdated or superseded findings presented as current, conflation of distinct technical terms, numerical or unit errors in reported domain quantities, and claims stated with more evidential force than the cited literature bears. Accuracy here is about correspondence to the field's evidence base, not about whether the argument is elegant or the writing is clear.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, da role):** This dimension covers whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together logically, whether the evidence presented actually licenses the conclusions drawn, and whether any reasoning fallacies hollow out the main argument. It is adjacent to my remit but distinct: a paper can be internally coherent while resting on factually wrong premises about the domain, and it can be domain-accurate while reasoning invalidly from accurate facts. I do not score it; where I find a factual error that also breaks a chain of inference, I will report the factual side and leave the inferential verdict to the discourse-analysis reviewer.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high, perspective role):** This dimension judges whether the framing, definitions, and stated implications are intelligible and useful to readers from adjacent fields, and whether any claim reaching beyond the home discipline is actually substantiated rather than asserted. My concern would only overlap when a cross-disciplinary claim is not merely inaccessible but factually wrong about the borrowed field's evidence; even then, the scoring belongs to the perspective reviewer, and I will confine myself to flagging the factual defect.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal, eic role):** This dimension assesses manuscript organisation, clarity of exposition, quality and legibility of figures and tables, and conformance to the venue's formatting and reporting conventions. It is presentational rather than evidential. A sentence can be beautifully written and factually false, or clumsy and entirely correct; only the second half of each pair is mine. I do not score this dimension.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, eic role):** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it offers an original and significant enough contribution for that readership. Judging originality requires knowing the prior literature, which is also the basis of my own accuracy assessment, so my findings about how prior work is represented may bear on the editor's novelty judgement. The scoring decision nonetheless rests with the editor-in-chief role, not with me.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Verify each domain claim against established evidence in educational technology and higher education — correct naming and characterisation of theories, models, and instruments; faithful representation of what cited prior studies found and concluded; correct and consistent use of field-specific constructs and terminology; internally consistent and arithmetically plausible reported quantities; and claim strength proportionate to the evidence actually cited.
what_triggers_block: One or more clear, load-bearing factual errors about the domain — for example a named theory, model, or instrument described as something it is not, a cited study's findings reversed or materially misstated, distinct constructs conflated in a way that changes what the results mean, or reported domain quantities that are internally inconsistent — where the error touches a claim the paper's conclusions depend on and cannot be fixed without new analysis or substantive rewriting.
what_triggers_warn: Domain inaccuracies that are real but peripheral or repairable in place — imprecise or non-standard terminology, an overstated generalisation beyond what the cited source supports, a citation attached to a claim it only partly supports, an outdated finding presented without acknowledging later work, or a construct label used loosely — none of which overturns the manuscript's central claims once corrected.
what_triggers_fatal: Pervasive or fabricated domain content that makes the manuscript unsalvageable as a factual account — for example cited sources that do not support anything attributed to them or appear not to exist, results reported that contradict the described measures or design outright, or a systematic misunderstanding of the core construct under study that invalidates the entire body of claims rather than any single one.
