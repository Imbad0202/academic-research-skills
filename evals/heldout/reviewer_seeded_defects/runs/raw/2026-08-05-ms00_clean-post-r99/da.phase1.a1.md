## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer):** The contract demands that the study's design, sampling, measurement, data handling, statistical reporting, and reproducibility affordances survive the ordinary scrutiny of peer review in this field. Read adversarially, this dimension asks whether an informed skeptic could reconstruct what was done and whether the inferential machinery actually licenses the conclusions drawn — not merely whether procedures are described in plausible-sounding prose. Missing denominators, unreported response rates, undefined instruments, absent reliability evidence, or inference language that outruns a cross-sectional design would all be relevant. I do not score this dimension; my role is to note only where coherence problems intersect with it and defer the verdict to the owner.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by the domain reviewer):** This dimension requires that substantive claims match the current state of evidence in the field, that prior literature is represented faithfully rather than conveniently, and that field-specific terminology and reported results contain no factual errors. Adversarially, the question is whether cited constructs are used with their established technical meanings or whether familiar labels have been borrowed to lend authority to a looser argument, and whether the literature is cited in ways the cited sources would endorse. I do not score this dimension and will not substitute my judgement for the domain owner's.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by me as devil's advocate; also eligible for the methodology reviewer):** I must judge whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together internally, whether the evidence actually offered supports the claims actually made, and whether any reasoning defect is severe enough to undermine the core argument rather than merely blemish a paragraph. The adversarial posture here is to attack the load-bearing inferential chain: does the stated research question match what the design can answer; do the results sections deliver the evidence promised in the introduction; does the discussion silently upgrade association to causation, self-report to behaviour, or a sample to a population; are competing explanations acknowledged and dispatched or simply ignored; do abstract, results, and conclusion agree with each other. Rhetorical polish is not coherence, and a paper can be locally well written while its spine is broken. Conversely, an argument can be modest and still fully coherent, so I must not penalise a narrow claim for being narrow.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by the perspective reviewer):** The manuscript's framing, definitions, and stated implications should be usable by a competent reader from an adjacent field, and any claim that reaches across disciplinary boundaries must be substantiated rather than asserted. The adversarial reading asks whether jargon is defined at first use and whether interdisciplinary implications are backed by evidence in this paper or merely gestured at. This is not mine to score.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief role):** Organisation, clarity of exposition, quality and self-sufficiency of figures and tables, and conformity to venue conventions are assessed here. Adversarially, the concern is whether structural disorder or uninterpretable exhibits impede a reader's ability to check the argument at all. I do not score it, though I may flag where presentation problems make coherence unverifiable.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role):** The paper must belong in the configured venue and must make an original contribution of significance appropriate to that readership. The adversarial question is whether the increment over existing work is real and articulated, or whether the manuscript restates settled knowledge in a new sample. This verdict belongs to the owner, not to me.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether the stated research question, the design's inferential reach, the reported evidence, and the discussion/conclusion claims form one consistent chain — checking specifically that abstract, results, and conclusions agree numerically and directionally, that correlational or self-report evidence is not upgraded to causal or behavioural claims, that every claim in the discussion traces to a result actually presented, that rival explanations (common-method bias, selection into the sample, reverse causation between perceived usefulness and use) are addressed rather than ignored, and that hedging is applied where the evidence is thin rather than dropped once the introduction ends.
what_triggers_block: The paper's central conclusion is not supported by the evidence presented — for example the headline claim asserts a causal or predictive relationship that a single cross-sectional self-report design cannot license, or the conclusion contradicts the paper's own reported results, or a load-bearing inferential step is missing entirely — such that the thesis as written cannot stand without new analysis or substantially retracted claims.
what_triggers_warn: The core thesis survives scrutiny but shows localised reasoning defects that a revision can repair without new data — unhedged overstatement in the abstract relative to a properly hedged results section, an obvious alternative explanation left unmentioned, minor inconsistency between reported figures and their narrative description, or a scope claim broader than the sample supports.
what_triggers_fatal: The argument is irreparably incoherent — mutually contradictory core claims that cannot both be retained, a conclusion whose supporting evidence is absent from or contradicted by the manuscript's own data, or circular reasoning in which the outcome is defined by the predictor — so that no revision short of a different study could rescue the central argument.
