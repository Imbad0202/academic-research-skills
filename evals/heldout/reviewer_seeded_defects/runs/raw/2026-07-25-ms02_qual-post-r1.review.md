### Reviewer Configuration Card #2

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 1 — Methodology
**Identity Description**: Professor of research methodology in a school of education, specializing in **integration quality in qualitative-dominant mixed-methods designs** (the Creswell–Plano Clark / Fetters joint-display tradition), with secondary applied training in the psychometrics of organizational climate and culture instruments. Regularly serves as the designated methods reviewer for mixed-methods submissions and has published on why "QUAL + quan" studies routinely fail the integration test.

**Review Focus**:
1. **Sample accounting and internal consistency.** The Abstract states "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth"; §3.2 states "Twelve senior administrators (n=12)." Determine which is correct and whether the discrepancy signals a mid-study change (e.g., two withdrawals, two excluded transcripts) that is not reported. Then, the survey: n=48 total, but §4.4's subgroup comparison uses n=9 and n=11 — 20 of 48 respondents. The disposition of the other 28 (including all private-university respondents) is never stated. Also: how many participants came from each of the three institutions?
2. **Qualitative analytic procedure.** §3.4 is four sentences and describes thematic analysis only as "coding proceeded iteratively... until a stable structure was reached." Absent: the analytic tradition (Braun & Clarke? framework analysis? something else), whether coding was inductive or deductive, how many coders and with what agreement procedure, whether member checking or an audit trail exists, any positionality statement, and any defensible account of saturation. Then §4.1 asserts "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol" — but §3.1 and §3.3 describe **semi-structured** interviews. Establish which it was; the contradiction matters because the sentence uses the word "structured" to warrant a confidence claim ("giving us confidence that the pattern was not an artifact"), which does not follow from either method.
3. **Survey instrumentation and inference.** No instrument is named or described: no item count, no response scale (M=3.9 on a 5-point scale? 6-point?), no source or adaptation history, no reliability coefficient, no factor structure, no evidence that "institutional quality culture" was measured as one construct. The inferential claim in §4.4 — "a statistically significant difference (p<.05)" between n=9 and n=11 — reports no test name, no test statistic, no degrees of freedom, no effect size, no confidence interval, and no assumption checks; with these cell sizes the result is fragile and the paper nonetheless builds an interpretive claim on it ("institutional type as a possible moderator").
4. **Handling of disconfirming evidence.** §4 (closing paragraph) states the study "achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views," and then, in the very next sentence, that participants with "more skeptical or dissenting views about the value of the entire QA enterprise" were "excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure that organized our analysis." Excluding cases *because* they do not fit the analytic structure inverts the logic of thematic analysis and is incompatible with the "full range" claim in the same paragraph. Assess how many participants were affected and whether the three-theme structure survives their reinstatement.
5. **Mixed-methods integration.** No design typology is named, no integration point is specified, no joint display or narrative weaving is offered. §4.4 says the survey "corroborated the qualitative picture" and then, three sentences later, that "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture" — i.e., the survey *diverged* from expectation. Corroboration and disconfirmation cannot both be true of the same result; and the expectation appears to have been stated post hoc.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether the analytic claims are traceable to procedures that were actually performed and reported — specifically, whether a reader could reconstruct how 12 (or 14) transcripts became exactly three themes, and whether a single p-value from a 9-versus-11 comparison is being asked to carry an institutional-type moderation argument it cannot bear.

**Possible blind spots**: May treat the missing foundational QA literature as outside the methods remit and leave it unmentioned; may not evaluate whether the *theoretical* framing (quality culture as negotiation) is the right frame at all; will likely not comment on the participant-confidentiality problem in the quotations, reading anonymization as an ethics-committee matter rather than a methods one. (Compensated by R2 and R3.)

The gap claim needs to be narrowed to something defensible. Separately, the national system is never named ("three universities in a single national system"), which makes the QA regime, its regulatory intensity, and its review cycle unknowable — and therefore makes the findings' transferability unassessable. **Will particularly care about**: Whether the paper's argumentative chain holds when each cited source is checked against what it actually claims — the Delacroix reversal is not a formatting slip but a load-bearing error, because it converts a source that undermines the paper's recommendation into a source that endorses it.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #4

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 3 — Cross-disciplinary / Practical
**Identity Description**: Director of institutional review at a national quality assurance agency, with 15 years of practice designing and chairing external review panels; background in public administration and regulatory accountability rather than education research; concurrently serves on a university research ethics committee, where the recurring caseload is **deductive-disclosure risk in interview studies of small elite populations**. Reads academic QA research primarily to ask: could an institution or an agency act on this, and was it obtained responsibly?

**Review Focus**:
1. **Participant confidentiality and deductive identification.** §3.5 states that "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified in reported findings." §4.1 then attributes quotations to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and "the associate dean for quality at the public research university." Given a stated sample of three named-by-type institutions in one national system, each of these descriptors points to approximately one living person, and the attributed content is professionally sensitive ("It's theater, and everyone knows it's theater"; "nothing behind that a teacher would recognize as helping them teach"). The §3.5 assurance and the §4.1 attributions cannot both stand. This is a correctable problem — strip role-plus-institution-type descriptors, use neutral participant identifiers — but it must be corrected before publication, and the ethics statement must be made truthful rather than aspirational.
2. **The tripartite claim rests on one party's testimony.** The Abstract and §6 both conclude that quality culture is "an ongoing negotiation among administrators, academics, and external bodies." The study sampled administrators (n=12) and mid-level staff (n=48). Academics as such were not interviewed; external reviewers, agency staff, and panel chairs were not approached; no documentary or observational data from actual review processes was collected. From an agency vantage this is the paper's most consequential asymmetry: the "theater" characterization is an account of the panel's behavior given entirely by the people preparing for the panel, with no corroborating data from the panel side. Assess whether the negotiation claim should be reframed as *administrators' account of* a negotiation.
3. **Actionability of the practical recommendation.** §5 recommends that "processes that maximize genuine participation should be favored," and §6 recommends QA processes "that create room for meaning-making rather than merely demanding documentation." From a practitioner standpoint these are directionally agreeable and operationally empty: they specify no mechanism, no trade-off against the evidentiary function that external review legally requires, and no account of cost. The paper also does not engage the obvious practitioner objection — that documentation is the only medium through which an external body can verify anything at all, so "less documentation, more meaning" is not a design available to a regulated institution without a change at the agency end. Determine what, concretely, an institution or agency could do differently on Monday morning after reading this paper.
4. **Whose interests the study represents.** Administrators in §4.3 position themselves as "custodians of student interests against a tide of box-ticking." No student data, no learning-outcome data, and no measure of whether QA improved anything for students appears anywhere in the study. The paper is therefore an account of how QA *feels* to those who administer it, presented at points as an account of whether QA *works*. Flag where that slippage occurs.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether the paper is honest about the limits of an administrator-only evidence base before it issues recommendations to the institutions and agencies whose behavior it is characterizing — and whether the people who spoke candidly about their own institutions are actually protected in the published text.

**Possible blind spots**: Will not evaluate thematic-analysis rigor or the statistical subgroup test; may under-weight the theoretical-framework absence, since practitioners tend to find theory sections dispensable; may be more indulgent than R2 about the thin literature base. ## Review Strategy Recommendations

**Special characteristics requiring particular attention**

1. **Polish–substance mismatch is the defining feature of this manuscript.** The prose is fluent, the structure is complete, the references are consistently formatted, and the argument moves smoothly. Underneath, the paper contains at least five internal self-contradictions — abstract *fourteen* vs. methods *twelve*; semi-structured protocol (§3.1, §3.3) vs. "structured protocol" (§4.1); "balanced representation... the full range of administrator views" vs. dissenting views "excluded" (§4, closing paragraph); "the survey corroborated the qualitative picture" vs. "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture" (§4.4); §3.5's "no individual could be identified" vs. §4.1's role-plus-institution attributions. **Direct all reviewers to read §4 and §5 against §2 and §3 rather than sequentially.** Every one of these defects is invisible on a linear read and obvious on a cross-read. The panel's dominant failure mode here is being carried by the prose.

2. **The Delacroix (2018) reversal is the single highest-severity finding and must not be lost in the aggregate.** It is not a citation-style problem: §5's central practical recommendation is warranted by a source that argues the opposite, and the paper's own §2 states the correct reading two pages earlier. Whichever reviewer raises it, the synthesizer should carry it forward with its full weight — because the fix is not editorial. If Delacroix is read correctly, he becomes a counterargument the Discussion has to answer, and the paper's third finding needs rebuilding rather than rewording.

3. **The disconfirming-case exclusion sits on the boundary between a methods weakness and a research-integrity concern.** Excluding skeptical participants "as they fell outside the three-theme structure" — while claiming in the adjacent sentence to have captured the full range of views — is analytically circular and is asserted, not concealed. Reviewers should treat it as a serious substantive finding requiring the authors to report how many participants were affected and to reanalyze with them included, and should raise it as a defect in the analysis as written, not as an allegation about intent.

4. **Do not let the survey's apparent precision launder its unknown provenance.** "M=3.9, SD=0.6" and "p<.05" read as hard numbers, but no instrument, scale range, item set, reliability estimate, or test statistic is reported anywhere, and the significant comparison rests on n=9 versus n=11. R1 is configured to press this; EIC and R2 are both likely to accept it. If R1's critique is diluted in synthesis, the panel will have under-reported the weakest evidentiary link in the paper.

5. **Reference-base age is a structural, not cosmetic, signal.** Twelve sources, none earlier than 2018, for a topic with four decades of scholarship. Whether this reflects a deliberately narrow recent-work strategy or an incomplete search, it needs to be named — and it interacts with the novelty claim in §6, since a literature review that starts in 2018 will naturally make almost anything look like a first.

6. **Verify the sources exist before characterizing their content.** All 12 DOIs use the reserved 10.5555 prefix and none of the cited works is independently familiar. Reviewers should flag this as an editorial verification item and should *not* build critiques on assumptions about what these sources contain beyond what the manuscript itself reports — R2's citation-fidelity work should stay anchored to the internal inconsistency (§2 vs. §5 vs. the annotated reference entry), which is demonstrable from the manuscript alone.

**Complementarity and tension among reviewers**

- **Designed complementarity**: EIC owns claim scale and contribution; R1 owns procedure, sample accounting, measurement, and integration; R2 owns citation fidelity, literature canon, and theory; R3 owns ethics, practice, and the asymmetry of the evidence base. The four focus sets are disjoint by construction. Three of the five internal contradictions are each assigned to exactly one reviewer, which means the synthesizer must ensure they are all carried forward — none will be independently corroborated by a second reviewer.

- **Anticipated tension 1 — R1 vs. R3 on sampling.** Both will observe that the evidence base is narrow, but they will mean different things. R1's concern is *inferential*: the numbers do not add up and the analytic procedure is unreconstructable. R3's concern is *evidentiary standing*: the paper makes a three-party claim on one party's testimony. These are genuinely different critiques and both should appear in the synthesis. The synthesizer should resist collapsing them into a single "small sample" comment, which would flatten the most interesting objection in the panel.

- **Anticipated tension 2 — EIC vs. R2 on what to do with the paper.** EIC will likely see a publishable smaller paper inside an over-claimed larger one, and lean toward major revision with the sector-level claims withdrawn. R2, on encountering the Delacroix reversal plus the missing canon plus the absent theoretical framework, may conclude the Discussion needs rebuilding from the literature up — which is a heavier lift than the EIC's framing implies. This is a productive disagreement about *revision depth*, not about facts; the synthesizer should surface it explicitly rather than average it, because the two positions imply different recommendations.

- **Anticipated tension 3 — R3 vs. everyone on the ethics finding.** R3 is the only reviewer configured to catch the deductive-identification problem, and the other three are likely to regard confidentiality as settled by §3.5's approval statement. R3's finding should not be down-weighted for lack of corroboration; it is uncorroborated by design.

- **Convergence to expect**: All four reviewers should independently arrive at some version of "the claims outrun the evidence." Because this convergence is near-certain, the synthesizer should treat unanimity here as expected rather than as unusually strong signal, and should weight the *specific, singly-sourced* findings — the citation reversal, the excluded dissenters, the identifiable quotations, the undocumented instrument — accordingly.

- **Devil's Advocate steer**: The strongest available counter-position is that this is a competent, honestly reported small-scale interpretive study whose real defects are all *rhetorical over-reach* — the "first comprehensive account," the "sector as a whole," the "balanced representation" — and that stripping three paragraphs of overstatement would leave a legitimate, modest, publishable contribution. That reading is worth pressure-testing, because it is largely right about §5 and §6 and largely wrong about §3 and §4: the missing instrument, the unreconstructable coding procedure, and the excluded dissenters are not rhetoric and do not disappear when the rhetoric is toned down.

## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory).** From the editor's chair, this dimension asks whether the study's evidentiary machinery is legible and load-bearing enough to be sent to a readership that will act on it. I am not adjudicating technical craft at the level the methodology seat will — that is Reviewer 1's work — but I am responsible for whether a competent reader can reconstruct where the evidence came from, how it was turned into claims, and whether the design chosen (administrator accounts of QA implementation) can carry the weight the manuscript puts on it. The metadata's 2,249-word budget makes this an explicit calibration question rather than a neutral one: I will judge reporting completeness against a declared short-form article type if one is claimed, and against full-article expectations if none is, and either way I will ask whether brevity has cost the reader the ability to locate the data behind the conclusions.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory).** Quality-culture scholarship is a field with a settled vocabulary and a well-populated literature, and my editorial exposure is to whether this manuscript is a competent citizen of it. That means the constructs it leans on are attributed to the work that actually established them, that "quality culture" is distinguished from quality assurance, quality management, and compliance rather than used interchangeably with them, and that any regulatory or accreditation framework named is characterised as it actually operates. The risk I am screening for at this altitude is not a stray citation slip but a domain error that the contribution claim rests on — a mischaracterised regime or a conflated construct that, once corrected, would dissolve the gap the paper says it fills.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory).** This is the dimension most native to the EIC seat: whether one argument runs unbroken from the question posed to the conclusion drawn. I will trace the through-line — question, evidence, themes, claims, implications — and check that the conclusion answers what was asked rather than what would have been convenient to ask. The title promises institution-building; the stated evidence base is administrator perspectives. Whether that gap is bridged by argument, hedged by acknowledgement, or silently jumped is precisely what this dimension resolves, and it is a coherence question rather than a methods question.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high).** My obligation here is to the readership rather than to the author: can a reader from public administration, organisational studies, management, or education policy — someone outside this manuscript's national quality-assurance regime — enter the paper, follow it, and take something portable away? That requires system-specific terminology and agency procedures to be glossed rather than assumed, and it requires any borrowed organisational-culture theory to be genuinely worked through rather than cited as decoration. This dimension is high-priority rather than mandatory in the contract, but under F3 a block here still carries a major-revision consequence, so I will apply it as a real gate and not as a stylistic preference.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal).** The editorial question is whether the manuscript is assembled well enough for its argument to be assessable and for the venue's conventions to be met — a complete and conventional architecture, a proportionate allocation of a short word budget across background and findings, traceable references, and an abstract that promises only what the body delivers. This is the dimension where I hold the lowest threshold for a negative score, because most defects here are repairable copy-editing rather than editorial obstacles; I reserve the severe end for structure that actively prevents review.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: A recoverable account of participants (how many administrators, in what roles, at what institution type and how many institutions, in which national system) and of sampling logic; how data were generated (interviews, focus groups, documents) and analysed (named analytic approach plus the procedure actually followed, coder arrangements, any trustworthiness or credibility measures); an ethics/consent statement; any reproducibility affordance — instrument, interview guide, coding frame, or data-availability note; whether the strength of the paper's headline claims (effectiveness, causation, prescription) is matched by what self-reported administrator accounts can establish; whether a limitations section names the design's real boundaries; and whether the 2,249-word length is declared as a short-form/research-note article type or is an under-reported full study.
what_triggers_block: The provenance of the evidence cannot be located anywhere in the manuscript — no usable statement of who was studied or how their accounts became findings — so the conclusions rest on data a reader cannot inspect even in outline; or the manuscript asserts causal or effectiveness claims about quality-assurance implementation that a single-timepoint, single-informant-class design cannot support, with no acknowledgement of that gap and no hedging in the abstract or conclusion.
what_triggers_warn: The core elements are present but thin or scattered — participant counts and roles given without sampling rationale, an analytic approach named but not described, no ethics or consent note, no instrument or coding frame available, limitations perfunctory or absent; or claims are mostly calibrated to the design but at least one over-reach appears that rewording and an explicit limitation would repair.

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether "quality culture" is given an operational definition rather than used as a slogan, and whether it is held distinct from quality assurance, quality management, and regulatory compliance; correct attribution of the field's load-bearing constructs and of the internal-versus-external QA and accountability-versus-improvement distinctions; accurate, dated description of whichever regulatory or accreditation framework governs the institutions studied; engagement with the last decade of quality-culture research rather than only its founding texts; whether gap claims ("little is known about…") are defensible against a literature that is in fact well populated; and whether prior studies are cited for findings they actually report.
what_triggers_block: A domain error the contribution rests on — quality culture and compliance-oriented quality assurance treated as the same object while the paper's novelty claim depends on their difference; a quality-assurance or accreditation regime described in a way that materially misstates what it requires or how it operates; or prior work cited for a result it does not contain, where correcting the error removes the gap the study claims to fill.

### Detailed Comments

#### Journal Fit

Topically this is close to an exact fit for *Quality in Higher Education*. The journal's remit is QA implementation, quality culture, and the internal/external interface; we regularly publish qualitative multi-site studies with modest samples, and we are receptive to work that takes QA professionals as subjects rather than as instruments. The manuscript already cites us (Iversen, 2021), which suggests the authors know the venue.

Fit at the level of *claim*, however, is not close. This journal's readers include agency staff and institutional QA professionals who will recognize the "sector as a whole" claim in §5 as unsupportable and will discount the rest of the paper accordingly. They will also, correctly, notice that §5's design recommendation cites Delacroix for the position Delacroix wrote against. Our readership is unusually well placed to catch both, which raises rather than lowers the bar here.

Length is a further fit problem: roughly 2,100 words of main text against a typical 6,000–8,000 for an empirical article in this field. Methods (~255 words) and Findings (~655 words) are both under-specified relative to what the claims require, and no short-form or research-note article type is declared. I read the thinness as a structural gap, not as concision.

If the authors decline the reanalysis this review requires, *Quality Assurance in Education* is the realistic placement for the paper as executed — more practitioner-facing and more tolerant of small-n interpretive work. I would note candidly that its lighter theoretical review means the defects in §3 and §4 might go unchallenged there, which would not serve the authors. *Journal of Higher Education Policy and Management*, where Silva and Tan (2021) sits, would force the positioning statement this manuscript most needs.

#### Originality

The source of originality is claimed as new data — administrators' own accounts where prior work is conceptual — and that claim is defensible. The claim actually made in §6 is a different and indefensible one. Between §2 and §6 the manuscript talks itself out of its own contribution: §2 says Silva and Tan's account "remains largely conceptual rather than grounded in administrators' own words," which is the correct, modest, publishable increment; §6 discards it for "the first comprehensive account." The narrower claim is the true one and it is already written.

I would add that the three themes will strike experienced readers as confirmatory rather than surprising. Ritual compliance, distributed leadership, and identity work each have a cited antecedent in §2. That is not disqualifying — grounding a conceptual claim empirically is a legitimate contribution — but it does mean the paper's originality lives in the texture of the accounts, not in the theme labels. §4.3's held-open-tension formulation is where that texture actually appears, and it deserves more space than it gets.

#### Significance

Conditional on the claims being scaled to the evidence, the significance is moderate and real: this journal's readers would find an empirically grounded account of the intermediary position useful, particularly the finding that administrators spend more energy on persuasion than documentation (§4.2) and that they locate their professional identity in the compliance/meaning gap rather than resolving it (§4.3). Both have implications for how agencies design review processes and how institutions staff quality offices.

Two things currently cap it. First, the practice implications are too abstract to act on (Weakness 14) — after reading this paper, an institutional QA director knows they should favour "genuine participation" but not what to change. Second, the study characterizes external review as theater on the testimony of the reviewed alone (Weakness 6). From an agency vantage that is the paper's most consequential asymmetry, and it is the one most likely to make agency readers set the paper aside — not because the characterization is wrong, but because the manuscript presents an interested party's account as a description of the process.

#### Structural Coherence

The architecture is complete and the transitions are fluent, but the coherence problem here is not sequential — it is that §§4–5 do not survive being read against §§2–3. The novelty claim fails against §2. The Delacroix recommendation fails against §2. "Structured protocol" fails against §3.1 and §3.3. "No individual could be identified" fails against §4.1. "Full range of administrator views" fails against the next sentence. And the sample size fails against the abstract.

This is the defining feature of the manuscript, and I want to record it plainly for the panel: the polish is not evidence about the substance. Every one of these six contradictions is invisible on a linear read and obvious on a cross-read, which means a reviewer carried by the prose will find nothing wrong. I would ask that all seats read §4 and §5 against §2 and §3 rather than in order.

#### Title & Abstract

The title is accurate, clear, and appropriately scoped — "Administrator Perspectives" is exactly the right framing, and notably more honest than §5 and §6 turn out to be. "Building Institutional Quality Culture" does promise institution-building that administrator accounts alone cannot deliver, but the subtitle bounds it adequately.

The abstract is ~133 words in a single unstructured paragraph. It is under-length for the venue and out of step with the body in three ways: it reports fourteen interviews where §3.2 reports twelve; it states the tripartite negotiation claim without the qualification that only one party was sampled; and it labels the study mixed-methods on the strength of a component whose instrument is nowhere described. It also promises "implications for how universities design QA processes," which the body delivers only at the level of Weakness 14's abstractions. Every claim in an abstract should be locatable in the body at the same strength; three here are not.

#### Conclusion

§6 is the weakest section in the manuscript and the one I would ask the authors to rewrite last, after the reanalysis, rather than first. In six sentences it makes the unsupportable novelty claim, restates the tripartite claim the design cannot reach, and issues a prospective effectiveness claim ("more likely to foster the internalized commitment") for which no outcome data exists. It does not answer the research question §1 poses — "how do their accounts illuminate the formation of institutional quality culture?" — so much as assert a conclusion about quality culture's nature that the accounts were used to illustrate rather than to establish.

The honest conclusion available from this data is narrower and, in my judgement, more interesting: administrators experience QA as ritual, work actively to recover meaning from it, and locate their professional identity in the unresolved gap — which suggests that quality culture, at least as administrators construct it, is sustained by the very tension that QA critiques treat as its failure mode. That is a claim this study can support, and it is not one I have read before.

### Questions for Authors

1. How many administrators were interviewed — twelve or fourteen? If the figures differ because of withdrawals or excluded transcripts, please report what happened and when, and state the per-institution distribution.
2. How many participants held the skeptical or dissenting views described in §4's closing paragraph, and what does the theme structure look like when their accounts are analysed rather than set aside? If the three themes do not survive their reinstatement, that is a finding, not a problem.
3. §2 reads Delacroix (2018) as arguing against consultation-as-evidence; §5 reads him as recommending consultation as the central mechanism. Which reading do you intend, and if it is §2's, what does your third finding become once Delacroix is treated as a counterargument your data may in fact support?
4. Given Pettersen (2022), Rahman (2020), and Silva and Tan (2021), what precisely does this study add? A two-sentence answer would substantially improve §6 — and §2 ¶4 already contains most of it.
5. On what basis can three institutions in one national system be read as "the higher education sector as a whole"? If the intended claim is transferability rather than generalization, please argue it: what about these settings makes the pattern portable, and to what kinds of system?
6. What instrument produced M=3.9 (SD=0.6)? Please give the scale range, item set, source or adaptation history, and reliability evidence, and account for the disposition of the 28 survey respondents not included in the §4.4 comparison.
7. Which national system was studied? If anonymization prevents naming it, please characterize the regime — regulatory intensity, review cycle length, sanctions available — so readers can assess where the findings travel.
8. How will you reconcile §3.5's anonymization assurance with §4.1's role-plus-institution-type attributions? Please confirm that participants consented to attribution at this level of specificity, or replace the descriptors with neutral identifiers.
9. Were the interviews structured or semi-structured, and on what basis do you conclude the cross-institutional pattern was "not an artifact of any single institution's local circumstances"?
10. Was the "more skeptical picture" mentioned in §4.4 an expectation formed before the survey results were seen, or after?

### Minor Issues

- The abstract is unstructured and short for the venue; this journal's readers are served by a structured abstract with explicit purpose, design, findings, and implications headings.
- §3.4's "subgroup comparisons were examined where role categories permitted" is not matched by §4.4, which reports an institution-type comparison rather than a role comparison.

## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** This dimension is my primary charge as Peer Reviewer 1. It asks whether the study's design, its data handling, its reporting of whatever analysis it performs, and the affordances it offers for replication clear the bar that a quality-assurance / higher-education governance journal would apply. Read through the lens of methodological rigor, this means the paper must first declare what kind of study it is — an interview study of administrators, a survey, a multi-case comparison, a document analysis, a conceptual argument — and then behave consistently with the standards of that paradigm. If administrator perspectives are the evidence base, I want the sampling logic (which institutions, which roles, why those and not others), the instrument (interview protocol, questionnaire), the procedure (how many, how long, recorded, transcribed), the analysis path (coding scheme, who coded, how disagreements were settled), and the trustworthiness apparatus appropriate to that paradigm — reflexivity about the researcher's own position inside the QA system, member checking, saturation reasoning, audit trail. If any quantitative claim appears, the Step 4a checklist applies to that portion: effect sizes, intervals, assumption checks, missing-data handling. Reproducibility here means a reader in another national QA system could re-run the procedure, not that they could re-run a script; instrument appendices, coding frames, and an ethics/consent record are the operative artifacts. A 2,249-word manuscript makes method compression a live risk, so I will assess whether brevity has removed information a replicator actually needs, distinguishing genuine absence from venue-imposed concision.

**D2 — domain_accuracy.** I read this dimension in its methodological register rather than as a literature-coverage audit, which belongs to Reviewer 2. What concerns me is whether the study's operational choices are faithful to how the quality-culture and internal-QA field actually defines its objects. "Quality culture" is a contested construct with a documented lineage (the EUA quality-culture work, the ESG framing of internal QA, the compliance-versus-improvement tension); a paper that measures or codes it must show that what it captured is what the field means by the term, not a private redefinition silently substituted. The same applies to "implementation," "administrator," and "institutional" — each carries a unit-of-analysis commitment. Methodologically, domain accuracy fails when construct definition and operationalization diverge: the paper names a field construct, then instruments something narrower or different, then reports back in the field's vocabulary. I will also check whether the national/system context of the sampled institutions is stated accurately enough that its QA regime constrains the interpretation appropriately, since QA governance arrangements are not portable across systems.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** This dimension asks whether the chain from data to conclusion holds. My methodological reading of it is the inference-warrant question: does each claim in the discussion trace back to evidence the design was actually capable of producing? The canonical failure in administrator-perspective research is level-jumping — collecting accounts from a set of administrators and concluding about institutional culture, about faculty experience, or about causal effects of QA implementation on quality outcomes. That is the ecological fallacy and the reverse-causation fallacy operating together, and a cross-sectional perceptual design cannot license either move. I will also watch for circularity in the construct (quality culture defined by the presence of practices, then those practices offered as evidence of quality culture), for confirmation-shaped reporting where only accounts consistent with the framework are shown, and for the hedge-drop pattern where a limitations paragraph concedes non-generalizability while the abstract and conclusion assert general prescriptions. Coherence for me is a data–claim relation, not a stylistic property of the prose.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** Marked high rather than mandatory, and primarily Reviewer 3's territory; my contribution is bounded to the methodological substrate of the claim. When a paper reaches toward adjacent fields — organizational culture, public management, implementation science, institutional theory — it inherits those fields' evidentiary standards for the claims it borrows. So I check whether the design supports the reach: is a construct imported from organizational-culture research measured in a way that field would recognize, or invoked as a label? Are transfer claims to other institutional types or other national systems backed by sampling breadth or design features, or asserted? I also check whether methods are described in vocabulary an adjacent-field reader could evaluate, since an unspecified analytic procedure is opaque within the field and doubly so outside it. I will not score this dimension for topical interest or citation breadth.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** Normal priority; the contract's failure conditions never elevate it to a decision driver on its own. My reading is confined to whether the manuscript's organization and exposition let a reviewer verify method at all. Concretely: does a locatable methods section exist, is procedural information contiguous rather than scattered across introduction and discussion, are participant characteristics and sample composition tabulated or at least stated, are any figures/tables self-describing with the Ns and units they report, and does the abstract's description of what was done match the body's. This is a legibility judgment about the method's presentation, not a prose-quality judgment; poor writing that still permits full methodological verification does not draw a penalty from me. Given 2,249 words, structural economy is expected and is not itself a defect.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor

**what_to_look_for**: An explicit named design and paradigm declaration. Sampling frame — how institutions and administrators were selected, inclusion criteria, role/seniority composition, number of institutions and participants, institutional-type spread, and the rationale tying sampling logic to the research question (purposive/theoretical/convenience, named as such). Data collection specifics — instrument type, whether the protocol is reproduced or appended, mode and duration, recording/transcription, language of interview versus language of reporting and how translation was handled. Analysis procedure — the named analytic approach (thematic analysis, grounded theory, framework analysis, content analysis) with its coding stages, who coded, intercoder arrangements, and how themes were derived rather than assumed. Trustworthiness apparatus appropriate to the paradigm — reflexivity about the author's own position relative to the QA system studied, member checking, negative-case analysis, saturation reasoning, audit trail. If any numbers are reported: denominators, effect sizes with interpretation, intervals, assumption checks, missing-data accounting, and APA-conformant statistical formatting (Step 4a). Reproducibility affordances — instrument or coding frame availability, data-availability statement, and an ethics/consent record naming the approving body. I will also read the paper's own limitations section as a signal of whether the author perceives the design's actual boundaries.

**what_triggers_block**: The manuscript reports empirical findings about administrator perspectives while omitting the information required to establish that any systematic procedure produced them — that is, the paper presents themes, quotes, or frequencies but no locatable statement of who was studied (number and composition of participants and institutions), how they were selected, and how their accounts were analyzed. Any one of those three absent at the level of a bare fact, not merely of detail, blocks: without them the findings are unverifiable in principle and no revision short of new reporting or new work repairs them. Also blocking: a design–question mismatch severe enough that the stated method cannot bear the study's central claim (e.g., causal or effectiveness claims about QA implementation drawn from a single-timepoint perceptual dataset with no comparison basis), an analysis whose stated procedure contradicts the data type it is applied to, or fabrication-class inconsistency where reported Ns, denominators, or participant counts conflict irreconcilably across sections. For any quantitative component: significance-based conclusions with no effect sizes, no denominators, and no assumption testing whatsoever, or red-flag evidence of uncorrected multiple testing driving the headline claim.

**what_triggers_warn**: The design, sample, and analysis are all identifiable and mutually consistent, but a rigor component is thin, under-specified, or unjustified in a way that a revision could remedy. Instances I will treat as warn: sampling stated but its rationale absent or purely convenience-driven without acknowledging the selection bias it introduces; participant composition given only in aggregate so role or institutional-type variation cannot be assessed; analytic approach named but its coding procedure left as a single sentence, with no indication of how themes were built or checked; single-coder analysis with no reflexivity statement and no member checking in a study where the author is plausibly embedded in the QA system studied; saturation asserted rather than evidenced; interview protocol neither reproduced nor described item-wise; ethics approval or informed consent unmentioned; no data-availability statement and no coding frame; limitations section that omits the design's principal threat (self-report from role-interested informants, single-system boundedness); or, for any quantitative element, effect sizes or intervals missing on secondary results, APA-format deviations, or missing-data handling unstated where non-response is plausible. Several such gaps together still score warn rather than block so long as the study's basic verifiability survives; I will name each rather than collapse them.

### D2: domain_accuracy

**what_to_look_for**: A stated definition of "quality culture" with its provenance, and evidence that the study's instrument and coding actually operationalize that definition rather than a substitute. Consistency of the QA vocabulary the paper deploys — internal quality assurance, external QA, quality culture, quality management, accreditation, compliance versus enhancement — used with the distinctions the field maintains rather than interchangeably. A specified unit of analysis (individual administrator, unit, institution, system) held constant between what was sampled, what was coded, and what is claimed. Accurate characterization of the national or regional QA regime governing the sampled institutions, including whether external accreditation is mandatory, cyclical, or voluntary there, since that materially conditions what "implementation" means. Correct attribution when named frameworks, standards, or models are invoked as the analytic scaffold.

**what_triggers_block**: A construct-validity failure at the core of the study — the paper claims to study quality culture (or the specific construct its title advances) while its instrument and coding demonstrably capture a different object, such as procedural compliance activity or administrator satisfaction, with the substitution unacknowledged and the findings then reported in the original construct's terms. Also blocking: a material misstatement about the QA regime or standards framework that the study's interpretation depends on, such that the interpretive frame collapses if corrected; or a named framework/model used as the analytic backbone but described with its content misattributed, so that every coded result inherits the error.

**what_triggers_warn**: The construct is broadly correctly handled but its treatment is loose in ways a revision fixes. Instances: "quality culture" used without an explicit definition or provenance, leaving the reader to infer the operational meaning; key QA terms used interchangeably where the field distinguishes them; unit of analysis stated once but drifting between sections; the institutional and national context under-specified so a reader cannot judge what the QA regime being implemented actually requires; a framework invoked to structure the coding but not described precisely enough to check the mapping; or claim-scope wording that treats one system's QA arrangements as the general case without saying so.

### D3: argumentative_coherence

**what_to_look_for**: A traceable chain for each substantive claim: which data element supports it, and whether the design could have produced that support. The scope of the discussion's assertions compared against the scope the sample and design license. Consistency of claim strength across abstract, findings, discussion, and conclusion — particularly whether hedges present in the body survive into the abstract and recommendations. Presence of disconfirming or divergent accounts, and whether variation among administrators is reported or smoothed into consensus. Handling of the framework's circularity risk: whether quality culture is defined independently of the practices offered as evidence for it. Explicit treatment of causal direction whenever the paper links QA implementation to institutional outcomes.

**what_triggers_block**: The paper's central conclusion is not supported by the design that produced it, and the gap is structural rather than a matter of phrasing. Concretely: causal or effectiveness claims about QA implementation asserted as findings from cross-sectional self-report with no comparison, temporal ordering, or counterfactual basis; conclusions about institutional-level culture or about non-sampled populations (faculty, students, other institutional types) presented as findings when only administrators at the sampled institutions were studied; or a defining circularity in which the construct is operationalized by the very practices then adduced to demonstrate it, so the argument cannot fail. Also blocking: an internal contradiction between findings and conclusion that changes what the paper recommends.

**what_triggers_warn**: The core argument holds but individual inferential steps overreach or are under-defended. Instances: recommendations pitched more prescriptively than the evidence base supports; occasional causal verbs ("led to", "improved", "drives") where the design supports only association or perception; abstract or conclusion stated at a confidence the body's hedged findings do not carry; consensus reported without indication of whether dissenting accounts existed or were sought; transfer of findings to other institutional types asserted without a stated basis; or a limitations section that names the boundary but is not honored in the surrounding claims. Each instance named separately with its locator.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

**what_to_look_for**: Whether claims that reach into adjacent fields — organizational culture, implementation science, public management, institutional theory — are backed by design features that those fields would accept for the claim being made, or are asserted by label. Whether imported constructs are operationalized in a form the originating field would recognize. Whether the methods narrative is specified in vocabulary that lets an adjacent-field reader evaluate the evidence without reconstructing higher-education QA conventions from scratch. Whether stated implications for other sectors or systems rest on sampling breadth, design, or explicit argument rather than on assumed portability.

**what_triggers_block**: An interdisciplinary claim is load-bearing for the paper's contribution and the design cannot support it at all — for example, a claim to advance organizational-culture theory generally, or to establish an implementation-science mechanism, resting on a single-context administrator perception dataset with no theoretical sampling, no comparison, and no acknowledgment of the gap. Blocking here requires that removing the unsupported interdisciplinary claim would remove the paper's stated contribution; a decorative cross-field gesture does not block.

**what_triggers_warn**: Cross-field constructs invoked as vocabulary without operational uptake in the instrument or coding; implications for adjacent fields or other sectors asserted in the discussion without a stated basis; methods described in QA-internal shorthand that an adjacent-field reader could not evaluate; or context (system, regulatory regime, institutional type) left implicit such that an outside reader cannot judge transferability. ### Strengths

The maximum-variation logic is legible even though it is never named as such, and it is the right sampling architecture for a question about how QA requirements are locally reconciled. — `text: §3.2, "three universities in a single national system: one large public research university, one mid-sized public teaching-focused university, and one private university"`
3. **Basic interview procedure reported**: mode (in person or video call), duration range (45–70 minutes), audio recording, and transcription are all stated. The duration range in particular is a detail many submissions omit, and it establishes that the interviews were substantial enough to support thematic work. — `text: §3.3, "lasting between 45 and 70 minutes, and were audio-recorded and transcribed"`
4. **Ethics approval and informed consent on the record**: §3.5 names institutional ethical approval and informed consent. The anonymization sentence in the same paragraph is contradicted by §4.1's attributions (a matter within Reviewer 3's charge), but the approval-and-consent record itself is a reproducibility affordance under Step 6 and is present. — `text: §3.5, "The study received institutional ethical approval. ### Detailed Comments

#### Research Questions & Hypotheses

The research question in §1 is clear, answerable, and correctly paired to an interpretive design: "How do senior university administrators experience and enact QA implementation, and how do their accounts illuminate the formation of institutional quality culture?" No hypotheses are stated, which is appropriate for the qualitative strand. The survey strand, however, has no stated question at all — §3.1 assigns it the role of contextualizing, and §4.4 then uses it to advance a moderation claim about institutional type that no stated question anticipated. The second clause of the research question ("how their accounts illuminate the formation of institutional quality culture") also does more work than the design supports: illuminating formation implies process and temporal development, which a single round of retrospective accounts cannot observe. Narrowing the second clause to how administrators *account for* quality culture would bring question and design into alignment at no cost to the contribution.

#### Research Design

A qualitative-dominant mixed-methods design is defensible here, and the priority declaration in §3.1 is a real asset. What is missing is the design's name and its architecture. Whether this is convergent (both strands answering the same question, integrated at interpretation), explanatory sequential (survey following interviews to test scope), or embedded (survey nested inside a qualitative case design) determines what §4.4 is permitted to claim, and the manuscript never says. On the qualitative side, the design sits between multi-site case study and interview-based qualitative description, and the choice matters: a case study would require site-level contextual data the paper does not report, while qualitative description would forbid the sector-level inference of §5. The internal/external validity trade-off is never discussed; the paper takes the internal-validity-favouring design and then claims the external-validity payoff.

#### Sampling Strategy

The site-selection logic is sound in structure and unnamed in the text; it should be labelled (maximum variation across mission and sector) and its rationale connected to the research question. Participant selection is the weak point: recruitment via the QA offices whose work is under study introduces a directional filter (W11) that is never acknowledged, the total is contradicted between Abstract and §3.2 (W1), the per-site allocation is absent (W9), and the analytic sample is further reduced by an undisclosed number of exclusions (W2). Twelve administrators across three sites is a defensible n for this design — my concern is not size but accounting. For the survey, no sampling frame, invitation denominator, or response rate is reported, and 28 of 48 respondents vanish from the only inferential analysis without comment.

#### Data Collection

Mode, duration, recording, and transcription are reported (a strength). Beyond that, the protocol is neither reproduced nor described item-wise, so the reader cannot judge whether the three themes are properties of the accounts or of the questions asked. §4.1's "structured protocol" contradicts §3.1 and §3.3 (W8). Language of interview versus language of reporting is not addressed, which matters because the national system is unnamed and may not be anglophone: if translation occurred, the procedure and its effect on the quoted material need reporting. The survey's administration is stated ("administered online") but its content is not (W3).

#### Analysis Methods

For the qualitative strand: unreconstructable (W5). Naming the tradition is not a formality — the confidence claim in §4.1, the "stable structure" claim in §3.4, and the frequency language in §4.1–§4.3 each require a different justification depending on which tradition is in force, and reflexive thematic analysis would specifically disallow the negative-case exclusion in §4's closing paragraph.

For the quantitative strand, against the Step 4a checklist: effect sizes — absent; confidence intervals — absent; statistical power — no a priori analysis, and with n=9 versus n=11 a post-hoc sensitivity statement is the minimum; assumption testing — absent, and untestable at these cell sizes without recourse to a non-parametric test that is neither used nor discussed; missing-data handling — unstated, with 28 of 48 respondents unaccounted for; APA format — the test is unnamed and unreported (no statistic, no df), and "*p*<.05" should be an exact value with italicized *p*, as should *M* and *SD*; red flags — the mismatch between §3.4's planned role comparisons and §4.4's reported institution-type comparison, with an unknown number of comparisons run and one significant result reported, is a selective-reporting pattern, and §4.4's post-hoc "we had anticipated" is HARKing-adjacent.

**Statistical reporting completeness: Unacceptable.** The reported quantitative content does not permit evaluation, replication, or even identification of the analysis performed.

#### Results Presentation

Non-significant results are not reported; the number of comparisons examined is not reported; and the one category of qualitative material the paper explicitly declines to report is the disconfirming category (W2). There are no tables or figures at all — the survey results appear in running prose with no scale range, no units, and no per-group means or dispersions for the two compared subgroups, so a reader cannot recompute or sanity-check anything. Participant composition likewise appears only in prose. Quoted material is presented well and is analytically apt; the attribution style raises a confidentiality problem that falls to Reviewer 3.

#### Reproducibility

Weak. Positively: ethics approval and consent are on the record (§3.5). Negatively: no protocol, no codebook, no instrument, no data-availability statement, no appendix (W13), and no named analytic tradition (W5). The national system is unnamed, so even the regulatory conditions under which the procedure would be re-run are unknown. A reader in another QA system currently has no basis for replication. As an editorial verification item rather than a review finding: every DOI in the reference list uses the 10.5555 prefix, which is a reserved non-production range. I flag this without presuming a cause and build nothing on it; source verification sits with the editor and Reviewer 2.

#### Methodological Fallacies Detected

- **Confirmation bias / selective reporting** — self-disclosed at §4's closing paragraph: cases inconsistent with the theme structure were excluded for not fitting it, making the structure unfalsifiable.
- **Ecological fallacy** — individual administrator accounts and individual staff survey responses are aggregated into claims about institutional culture (§5) and then sector culture (§5, second finding), with no institution-level or system-level data at any point.
- **Selection bias, undiscussed** — recruitment through the QA offices under study, compounding in the same direction as the disclosed exclusion (W11).
- **HARKing-adjacent post-hoc expectation** — §4.4's "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture," stated after the result and absent from §3.
- **Uncorrected / unreported multiple comparisons** — §3.4 plans comparisons "where role categories permitted"; §4.4 reports one significant comparison on a different variable, with the comparison set unreported.
- **Reverse causation / process inference from cross-sectional data** — the negotiation thesis (§6) and the "formation" language (§1) assert dynamics over time from a single round of retrospective self-report.
- **Confirmation of the framework by the framework** — the three themes are used both as the analytic structure and as the evidence that the structure is correct (§4.1's "emerged systematically... giving us confidence"), a circularity that the negative-case exclusion completes.

2. How many participants came from each of the three institutions, and what was the role composition at each site?
3. How many participants held the "skeptical or dissenting" views described in §4's closing paragraph, and what does the theme structure look like when they are reinstated? Does a fourth theme appear, or does theme 1 change shape?

The design this manuscript chose is the right one for the question it asks, and the question is worth asking. A qualitative-dominant multi-site interview study of the administrators who sit between external requirement and internal practice is well matched to a puzzle about how QA requirements get locally reconciled, the three sites are structurally well chosen, and §3.1's declaration that the qualitative strand is primary is a discipline many mixed-methods submissions skip. My assessment concerns execution and reporting, and it is severe for a reason that survives the most generous available reading of the paper.

That generous reading — that the real defects are rhetorical over-reach, and that deleting the "first comprehensive account," the "sector as a whole," and the "balanced representation" claims would leave a legitimate modest study — is largely right about §5 and §6 and largely wrong about §3 and §4. Three defects are not rhetoric and do not dissolve when the rhetoric is toned down. The analytic sample is not established: the Abstract says fourteen, §3.2 says twelve, and §4's closing paragraph removes an unstated further number, so no denominator exists for any of the manuscript's frequency claims — including "recurred across every interview." The instrument that produced the survey's headline number is entirely undocumented, so *M*=3.9 sits on an unknown scale with unknown items, unknown reliability, and untested dimensionality, and cannot be interpreted in either direction. And the qualitative analytic procedure is four clauses long, naming no tradition, no coder arrangement, no member checking, no audit trail, no positionality, and no saturation argument, so how twelve or fourteen transcripts became exactly three themes is not reconstructable by any reader.

The exclusion disclosed in §4's closing paragraph deserves separate emphasis, and I raise it as a defect in the analysis as written rather than as an allegation about intent — the authors state it plainly, which is to their credit and is also what makes it assessable. Removing participants from the analysis on the ground that their views "fell outside the three-theme structure that organized our analysis" reverses the accountability relation between structure and cases. In thematic work the structure answers to the cases, and negative cases are the main instrument by which a theme set is tested rather than confirmed; excluding them because they do not fit means the structure cannot fail. The claim in the immediately preceding sentence to have captured "the full range of administrator views" cannot stand alongside it. This is compounded upstream by a filter the paper does not acknowledge: participants were recruited through the QA offices whose work the study evaluates, so the least QA-invested administrators were the least likely to reach the sample in the first place. Two filters operating in the same direction, toward accounts by people invested in QA, is exactly the direction of the identity-work and distributed-leadership findings.

On the quantitative side, the reporting is not evaluable against APA 7.0 on any of the seven Step 4a checks that apply. §4.4 advances a moderation claim about institutional type from "a statistically significant difference (*p*<.05)" between n=9 and n=11, with no test name, statistic, degrees of freedom, exact *p*, effect size, interval, or assumption check — at cell sizes where the assumptions of a parametric test cannot be evaluated from the data and a non-parametric alternative would ordinarily be obligatory. Twenty of forty-eight respondents appear in the comparison and the other twenty-eight are never accounted for; no invitation denominator or response rate is given, so non-response bias is unassessable. Most consequentially for how the result should be read: §3.4 states that subgroup comparisons were examined "where role categories permitted," while §4.4 reports a comparison by institutional type. The number of comparisons actually run is therefore unknown, and one significant result surfacing from an unspecified exploratory set is the standard condition for selective reporting. I record the statistical reporting completeness as Unacceptable — not as a judgement of the underlying analysis, which I cannot see, but because the manuscript does not report enough to identify what analysis was performed.

Integration is where a qualitative-dominant mixed-methods design earns its label, and this study does not yet do the work. No typology is named, no integration point or timing is specified, and no joint display or narrative weaving connects the strands. §4.4 then states that the survey "corroborated the qualitative picture" and, three sentences later, that "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture" — two incompatible characterizations of one result, of which the second appears to be what the authors mean. The anticipation is stated only after the result and appears nowhere in §3, which makes it a post-hoc expectation presented as a prior one. This matters beyond bookkeeping: a genuine discordance between administrator accounts and staff sentiment is the most interesting thing the mixed design produced, and labelling it corroboration forfeits it. Analysed properly as discordance across organizational levels, it would strengthen the paper.

The claims then travel well past what any of this supports. §5's second finding states that the findings "demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment" and reads the study as "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture," which moves from individual accounts to sector-level culture in one step and asserts it as demonstration. The Abstract and §6 locate quality culture in a negotiation "among administrators, academics, and external bodies" — the paper's stated contribution, resting on testimony from one of those three parties, with no academic interviews, no agency or panel data, and no documentary or observational material from actual review processes. §5's practical recommendation is separately unwarranted on the manuscript's own record: it aligns the findings with Delacroix (2018) as recommending stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for quality culture, where §2 and the reference-list annotation both report Delacroix as arguing against exactly that. Whatever the external source says — a matter for the editor and the domain reviewer — the manuscript contradicts itself here, and the recommendation currently has no warrant inside its own text. Read as §2 reads it, Delacroix becomes a counterargument the Discussion must answer, and §4.2's material on persuasion and coalition-building is well placed to answer it. Finally, there is no limitations section: the one place where these scope claims could have been disciplined does not exist.

What I would ask for is not rewriting. Settle the participant count and report the analytic N with its per-site allocation. Reinstate the excluded dissenting cases and reanalyse; report what the theme structure looks like with them in, and expect it to change. Name the analytic tradition, the coder arrangement, and whatever checking was done, and add a positionality paragraph given the recruitment route. Document the survey instrument or withdraw the survey — either is publishable; an undocumented instrument reported as a number is not. Report the full comparison set with proper statistics, or designate the institution-type contrast as exploratory and drop the moderator language. Name the mixed-methods design and its integration point, and present the discordance as a finding. Add a limitations section, then bring §5 and §6 into line with it. That is reanalysis and re-reporting of data the authors already hold, and the study that comes back from it — smaller, bounded to three institutions in a characterized system, honest about a single-party evidence base — would be a real contribution to this literature.

### Detailed Comments

#### Assumption Audit

**Explicit assumptions.** Three are stated and I accept two of them. That quality culture is a coherent object worth studying at the level of everyday practice (§1) is reasonable. That administrators occupy a structurally distinctive hinge position (§2) is the paper's best move and I would defend it. The third — §3.1's declaration that "the qualitative component was primary; the survey served to contextualize" — is stated but not honoured, since §4.4 then asks the survey to corroborate the interviews and §5 asks it to complicate them. Whether that is a design problem is Reviewer 1's call; what I note is that the paper's own statement of what the survey is for does not match what it uses the survey for.

**Implicit assumptions.** Four premises do load-bearing work without ever being stated.

*That more quality culture is self-evidently good.* The paper never entertains a cost or a downside. From accountability-regulation research the obvious complication is that an institution with a very strong internal quality culture and weak external verification is precisely the configuration in which self-referential standards drift — internalisation and accountability are not the same good, and can trade against each other. A paper recommending more of the first to a regulated sector should say something about the second.

*That administrator report is valid evidence about institutional reality.* This is the load-bearing one and I score it at D3. What makes it worse here is that the paper's own data contradicts it and the paper does not notice: §4.4 and §5 report that mid-level staff sentiment did not match administrator accounts, and this is filed as "a note of complication... worth pursuing in future work" rather than treated as evidence that vantage point determines finding. The paper had the disconfirmation in hand.

*That ritual is pathology.* §4.1's finest observation is that QA "arrived on a calendar, consumed the institution's attention for a defined window, and then receded." The paper reads this as decay. It is at least as plausibly a rational allocation of scarce attention under episodic review, and the functional reading is standard in regulatory research: a predictable, calendarised documentation cycle is how an organisation makes itself legible to an external body, and legibility is the precondition of the autonomy the institution enjoys the rest of the time. I am not asserting the functional reading is correct. I am noting the paper never considers it, and that a study designed to test between the two readings would be a considerably more interesting paper.

*That culture is buildable by design.* §6 claims processes "designed with this negotiated character in mind... are more likely to foster the internalized commitment." That is a design claim about an emergent property, and it sits awkwardly against the paper's own central finding: if quality culture is a negotiation, its outcome is by definition not designable by one party. The paper cites Ferreira & Lund and Nakamura for the cultivation position in §2 but never defends it against its own result.

**Paradigmatic assumptions.** The manuscript is interpretivist about administrators and quietly positivist about staff. Administrators' accounts are treated as meaning-making to be interpreted; staff perception is treated as a measurable institutional state ("respondents' overall institutional quality culture score"). The two paradigms sit side by side with no bridge, and §4.4's "The survey corroborated the qualitative picture" papers over the join. Reviewer 1 owns the integration procedure; my point is prior to procedure — the paper has not decided what kind of thing quality culture is, and the abstract shows the strain, reporting a "moderately positive institutional quality culture (M=3.9, SD=0.6)" two sentences before arguing that quality culture is "an ongoing negotiation." A negotiation is not the kind of object a Likert mean can be moderately positive about.

#### Cross-Disciplinary Connections

**Parallel research.** Regulatory governance has been asking this paper's exact question for forty years, in a developed vocabulary the manuscript does not use: when does a regulated entity internalise a standard versus perform compliance? The responsive-regulation tradition (Ayres & Braithwaite) and the regulatory-unreasonableness literature (Bardach & Kagan) supply the concepts — creative compliance, gaming, ritualistic compliance, the compliance trap — that the paper reinvents as "ritual." Power's *The Audit Society* is the direct ancestor of the "audit university" framing the paper borrows through Harlow (2019). Meyer and Rowan's account of ceremonial conformity and decoupling is where §5's "decouple from its ostensible purpose" comes from. And Lipsky's street-level bureaucracy is almost exactly §4.2 and §4.3: front-line actors reconciling policy with local conditions, building coping resources and a vocational identity in the gap. The paper's three themes are, from a public-administration seat, three well-described instances of phenomena that already have names.

I want to be careful about what this criticism is and is not. Reviewer 2 owns literature-coverage completeness and I am not conducting one. My point is narrower and is a cross-disciplinary one: the manuscript uses institutional-theory and regulatory-studies vocabulary — ritual, decoupling, steering at a distance, accountability regimes, distributed leadership, identity work — as labels applied after the fact, and engages none of the source literatures' actual claims. Remove the labels and the argument is unchanged, which is the test for ornament. §5's "confirms long-standing concerns" is the sharpest instance: the paper claims to confirm a body of theory it never identifies, in a reference base with nothing published before 2018.

**Borrowing opportunities.** Three would materially strengthen the paper without new data. The regulatory-conversation frame gives the "negotiation" claim a theorised foundation rather than an asserted one, and it comes with an account of when negotiation produces internalisation and when it produces capture. Reactivity — Espeland and Sauder's demonstration that public measures reorganise the worlds they measure — explains §4.1's "season" pattern as a measurement effect rather than a moral failing. Street-level bureaucracy handles §4.2 and §4.3 directly, and would let the authors say what is distinctive about *these* street-level actors rather than describing the general case as though it were new.

**Methodological borrowing.** Regulatory research routinely triangulates in ways available here cheaply. Trace one quality claim through a full cycle in the documents the institution actually produced — self-evaluation report, panel report, action plan, subsequent evidence — which converts "theater" from an assertion into a demonstrable decoupling or refutes it. Interview panel chairs and agency officers about the same review cycles the administrators describe; this is a handful of interviews and it directly tests the paper's central characterisation. Observe one review event. Any one of these moves the paper from single-vantage to triangulated, and the first requires no new fieldwork at all.

#### Practical Impact

**Real-world application.** As written, an agency official finishes this paper with a mood rather than a lever. The most usable content is §4.2's finding that the reviewed institution's real cost is internal persuasion, not documentation — which has an immediate methodology implication, because review timetables and burden estimates are built on the documentation assumption. The paper does not draw it. The second most usable is §4.1's season pattern, which bears directly on the episodic-versus-continuous review question agencies are currently arguing about. The paper does not draw that either.

**Implementation feasibility.** "Less documentation, more meaning-making" is not a design available to a regulated institution acting alone. The barriers are concrete: statutory or contractual evidence requirements; a panel's need for a comparable evidence base across institutions; and risk transfer, since a panel that accepts narrative in place of evidence leaves the agency holding any adverse outcome that surfaces later. The unintended consequences the paper does not consider are real ones. Reducing documentation without changing the accountability structure pushes verification into the site visit, which is more expensive and considerably more intrusive for staff, and it advantages institutions with better-presenting senior people. And "maximize genuine participation," recommended without a resource account, loads cost onto exactly the academics the paper's own §2 describes, via Montero (2021), as already experiencing review as burden.

**Stakeholders.** Academics appear as characters in administrators' accounts, never as informants. Students appear once, rhetorically. The agency, its officers and its panel members are characterised throughout — as the audience for theatre — and are never asked. Professional-services staff below the "mid-level" band do most of the actual portfolio assembly and are absent from both the interviews and the survey. And the three sites are all reasonably well-resourced institutions; small specialist providers, colleges, and for-profits, where the resource assumptions behind every recommendation break down, are not represented and are not mentioned as a boundary. On power asymmetry: §4.2's "You can't do quality *to* a department" is reported at face value, said by someone who holds the reporting line to the panel and can in fact escalate. The paper takes the speaker's self-description of powerlessness as a description of the power relation.

#### Broader Implications

**Ethical dimensions.** W1 is the operative finding and I will not restate it. Two further points. First, institutional confidentiality is not addressed at all: even if individuals were protected, this manuscript publishes an unflattering characterisation of three institutions identified by type within one system, and §3.5 says nothing about what was promised to them. Second, consent scope. If participants agreed to be quoted on the understanding recorded in §3.5, the §4.1 attributions may fall outside what they agreed to, and the remedy is not only editorial — the authors may owe those two participants a conversation before this appears.

**Social impact.** If this paper's conclusion travels, the most readily available policy reading of it is deregulatory: QA is theatre, therefore less of it. That argument is live in several systems, and this study cannot adjudicate it, because it never sampled anyone positioned to defend the regime. Authors who do not intend that use should say so; authors who do intend it need a much stronger evidence base than twelve administrators. There is also an equity dimension the paper misses entirely: a review regime that rewards narrative sophistication and meaning-making over documentary evidence systematically advantages institutions with the senior capacity to produce narrative, which is not the under-resourced ones.

**Future directions.** In descending order of value from where I sit: interview panel chairs and agency officers about the same cycles these administrators describe, which is the cheapest direct test of the paper's central claim; trace claims through the documentary record of one full cycle; compare across regime types, which §6 gestures at and which is the finding agencies would most use; and exploit the natural experiment that exists wherever an agency has moved from episodic to continuous or risk-based review, to test whether the season pattern is a property of QA or of cycle design.

## Review Body

From the cross-disciplinary and practitioner seat, this manuscript has one clear virtue and one structural problem that generates most of its defects.

The virtue is legibility and vantage. It is written so that someone outside higher-education QA scholarship can follow it, which is rarer in this literature than it should be, and it looks at the party in the QA system that external reviewers know least well. §4.2's finding — that persuasion, not paperwork, is where the institutional cost actually lands — is a datum I would take into a review-methodology discussion. §4.3's refusal to resolve the compliance-versus-meaning tension in either direction is more accurate than most practitioner-facing writing on the subject.

The structural problem is that a study of one vantage point is written as an account of a system. Twelve administrators were interviewed. The abstract and §6 conclude that quality culture is "an ongoing negotiation among administrators, academics, and external bodies" — two of those three parties were never approached. No panel chair, agency officer, or external reviewer was interviewed; no academic was interviewed as such; no self-evaluation document, panel report, or action plan was examined; no review event was observed. The paper's most quotable line, "It's theater, and everyone knows it's theater," is a characterisation of a panel's conduct sourced entirely to the people preparing for that panel. From the panel side, that characterisation is a live empirical question, not a settled one.

What makes this a block rather than a limitation is that nothing in the manuscript holds it. There is no limitations section. §6's future-research sentence addresses breadth of sites, not breadth of informants. And §4's closing paragraph asserts the reverse — "balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views" — one sentence before reporting that dissenting participants were removed from the analysis. Most tellingly, the paper's own survey produced a divergent picture from a different informant group and §5 files that as a curiosity for future work rather than as evidence about the fragility of its own inference. The paper had the disconfirmation and did not use it.

Two consequences follow that matter more to a practitioner than to an academic reader. First, the recommendations cannot be acted on. "Processes that maximize genuine participation" and processes that "create room for meaning-making rather than merely demanding documentation" name no mechanism, no cost, and no trade-off against the evidentiary function that external review exists to perform. Documentation is the medium through which an external body verifies anything; a regulated institution cannot produce less of it unilaterally, because the lever sits at the agency end. The paper addresses institutions and does not say this. The three actionable questions its own data raises — which parts of an evidence base are decision-relevant versus ceremonial, whether evidence can be drawn from systems institutions already run, and whether the "season" pattern is an artifact of episodic review — go undeveloped. Second, the unnamed national system makes the finding unusable and untransferable. Ritual compliance means different things, and has different remedies, under high-stakes accreditation than under enhancement-led audit, and no reader can tell which regime this is.

Two further points belong to my seat specifically. The confidentiality assurance in §3.5 is not honoured by §4.1: role-plus-institution-type attributions in a three-site, one-system sample identify approximately one person each, and the attributed content is career-consequential. That is correctable — neutral identifiers, stripped descriptors, a truthful ethics statement, and a conversation with the two participants about consent scope — but it is correctable *before* publication, not after. And the paper reports how QA feels to those who run it while at points presenting this as evidence about whether QA works: §5's "confirms long-standing concerns that QA can decouple from its ostensible purpose" is warranted by administrator perception in a study with no teaching, learning, or student data anywhere.

On the assumption audit that is this seat's distinctive contribution: four premises carry weight without being stated. That more quality culture is self-evidently good, when internalisation and external accountability can trade against each other. That administrator report indexes institutional reality — the load-bearing one. That ritual is pathology, when a calendarised documentation cycle is at least as plausibly a rational response to episodic review and is how an organisation makes itself legible to a regulator. And that culture is buildable by design, a claim §6 makes and the paper's own negotiation finding works against. The regulatory-governance, institutional-theory, and street-level-bureaucracy literatures the paper borrows its vocabulary from would supply defensible answers to all four; the manuscript uses their words and engages none of their arguments.

None of this requires new fieldwork to fix. Reframing every claim about what QA *is* into a claim about *administrators' accounts of* what QA is, adding a limitations section, characterising the regime, correcting the attributions, and withdrawing §5's sector-level paragraph would leave a smaller paper that is defensible and that a practitioner could use. Adding a handful of panel-side interviews, or tracing one quality claim through one cycle's documents, would leave a considerably better one.

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** The contract asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical or analytic reporting, and reproducibility affordances clear the bar this field actually applies. As Devil's Advocate I read this dimension not as "is the method good" but as "what would a hostile reviewer do to this method if they wanted it dead." For a 2,249-word qualitative-sounding study of administrator perspectives, the natural attack surfaces are sampling logic that quietly selects for people already invested in QA success, an analytic procedure that is asserted rather than described, and an absence of any account of how interpretations were disciplined against the researcher's expectations. My job is the logic-of-inference half of this — whether the method as described can in principle produce the claims that will be made from it — while leaving design-quality scoring and power/statistical-design assessment to the Methodology Reviewer. I also bind myself here to Challenge Dimension 9: any severity I assign that rests on "a QA study should report X" must name the field's actual accepted-practice boundary from a checkable source, or it down-rates.

**D2 — domain_accuracy.** The contract asks whether claims align with current domain evidence, whether prior work is represented correctly, and whether domain terminology and results are used without factual error. Adversarially, "quality culture" is an unusually treacherous construct for this: it is a contested term with distinct lineages (the EUA quality-culture work, the compliance-versus-improvement dichotomy, the internal-QA governance literature), and papers routinely invoke it as if it had a settled definition while quietly using a different one than the sources they cite. My angle is not coverage completeness — that belongs to the Domain Reviewer — but misrepresentation and convenient reading: a cited source stretched past what it says, a contested finding presented as consensus, a dichotomy attributed to the literature that the literature itself problematises, or a body of contradicting evidence that goes unmentioned while supporting evidence is cited in full. Cherry-picking and confirmation bias in the evidence base are squarely mine.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** The contract asks whether the core thesis holds together internally, whether the evidence presented actually supports the claims made, and whether any fallacy undermines the central argument. This is my home dimension and I will be least forgiving here. The recurring failure shape for administrator-perspective QA work is a slide from what administrators *say* about quality culture to what quality culture *is* in the institution — a self-report-to-reality inference — compounded by a second slide from association to mechanism ("institutions with X reported stronger culture, therefore X builds culture"). I will also test whether the paper's own conclusion could be equally well produced by a more parsimonious rival account (self-presentation by role incumbents whose job performance is the object of study; institutional isomorphism; social desirability in an evaluative context), and whether such rivals are engaged or merely unmentioned. Hidden premises count here: an unstated assumption that quality culture is a desirable, coherent, and achievable end-state is exactly the kind of frame-lock this dimension catches.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** The contract asks whether framing, definitions, and implications travel to adjacent-field readers, and whether any interdisciplinary claim is substantiated rather than gestured at. Adversarially, this cuts both ways and I will not treat it as a courtesy dimension. Under-substantiation: QA governance papers habitually import organisational-culture theory, institutional theory, or implementation science as decoration — a name invoked to license a claim the paper never earns from its own data. Over-restriction: a paper written entirely in one national QA regime's vocabulary, with undefined system-specific terms, makes its claims unfalsifiable to any reader outside that system, which is an argumentative defect and not merely a stylistic one. What I do *not* do here is opine on stakeholder implications or practical utility — that is the Perspective Reviewer's seat; I only ask whether the border-crossing claims the paper makes are supported.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** The contract asks about organisation, clarity of exposition, figure and table quality, and venue conventions. This is the dimension where an adversarial reviewer is most likely to do damage by inflating presentation complaints into substantive ones, and #574 B1 plus Dimension 9 forbid that: a defect that does not change the decision does not get a decision-changing band merely because I am the seat that attacks. None of this is decorative: the paper's most serious defects are findable *because* the authors wrote down what they did. That is worth saying before I attack, and it is the reason the attack can be this specific.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

Suppose I hold the opposite view: that quality culture is not something administrators negotiate into being, but a vocabulary the quality-assurance apparatus has issued to them. I can refute this paper without disputing a single quotation.

Every datum here is an account given by a role incumbent, to a researcher studying quality culture, about a process the incumbent runs and is judged on. Under my rival account, the three themes are precisely what that situation produces. "It's theater, and everyone knows it's theater" is not a report on institutional reality; it is the standard move by which a functionary establishes critical distance from the function they perform. §4.3 is even better evidence for me than for the authors: administrators positioning themselves as "custodians of student interests against a tide of box-ticking" is impression management with the mechanism showing. The authors read this as a coping resource that reveals where professional identity is located. I read it as the interview doing what interviews with role incumbents do.

My account is more parsimonious. It requires no cultural property, no negotiation among three parties, and no institutional formation process. It predicts the same recurrence across sites — better, in fact, since a shared professional idiom diffuses more reliably than a shared experience. And it explains the paper's one genuinely awkward result: staff sentiment was *positive* (§4.4) where administrators reported ritualism. The authors call this "more acute at the administrative level." I call it the signature of an account produced by the people whose occupational identity requires it.

The manuscript contains no data that can distinguish us — no academics, no panel-side observation, no documents, no student outcomes. It never names me as a rival.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. **Occupational account-giving by role incumbents.** The most parsimonious rival to the paper's thesis, developed above. Requires no cultural property and no formation process, predicts the same data, and is better supported by §4.3 than the paper's own reading is. Not named anywhere in the manuscript.
2. **Vocabulary diffusion rather than shared experience.** "Theater," "feeding the machine," "the forms are the easy part" are circulating idioms in QA professional discourse. §4.1 treats recurrence ("recurred repeatedly," "across every interview") as evidence that the pattern is robust. Recurrence of a *phrase* across twelve people in one professional community in one national system is at least as good evidence that they share a lexicon as that they share an experience. The paper never distinguishes these.
3. **Protocol-shaped themes.** A common instrument applied at three sites, analyzed by iterative coding "until a stable structure was reached," with non-conforming cases removed. The three themes may be an artifact of the protocol's own structure — a possibility §4.1 explicitly forecloses by the inverted warrant in M3 rather than by evidence.
4. **Gatekeeper selection.** §3.2: administrators were "recruited through institutional QA offices." The QA office selecting which QA administrators speak to a researcher studying QA is a selection route with an obvious direction, and it is not discussed.
5. **Designs not taken, and no account of why.** Documentary analysis of actual self-evaluation portfolios, observation of a live review visit, or interviews on the panel side would each have furnished a test of the "theater" characterization from outside the group that supplied it. None is used; none is mentioned as forgone.
6. **Measurement non-equivalence as the reading of §4.4.** The paper reads administrator/staff divergence as "the felt tension is more acute at the administrative level." An equally available reading is that an undescribed survey instrument (m4) does not measure the construct the interviews were about, in which case the divergence is not a finding at all. The paper selects the reading that preserves its narrative and does not name the other.

### Unexamined Premise

The manuscript assumes throughout that "quality culture" names a real, singular institutional property — something an institution can have more or less of, that forms or fails to form, and that administrators are positioned to perceive. Every research question the paper can ask follows from that premise: *how* it forms, *how much* of it there is, how to design processes that *foster* it.

The possibility never entertained is that "quality culture" is an artifact of the apparatus being studied — a term supplied to institutions by QA policy discourse — and that administrators fluent in that apparatus will reproduce its vocabulary when a researcher asks them about it. On that reading, the paper's data are evidence about the diffusion of a governance discourse among its professional carriers, not about a cultural property of institutions, and §6's conclusion would become "administrators share a vocabulary for describing compliance" rather than "quality culture is an ongoing negotiation."

Two things make this more than a philosophical objection. First, the paper's own §2 characterization of Delacroix (2018) points exactly here — the consultation that "becomes one more artifact to be documented for the panel" is this premise being questioned by the manuscript's own strongest source, two sections before §5 inverts him (C1). Second, §1 explicitly notes the concept is "under-theorized," which is the moment at which a paper would normally ask whether the concept picks anything out — and then proceeds as though it does.

## Review Body

No scoring-plan dissent is filed. Every score above was reached by applying the triggers I committed to in Phase 1 before seeing any paper content, and each dimension's justification names the specific committed limb that fired. Where a trigger did not fire I have said so explicitly (D4 limb 1; D5 block), because a seat that finds its own precommitments satisfied at every turn is a seat worth doubting.

The adversarial case against this manuscript is not that its claims are ambitious. It is that the manuscript contains, in its own words, the material that defeats them, and that this material is invisible on a linear read and unmissable on a cross-read. Seven internal contradictions sit in the text: fourteen administrators against twelve; a semi-structured protocol against a structured one; the full range of views against the exclusion of dissenting views; a survey that corroborated against a survey that diverged from expectation; full anonymization against quotations attributed to approximately one identifiable person each; a first comprehensive account against three prior works cited two pages earlier whose titles are the paper's own themes; and a source that argues against consultation-as-evidence against the same source recommending consultation as the engine of internalization. The last of these carries the paper's only practical recommendation.

Read together, these are not seven separate slips. They share a shape: at every point where the evidence and the desired claim diverge, the manuscript resolves in favour of the claim, and the resolution is stated rather than concealed. Dissenters do not fit the structure, so the structure is retained and the dissenters removed — and the removal is written down. The survey does not behave as expected, so it is declared corroborative, and the disconfirmed expectation is written down in the next sentence. Three institutions do not license a sector, so the sector is claimed anyway and the leap is written down as a permission ("can be read as"). Delacroix does not support the recommendation, so he is described as supporting it — while §2's accurate characterization stays on the page. I take the transparency at face value and make no allegation about intent; the point is narrower and harder to escape. Even granting every quotation, every count, and every reported number exactly as given, the manuscript's own record of its procedures cannot produce the conclusions it draws.

The strongest version of the defense — that this is a competent small study wearing an oversized rhetorical coat, and that removing three paragraphs of overstatement leaves something modest and publishable — is right about §5 and §6 and wrong about §3 and §4. I have tested it and reported the test in Observations. Withdrawing the sector claim, the novelty claim, and the full-range claim would repair D4 and would repair part of D3. Those are not rhetoric and they do not tone down.

### Step 1c — Surface-Form Parity Check (#216)

All five cards are written in a uniformly formal, technically precise register, so the documented failure mode here is the *crediting* direction rather than the penalising one — treating precise-sounding phrasing as if it were corroboration. Three specific applications:

1. **Technical specificity was not credited as evidence.** SC-11's weight rests on R1's checklist-form finding, which reads as authoritative. The opposite-style counterfactual — "the stats section doesn't say what test they ran and 28 people just vanish" — carries the same substance and the same anchor (`text: §4.4`; `text: §3.4`), and would receive the same weight. Weight retained on substance.
2. **Minor-channel and informal-channel corroborations were not down-weighted for their channel.** R3's and EIC's Minor-Issues corroborations of SC-5, SC-10, SC-15, SC-28, and SC-29, and R1's and R2's parenthetical corroborations of SC-16, arrived outside numbered weakness lists — often in a deliberately brief register because the seat was deferring to another's lane. Each was checked against the manuscript anchor it cites and counted at full corroborative weight, with only its *severity* recorded at the source level (tagged `[SEVERITY-SOURCE: letter-fallback]`). Channel affected transported severity, not whether the position counted.
3. **R2's hedged phrasing on SC-9 was not treated as weakness of the claim.** R2's `[FIELD-NORM UNVERIFIED]` tag reads as diffidence; it is in fact a precise epistemic restriction. The arbitration (Disagreement 1) turns on what R2 restricted its severity *to* — the norm basis — not on how tentatively it was phrased. Had R2 written "I don't buy that the journal requires this," the arbitration would be identical.

No sub-claim was marked unevaluable on wording grounds. Authorship was not a weighting input.
