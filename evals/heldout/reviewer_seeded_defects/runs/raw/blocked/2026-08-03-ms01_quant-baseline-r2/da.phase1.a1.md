## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** The contract asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical reporting, and reproducibility affordances would survive scrutiny from a competent methodologist in this field. Read adversarially, this dimension is a demand that the manuscript expose enough about how it produced its numbers that a hostile reader could attempt to break them: sampling frame, attrition, measurement definitions, model specification, uncertainty, and whether anything about the pipeline is recoverable by a third party. Vagueness is not neutral here — an unstated procedure is an untestable claim, and the burden of proof sits with the authors, not with the reviewer who cannot reconstruct it.

**D2 — domain_accuracy.** This dimension demands that every substantive claim about the domain be consistent with what the field currently holds, that prior work be characterised as its authors would recognise, and that technical terminology and reported results contain no factual errors. The adversarial reading is that misrepresentation of prior work is often not a citation slip but a rhetorical convenience: literature gets flattened into a gap the paper conveniently fills. A challenger should test whether cited work actually says what it is claimed to say and whether domain terms are used in their accepted sense rather than a locally redefined one that makes the paper's claim easier to sustain.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** This is the dimension I own. It asks whether the central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether the premises the paper asserts actually entail the conclusion it draws, whether the evidence presented is the evidence the argument needs, and whether any inferential move is a fallacy load-bearing enough to collapse the contribution. Adversarially, my job is to grant the paper its data and its methods provisionally and still try to break the chain from findings to claims — hunting for correlation narrated as causation, conclusions scoped wider than the evidence base, unstated assumptions doing silent work, internal contradictions between abstract, results, and discussion, and hedged findings that are relied upon unhedged later. A paper can be methodologically clean and still argue for something its own results do not license; that failure is what this dimension catches.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** The contract requires that framing, definitions, and implications be intelligible to readers from adjacent fields, and that any claim reaching across disciplinary lines be substantiated rather than merely gestured at. The adversarial reading is that interdisciplinary framing is frequently used to inflate perceived significance: a narrow finding is dressed in the vocabulary of a larger field it has not actually engaged. A challenger should ask whether an adjacent-field reader could state what was found and why it matters without importing charitable assumptions the manuscript never supplies.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** This dimension covers organisation, clarity of exposition, quality of figures and tables, and conformity to venue conventions. Adversarially, presentation defects matter because they conceal: an unlabelled axis, a table whose denominators are unstated, or a discussion that reorders the results can make weak evidence look stronger than it is. The test is not whether the prose is pleasant but whether the structure lets a sceptical reader locate and audit each claim's support.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution.** The contract requires that the manuscript belong in the configured venue and offer something original and significant to that readership. Read adversarially, this is a question about marginal value: not whether the work is competent but whether it advances anything a reader of this venue did not already have. Replication of the obvious, a deployment report with no transferable insight, or a contribution claim resting on novelty of setting rather than novelty of knowledge should all be pressed hard here.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether the stated thesis is entailed by the evidence actually presented — specifically, whether observational or correlational findings are converted into causal or prescriptive language, whether abstract/results/discussion assert mutually consistent things, whether every load-bearing premise is stated rather than assumed, whether the scope of the conclusion (population, timeframe, mechanism) matches the scope of the evidence, whether alternative explanations are engaged rather than named and dropped, and whether hedges attached to a finding survive into the places that finding is later relied upon.
what_triggers_block: A load-bearing inferential defect that the central claim cannot survive — e.g. the headline conclusion asserts causation or an intervention effect that the presented evidence structurally cannot support, a direct contradiction between what results report and what the discussion or abstract concludes, circular reasoning where the outcome is defined in terms of the predictor, or a conclusion whose scope is materially wider than the evidence base and on which the paper's contribution depends.
what_triggers_warn: Localised reasoning weaknesses that damage but do not collapse the thesis — e.g. one or two overstated sentences alongside a correctly hedged main claim, alternative explanations acknowledged but not seriously addressed, an unstated assumption that is plausible and could be made explicit in revision, or selective emphasis where non-supporting results are underweighted in the discussion.
what_triggers_fatal: The argument is unsalvageable within this manuscript's evidence — the central thesis is contradicted by the paper's own reported findings, the conclusion is asserted independently of any presented evidence, or the reasoning is so internally inconsistent across sections that no single coherent claim can be identified for revision to repair.
