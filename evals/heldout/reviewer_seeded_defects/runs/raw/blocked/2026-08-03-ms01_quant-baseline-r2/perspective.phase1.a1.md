## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical reporting, and reproducibility supports would survive scrutiny by the field's own methodologists. Read from my seat as the cross-disciplinary reader, rigor matters because a design that only makes sense to specialists cannot be audited by anyone outside the specialty; but the scoring itself belongs to the methodology reviewer, and I do not assign a score here. My interest is limited to whether methodological choices are described in terms an adjacent-field reader could follow well enough to judge their scope.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by the domain reviewer).** This dimension checks that the manuscript's claims are consistent with current evidence in its own field, that prior work is represented faithfully rather than strawmanned or over-credited, and that domain-specific terms and reported results contain no factual errors. From the cross-disciplinary angle, terminology accuracy and accessibility are close cousins: a term used incorrectly and a term used without definition both leave the outside reader unable to verify anything. Scoring this dimension is nonetheless the domain reviewer's job, not mine.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the discourse-analysis reviewer, also open to methodology).** Here the question is whether the central thesis holds together on its own terms: that stated evidence actually supports the conclusions drawn, that the chain from data to claim has no missing links, and that no fallacy quietly carries the argument. My cross-disciplinary concern overlaps because incoherence often first shows up as a claim that travels further than its evidence, especially claims aimed at readers outside the study's own setting. Even so, this dimension is not in my scoring remit.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by me as the perspective reviewer).** This is my dimension. I must judge whether the framing, the operational definitions, and the stated implications are usable by a reader from an adjacent field — someone competent but not native to this literature. Two things are being tested at once: accessibility, meaning that core constructs, measures, and outcome variables are defined rather than assumed, and substantiation, meaning that any claim reaching beyond the paper's home discipline is actually backed by what the study did. A paper can be perfectly rigorous inside its own walls and still fail this dimension if it exports conclusions it did not earn, or if it hides its constructs behind unexplained in-group vocabulary. I will also watch for the inverse failure: relevance asserted through generic gestures toward other fields without any mechanism connecting them.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This covers organisation, clarity of exposition, the quality and self-sufficiency of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's conventions. It sits adjacent to my own dimension because poor structure and poor cross-field accessibility often co-occur, but they are distinct: prose can be clean and well-ordered while still being unreadable to an outsider, and vice versa. Scoring belongs to the EiC role, so I will not issue a score for it.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it advances something original and significant for that venue's readership. It touches my remit because a claim of broad interdisciplinary reach is sometimes used as a substitute for a genuine contribution; but deciding fit and originality is the EiC's call, and I confine myself to noting whether interdisciplinary claims are load-bearing for the contribution.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Explicit operational definitions of the paper's central constructs and outcome measures; a framing section that states the problem in terms an adjacent-field reader could restate; implications and generalisation claims whose scope is tied to what was actually measured, in what population, over what period; any borrowing from or claim about another discipline being accompanied by a stated mechanism and a citation rather than an assertion; figures, tables, and variable names that are interpretable without insider vocabulary; and stated boundary conditions telling an outside reader where the findings should not be transported.
what_triggers_block: Central constructs or outcome measures are never operationally defined, or the paper's headline implications are addressed to audiences and settings the study design cannot speak to, so that an adjacent-field reader would be systematically misled about what the result licenses.
what_triggers_warn: Definitions and framing are recoverable but require effort — key terms defined only implicitly or late, interdisciplinary relevance asserted in the abstract or discussion without supporting mechanism or citation, or generalisation stated without stated boundary conditions — leaving the outside reader able to follow the paper but liable to overread its reach.
