## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data handling, statistical reporting, and reproducibility affordances would survive scrutiny from a competent methodologist in the field. From my seat as the domain-accuracy reviewer I read it as the "is the machinery sound" test: whether the inferential apparatus can bear the weight the authors place on it, and whether another researcher could in principle re-run the analysis. I am not scoring it, but I note that domain misstatements and methodological weakness often co-occur, so anything I find that bears on design will be flagged narratively for the owning reviewer rather than scored by me.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, my dimension).** This is my ownership: every substantive claim the manuscript makes about its subject matter must be consistent with the current state of evidence in the field, prior literature must be characterised as that literature actually stands rather than as a convenient foil, and field-specific terminology, constructs, measures, and reported results must be used with their accepted technical meanings. Errors of fact, misattributed findings, constructs conflated with adjacent-but-distinct constructs, effect sizes or benchmark values misquoted, or claims asserted as settled when the field treats them as contested all fall inside this dimension. Because the dimension is mandatory, a sufficiently severe factual failure here can drive the panel's editorial outcome on its own.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the devil's-advocate reviewer with methodology also eligible).** This dimension tests whether the central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether stated evidence actually licenses the stated conclusions, whether the argument avoids internal contradiction between sections, and whether any inferential fallacy undercuts the paper's core claim. It is distinct from mine in that a paper can be domain-accurate in every particular and still reason invalidly from those particulars, or be logically tight while resting on a factually false premise.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by the perspective reviewer).** This dimension asks whether readers from adjacent fields can enter the paper: whether framing and definitions are supplied rather than presumed, whether jargon is glossed, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplinary boundaries is actually substantiated rather than gestured at. My concern touches it only where an interdisciplinary borrowing is described inaccurately, which I would score under D2 while leaving accessibility judgements to the owner.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension covers manuscript craft: whether the organisation serves the argument, whether exposition is clear, whether figures and tables are legible and self-sufficient, and whether venue formatting and citation conventions are observed. It is a presentation judgement, separable from whether the content is true.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it advances something original and consequential for that venue's readership, as opposed to restating known results or addressing a question the venue's audience does not hold. Novelty assessment depends on an accurate picture of prior work, so my D2 findings about how the literature is represented may inform this dimension even though I do not score it.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether each substantive empirical and conceptual claim is consistent with the established evidence base in the stated field; whether cited prior work is characterised as it actually reports (correct findings, correct populations, correct direction and magnitude of effects, no straw-manning of competing accounts); whether field-specific constructs, measures, instruments, and technical terms are used with their accepted definitions and not conflated with adjacent constructs; whether reported quantities, benchmarks, and comparisons to prior results are numerically and definitionally accurate; whether contested questions are presented as contested rather than settled; and whether the manuscript's own internal numbers and terminology stay consistent across abstract, body, tables, and discussion.
what_triggers_block: One or more load-bearing factual errors — a central claim contradicted by well-established field evidence, a key cited source materially misrepresented in a way that props up the paper's own argument, or a core construct systematically conflated with a distinct construct — such that the paper's main domain claim cannot stand as written without substantive rework and re-grounding in the literature.
what_triggers_warn: Localised domain inaccuracies that do not topple the central claim: imprecise or nonstandard use of technical terminology, a secondary citation characterised loosely, an overstated generalisation beyond the population or setting the evidence supports, a contested finding asserted without acknowledging the dispute, or minor inconsistencies in reported figures or definitions across sections — all correctable by targeted revision.
what_triggers_fatal: Fabricated or non-existent sources, reported results or quantities that cannot be reconciled with any plausible reading of the described data, or claims presented as domain fact that are demonstrably false and pervasive enough that no revision short of retracting the paper's substantive contribution could repair the evidentiary basis.
