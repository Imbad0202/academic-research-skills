## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** This dimension asks whether the study's design and analytic execution would survive scrutiny by a competent peer reviewer in the field: is the sampling and data-collection procedure defensible, are the measures and their construction transparent, is the statistical reporting complete enough to judge (effect sizes, uncertainty, model specification, handling of missing or dropped cases), and are there enough materials or descriptions for another team to attempt reproduction? From my vantage point as the domain-accuracy reader I do not score this, but I note that methodological choices in this area frequently determine whether a substantive claim is even interpretable, so I will read design details for context rather than for scoring.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, my dimension).** This is the dimension I own. It asks whether the manuscript's substantive claims are compatible with what the domain evidence base currently supports, whether prior literature is characterised faithfully rather than stretched or straw-manned, and whether the field's technical vocabulary, constructs, instruments, and reported quantities are used in their accepted senses. Errors here include misdefining an established construct, attributing a finding to a cited source that the source does not contain, asserting a settled consensus where the literature is contested, mis-stating a well-known prior effect magnitude or direction, and using domain terms as if they were interchangeable when the field treats them as distinct. My scoring turns on factual correctness relative to the domain, not on whether I find the argument persuasive or the writing pleasant.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the devil's-advocate reviewer, methodology also eligible).** This dimension concerns whether the paper's central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether the stated conclusion actually follows from the evidence presented, whether the paper avoids reasoning errors that would sink the main claim (such as treating association as causation, generalising past the sampled population, or quietly changing the referent of a key term between sections), and whether internal statements are mutually consistent. I am not scoring it, but coherence failures often surface as domain misstatements, so any overlap I detect will be reported as commentary directed to the owning role.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by the perspective reviewer).** This dimension evaluates how legible and useful the work is to readers one field over: whether the framing explains why the problem matters beyond the immediate subfield, whether specialised terms are defined at first use for an adjacent-field audience, and whether any claim that reaches into another discipline is backed by evidence from that discipline rather than asserted. Not my dimension to score; I will flag only cases where an interdisciplinary borrowing is factually wrong about the borrowed field, and route that to the perspective reviewer.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension covers craft: whether sections are ordered so the argument unfolds legibly, whether exposition is clear and free of ambiguity, whether figures and tables are self-explanatory, correctly labelled, and consistent with the text, and whether the manuscript follows the target venue's formatting and reporting conventions. I do not score it. If a figure or table contradicts the numbers in the prose in a way that changes the substantive claim, I will treat that as evidence bearing on my own dimension and say so explicitly.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension asks whether the paper belongs in the configured venue and whether it adds something original and consequential for that venue's readership rather than restating known results or offering an increment too small to warrant publication. Novelty judgements depend on an accurate map of prior work, so my findings about how the literature is represented may inform this dimension, but the score itself is not mine to assign.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether every substantive claim is consistent with the current domain evidence base; whether each cited source is characterised as it actually reads (including direction, magnitude, population, and scope conditions of the borrowed finding); whether established constructs, instruments, metrics, and technical terms are defined and used in their field-accepted senses and not silently conflated with adjacent-but-distinct constructs; whether reported quantities, units, denominators, and derived indicators are internally consistent and consistent with the definitions the paper itself gives; and whether contested areas of the literature are presented as contested rather than settled.
what_triggers_block: One or more domain errors that a knowledgeable reader would have to correct before the claim could be believed — for example a central construct defined incorrectly and then used as the basis of the headline claim, a cited work asserted to show something it does not show in support of the paper's main argument, a mis-stated prior effect direction or magnitude that props up the contribution claim, or a key domain metric computed on a denominator inconsistent with its accepted definition — where the correction would materially change the paper's substantive conclusion.
what_triggers_warn: Domain inaccuracies that are real but peripheral or repairable in place without disturbing the headline claim — imprecise or non-standard use of a technical term, an over-broad "the literature shows" framing over a genuinely mixed evidence base, a citation that is thematically apt but not the appropriate support for the specific sentence, a stale reference where the field has since moved, or an undefined field-specific term whose intended sense can nonetheless be recovered from context.
what_triggers_fatal: A pattern of domain misrepresentation severe enough that the manuscript's evidentiary basis cannot be trusted at all — for example claims presented as domain findings that contradict well-established results in the field with no acknowledgement or argument, or a literature account so systematically at odds with the cited sources that revision would require rebuilding the paper's substantive foundation rather than correcting identified passages.
