## Review Body

The argument is short enough to state in full, which makes it easy to test link by link. (i) Perceived usefulness and self-reported use were measured on one instrument, in one sample, at one time. (ii) They correlate at r = .42, 95% CI [.30, .52], with a Spearman check at ρ = .40. (iii) Therefore a positive, moderate association exists in this sample. (iv) That association is "consistent with prior technology-acceptance research." (v) Therefore the paper contributes an incremental data point "comparable with prior work." (vi) And onboarding that helps students see concrete usefulness "may be worth institutional attention." Links (i) through (iii) hold, and I want to be explicit that the manuscript's restraint about causal language is real rather than decorative: the Discussion states the reverse pathway plainly, the Limitations are specific rather than boilerplate, and the Results paragraph does not inflate a moderate coefficient. Links (iv) through (vi) are where the rhetoric outruns the warrant, and that is where I press.

The sharpest defect is the practical implication. The Discussion states that the reverse pathway "is equally consistent with the data" and then, two sentences later, recommends onboarding designed to raise perceived usefulness. If direction is genuinely undetermined, the data are equally consistent with that intervention doing nothing at all — a manipulation of perception has no correlational warrant when the correlation may run entirely the other way. The authors will point to their hedge ("suggested by, not proven by, the present correlation"). That hedge does not repair the inference; it converts an unsupported claim into an unfalsifiable one, which is worse for a reader trying to decide whether to act on it. The correct repair is not more hedging but deletion of the direction-dependent recommendation, or its explicit relocation to future work contingent on a longitudinal design.

The second line of attack concerns what the outcome variable actually is. The Literature Review concedes, citing Vasquez (2020), that self-report captures "perceived rather than actual engagement," and the paper promises to treat its measure accordingly. The Abstract's closing sentence then spends that concession by reporting that perceived usefulness "tracks with LMS engagement" — a behavioural construct the study never measured. Compounding this, the manuscript never names the rival explanation that follows directly from its own framing: both variables are self-reports from the same respondent, on the same instrument, in the same five-point format, in one sitting, so some share of r = .42 is attributable to shared method and consistent response style rather than to any relation between the constructs. The Limitations enumerate four threats and miss this one, which is conspicuous precisely because the paper is otherwise so careful.

Third, "consistent with prior technology-acceptance research" is doing load-bearing work while being untestable as deployed. The paper itself reports that "effect sizes vary across samples and instruments" and that Song (2018) found association strengths varying by institution. Under those stated conditions, no positive coefficient the authors could have obtained would have been declared inconsistent. No prior effect estimate appears anywhere in the manuscript. Since the claimed contribution is comparability rather than novelty, an unfalsifiable comparison is not a minor stylistic matter — it is the premise the contribution rests on.

Fourth, comparability also requires that the instrument be the instrument. The Methods report "a six-item scale adapted from Costa and Wren (2019)"; the Literature Review drops the qualifier and asserts "a previously validated measure." What was adapted, and why, is never stated, and Cronbach's α = .88 establishes internal consistency, not that the adapted items measure what the validated original measured. The comparability premise therefore stands on an undescribed modification.

Fifth, two procedural statements bear directly on the argument. Section 3.1 reports that five duplicate entries were removed; section 3.3 states that no identifying information was collected and that responses could not be linked to individuals. Both cannot be true as written, and the resolution matters — either an identifier existed (and the ethics statement is inaccurate as published) or duplicates were inferred by some heuristic the reader cannot evaluate. Separately, recruitment ran "through the institution's course-announcement channel," which the Introduction describes as an LMS function. If invitations were delivered inside the system whose use is the outcome variable, the sampling frame is endogenous to that outcome and low-use students are structurally less likely to have been reachable. The Limitations note voluntary response but not this, and the manuscript gives the reader no way to settle it.

Minor points I record without tabling: r² is gestured at ("the proportion of variance shared ... was accordingly modest") rather than reported as .18, which a reader must compute; the use item's median category is given with no dispersion; "spanned all four year levels" implies breadth with no distribution attached; and the eligible-population denominator is never stated, so the response rate — and hence the magnitude of the self-selection concern the authors themselves raise — cannot be assessed.

Coverage receipt for the empty CRITICAL band: I attempted to break the core claim at every point where such papers usually break. The case arithmetic reconciles (233 − 14 − 5 = 214). The sensitivity statement is correct for n = 214 at α = .05 two-tailed. A confidence interval, a non-parametric robustness check, and distributional inspection are all reported. The Abstract, Results, Discussion, Limitations, and Conclusion agree on the coefficient and on its correlational status; I found no place where the paper states mutually contradictory conclusions on its central question, and no place where the reported evidence contradicts the conclusion drawn from it. The narrowly stated thesis — that a moderate positive association exists between these two self-reports in this sample — survived every attack I mounted. Each defect above is repairable by rewriting or clarification, and none of them alone reaches rejection-level impact, so none is Critical.
