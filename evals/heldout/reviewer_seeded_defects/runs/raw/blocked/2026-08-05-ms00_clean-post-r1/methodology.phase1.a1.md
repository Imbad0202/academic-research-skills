## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology-owned).** From a methods standpoint, this dimension asks whether the study's design can actually bear the weight of the inferences drawn from it. I read it as covering four linked things: whether the design (sampling frame, recruitment, timing, comparison structure) is fit for the stated research question; whether the data were handled defensibly (instrument provenance, response rates, missing-data treatment, exclusions, construct operationalisation); whether the statistical reporting is complete and correctly specified (effect sizes with uncertainty intervals, assumption checks, correction for multiplicity, no confusion of association with effect); and whether a competent independent reader has enough reproducibility affordance — instrument items, analysis specification, data or code availability — to re-run the analysis in principle. The bar is the ordinary peer-review bar of the field, not perfection, but the burden of demonstrating validity sits with the manuscript, not with my charity as a reader.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain-owned).** This dimension governs whether the substantive claims are consistent with what the field currently knows, whether prior literature is characterised accurately rather than strawmanned or over-credited, and whether domain-specific constructs, measures, and reported prior results are described without factual error. I am not scoring this dimension; I note it because methodological adequacy and domain adequacy can be confounded, and if I encounter a construct-definition problem I should be careful to route it to the methodology reading (does the operationalisation support the inference?) rather than adjudicating the field's substantive consensus, which is the domain reviewer's remit.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, DA-owned, methodology-eligible).** Here the question is whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together internally and whether the evidence presented is logically capable of supporting the conclusions asserted. My methodological angle on this dimension is narrower than the DA reviewer's: I attend to inference-level coherence — whether the strength and directionality of stated conclusions track the strength and directionality of what the design and analysis can actually license, whether hedges in the results section survive into the abstract and discussion, and whether any step in the argument chain silently converts a measured quantity into a different construct or a correlational pattern into a causal or prescriptive claim.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high, perspective-owned).** This dimension concerns whether framing, terminology, and stated implications are intelligible and useful to readers in adjacent fields, and whether any claim reaching across disciplinary boundaries is actually substantiated rather than gestured at. I am not scoring it. Its methodological neighbour is the temptation to generalise findings beyond the sampled population or setting, but the accessibility-and-substantiation judgement belongs to the perspective reviewer.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal, EiC-owned).** This covers manuscript organisation, expositional clarity, quality and self-sufficiency of figures and tables, and conformity to venue conventions. I am not scoring it. I will only care about presentation insofar as missing numbers in a table or an unlabelled figure axis makes a methodological claim unverifiable, and in that case the defect belongs in my methodology reasoning as an evidence gap, not as a writing complaint.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, EiC-owned).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it delivers an original, significant contribution proportionate to that readership. I am not scoring it. Scope, novelty, and audience-appropriateness judgements are the editor's; my scoring must not smuggle in a verdict about whether the contribution is worth publishing, only about whether the methods support what is claimed.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor

dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: Explicit statement of design and sampling frame with recruitment procedure and response/completion rate; instrument provenance and reliability/validity evidence for each measured construct; sample size with power or precision justification; complete statistical reporting including test specification, effect sizes, uncertainty intervals, assumption checks, and handling of missing data and multiplicity; and reproducibility affordances such as item wording, analysis specification, and data/code or IRB/consent statements.
what_triggers_block: A core reported inference cannot be evaluated or reconstructed at all — for example a headline association is asserted with no test statistic, no effect size, no uncertainty estimate, or no denominator — or the analysis is specified in a way that is invalid for the data structure and the manuscript offers no correction.
what_triggers_warn: The design and analysis are recoverable and broadly appropriate, but specific rigor elements are thin or absent — for example missing response rate, unreported reliability for a multi-item scale, no missing-data treatment, no correction for multiple comparisons, or no data/instrument availability statement — such that conclusions stand but with reduced verifiability.
what_triggers_fatal: The methodological foundation is unsalvageable within the study as conducted, for example the reported numbers are mutually contradictory or arithmetically impossible, the sample or measurement does not exist as described, or the single-timepoint self-report design is structurally incapable of supporting a causal or effectiveness claim that the manuscript treats as its primary result.

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: A traceable chain from research question to measures, to reported results, to conclusions, in which the conclusion's strength, directionality, and construct referents match what the reported evidence licenses, and in which qualifications present in the results are preserved in the abstract, discussion, and any practical recommendations.
what_triggers_block: The central conclusion asserted in the abstract or discussion is not supportable by the evidence presented — for example a correlational, cross-sectional, self-report result is carried forward as an established causal or effectiveness claim, or a stated conclusion contradicts the manuscript's own reported numbers.
what_triggers_warn: The core thesis is defensible but the argument contains localised slippage — inconsistent hedging between sections, one construct substituted for a related but non-equivalent construct, or an implication drawn about a population or setting beyond the sampled one — without invalidating the main claim.
what_triggers_fatal: The manuscript has no coherent central argument to evaluate, or its stated thesis is self-refuting, such that no revision short of a different study could reconcile the claims with the evidence offered.
