## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data handling, statistical reporting, and reproducibility affordances clear the bar that peer reviewers in the manuscript's home field would apply. Read through a cross-disciplinary lens, rigor also determines whether a reader trained in a neighbouring discipline could audit the inferential chain without having to trust field-specific conventions on faith: the design must be describable, the analysis must be re-runnable in principle, and the reporting must not hide decisions behind unstated local norms. I do not score this dimension, but its outcome constrains how far any claim of broader relevance can legitimately travel.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role).** Here the question is whether the manuscript's claims match the current state of evidence in its own domain, whether prior work is characterised faithfully rather than caricatured, and whether domain-specific terminology and reported results are factually correct. From the adjacent-field vantage point, accuracy matters because outside readers cannot detect a misused construct name or a misattributed prior finding; they will import it wholesale. Terminological drift that a specialist would silently correct becomes a transmission error at the disciplinary boundary. This dimension is owned by the domain reviewer and I do not assign it a score.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, DA role).** This dimension covers internal consistency of the core thesis, whether the evidence presented actually supports the claims made, and whether any reasoning defects undermine the central argument. In cross-disciplinary terms, coherence is what allows a reader without the author's tacit background to reconstruct the argument from the page alone; gaps that specialists fill from shared assumptions are exactly the gaps that break for outsiders. I do not score this dimension, but incoherence at the core would make questions of accessibility moot.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, perspective role — mine).** This is my assigned dimension. It asks whether the framing, the operational definitions, and the stated implications are genuinely accessible to readers in adjacent fields, and whether any claim that the work matters beyond its immediate specialty is actually substantiated rather than asserted. Accessibility here is not about simplification: it is about whether constructs are defined before use, whether jargon is glossed or at least anchored, whether the scope of the population and setting is stated plainly enough for a reader elsewhere to judge transferability, and whether implications drawn for other disciplines, practice communities, or policy audiences are backed by the evidence actually collected. Overreach — claiming portability the design cannot support — counts against this dimension just as much as opacity does.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, EiC role).** This dimension concerns organisation, clarity of exposition, quality of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's conventions. It overlaps with my dimension but is distinct: a manuscript can be clean, conventional prose and still be hermetic to outsiders, and it can be structurally rough while remaining conceptually portable. The editor-in-chief role owns this; I do not score it and will avoid substituting stylistic complaints for substantive accessibility findings.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, EiC role).** This asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it offers an original, significant contribution to that readership. Its relationship to my dimension is one of direction: venue fit is about the primary audience, while cross-disciplinary relevance is about audiences beyond it. A paper can fit its venue perfectly and still be inaccessible outside it, or be broadly legible while contributing nothing new. The EiC owns this judgement and I do not score it.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether key constructs, instruments, and outcome measures are explicitly defined at first use rather than assumed; whether the population, setting, and sampling frame are stated concretely enough for an adjacent-field reader to judge transferability; whether jargon and field-internal abbreviations are glossed; whether stated implications for other disciplines, practice, or policy are tied to evidence the study actually produced; and whether the limitations section names the boundaries of generalisation in plain terms.
what_triggers_block: The manuscript asserts substantive implications for audiences or domains beyond its own field while the reported design cannot support them (for example, generalisation to other institutions, populations, systems, or causal/behavioural claims from a design that yields no such evidence), or core constructs are never operationally defined anywhere in the text so that no outside reader can determine what was measured.
what_triggers_warn: Framing is serviceable but leaks field-internal shorthand — undefined abbreviations, instrument names used without description, or a construct treated as self-evident — and the interdisciplinary framing is present but thinly substantiated, such that an adjacent-field reader could follow the study with effort but would have to look elsewhere to interpret the terms or gauge transferability.
