## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data collection and handling, analytic procedures, statistical or qualitative reporting, and the affordances that would let another researcher reproduce or audit the work all reach the standard a peer reviewer in this field would demand. From my seat as the domain-accuracy reviewer I read this as the "was the evidence produced properly" question, distinct from "is the evidence consistent with what the field already knows" — it is owned by the methodology reviewer, and I will not score it.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory).** This is my dimension. It asks whether the manuscript's substantive claims are consistent with the current state of evidence in higher education policy and quality assurance; whether prior scholarship, frameworks, standards, and policy instruments are represented faithfully rather than caricatured or misattributed; and whether the paper's use of field-specific terminology, institutional categories, regulatory descriptions, and reported results is factually correct. Errors here include misdefined technical terms, misstated features of accreditation or quality-assurance regimes, citations that do not support the claim attached to them, superseded evidence presented as current, and overreach in which a narrow finding is asserted as settled domain knowledge.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory).** This dimension covers whether the paper's central thesis holds together internally, whether the evidence offered actually bears on the claims it is recruited to support, and whether any reasoning defects are severe enough to undermine the core argument. It overlaps with my concerns where a domain misstatement is also a load-bearing premise, but the scoring belongs to the devil's-advocate and methodology reviewers, so I will not assign it a score.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high).** This dimension asks whether the framing, definitions, and stated implications are intelligible and useful to readers from adjacent fields, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplinary boundaries is actually substantiated rather than asserted. It is owned by the perspective reviewer; my role is limited to noting, in prose if relevant, where an in-field term is used in a way that would mislead an outside reader.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal).** This dimension concerns manuscript organisation, clarity of exposition, the quality and legibility of figures and tables, and conformity to the target venue's conventions. It is the editor-in-chief role's to score, and I will not score it.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it offers an original and significant contribution to that venue's readership. Originality judgements lean on domain knowledge of what already exists in the literature, but the scoring authority sits with the editor-in-chief role, so I will confine myself to my own dimension.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Verify that every substantive claim about quality assurance and higher education policy is consistent with current field evidence; check that named frameworks, standards, accreditation bodies, review cycles, and governance concepts are defined and described correctly; check that cited prior work is characterised as its authors actually argued and is not superseded or misattributed; check that reported results and any numbers, categories, or institutional descriptions are internally consistent and not stated with more certainty than the underlying evidence supports.
what_triggers_block: A load-bearing claim contradicts well-established domain evidence, or a central framework, standard, or prior study is materially misrepresented in a way that changes the paper's substantive conclusions and cannot be fixed without new evidence or a rewritten argument.
what_triggers_warn: Localised domain inaccuracies that do not destabilise the main conclusions, such as an imprecise or non-standard definition of a technical term, a citation that only partially supports the attached claim, an outdated policy description, or an overstated generalisation from a limited evidence base.
what_triggers_fatal: Fabricated or non-existent sources, invented results or data attributed to real bodies, or a pattern of domain falsehoods so pervasive that the manuscript's factual base cannot be trusted at all.
