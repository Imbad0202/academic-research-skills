## Recommended Target Journals (Top 3)

1. **Quality in Higher Education** (Routledge) — Closest scope match by a wide margin. Its remit is precisely the QA/quality-culture nexus, it publishes qualitative institutional work, and it serves a mixed scholar-practitioner readership that would find administrator accounts intrinsically interesting. The paper already cites it (Iversen, 2021). It is also the journal most likely to notice the absence of the field's foundational quality-culture sources, since its own back catalogue *is* that literature.

2. **Journal of Higher Education Policy and Management** (Routledge) — Strong fit for the "administrators as intermediaries" framing; the paper's nearest antecedent (Silva & Tan, 2021) is cited there. JHEPM welcomes management-level empirical work and is somewhat more tolerant of modest samples, provided claims are scaled to them. The paper's current over-generalisation to "the sector as a whole" is exactly what this journal's reviewers police.

3. **Higher Education Quarterly** (Wiley) — Publishes qualitative and mixed-methods institutional research with a conceptual contribution; Ferreira & Lund (2022) is cited there. Would require the theoretical framework to be named and developed rather than gestured at.

**Stretch option, not currently supportable**: *Studies in Higher Education* or *Higher Education*. Both would require a declared theoretical apparatus, a defensible sample, and a cross-system comparative argument. The present draft cannot reach either.

**Adjacent alternative if the survey is dropped**: *Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education* (cited: Nakamura, 2020) — but only if the paper is reframed around QA process design, since A&EHE's centre of gravity is assessment practice.

## Reviewer Configuration Cards

### Reviewer Configuration Card #1

**Role**: EIC
**Display role**: Journal-Fit Reviewer
**Identity Description**: Editor of *Quality in Higher Education*, twenty years in the field, formerly director of institutional quality at a research-intensive university and a serving external review panel chair for a national QA agency. Reads roughly 200 submissions a year on quality culture and has personally handled the journal's special issues on internalisation of external QA. Bridges the scholar-practitioner divide by instinct: asks both "is this new to the literature?" and "could a quality director act on this next Monday?"

**Review Focus**:
1. **Scope and readership fit against the configured journal.** Does the manuscript tell *QiHE*'s readership — QA scholars and practising quality officers — something it does not already know? Administrator experience of QA-as-ritual is a well-worked seam; the burden is on the author to show the increment.
2. **Interrogate the novelty claim directly.** The Conclusion asserts "the first comprehensive account of how university administrators experience and enact QA implementation." The paper's own literature review cites Pettersen (2022) on distributed leadership *in the quality office*, Rahman (2020) on identity and vocation *among quality professionals*, and Silva & Tan (2021) on administrators as quality intermediaries — three sources covering the paper's three themes. The claim is contradicted by the manuscript's own citations. Also test "comprehensive" against n=12–14 in one unnamed system.
3. **Practitioner usability of the implications.** §5's third finding and the Conclusion recommend "processes that create room for meaning-making." Ask what a quality director would actually change. As written, the guidance is unactionable, and the one specific recommendation offered (maximise stakeholder consultation) rests on a misreading of the source cited to support it.
4. **Whether the contribution survives correction.** If the sector-level generalisation is scaled back to three institutions, the survey inference is withdrawn, and the excluded dissenting accounts are reinstated, what contribution remains? Judge the paper on that residue, not on the current framing.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether the paper's ambition is calibrated to its evidence. A three-site, 12-participant interview study can make a real contribution to this journal; a three-site, 12-participant study claiming to characterise "the higher education sector as a whole" cannot, and the overreach will read to this editor as a signal about the author's judgement generally.

**Possible blind spots**: Likely to reason from editorial experience and field convention rather than technical detail; may not independently verify the survey statistics or diagnose the mixed-methods integration failure. May also under-weight the research-ethics problem in the role-identifying quotations, treating it as a copy-editing matter rather than a consent and confidentiality issue. Synthesizer should ensure Cards #2 and #4 cover these.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #2

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 1
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 1
**Identity Description**: Associate professor of research methodology in a school of education, specialising in **qualitatively driven mixed-methods designs**; trained in reflexive thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke lineage) and competent in small-sample inferential statistics. Teaches a doctoral seminar on integration failure in mixed methods and has published on trustworthiness criteria for multi-site interview studies. Habitually reconstructs a study's evidentiary chain from sampling through to claim, and stops at the first link that will not bear load.

**Review Focus**:
1. **Sample integrity and internal consistency of the reported design.** The abstract reports "Fourteen administrators were interviewed"; §3.2 reports "Twelve senior administrators (n=12)." One of these is wrong, and until it is resolved nothing downstream can be assessed. Compound this with the survey arithmetic: n=48 total, but §4.4 accounts for only 20 respondents (n=9 research university, n=11 teaching-focused). Where are the remaining 28, and what is the response rate against what sampling frame? Also: §3.4 promises subgroup comparisons "where role categories permitted," but §4.4 delivers a comparison by *institution*. The analysis reported is not the analysis planned.
2. **Exclusion of disconfirming evidence — treat as the central methodological defect.** §4.3 states the study "achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views," and then, two sentences later, that "a minority of participants expressed more skeptical or dissenting views... these were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure." These statements cannot both hold. More seriously, the stated rationale inverts the logic of thematic analysis: cases that fall outside the theme structure are precisely the cases that test it. Negative-case analysis is not an optional extra here — dropping dissenters from a study whose headline finding is that QA is experienced as ritual removes the only evidence that could have disciplined that finding. Require reinstatement and full reporting, not a word-count explanation.
3. **Survey instrument provenance and the inferential claim.** No scale is named, no item count given, no response format or range stated (M=3.9 against what ceiling?), no validity or reliability evidence (no α, no factor structure), no indication whether "institutional quality culture" is a single item or a composite. Then a statistically significant difference (p<.05) is asserted between groups of 9 and 11, with no test named, no effect size, no assumption checks, and no correction despite this being one contrast among an unreported number examined. §4.4 concedes the numbers are small and nonetheless proposes "institutional type as a possible moderator" — a moderation claim requires variance the design cannot supply. Recommend the inferential claim be withdrawn and the survey reported descriptively, or the survey component dropped.
4. **Thematic analysis procedure and trustworthiness.** §3.4 is four sentences and specifies almost nothing: no analytic approach named, no coding framework, no statement of whether coding was single- or multi-coder, no audit trail, member checking, or reflexivity statement — notable given the author is plausibly positioned inside this QA world. "Until a stable structure was reached" is not a saturation criterion. Prevalence language is likewise unquantified: "recurred repeatedly" (§4.1), "recurred across every interview" (§4.2), "several administrators" (§4.3) — how many, at how many sites?
5. **Method-to-finding contradiction and mixed-methods integration.** §3.3 describes *semi-structured* interviews; §4.1 attributes the themes to "the structured protocol" and reasons that because they "emerged systematically" they cannot be an artifact of local circumstance. That inference does not follow — a common protocol produces common themes by construction, which is the opposite of triangulation. Finally, no integration strategy is specified beyond "contextualize": no design typology named, no joint display, no point of interface. §5 then treats the survey as having "corroborated" the qualitative picture while simultaneously reporting that it contradicted expectations — an integration claim that cannot be both.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether the reported conclusions are entailed by the reported evidence. This reviewer's threshold question is not "is the topic worthwhile" (it is) but "can I reconstruct how the author got from 12 transcripts to a claim about the sector?" On the current text the answer is no, and the exclusion of dissenting cases means the qualitative finding is not merely under-evidenced but structurally biased toward the conclusion drawn.

**Possible blind spots**: May treat the Delacroix (2018) misreading as a citation slip rather than a substantive theoretical error, and is unlikely to notice which canonical QA literature is absent, since that requires domain-specific reading history. Will probably not engage the paper's theoretical framing at all. Cards #3 and #4 must cover both.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #3

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 2
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 2
**Identity Description**: Professor of higher education policy, fifteen years researching quality assurance across the European Higher Education Area and East Asian systems; contributed to a national consultation on ESG implementation and has published comparative work on how the *same* QA instrument produces different institutional behaviour under different regulatory regimes. Knows the quality-culture literature genealogically — from Harvey & Green's definitional work through Newton's studies of QA as ritual and the EUA Quality Culture Project to Ehlers' structural-cultural distinction — and reads new submissions in that lineage by reflex.

**Review Focus**:
1. **The Delacroix (2018) reversal — the paper's most serious scholarly error.** §2 states plainly that Delacroix "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual, and argues *against* treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of a healthy quality culture." The reference-list annotation repeats this. Yet §5 claims: "our findings align with Delacroix (2018), who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture. Consultation, on this reading, is the engine of internalization." That is the source's argument inverted, and it is inverted in service of the paper's central practical recommendation. Note the collateral damage: §2 identifies the Delacroix-versus-Ferreira/Nakamura tension as the study's animating problem, so misreading Delacroix dissolves the paper's own stated rationale. This must be corrected before any assessment of the contribution is possible, and the author should be asked to re-verify every substantive citation, since a reversal of this magnitude raises the question of whether sources were read or paraphrased at second hand.
2. **Absent canonical literature.** Every one of the 12 references dates from 2018–2022. The literature on QA-as-ritual and on quality culture did not begin in 2018. Finding 4.1 in particular re-describes a well-established phenomenon without acknowledging the tradition that named it, which makes the "first comprehensive account" claim untenable and prevents the reader from locating the increment. The gap is not decorative: the paper's three themes each sit on a substantial prior literature, and the author needs to show what is being extended rather than reinvented. Ask specifically for engagement with the definitional work on "quality," the earlier empirical work on ritual compliance and decoupling in institutional self-evaluation, and the structural/cultural distinction within quality-culture theory.
3. **Undefined national and regulatory context — a transferability failure.** §3.2 places the study at "three universities in a single national system" and says nothing further. But "QA implementation" is not a constant across systems: what an administrator is *doing* differs fundamentally under an ESG-aligned cyclical external review, a US-style regional accreditation regime, an outcomes-based national ranking-linked audit, or a ministry-run programme accreditation. Without the regime specified, readers cannot judge what the ritual-compliance finding means, whether the three institutions are comparable, or whether anything transfers. The masking may be intended to protect participants, but the cost is that the central finding is uninterpretable — and it does not in fact protect them (see Card #4).
4. **Over-generalisation from three institutions to the sector.** §5's second finding claims the study "demonstrate[s] that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment," that the work "can be read as a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture," and that "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension." Twelve participants in three institutions in one unnamed system cannot support any of these. Note the internal contradiction: the Conclusion then calls for "future research... across a wider range of institutional types and national systems," conceding the limit the Discussion has just breached. There is also no limitations section anywhere in the manuscript.
5. **Under-theorisation relative to the journals targeted.** The three themes are presented as inductive discoveries but map closely onto existing constructs. "Quality culture" itself is never operationally defined despite being the title concept, and the paper does not distinguish quality assurance from accreditation from quality enhancement — distinctions this field treats as basic. For any of the three recommended journals, the conceptual apparatus needs to be named and used, not left implicit.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether the author has actually read the field they claim to advance. The Delacroix reversal combined with a reference list that reaches back only four years suggests engagement with a recent slice of literature rather than with the field, and this reviewer will weight that heavily because it determines whether the paper's claimed gap is a real gap.

**Possible blind spots**: Likely to accept the survey numbers at face value (this reviewer's expertise is policy and comparative analysis, not psychometrics) and may not independently detect the participant-count discrepancy. May also be relatively forgiving of thin methods reporting if the domain argument is strong. Card #2 covers both.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #4

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 3
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 3
**Identity Description**: Reader in the **sociology of organisations and professions**, working on audit cultures, quantification, and organisational responses to external evaluation — a neo-institutionalist by training (decoupling and ceremonial conformity), extended by work on the audit society and on the reactivity of measurement. Not a higher education researcher: comes to this manuscript as a study of how professionals absorb external evaluation regimes, and has run comparable interview fieldwork inside organisations where informants are themselves fluent in audit language. Also sits on a university research ethics committee, where the recurring issue is deductive disclosure in small-N organisational research.

**Review Focus**:
1. **The paper names a phenomenon that already has a theory, and forgoes the theory.** "QA as ritual compliance" is a textbook description of **decoupling** — the gap between an organisation's ceremonial conformity to institutional expectations and its technical activity — and the evidence portfolio "that nobody reads except the panel" is ceremonial conformity in its purest form. By treating this inductively rather than engaging the organisational-theory literature on why decoupling is *stable* and *functional*, the paper cannot explain its own most interesting finding: why administrators sustain a system they describe as theatre. That literature would also give the author leverage on the survey puzzle in §4.4 (staff sentiment positive where administrators report ritualism), which decoupling predicts rather than merely notes. Similarly, "QA as identity work" invokes a substantial literature on identity regulation and professional identity work that goes uncited.
2. **Reactivity: the accounts are performances, and the paper does not consider this.** These informants are QA professionals — expert producers of accounts for evaluative audiences. When a quality director tells a researcher "it's theater, and everyone knows it's theater," that utterance is itself a positioning move: it establishes the speaker as someone who sees through the system while still running it. The paper reads such statements as transparent reports of experience. Note that §4.3 observes the vocational framing "function[ed] as a coping resource" — the author is one step from the reflexive point and does not take it. What would it change if the ritual-compliance theme were partly an artifact of skilled informants performing critical distance for a sympathetic interviewer? At minimum this requires a positionality statement and analytic attention to how accounts were produced.
3. **Deductive disclosure — a live confidentiality breach, not a stylistic issue.** §3.5 asserts that "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified in reported findings." The Findings then attribute quotations to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and "the associate dean for quality at the public research university." Within a three-institution design in a single national system, those descriptors are effectively identifying: the population holding each role is one person. The most critical quotations in the paper ("it's theater"; "feeding the machine"; "nothing behind that a teacher would recognize as helping them teach") are therefore attributable to named individuals speaking critically about their own employers. This is a foreseeable harm to participants, it contradicts the ethics statement, and it is the kind of thing an ethics committee treats as a reportable deviation rather than a revision request. Also absent: any indication that consent covered publication of role-attributed quotations, and no ethics approval reference. Note the interaction with Card #3's point 3 — the paper simultaneously over-masks the analytically necessary context (the national QA regime) and under-masks the ethically sensitive detail (individual roles). Both directions are wrong.
4. **The three themes are not held in tension with each other.** Sections 4.1 and 4.2 sit awkwardly together: QA is empty theatre, yet administrators spend most of their energy building genuine local ownership. Either the persuasion work succeeds (in which case the ritual account is overstated) or it fails (in which case §4.2's distributed-leadership finding is aspiration reported as practice). The paper reports both and reconciles neither. §4.3's move — that administrators "held the tension open" — is interesting but is asserted rather than evidenced, and the excluded dissenters (Card #2, point 2) are precisely the participants who might have resolved which reading holds.
5. **Whether the intermediary position is theorised or merely observed.** §2 identifies the administrator as occupying "the hinge between external requirement and internal practice" and rightly notes that Silva & Tan (2021) left this conceptual. But the paper does not supply the missing apparatus either. Sociology of professions offers directly applicable tools here: institutional work, boundary-spanning roles, the structural strain of dual accountability, professional legitimacy under external audit. Using any of them would convert a set of descriptive themes into an explanation.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether the paper takes seriously that its informants are strategic actors and that its own data-collection encounter was part of the institutional field it studies. And, non-negotiably, whether participants have been protected — this reviewer will not recommend publication in any form while the role-identifying attributions stand, independent of the manuscript's other merits.

**Possible blind spots**: Unfamiliar with higher education QA conventions and the ESG/accreditation landscape, so will not detect the missing canonical HE literature or the Delacroix misreading (Card #3 covers both). May undervalue the paper's potential practitioner contribution, since the sociological reflex is to treat practical recommendations as naive (Card #1 covers this). Will not scrutinise the survey statistics (Card #2 covers this). May also push the theoretical demand further than the target journals require — the synthesizer should treat "engage decoupling and identity-work literatures" as a required revision but "reframe the paper as organisational sociology" as advisory only.

## Review Strategy Recommendations

**Special characteristics requiring particular attention**

1. **Form-substance mismatch is the defining feature of this manuscript.** The prose is fluent, the structure complete, the references formatted. That polish will make reviewers read faster and may cause them to miss defects that a rougher draft would expose. Instruct all four reviewers to run an explicit **internal-consistency pass** before evaluating quality, checking abstract against methods, methods against findings, literature review against discussion, and ethics statement against reported data. Every one of the four contradictions below is detectable without external verification, which means none of them requires resolving the reference-verification problem first.

2. **Four verifiable internal contradictions — route these so they are not duplicated:**

   | # | Contradiction | Location | Primary reviewer |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | 14 administrators (abstract) vs. 12 (§3.2) | Abstract / §3.2 | R1 |
   | 2 | "Balanced representation... full range of views" vs. dissenting views "excluded for space" | §4.3 | R1 |
   | 3 | Delacroix argues *against* consultation-as-sufficient (§2, reference annotation) vs. "recommends... consultation as the central mechanism" (§5) | §2 / §5 / References | R2 |
   | 4 | "Fully anonymized... no individual could be identified" vs. role-identifying attributions | §3.5 / §4.1 | R3 |

   Secondary inconsistencies, lower priority but worth logging: semi-structured interviews (§3.3) vs. "the structured protocol" (§4.1); subgroup comparison by role planned (§3.4) vs. by institution delivered (§4.4); survey described as supplementary/contextualising (§3.1) vs. load-bearing corroboration and a moderation claim (§4.4, §5); Discussion generalises to the sector (§5) vs. Conclusion calls for wider-system research (§6); survey n=48 vs. 20 accounted for (§4.4).

3. **Rank-order the defects for the synthesizer.** Not all of these are equal. Contradiction 4 (participant confidentiality) and 2 (exclusion of disconfirming cases) go to research integrity and are independently sufficient to block publication. Contradiction 3 (reversed citation) goes to scholarly accuracy and undermines the paper's own stated rationale. Contradiction 1 and the survey statistics are correctable reporting failures. The missing canonical literature and the under-theorisation are substantial but ordinary major-revision matters. The synthesizer should not let volume of criticism flatten this hierarchy — a report listing fifteen equal-weight problems is less useful to the author than one that says which two must be fixed before anything else can be assessed.

4. **Register: developmental, verdict: unchanged.** The paper's core question is genuinely worth asking, the administrator-as-hinge framing in §2 is the manuscript's best idea, and §4.3's observation that administrators locate their professional identity in the gap between compliance and meaning is a real insight that survives every criticism above. Reviewers should say so, and should write toward a recoverable paper. But the constructive tone must not migrate into the recommendation: the confidentiality breach and the excluded dissenting cases are not softenable by framing.

**Complementarity and tension between reviewers**

- **Designed coverage of the four contradictions is complete and non-overlapping**: R1 owns 1 and 2, R2 owns 3, R3 owns 4. No reviewer is configured to catch all four, which is intentional — it lets the synthesizer see which defects are field-visible versus discipline-specific and report that to the author as a signal about who will catch what at submission.

- **Productive tension between R2 and R3 on theory.** R2 wants the *higher education* quality-culture canon (Harvey & Green, Newton, EUA, Ehlers); R3 wants the *organisational sociology* apparatus (decoupling, audit cultures, identity work). These are different remedies for the same defect, and the author cannot fully satisfy both within one paper's word limit. The synthesizer should present this as a genuine authorial choice with consequences rather than merging it into "engage more literature": the HE route suits *Quality in Higher Education*, the sociological route suits a stronger theoretical contribution but a harder sell to a QA readership. Frame it plainly for the author — this is a decision about which conversation the paper wants to join, not a technical fix.

- **Direct tension between R2 and R3 on context disclosure.** R2 requires that the national QA regime be specified for the findings to be interpretable; R3 requires tighter protection of participant identity. These pull in opposite directions and the resolution is not obvious. Likely path: name the *regime type* and regulatory features (which is what R2 actually needs) while stripping institution-and-role-specific descriptors from quotations (which is what R3 needs). The synthesizer must resolve this explicitly rather than passing both demands through unreconciled, or the author will receive instructions that cannot both be followed.

- **Tension between R1 and R3 on the survey.** R1's likely recommendation is to withdraw the inferential claim or drop the survey; R3's decoupling framing makes the survey's central puzzle (positive staff sentiment alongside administrator-reported ritualism) theoretically valuable and worth keeping. Reconcilable — keep the survey descriptively as a puzzle to be explained, drop the p-value and the moderation claim — but the synthesizer should perform that reconciliation rather than reporting both positions.

- **Watch for R1 (methodology) dominating the report by volume.** This manuscript has more methodological problems than any other kind, so R1's contribution will be the longest. The synthesizer should resist letting the report become a methods critique: the Delacroix reversal (R2) and the confidentiality breach (R3) are individually more consequential than any single methods gap, and a length-weighted synthesis would bury them.

- **Guard against pile-on.** Four reviewers with non-overlapping remits will collectively identify a large number of problems in a 2,500-word manuscript. The synthesizer should open with what the paper gets right and what is worth preserving, then deliver the hierarchy in point 3. The author needs a route forward, and the route exists.
