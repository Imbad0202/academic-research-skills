## Review Body

I read this manuscript in the lineage it claims to join — the definitional work on "quality," the empirical tradition on QA as ritual and decoupling in institutional self-evaluation, and the quality-culture literature's long argument about whether culture can be engineered or only cultivated. Judged on domain accuracy alone, the paper contains one error that I regard as disqualifying in its present form, and a cluster of substantive problems around literature coverage, contextual specification, conceptual definition, and internal factual consistency.

The disqualifying error is the treatment of Delacroix (2018). Section 2 renders that source correctly and, in doing so, makes it the pivot of the study's rationale: Delacroix cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual and argues against treating consultation as evidence of a healthy quality culture, and the reference-list annotation repeats this. Section 5 then recruits the same source for the opposite proposition — that consultation is the central mechanism, "the engine of internalization." This is not a nuance lost in paraphrase; it is the source's thesis inverted, and it is inverted precisely where the paper states its central practical recommendation. The collateral damage is worse than the citation itself: because §2 builds the study's animating problem out of the Delacroix-versus-Ferreira/Nakamura tension, the §5 reading dissolves the paper's own stated gap. There is no version of the Discussion that keeps this sentence and keeps the Introduction. Correcting it is possible without new data, which is why I score this repairable rather than fatal — but the repair is a rewritten Discussion, not a corrected parenthesis, and I would ask the author to re-verify every substantive citation, because a reversal of this magnitude raises a reasonable question about whether sources were read or absorbed at second hand. I stop short of the fatal band deliberately: I cannot verify from the manuscript whether the cited works exist, and I flag the reference-provenance anomaly below as a verification request rather than an accusation.

Second, the reference list spans 2018 to 2022 and nothing else. The scholarship on quality culture, and on QA as ritual, did not begin four years before submission. Finding 4.1 re-describes ritual compliance and decoupling as though the phenomenon were being discovered here, when the earlier empirical literature on institutional self-evaluation named and theorised it, and the definitional literature on "quality" long ago established why the term resists the operational treatment this paper gives it. The consequence is not merely a thin bibliography: without the prior tradition on the page, neither the reader nor I can locate the increment, and the Conclusion's "first comprehensive account" is untenable as written.

Third, the study is placed at "three universities in a single national system" and that is all the reader is told. In comparative QA research this is not a tolerable omission, because what an administrator is *doing* when they "implement QA" differs fundamentally across regimes: an ESG-aligned cyclical institutional review, a US-style regional accreditation, a ranking-linked outcomes audit, and a ministry-run programme accreditation generate different documentary demands, different panels, and different incentives to perform. The ritual-compliance finding is therefore uninterpretable in the strong sense — I cannot tell whether the "season of the year given over to feeding the machine" reflects a six-yearly external review cycle, an annual monitoring return, or a permanent audit posture, and I cannot judge whether the three institutions are even comparable instruments of the same regime. The masking appears intended to protect participants, but as the manuscript stands it does not achieve that (see the anonymisation finding below), so the cost is paid for nothing.

Fourth, the Discussion generalises past what the design can carry. Twelve administrators in three institutions in one unnamed system do not license claims about "universities across the sector," about the paper as "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture," or about what "administrators everywhere" face. The manuscript then contradicts itself: the Conclusion calls for future research across a wider range of institutional types and national systems, conceding the boundary the Discussion has just crossed. There is no limitations section anywhere.

Fifth, on conceptual apparatus: "quality culture" is the title concept and is never operationally defined, and the paper nowhere distinguishes quality assurance from accreditation from quality enhancement. In this field those are basic distinctions, and the structural-versus-cultural debate within quality-culture theory would have given the three themes a home rather than leaving them as free-standing inductions. Two of the three themes — distributed leadership and professional identity work — map so closely onto the constructs in the paper's own cited sources that presenting them as discoveries misdescribes their status.

Finally, several internal factual inconsistencies bear directly on whether the reported evidence can be taken at face value: the Abstract reports fourteen interviewees against the Methods' twelve; §4.1 identifies participants by role and institution in a manuscript that claims full anonymisation; and §4.3 asserts balanced representation and the full range of views in the same paragraph that reports excluding dissenting accounts. Each of these is a claim about the paper's own evidence that the paper's own text contradicts.

The manuscript's strengths are real and worth preserving through revision: §2's construction of the field's central tension is accurate and well-posed, the decoupling mechanism is rendered correctly, the identity finding is described without overreach, and the survey-versus-interview divergence is reported rather than smoothed away.

### S1: Section 2 poses the field's central tension accurately and productively

Before the Discussion mishandles it, §2 states the participatory-cultivation versus sophisticated-performance opposition in terms a specialist would recognise as the live disagreement in the quality-culture literature, and it correctly assigns the sceptical position to Delacroix and the cultivation position to Ferreira/Lund and Nakamura. This is a genuinely well-framed problem statement, and it is the reason the §5 error is diagnosable at all.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2, final sentence of second paragraph "This tension — between QA as participatory cultivation and QA as sophisticated performance — animates the present study"

### S2: Correct rendering of the documentation-decoupling mechanism

The characterisation of evidence portfolios crowding out the reflective practice they were meant to capture is a faithful statement of the decoupling mechanism as the institutional self-evaluation literature describes it, and the paper's own empirical material in §4.1 is consistent with it rather than being stretched to fit.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2, third paragraph "evidence portfolios, originally intended to demonstrate quality, can crowd out the reflective practice they were meant to capture"

### S3: Identity finding stated without domain overreach

Section 4.3 reports the vocational framing as a coping resource that holds the compliance/meaning tension open rather than resolving it. That is a defensible and appropriately modest reading, consistent with what the literature on quality professionals' identity would predict, and it is the part of the Findings that most clearly earns its interpretive claim.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "Rather than resolving the tension between compliance and meaning, they held it open"

### S4: Disconfirming survey result reported rather than harmonised

The moderately positive staff sentiment cut against the expectation the interviews generated, and the manuscript says so explicitly instead of reconciling the two strands rhetorically. Preserving that divergence is the right instinct and is where the paper's most promising unexploited insight sits.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "This is notable because the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture"

### W1: Delacroix (2018) is cited in §5 for the opposite of the argument the source makes and §2 reports

Section 2 and the reference-list annotation both state that Delacroix argues *against* treating stakeholder consultation as evidence of quality culture. Section 5 attributes to Delacroix the recommendation that consultation be the central mechanism of internalization, and builds the paper's principal practical recommendation on that attribution. The misrepresentation is load-bearing twice over: it fabricates support for the design recommendation, and it destroys the study's stated rationale, since §2 derives the research problem from the disagreement between Delacroix and the cultivation strand. As written, the Introduction's gap and the Discussion's conclusion cannot both stand. Repair requires rewriting §5's third paragraph, restating what the study actually shows about consultation given the ritual-compliance finding, and re-verifying every substantive citation in the manuscript.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5, third paragraph "our findings align with Delacroix (2018), who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture"
**Confidence**: 5 — the reversal is established internally against the manuscript's own §2 characterisation and reference annotation; no external verification is needed.

### W2: Reference base spans only 2018–2022, omitting the canonical literature the findings restate

All twelve references postdate 2017. The paper's three themes each sit on substantial prior scholarship — the definitional literature on what "quality" means in higher education, the earlier empirical work on ritual compliance and decoupling in institutional self-evaluation, and the structural/cultural distinction within quality-culture theory — and none of it appears. Finding 4.1 therefore re-describes a named and well-documented phenomenon as an emergent theme, and the Conclusion's claim to the "first comprehensive account" cannot be sustained against a literature the manuscript has not surveyed. Revision needs to show what is being extended rather than reinvented, which will change how the contribution is stated.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: References list, all twelve entries dated 2018-2022 — expected engagement with the pre-2018 canonical scholarship on the definition of quality, on QA as ritual and decoupling in institutional self-evaluation, and on the structural versus cultural account of quality culture; checked References, §1, §2, §4.1, §6
**Confidence**: 5 — direct competence; this is the literature I work in and the omissions are systematic rather than incidental.

### W3: The national and regulatory regime is never specified, leaving the central finding uninterpretable

"QA implementation" is not a constant across systems. Whether administrators are preparing for cyclical ESG-aligned external review, regional institutional accreditation, a ranking-linked outcomes audit, or ministry-run programme accreditation determines what documentation is demanded, who reads it, what sanction follows, and therefore what "ritual compliance" even denotes. Without the regime named, the reader cannot judge what the ritual finding means, whether the three institutions are subject to the same instrument, or whether anything transfers to another system. Naming the regime type — and, if identification is a concern, describing the instrument at the level of regime family rather than country — is a prerequisite for interpreting §4.1 and for the Discussion's comparative ambitions.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.2 "three universities in a single national system"
**Confidence**: 5 — comparative QA regime analysis is my primary area; the interpretive dependence on regime type is well established.

### W4: Discussion generalises from three institutions to the sector, and the Conclusion concedes the limit

The second finding claims that the study demonstrates how "universities across the sector" treat QA, offers itself as a general account of the sector as a whole, and asserts that administrators everywhere face the same tension. Twelve interviewees in three institutions in one unspecified system cannot support any of the three claims, and the Conclusion's call for future work across wider institutional types and national systems contradicts them directly. Compounding this, the manuscript contains no limitations section, so the boundary conditions are never stated anywhere. Revision must rescope these claims to the case and add an explicit limitations discussion covering system-specificity, institutional count, and role coverage.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5, second paragraph "universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment" and "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension between external requirement and internal meaning"
**Confidence**: 5 — the overreach is visible against the stated design and against the manuscript's own Conclusion.

### W5: The title concept is undefined and basic field distinctions are collapsed

"Quality culture" is used throughout as though its referent were settled, yet the manuscript offers no operational definition, no indication of which strand of the definitional debate it adopts, and no account of how the survey instrument's "institutional quality culture score" corresponds to whatever the interviews are said to reveal about culture. The manuscript also treats quality assurance, accreditation, and quality enhancement as interchangeable, a conflation this field does not accept: assurance establishes threshold compliance, accreditation is a status decision by an external body, and enhancement is a developmental activity with different incentives and different failure modes. Because the paper's thesis is a claim about how quality culture forms, the undefined construct weakens the central argument rather than merely the exposition.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: Conceptual framing of the title concept across the Abstract, §1, §2, §4.4 and §5 — expected an operational definition of quality culture tied to the survey measure, plus explicit differentiation of quality assurance, accreditation, and quality enhancement; checked Abstract, §1, §2, §3.4, §4.4, §5, §6
**Confidence**: 5 — definitional discipline in this literature is a matter of direct expertise.

### W6: Interview sample size is reported inconsistently between the Abstract and the Methods

The Abstract reports fourteen administrators interviewed in depth; §3.2 reports twelve, with an explicit n=12. The interview strand is the study's primary component, so the discrepancy is not cosmetic: a reader cannot establish the evidentiary base of the qualitative findings, and any judgement about thematic saturation or about coverage across the three institutions is unavailable until the correct figure and its per-institution distribution are stated. Given the citation problem above, an unreconciled count also compounds the impression of unchecked reporting.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract and §3.2 "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth" and "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 4 — the contradiction is plain on the page; which figure is correct I cannot determine.

### W7: The anonymisation claim is contradicted by identifying descriptors in the Findings

Section 3.5 states that all data were fully anonymised so that no individual could be identified. Section 4.1 then attributes quotations to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and to the associate dean for quality at the named-by-type public research university. In a study of three institutions, a role-plus-institution-plus-superlative descriptor identifies a single post-holder to anyone familiar with the system, and the phrase "in the region" additionally leaks geographic scope that §3.2 withholds. The manuscript therefore both makes a false claim about its own data handling and undermines the confidentiality on which the masking of the national context was presumably justified. Either the descriptors must be removed or the ethics claim must be restated accurately.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 and §4.1 "no individual could be identified in reported findings" and "the quality director of the largest private university in the region"
**Confidence**: 4 — the internal contradiction is certain; re-identification risk depends on the unnamed system's size.

### W8: Balanced-representation claim contradicted by disclosed exclusion of dissenting accounts

Section 4.3 asserts that the study achieved balanced representation and captured the full range of administrator views, and then, in the following sentence, reports that participants with more sceptical views about the QA enterprise were excluded because they fell outside the three-theme structure. Excluding cases that do not fit the analytic frame and then claiming full coverage misstates what the findings represent, and the excluded views are precisely the ones that would test the paper's central argument that administrators recover meaning from ritual. The claim must be withdrawn and the dissenting accounts reported, since they bear directly on whether the negotiated-accomplishment thesis holds.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3, final paragraph "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views" and "these were excluded for space"
**Confidence**: 5 — both statements are explicit and mutually exclusive as written.

### W9: Reference provenance cannot be verified and shows an anomalous DOI pattern

All twelve references carry DOIs in one sequential block under the 10.5555 prefix, which is a reserved test prefix rather than a registered publisher prefix, so none of the identifiers resolves to locatable content. I state this as a verification request, not as a finding of fabrication: I cannot establish from the manuscript whether the works exist. But combined with the Delacroix reversal, the pattern means the editor should not accept the citation base on trust. I would ask for the full records — publisher DOIs, or where unavailable, page images or accessible URLs — for every reference before any substantive assessment of the literature engagement is finalised.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: References "https://doi.org/10.5555/1042001" and "https://doi.org/10.5555/1042012"
**Confidence**: 3 — I am confident about the sequential pattern and the reserved status of the prefix; I have not attempted resolution and draw no conclusion about the sources' existence.

### W10: Delacroix is assigned a gender the citation does not support

Section 2 refers to "his account" for an author given only as "Delacroix, P." Nothing in the manuscript establishes the author's gender, and in a paper whose central citation error concerns this same source, an unwarranted inference about the author is a small but telling indicator of how the source was handled. Use the surname or a neutral formulation.

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2, second paragraph "On his account, an institution can hold elaborate consultations and still lack any genuine ownership of standards"
**Confidence**: 4 — the inference is unsupported by anything in the manuscript or reference list.

### W11: Themes are described as emerging from a "structured protocol," contradicting the stated design

Section 3.1 specifies semi-structured interviews and §3.4 describes iterative inductive coding, but §4.1 attributes the themes to systematic emergence "from the structured protocol" and treats that as grounds for confidence that the pattern is not institution-specific. Inductive emergence and structured elicitation are not interchangeable in this literature, and the substituted term also does the argumentative work of a cross-site robustness claim that the design does not support. Align the terminology with the stated method and drop the inferential claim it carries.

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.1 and §4.1 "semi-structured interviews" and "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol"
**Confidence**: 4 — terminological, and visible against the manuscript's own Methods.
