## Review Body

My remit is narrow: can a determined opponent dismantle this paper's central reasoning using only material the paper itself supplies? The answer is yes, and the dismantling does not require outside evidence. The manuscript contradicts its own literature review, contradicts its own conclusion about scope, and secures its principal empirical claim by removing the data that would test it. Each of these is visible on the page.

The most damaging defect is a straightforward inversion of the paper's key counter-authority. Section 2 sets up Delacroix (2018) as the sceptical voice who "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual" and who argues "*against* treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of a healthy quality culture" — a reading the paper's own reference-list annotation independently confirms, and the reference title ("Against the participation ritual: Why stakeholder consultation is not enough for quality culture") states outright. Section 5 then delivers the paper's central practical recommendation by claiming alignment with Delacroix as an author "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture," concluding that "Consultation, on this reading, is the engine of internalization." This is not a nuance dispute. The paper identifies the participation-versus-performance tension as the thing that "animates the present study," and then resolves that tension by attributing to its sceptic the exact position the sceptic denies. Strip the misattribution and the third discussion finding has no cited support and the animating tension is left unresolved; keep it and the paper contradicts its own Section 2. At least one of the two passages must be abandoned.

The second defect is scope inflation of a kind the paper elsewhere concedes it cannot license. Section 5 asserts that "these findings demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment," that the work "can be read as a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture," and that "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension." The evidentiary base is a purposive sample of twelve (or fourteen — see below) administrators recruited through QA offices at three institutions in one national system, with no sampling frame, no saturation argument, and no cross-site distribution reported. Section 6 then instructs that "Future research should test these dynamics across a wider range of institutional types and national systems" — an admission that the sector-wide claim is precisely what has *not* been tested. "Demonstrate" and "everywhere" are also epistemically wrong for an interpretive design whose stated purpose in Section 1 was to "complicate the tidy managerial narrative." A study that sets out to complicate cannot arrive at a universal.

Third, and structurally the most serious for the empirical claim, the three-theme finding is protected from disconfirmation by a circular exclusion rule. Section 4.3 claims the study "achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views on QA implementation," and in the very next sentence reports that dissenting participants "were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure that organized our analysis." The theme structure is presented in Section 3.4 as inductively derived — coding moved "between transcripts and an emerging set of themes until a stable structure was reached" — yet cases that would destabilise the structure were removed *because* they did not fit it. The premise that the three themes are "dominant" is thereby secured by the conclusion it is supposed to support. The claim in Section 4.2 that a distributed-leadership understanding "recurred across every interview" is not credible alongside the admission that sceptics of "the entire QA enterprise" were dropped. Space is also not a defensible justification: negative cases are shorter to report than the paragraphs spent asserting balance.

A cluster of smaller inconsistencies compounds the impression that the manuscript's claims were not reconciled against its own reported material. The abstract interviews fourteen administrators; Section 3.2 interviews twelve. Section 4.4 says the survey "corroborated the qualitative picture" and then, three sentences later, that the interviews "had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture" — corroboration and disconfirmation cannot both be true of the same comparison, and Section 5 in fact reclassifies the survey as "a note of complication." The subgroup contrast tests n=9 against n=11 from a 48-respondent survey, leaving 28 respondents unaccounted for, names no test statistic or effect size, and is nonetheless used to nominate "institutional type as a possible moderator." Section 4.1 attributes its lead quotation to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and a second to "the associate dean for quality at the public research university," which in a three-institution sample containing exactly one private university identifies both the institution and, given single-holder roles, the individual — flatly contradicting the Section 3.5 undertaking that data were anonymised "so that no individual could be identified." And Section 6's priority claim to "the first comprehensive account" is contradicted by Section 2's own concession that Pettersen (2022) and Rahman (2020) already examine distributed leadership and professional identity among quality staff, work the authors describe as "closest in spirit to the present study."

I score D3 `block` rather than fatal because a recoverable argument exists underneath the damage. A version of this paper that reads Delacroix correctly, states its claims at three-institution scope, restores and analyses the excluded dissenting accounts, reconciles its participant counts, and redacts identifying role descriptions would have a defensible — if more modest — thesis about administrators as meaning-makers at the compliance hinge. That is major revision, not a different paper. The one condition that would convert this to fatal is if the excluded dissenting cases cannot be recovered and re-analysed: absent them, no amount of rewriting can establish that the three themes were dominant rather than selected.
