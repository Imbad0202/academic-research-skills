### D5: writing_and_structure

score: warn
trigger: "Localised clarity or presentation defects that slow but do not defeat comprehension"

The manuscript is well written and conventionally organised; an attentive reader can extract the argument's shape without difficulty, which is why this is not a block under my Phase 1 threshold. What it carries is a cluster of presentation and reporting-hygiene defects: an unresolved contradiction in the reported sample size between Abstract and §3.2, no tables or figures anywhere in a paper that reports a survey mean, standard deviation, and a significance test, an undeclared response-scale range that leaves "moderately positive (M=3.9, SD=0.6)" uninterpretable on its face, a stray editorial annotation left inside the reference list, and a Discussion that announces "three findings" and then delivers two findings plus a recommendation plus a fourth unnumbered paragraph.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution

score: block
block_class: repairable
trigger: "novelty asserted without positioning against prior work"

Scope fit is not in question: administrator experience of QA implementation and the internalisation of external requirements sit squarely inside this journal's remit, and the paper engages work the journal itself has published. The contribution claim is the problem. The Conclusion asserts primacy — "the first comprehensive account" — while §2 cites Pettersen (2022) on distributed leadership in the quality office, Rahman (2020) on identity and vocation among quality professionals, and Silva & Tan (2021) on administrators as quality intermediaries, which between them cover all three of the paper's themes. The manuscript acknowledges Pettersen and Rahman as "closest in spirit to the present study" and then never states what it adds to them. The result is that after the primacy claim is withdrawn, no articulated increment remains anywhere in the manuscript, and the burden of stating one has not been discharged. I classify this as repairable rather than fatal because a genuine increment is available from the existing data — the identity-work analysis in §4.3 is a real analytic move that Rahman's account does not supply — but it must be argued, not asserted.

## Review Body

I read this as the handling editor, so my remit is narrower than the panel's: whether the manuscript belongs here, whether it tells this readership something it does not already know, and whether it is presented to the standard a reader can act on. I do not score design, statistics, or mixed-methods integration, and I have deliberately not attempted to verify the survey inference in §4.4 — the significance claim from n=9 versus n=11, the unnamed test, and the relationship between the survey and the interview strand are for the methodology seat, and I flag only that the survey is doing rhetorical work in §4.4 and §5 that an editor would want independently checked before it is allowed to stand. I also want to record explicitly that I do *not* treat the role-identifying quotations as a copy-editing matter; they read to me as a consent and confidentiality problem that directly contradicts the manuscript's own ethics statement, and I have scored my commentary accordingly (W5) while leaving the ethics dimension itself to the seats that own it.

My substantive editorial judgement is about calibration. A three-site, twelve-participant interview study on how QA administrators hold the compliance/meaning tension open is a publishable contribution to this journal. The manuscript in front of me is not that paper; it is that paper wearing claims it cannot carry — primacy over three cited literatures, comprehensiveness on twelve interviews in one unnamed system, and a sector-level generalisation ("the higher education sector as a whole") drawn from three institutions in a single national system. Editors read overreach as a signal, and the signal here is amplified because the overclaims are contradicted by the manuscript's own text rather than by outside knowledge: the Delacroix inversion in §5 is refuted by §2 and by the reference list annotation four pages later; the sample size in the Abstract is refuted by §3.2; the claim of "balanced representation of perspectives" in §4.3 is refuted by the sentence immediately following it. This pattern of internal self-contradiction is the reason I am blocking D6 rather than warning.

The test I apply to the residue is what a quality director could act on next Monday. Currently: nothing. "Processes that create room for meaning-making" is not guidance, and the single specific recommendation the paper does offer — maximise stakeholder consultation — is derived from a source that argued the opposite. If the authors scale the claim back to three institutions, withdraw or properly qualify the survey inference, reinstate the dissenting accounts, and replace the primacy claim with a stated increment over Pettersen, Rahman, and Silva & Tan, I would expect a modest but real contribution to survive, and I would want the implications section rebuilt from the identity-work finding, which is where the paper's actual novelty lies.

### S1: Literature review draws a genuine analytic tension rather than a citation list

§2 sets Ferreira & Lund and Nakamura against Delacroix and states the resulting tension precisely, giving the study a real question to answer rather than a gap-shaped placeholder. This is the strongest writing in the manuscript and correctly represents Delacroix at this point in the text.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 "This tension — between QA as participatory cultivation and QA as sophisticated performance — animates the present study"
**Confidence**: 5 — I commission and edit literature reviews in this exact subfield.

### S2: The identity-work finding is more than a restatement of QA-as-ritual

§4.3 does not stop at "administrators find meaning despite the ritualism"; it argues that the unresolved gap is itself the location of professional identity. That is an analytic claim the QA-as-burden literature does not already make, and it is where a revised paper's contribution should be anchored.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "they held it open, treating the gap itself as the space in which their professional identity was located"
**Confidence**: 4 — judgement from roughly 200 submissions a year on quality culture.

### S3: Research question is stated explicitly and the architecture follows it

The question posed in §1 is answerable, appropriately scoped to administrator accounts, and the IMRaD structure and prose register conform to this journal's conventions without editorial intervention.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §1 "How do senior university administrators experience and enact QA implementation, and how do their accounts illuminate the formation of institutional quality culture?"
**Confidence**: 5 — venue conventions are the part of this I know best.

### W1: Primacy claim is contradicted by the manuscript's own citations, and no alternative increment is stated

The Conclusion claims a first and comprehensive account. §2 cites three studies covering the paper's three themes — Pettersen (2022) on distributed leadership in the quality office, Rahman (2020) on identity and vocation among quality professionals, Silva & Tan (2021) on administrators as intermediaries — and concedes the first two are "closest in spirit to the present study" without differentiating the present contribution from them. "Comprehensive" is separately unsustainable on twelve participants in three institutions in one unnamed national system. Because the manuscript nowhere states an increment other than the primacy claim, withdrawing that claim currently leaves the contribution unarticulated; this single defect is what makes acceptance impossible in the present form and is what I have bound the D6 block to.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §6 "the first comprehensive account of how university administrators experience and enact QA implementation"
**Confidence**: 5 — this is the judgement my seat exists to make, and the contradicting sources are the paper's own.

### W2: Sector-level generalisation from three institutions in one system

§5 converts an inquiry into three institutions into an account of the sector as a whole, and asserts that administrators "everywhere" face the same tension. Nothing in the design supports transfer beyond the three sites, and the system is not even named, so a reader cannot assess comparability. This requires rewriting the Discussion and Conclusion to site-level claims with transferability argued rather than assumed.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "can be read as a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture"
**Confidence**: 5 — external review panel chairing makes cross-system generalisation claims familiar territory.

### W3: The sole actionable recommendation inverts the source cited to support it

§5 attributes to Delacroix (2018) the recommendation that consultation be treated as the central mechanism for building quality culture. §2 of the same manuscript states, correctly, that Delacroix argues *against* treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of quality culture, and the reference list annotation repeats that reading. The paper's one concrete design implication therefore rests on a reversal of its own cited authority, and the implication must be re-derived or dropped. I note the accuracy judgement itself belongs to the domain seat; my concern is that the practitioner payload of the contribution collapses with it.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture"
**Confidence**: 5 — the contradiction is internal to the manuscript and requires no outside verification.

### W4: Sample size is reported inconsistently and cannot be resolved from the text

The Abstract reports fourteen interviewees; §3.2 reports twelve and labels them n=12. Nothing in the manuscript reconciles the two, so a reader cannot determine how many administrators the three themes rest on. I band this Major rather than Minor not because a number is mistyped but because it is unresolvable from the submission and bears on whether all interview data are accounted for in the reported analysis — a question W6 makes non-trivial.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth" and §3.2 "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct textual comparison.

### W5: Role-identifying descriptors contradict the anonymisation claim

§3.5 states that data were fully anonymised such that no individual could be identified. §4.1 then attributes a quotation to the quality director of the largest private university in the region, and elsewhere to the associate dean for quality at the public research university — descriptors that identify single post-holders to anyone who knows the system, especially given that the sample is described as three named institutional types. This is a confidentiality and consent matter, not a presentation matter: it requires the quotations to be re-attributed at a level the consent actually covers, and the ethics statement corrected. I record it here so the panel does not receive it from me as a copy-editing note.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.1 "As the quality director of the largest private university in the region said" and §3.5 "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified"
**Confidence**: 4 — grounded in institutional practice and national agency review experience rather than the specific ethics protocol, which is not supplied.

### W6: Dissenting accounts were excluded while balance is claimed in the same paragraph

§4.3 asserts that the study captured the full range of administrator views, then states in the next sentence that more sceptical participants were excluded because they fell outside the three-theme structure. Excluding cases for failing to fit the coding frame is the mechanism by which the frame becomes self-confirming, and it directly undercuts the "pervasiveness" language in §5's first finding. The dissenting accounts must be reinstated and the theme structure re-derived with them in view; that is re-analysis, not rewriting.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives" and "these were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure that organized our analysis"
**Confidence**: 5 — the contradiction is stated in adjacent sentences.

### W7: Implications are not actionable for the readership the paper addresses

The Abstract promises implications for how universities design QA processes, and this journal's readership includes practising quality officers who will read that promise literally. What arrives is a call for processes that create room for meaning-making, with no specification of what a quality director would stop doing, start doing, or resource differently. With the Delacroix-derived recommendation withdrawn (W3), nothing operational remains. Delivering the claimed implication requires a rebuilt section grounded in the identity-work finding — for example, what the documentation cycle would look like if the tension administrators hold open were designed for rather than papered over.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §6 "processes that create room for meaning-making rather than merely demanding documentation"
**Confidence**: 5 — practitioner usability is the core of my seat's remit.
