## Review Body

My task on this panel is to reconstruct the evidentiary chain from sampling through to claim and identify the first link that will not bear load. On this manuscript the chain breaks at the first link and does not recover.

The study asks a worthwhile question. Administrators genuinely do occupy the hinge position the introduction describes, the three-site sampling of institutional types is a sensible way to get at it, and a qualitatively driven design with a contextualising survey is a defensible answer to the question posed. None of that is at issue. What is at issue is whether the printed text lets a reader — or a replicating researcher — get from transcripts to the conclusions drawn.

It does not, for three compounding reasons. First, the reported sample is internally contradictory: the abstract and §3.2 give different participant counts, and the survey arithmetic does not close, with only 20 of 48 respondents accounted for anywhere in the results and no response rate or sampling frame stated. Until the denominators are fixed, nothing downstream can be evaluated, because every prevalence and every comparison is computed against an unknown base.

Second, and more seriously, §4.3 removes the disconfirming cases and says so. This is the central methodological defect of the manuscript, and it is not a word-count problem. In any recognisable thematic tradition, cases that sit outside the emerging theme structure are the cases that discipline that structure; negative-case analysis is how a thematic account earns the right to be called a finding rather than a summary of what the analyst was already looking for. Dropping dissenters from a study whose headline claim is that QA is experienced as ritual compliance eliminates precisely the evidence that could have falsified or bounded that claim. The result is not merely under-evidenced; it is structurally biased toward the conclusion drawn. Compounding this, the same paragraph asserts balanced representation and full coverage of administrator views two sentences before disclosing the exclusion — a flat self-contradiction on a load-bearing point.

Third, the quantitative component asserts an inferential result that the printed methods cannot license. No scale is named, no item count or response format is given (M=3.9 against what ceiling?), no reliability or validity evidence appears, and the reader cannot tell whether the outcome is a single item or a composite. Against that background a significance claim is made between groups of 9 and 11 with no test named, no effect size, no assumption checks, and no accounting for multiplicity — and this in a paper whose Methods promised subgroup comparisons by *role*, not by institution. The analysis reported is not the analysis planned, which converts an already fragile contrast into a selective-reporting concern. The proposal of institutional type as a "possible moderator" then asks a design with no capacity to model interaction to speak to interaction. My recommendation is unambiguous: withdraw the inferential claim, report the survey descriptively with full instrument documentation, or drop the survey component.

Two further structural problems are worth naming because they bear on how the argument is warranted rather than merely on how it is documented. §3.3 describes semi-structured interviews; §4.1 attributes the themes to "the structured protocol" and infers that because they emerged systematically they are not an artifact of local circumstance. That inference runs backwards. A common protocol produces common themes by construction; convergence under a shared instrument is the expected consequence of the instrument, not independent corroboration across sites. And for a paper presented as mixed methods, there is no integration strategy at all: no design typology, no point of interface, no joint display, and a §5 that describes the survey as corroborating the qualitative picture while simultaneously reporting that it contradicted expectations. Corroboration and disconfirmation are not the same relation, and the manuscript needs to choose.

On argumentative coherence, my contribution is the inference-validity half. The Discussion's second finding states that the results demonstrate something about universities across the sector, and reads three institutions in one national system as a general account of the sector as a whole. Twelve (or fourteen) purposively recruited administrators at three sites, with dissenters removed, cannot support a sector-level claim of any kind; the conclusion's priority claim to a first comprehensive account compounds it. Separately, §2 and §5 say opposite things about the same cited position on consultation, and that contradiction sits underneath the paper's practical recommendation. Whether the underlying source is also being misread substantively is a question for the domain seat; what I can score from the text alone is that the manuscript asserts a proposition in §5 that it has itself denied in §2, and builds a design recommendation on the §5 version.

I want to be explicit about what would clear these blocks, because both are repairable. Reconcile the participant count and the survey denominator with a stated frame and response rate; reinstate and fully report the dissenting cases with a negative-case analysis and revise the theme structure to whatever survives that reinstatement; specify the analytic approach, coding arrangements, and any audit trail or reflexivity position; document or drop the survey instrument and withdraw the inferential and moderation claims; deidentify the illustrative quotations; and rescope every sector-level statement to the three institutions actually studied. That is a substantial revision, not a rewrite of the study.

### W1: The reported participant count is internally contradictory

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth"; §3.2 "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct textual comparison, no specialist judgement required.

One of these numbers is wrong. The discrepancy is not cosmetic: every prevalence statement in §4 ("across every interview," "several administrators") is computed against a base the reader cannot fix, and the two figures differ by the approximate size of the excluded dissenting subgroup, which raises the question of whether the two numbers reflect pre- and post-exclusion counts. The authors must state the achieved sample, the number approached, and non-participation.

### W2: Disconfirming cases were excluded, and the exclusion contradicts the representativeness claim made two sentences earlier

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views"; §4.3 "these were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure"
**Confidence**: 5 — negative-case analysis and trustworthiness criteria are my published specialism.

These two statements cannot both be true. Beyond the contradiction, the stated rationale inverts the logic of thematic analysis: falling outside the theme structure is the reason to retain a case, not to drop it, because such cases are the only available test of whether the structure is an artifact of the analyst's framing. The paper's headline finding is that QA is experienced as ritual compliance; sceptics of the whole QA enterprise are the participants whose accounts could most sharply have qualified, redirected, or falsified that finding. Their removal makes the qualitative result structurally biased toward the conclusion drawn, and a space constraint is not an admissible reason. Reinstatement and full reporting, with the theme structure revised to accommodate whatever the dissenting accounts show, is required — not a footnote acknowledging the omission.

### W3: The anonymisation commitment is contradicted by identifying descriptors in the findings

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified"; §4.1 "the quality director of the largest private university in the region"
**Confidence**: 4 — human-subjects reporting in small-n elite interview samples.

With one participant per senior role per institution, a descriptor naming the role and uniquely specifying the institution identifies the individual to anyone in the national system, and the same applies to the associate dean for quality at the public research university in the same section. The manuscript therefore breaches, in its own results, the confidentiality assurance recorded in its ethics statement — an assurance participants consented under. As printed the paper cannot be published; the descriptors must be reduced to non-identifying role categories, and the authors should confirm what participants were told about attribution.

### W4: A significance claim is asserted with no test, no effect size, no assumption checks, and no multiplicity control

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "we found a statistically significant difference (p<.05) in perceived quality culture, with the teaching-focused institution scoring higher"; §4.4 "points to institutional type as a possible moderator of quality culture"
**Confidence**: 5 — small-sample inferential reporting standards.

Nothing about this contrast is re-derivable. The test is unnamed, so the reader cannot know whether distributional assumptions were tenable at n=9 and n=11; no effect size or interval is given, so the magnitude is unknown; no correction is reported despite this being one contrast among an unstated number examined; and a bare p<.05 conveys almost nothing at this sample size, where a single respondent can move the result. The moderation language is a further step beyond what the design can supply: moderation is a claim about variance in a relationship, and two institution-level cells of nine and eleven contain no such variance to model. The inferential and moderation claims should be withdrawn and the survey reported descriptively, or the component dropped.

### W5: The survey denominator does not close and no response rate or frame is given

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.2 participants and §4.4 survey findings — expected a stated sampling frame, a denominator reconciliation accounting for all 48 respondents, and a response rate; checked Methods §3.2-§3.3, Findings §4.4, and the Abstract
**Confidence**: 5 — arithmetic reconciliation of reported subsamples.

Forty-eight respondents are recruited; twenty appear in the only reported analysis. The remaining twenty-eight are never mentioned again. Are they the private-university respondents, excluded for some reason, or non-completers? Without a frame (how many staff were eligible at each site), a response rate, and a disposition table, the survey's descriptive statistic is of unknown provenance and its subgroup contrast is of unknown selectivity.

### W6: The reported M=3.9 is uninterpretable because no instrument is specified

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "respondents' overall institutional quality culture score was moderately positive (M=3.9, SD=0.6)"
**Confidence**: 5 — measurement documentation standards.

No scale is named, no item count is given, no response format or range is stated, and the reader cannot tell whether "institutional quality culture" is a single item or a composite. A mean of 3.9 is "moderately positive" only against an assumed five-point ceiling the manuscript never states. There is no reliability evidence (no alpha), no factor structure, and no evidence of validity for the construct in this population. Either the instrument, with its items and psychometrics, is reported in full, or the number should not be reported at all.

### W7: The analysis reported is not the analysis planned

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.4 "subgroup comparisons were examined where role categories permitted"; §4.4 "Comparing staff at the research university (n=9) with staff at the teaching-focused university (n=11)"
**Confidence**: 4 — analysis-plan-to-result tracing.

The Methods promise comparison by role; the Findings deliver comparison by institution, with the private university silently absent from the contrast. The divergence matters not as pedantry but because it indicates that more contrasts were examined than are reported, which is exactly the condition under which an uncorrected p<.05 from a single reported comparison ceases to mean what it appears to mean. The authors should state every comparison conducted and the basis on which the reported one was selected.

### W8: The thematic procedure is specified too thinly to be audited or replicated

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.4 "Interview transcripts were analyzed thematically"; §3.4 "Coding proceeded iteratively, moving between transcripts and an emerging set of themes until a stable structure was reached"
**Confidence**: 5 — reflexive thematic analysis is my primary methodological training.

Four sentences carry the entire analytic warrant. No analytic tradition is named, so the reader cannot know which quality criteria apply — the standards for a codebook approach, a coding-reliability approach, and reflexive thematic analysis are mutually incompatible, and the manuscript invokes none of them. There is no coding framework, no statement of whether coding was single- or multi-coder, no audit trail, no member checking, and no reflexivity statement, the last of which is conspicuous given that the authors are plausibly positioned inside the QA world they are studying. "Until a stable structure was reached" is a description of stopping, not a saturation criterion: it specifies no unit of assessment, no stopping rule, and no evidence that stability was tested rather than declared.

### W9: The Methods describe semi-structured interviews; the Findings reason from a structured protocol, and the inference drawn is invalid either way

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.1 "combining semi-structured interviews with a supplementary staff survey"; §4.1 "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol, giving us confidence that the pattern was not an artifact"
**Confidence**: 5 — instrument-to-inference tracing in interview studies.

The two descriptions of the instrument are inconsistent, and the manuscript should say which is accurate. More consequentially, the inference fails under both readings: a common protocol asked of administrators at three sites produces common themes by construction. Cross-site convergence under a shared instrument is evidence about the instrument, not independent corroboration that the pattern transcends local circumstance — it is the opposite of triangulation, which requires evidence sources that could have disagreed. The confidence claim in §4.1 should be removed.

### W10: The Discussion generalises from three institutions to the sector

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "these findings demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment"; §6 "This paper has offered the first comprehensive account"
**Confidence**: 5 — scope-of-inference assessment is the core of my remit.

A purposive sample of a dozen administrators at three institutions in one national system, minus the excluded dissenters, cannot demonstrate anything about universities across the sector, and "demonstrate" is not a hedge. The sentence immediately following invites the reader to treat the three-institution inquiry as a general account of the sector as a whole and asserts that administrators everywhere face the same tension — a universal claim from a design with no sampling relationship to any population. The conclusion's priority claim to a comprehensive account compounds the same error by asserting coverage the design never sought. Uncorrected, this makes the paper's central Discussion claim false as stated; corrected, the finding is a legitimate and interesting three-site account.

### W11: §2 and §5 attribute opposite positions to the same cited source, and a recommendation rests on the later one

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual"; §5 "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism"
**Confidence**: 4 — internal consistency check across sections; substantive accuracy of the source is outside my seat.

The Literature Review presents this source as arguing against treating consultation as sufficient evidence of quality culture; the Discussion presents it as recommending consultation as the central mechanism and claims alignment with it. These are contradictory, and the contradiction is load-bearing because the paper's practical recommendation — favour processes that maximise participation — is warranted by the §5 version. If the §2 reading is correct, the manuscript's own findings on ritual compliance sit closer to the cited caution than to the recommendation, and the Discussion's third finding needs rebuilding rather than editing. Whether this is also a substantive misreading of the source is for the domain reviewer.

### W12: The survey is described as corroborating the qualitative picture and as contradicting expectations in the same paragraph

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "The survey corroborated the qualitative picture"; §4.4 "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture"
**Confidence**: 5 — mixed-methods integration assessment.

Corroboration and disconfirmation are different inferential relations with different implications, and the manuscript asserts both about the same result within four sentences. §5 then reclassifies the same finding a third way, as "a note of complication." Divergence between strands is a legitimate and often productive mixed-methods outcome, but it must be named as divergence and worked through, not reported as agreement. As written, the reader cannot tell what the survey is supposed to have established.

### W13: No integration strategy is specified for a paper presented as mixed methods

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: mixed-methods integration reporting in Methods §3.1 and §3.4 — expected a named integration design typology, a stated point of interface, and a joint display or equivalent integrative analysis; checked §3.1, §3.4, §4.4, and Discussion §5
**Confidence**: 5 — mixed-methods integration failure is the subject of my doctoral seminar.

"Contextualize" names an aspiration, not a procedure. The manuscript does not state when the strands were brought together (design, methods, or interpretation level), what the point of interface was, or how the two datasets were jointly examined. The survey and the interviews are reported in adjacent but non-communicating subsections, and §5 asserts a relationship between them that no described procedure produced. A joint display mapping themes against survey items would make the integration claim inspectable and is achievable with the existing data.

### W14: No study materials or data affordances are provided

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: study materials and data availability across Methods §3.1-§3.5 and the end matter — expected an interview guide, the survey instrument, a coding frame, and a data availability statement; checked §3.1-§3.5, Findings §4.1-§4.4, and References
**Confidence**: 4 — reproducibility affordances for qualitative and mixed designs.

There is no appendix, no supplementary materials pointer, and no statement about whether anonymised transcripts, the codebook, or the survey items can be obtained. For a qualitative-dominant study, the coding frame and interview guide are the minimum that would let a reader assess whether the themes follow from the questions asked; without them the analysis is unauditable even in principle, independent of the specification problems at W8.

### W15: Prevalence and site attribution are asserted without numbers

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.1 "The image of theater recurred repeatedly across the interviews"; §4.3 "Several administrators framed their commitment to QA in moral and vocational terms"
**Confidence**: 4 — reporting conventions for thematic prevalence.

"Repeatedly," "several," and "across every interview" are unquantified. With a sample this size, exact counts are trivial to supply and materially change how a reader weighs each theme: three participants at one site is a different finding from ten across three. Counts should be given per theme and per site. The claim in §4.2 that a theme recurred across every interview additionally needs restating once the excluded cases are reinstated, since "every" currently refers to a censored set.
