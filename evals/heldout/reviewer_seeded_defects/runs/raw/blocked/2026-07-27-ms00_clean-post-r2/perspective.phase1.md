## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data handling, statistical treatment, and reproducibility supports would survive scrutiny by a competent methodologist in the paper's own field. From my vantage point as the cross-disciplinary reader, I note that methodological soundness is the substrate on which any transferable lesson rests: a reader in an adjacent field can only borrow a finding if the procedure that produced it is legible and defensible. I do not score this dimension; the methodology reviewer owns it, and my paraphrase records only that I understand its scope as covering design choices, measurement, analysis reporting, and whether enough is disclosed for another team to repeat the work.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role).** This dimension concerns whether the manuscript's substantive claims match the current state of evidence in its home discipline, whether prior literature is characterised faithfully rather than strawmanned or over-credited, and whether field-specific terms, constructs, and reported results are used correctly. The domain specialist owns this judgement. I record it here because errors of domain fact tend to propagate outward with amplification: an adjacent-field reader has no local expertise to catch a mis-stated construct and will import it wholesale. I will not assign a score to D2.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, DA role, methodology also eligible).** This dimension tests whether the paper's central thesis holds together on its own terms — whether the stated evidence actually licenses the stated conclusions, whether intermediate steps follow, and whether any reasoning defect is severe enough to undercut the main argument rather than merely blemish a side remark. Ownership sits with the devil's-advocate reviewer. My interest in it is adjacent but distinct: coherence failures often surface first as claims that read as sweeping to outsiders, but the formal adjudication of the argument's validity is not mine to make here, and I will not score it.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, perspective role — mine).** This is the dimension I own. It asks three related things. First, framing: does the manuscript situate its problem so that a competent researcher from a neighbouring discipline can understand what is at stake without first acquiring the sub-field's tacit knowledge? Second, definitions: are constructs, instruments, acronyms, and technical vocabulary defined at first use, or does the text presume insider fluency that a cross-field reader lacks? Third, and most demanding, substantiation of interdisciplinary claims: where the paper reaches beyond its own evidence base to assert relevance for other fields, practice communities, policy audiences, or generalisable theory, are those reaches backed by what was actually measured, or are they rhetorical extensions that outrun the data? The dimension is high priority rather than mandatory, which means a severe deficiency here escalates the editorial outcome but cannot by itself terminate the manuscript. My scoring must therefore be calibrated: I judge accessibility and the warrant behind outward-facing claims, not whether the study is correct, well-designed, or novel — those belong to other seats on the panel.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, EiC role).** This dimension covers manuscript organisation, clarity of exposition, quality and necessity of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's formatting and reporting conventions. The editor-in-chief owns it. It overlaps my territory at the edges — prose that is impenetrable is also inaccessible to outsiders — but the two are separable: a paper can be beautifully written for insiders and still opaque across a disciplinary boundary, and a paper can be structurally untidy yet conceptually portable. I will not score D5, and I will keep my own findings framed in terms of cross-field comprehension rather than general writing quality.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, EiC role).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it advances knowledge in a way that matters to that venue's readership — originality, significance, and appropriateness of scope. The editor-in-chief owns it. I note the boundary carefully, because a paper's reach across fields is not the same question as its fit for one venue; a manuscript may be admirably accessible to outsiders while contributing little that is new, or highly novel while remaining hermetic. I will not score D6.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether the introduction and discussion state the problem, the constructs, and the practical stakes in terms an adjacent-field reader can follow without insider background; whether every specialised term, instrument, model, and acronym is defined or glossed at first use; whether the paper explains why its setting and population were chosen and what features of that setting bound transfer elsewhere; and whether each claim addressed to audiences beyond the home sub-field — other disciplines, practitioners, institutions, or policy — is traceable to evidence the study actually produced and is stated with scope conditions attached.
what_triggers_block: The manuscript asserts substantial value, applicability, or implications for audiences outside its own sub-field while offering no evidence, no stated boundary conditions, and no reasoning that connects those outward claims to what was measured, so that an adjacent-field reader would be actively misled about what transfers.
what_triggers_warn: Core framing is broadly followable but specific constructs, instruments, or technical vocabulary go undefined at first use, or outward-facing implications are stated with insufficient hedging and only partial linkage to the reported evidence, such that a cross-field reader would need external sources to interpret the contribution correctly.
