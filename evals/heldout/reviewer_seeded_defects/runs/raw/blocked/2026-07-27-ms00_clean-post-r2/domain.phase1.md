## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** This dimension asks whether the study's design and execution would survive scrutiny from a competent methodologist in the manuscript's home field: whether the sampling frame, instrumentation, and data collection procedures are described well enough to be judged, whether the statistical treatment matches the design and the measurement level of the variables, whether reporting includes the quantities a reader needs (effect sizes, uncertainty, sample attrition, assumption checks), and whether enough material is available for another researcher to reproduce or at least retrace the analysis. It is a mandatory dimension, and it is owned by the methodology reviewer rather than by me.

**D2 — domain_accuracy.** This is the dimension I own. It asks whether the substantive claims the manuscript makes are consistent with what the field currently knows, whether the literature it cites is characterised correctly rather than bent to fit the argument, and whether the technical vocabulary, constructs, instruments, and reported prior results are used with their accepted meanings. Misattributing a theoretical construct, describing a cited study's findings inaccurately, using a domain term in a way the field would not recognise, or asserting a domain fact that the current evidence base contradicts all fall here. Because it is mandatory, a serious defect on this dimension can by itself drive an adverse editorial outcome.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** This dimension examines whether the paper's central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether the stated evidence actually licenses the stated conclusions, whether intermediate steps follow from one another, and whether any reasoning defect — overreach, equivocation, circularity, unwarranted causal leaps from correlational material — is severe enough to undermine the core argument rather than merely a peripheral passage. It is mandatory, and it is assigned to the devil's-advocate reviewer with methodology as a secondary eligible role; I do not score it.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** This dimension asks whether a reader from a neighbouring field can enter the paper: whether framing, construct definitions, and stated implications are intelligible without insider knowledge, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplinary boundaries is actually backed by evidence rather than asserted by analogy. It is a high-priority dimension owned by the perspective reviewer, so it is outside my scoring remit.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** This dimension covers craft: whether the manuscript is organised so the argument is findable, whether exposition is clear at the sentence and section level, whether figures and tables are legible, correctly labelled, and carry their own weight, and whether the submission observes the target venue's formatting and structural conventions. It carries normal priority and is owned by the editor-in-chief role, not by me.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution.** This dimension judges whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue at all and whether what it adds is both original and consequential for that venue's readership — that is, whether the increment over existing work is real and large enough to warrant the space. It is mandatory but owned by the editor-in-chief role, so I do not produce a score for it.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether every substantive claim about the domain — construct definitions and their theoretical provenance, the characterisation of each cited prior study's design and findings, the use of field-specific instruments and terminology, and any assertion about what the evidence base currently shows — is accurate, correctly attributed, and consistent with established knowledge in the paper's field, including whether the paper's own reported results are described in domain terms that match what was actually measured.
what_triggers_block: One or more central domain claims are demonstrably wrong or a load-bearing citation is materially misrepresented, such that the paper's substantive contribution as stated cannot stand without the authors correcting the underlying domain content.
what_triggers_warn: Localised domain inaccuracies, imprecise or non-standard use of established terminology, incomplete or dated engagement with the relevant literature, or overstated characterisations of prior findings that are correctable through revision without disturbing the paper's core substantive claim.
what_triggers_fatal: Pervasive fabrication or invention of domain content — non-existent citations, invented instruments or constructs presented as established, or reported prior results that do not exist — indicating the manuscript's domain foundation is not recoverable through revision.
