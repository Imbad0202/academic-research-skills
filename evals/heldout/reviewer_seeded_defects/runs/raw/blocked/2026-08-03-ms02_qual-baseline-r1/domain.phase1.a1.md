## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer):** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data collection and handling, analytic procedures, and statistical or qualitative reporting would survive scrutiny by competent peer reviewers in the field, and whether enough procedural detail is disclosed for another researcher to reproduce or audit the work. From my domain-accuracy vantage point I read this as the "was it done properly" axis, distinct from the "is what it says about the field true" axis I own; I will not score it, but I note that methodological weakness and domain misstatement can share a root cause, and I will flag any factual observation that plausibly informs the methodology reviewer's judgement without claiming that dimension's score.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by me as domain reviewer):** This is my dimension. It asks whether the manuscript's substantive claims are consistent with the current state of evidence in higher education policy and quality assurance, whether prior scholarship, frameworks, standards, and agency practices are represented faithfully rather than caricatured or misattributed, and whether field-specific terminology, typologies, regulatory instruments, and reported empirical findings are used correctly. It also covers whether cited results are stated as their sources actually state them, whether contested positions are presented as contested rather than settled, and whether the paper's characterisation of the institutional and regulatory landscape it studies is factually defensible.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the discourse-analysis reviewer):** This dimension concerns internal logic: whether the central thesis holds together, whether the evidence actually offered supports the conclusions actually drawn, and whether reasoning defects such as equivocation, circularity, or unwarranted generalisation compromise the argument's spine. As domain reviewer I recognise the boundary: a claim can be logically well-constructed yet factually wrong about the field, or factually accurate yet inferentially overreaching. I will confine my own scoring to the factual axis and leave the inferential audit to its owner, while making my factual findings legible enough to be reused there.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by the perspective reviewer):** This dimension evaluates whether the framing, key definitions, and stated implications are intelligible and useful to readers in adjacent fields, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplinary boundaries is genuinely substantiated rather than gestured at. From a domain-accuracy standpoint the relevant adjacency is that terms borrowed from organisational theory, management, or public administration must retain their meaning in the source discipline; but judging accessibility and interdisciplinary payoff is not my assignment, and I will not score it.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief role):** This dimension covers organisation, expositional clarity, the quality and informativeness of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's formatting and reporting conventions. I treat presentation defects as within someone else's remit unless a passage is so imprecise that it becomes a factual misstatement about the domain, in which case I will record the substantive error under my own dimension rather than reclassifying it as a writing problem.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role):** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it advances something original and consequential for that readership. My factual assessment may bear on it indirectly, because a contribution claim rests on an accurate account of what is already known; nevertheless the originality and fit determination is the editor's, and I will not assign a score to it, restricting myself to supplying accurate statements about the prior-work landscape that the editor can use.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether claims about quality assurance policy, quality culture, accreditation and evaluation frameworks, and institutional governance match the established evidence base; whether cited prior work, standards documents, and agency practices are attributed and characterised accurately; whether field terminology (e.g., quality culture, quality assurance mechanisms, internal versus external QA, accountability versus improvement orientations) is used with its accepted disciplinary meaning; whether reported findings, figures, and definitions are internally consistent with the sources and context invoked; and whether contested or jurisdiction-specific positions are correctly marked as contested or context-bound rather than asserted as universal fact.
what_triggers_block: One or more substantive domain errors that would materially mislead a specialist reader — for example a core construct defined contrary to its accepted meaning in the literature, prior work misrepresented in a way that inflates the paper's novelty or supports a conclusion the cited source does not support, a factual error about a quality assurance regime or standard that the argument depends on, or unmarked treatment of a genuinely contested claim as settled evidence.
what_triggers_warn: Localised or non-load-bearing inaccuracies that a specialist would want corrected but that do not distort the paper's substantive conclusions — for example imprecise or inconsistent use of a field term, an outdated or thinly evidenced characterisation of current practice, an under-hedged generalisation from one national system to others, or an attribution that is loose rather than wrong.
what_triggers_fatal: Domain misrepresentation so severe that the manuscript's factual foundation collapses and no revision short of a different study could repair it — for example fabricated, non-existent, or systematically misattributed sources; a regulatory or framework description that is invented rather than inaccurate; or a pattern of errors across the paper indicating the author is not working from the actual quality assurance evidence base at all.
