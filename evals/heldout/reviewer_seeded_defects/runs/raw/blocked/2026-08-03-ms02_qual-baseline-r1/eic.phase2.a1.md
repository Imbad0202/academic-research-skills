### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution

score: block
block_class: repairable
trigger: "too thin or too weakly differentiated from established work to warrant publication in this venue at this length"

Topical fit is not in doubt: internal QA translation and quality culture are core business for this readership, and the "administrators as hinge actors" framing in §2 identifies a real lacuna and names the closest prior work honestly. My problem is the size of the delivered increment relative to both the claims and the space requested. Read against the paper's own literature review, each of the three findings maps onto an already-cited publication — ritual compliance to Okonkwo (2018) and Iversen (2021), distributed leadership to Pettersen (2022), identity work to Rahman (2020). The one genuinely new move, the reading of the compliance/meaning gap as the site where administrative identity is located, occupies roughly a paragraph and is never developed. Meanwhile §5 claims a sector-wide general account and §6 claims priority, on twelve interviews in one national system, with dissenting cases removed from the corpus and no pre-2018 foundational literature engaged. I asked myself the question my seat exists to ask: can this be fixed by rescoping the claims? My answer is no. Cutting the claims to the evidence leaves three replicated themes at 2,400 words. Delivering the contribution the paper actually has requires restoring and analysing the excluded cases, stating an operational definition of quality culture, developing the hinge-actor argument, and building the positioning literature — new analytic work, not editing. Hence block. I record it as repairable rather than fatal: the hook is real, the topic is in scope, and the underlying interview material appears capable of supporting a publishable paper.

## Review Body

I am reviewing this as a fit-and-readiness judgement, and I want to be explicit about where my authority stops. On design, coding, and inference I defer to the methodology seat; on the representation of the cited literature I defer to the domain seat; on the internal logic of the argument I defer to the discourse-analysis seat. Several of my findings below touch their territory, because a defect's decision impact does not respect dimension ownership. Where that happens I say whose score should carry it, and I ask the synthesizer not to let my fit-level verdict absorb or soften their granular findings — my configuration flags precisely that risk, and having now read the paper I think the risk is live. The anonymity contradiction and the excluded-dissent passage are, in my judgement, more serious than the framing inflation that first drew my attention.

The editorial situation is unusual. This is a well-written manuscript on a well-chosen problem, with a genuine hook, that fails three separate submission-readiness gates before any substantive review question arises: its references cannot be verified, its stated sample size contradicts itself, and its ethics statement contradicts its own findings text. Any one of those returns the file to the authors from my desk. I would not normally send a manuscript in this condition to referees at all, and I flag that as context for how much weight the panel's substantive findings can bear: they are being made on a text whose factual record is not yet stable.

On the framing question specifically, the gap between claim and evidence is not a stylistic tic. §5 asserts that "these findings demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment," §6 asserts a first comprehensive account, and then the final sentence of §6 concedes that future research "should test these dynamics across a wider range of institutional types and national systems." The manuscript retracts its own generalisation two sentences after making it. A practitioner reader — a quality director deciding whether to change a process — needs to know which of those two registers to trust, and the paper does not say.

On reader value, I pushed hard on the third discussion finding, because that is where a QA journal's readership looks for the payoff. "Processes that maximize genuine participation should be favored" restates the premise the paper imported from Ferreira and Lund (2022) and Nakamura (2020). Worse, the sentence that follows attributes to Delacroix (2018) a recommendation that broad consultation is "the central mechanism" for quality culture, when §2 of this same manuscript, and the annotation on the reference itself, both state that Delacroix argues against exactly that. The paper's only actionable recommendation therefore rests on an inverted reading of the one source that would complicate it. What a practitioner actually needs — how to tell Delacroix's participation ritual from genuine participation, given that both look identical in an evidence portfolio — is the question this paper is positioned to answer and does not.

Finally, on the central construct. "Quality culture," "quality assurance," "institutional culture," and "compliance behaviour" circulate through this manuscript as near-synonyms, and the survey reports a single "institutional quality culture score" with no instrument, no items, and no scale described. For this venue that is a fit problem rather than a mere definitional lapse: the readership's whole professional interest is in the distinction between having QA and having a quality culture, and the paper never operationalises it.

### W1: No cited source can be verified; all twelve DOIs use the reserved test prefix in sequential order

### W3: Dissenting cases were excluded from the analysis while the paper claims to capture the full range of views

This also bears directly on my D6 verdict — it is part of why rescoping the claims cannot repair the contribution, since the evidence base itself must be reconstituted.
