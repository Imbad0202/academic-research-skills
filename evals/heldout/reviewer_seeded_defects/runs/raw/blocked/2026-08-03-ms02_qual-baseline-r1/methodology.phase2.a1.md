## Review Body

This is a QUAL-dominant sequential design on a worthwhile question, and the interview material that does appear is vivid. My difficulty is that almost nothing in the manuscript lets a reader assess how that material became the three-theme structure, and in one place the manuscript tells us that the structure governed which data were retained rather than the reverse. That is the axis on which I have scored.

Taking the qualitative strand first: §3.4 gives four clauses to analysis, of which the operative one is that coding "proceeded iteratively" until "a stable structure was reached." Iteration is not a procedure; it is a description of any coding activity whatsoever. From the text I cannot tell whether analysis was inductive or template-based, whether one person or several coded, how disagreements were reconciled if there were several, whether a codebook exists, or whether any trustworthiness strategy — member checking, audit trail, negative-case analysis, peer debriefing — was employed. For a paper whose primary strand is interviews, this is not a thin patch; it is the absence of the analysis. The manuscript then compounds the gap in §4.1 by offering a warrant that its own methods section contradicts: themes are said to have "emerged systematically from the structured protocol," while §3.1 and §3.3 describe *semi-structured* interviews and no protocol is appended or cited. A reader is asked to accept cross-institutional robustness on the strength of an instrument that the paper both mischaracterises and withholds.

The §4.3 closing paragraph is the most serious problem in the manuscript, and I want to be explicit that I am *not* reading it as a writing slip. Dissenting participants were removed because they "fell outside the three-theme structure that organized our analysis." Under every recognised qualitative standard I know, negative cases are the instrument by which a thematic structure is tested; a structure that determines its own disconfirming evidence is unfalsifiable in principle. The same sentence sits two lines after a claim of "balanced representation… capturing the full range of administrator views," which the exclusion directly falsifies. This is a defect of analysis, not of word count, and it is why I have not treated the manuscript as salvageable purely by fuller reporting. The related prevalence language ("recurred across every interview," "recurred repeatedly") compounds it: with the excluded cases removed, universality claims are partly artefacts of the exclusion, and no coding counts or saturation assessment are offered against which to check them across twelve (or fourteen) interviews.

On the quantitative strand, the reporting is not at a standard at which the numbers can be read. The instrument has no provenance, no item wording, no scale range, no anchors, and no reliability estimate, which leaves the abstract's headline "M=3.9, SD=0.6" uninterpretable — 3.9 on what, out of what? The subgroup claim names no test, reports no test statistic, no degrees of freedom, no effect size and no interval, and rests on n=9 against n=11; on those materials no inferential verdict is defensible, and "statistically significant" should either acquire an apparatus or be deleted. Nor can the survey sample be reconstructed: 9 and 11 leave 28 respondents unaccounted for across the third site, with no sampling frame, recruitment route, or response rate given. Integration then fails in the direction that matters: §4.4 announces that the survey "corroborated the qualitative picture" and, three sentences later, concedes that the interviews "had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture." That is a divergence, and a genuinely interesting one, being relabelled as convergence.

Two consistency problems bear directly on verifiability rather than on prose. The abstract reports fourteen administrators; §3.2 reports twelve, twice, once numerically. And §3.5's guarantee that anonymisation was such that "no individual could be identified" is broken in the next section by descriptors that identify single post-holders at named institution types. I have weighed whether the manuscript reaches my fatal threshold and concluded it does not: ethical clearance is stated as obtained rather than absent, the subgroup arithmetic is unreported rather than impossible, and the sample-size discrepancy is of a kind that correction can resolve. The block is therefore repairable — but repair means re-analysis including the negative cases, not a longer methods paragraph.

On D3, where I am eligible rather than owning: the central thesis about negotiated quality culture is defensible at the scale of three institutions, but §5 does not keep it there. "Universities across the sector," "the higher education sector as a whole," and "administrators everywhere" generalise well past the sampling frame, and §6's "first comprehensive account" is both an unsupported priority claim and, given §4.3, a claim the paper's own analysis contradicts. Separately, the third discussion finding attributes to Delacroix (2018) a recommendation that consultation be treated as "the central mechanism" for quality culture, when §2 and the paper's own reference annotation record Delacroix as arguing precisely against that. A design recommendation is thus resting on a premise the manuscript elsewhere refutes.

### S1: Priority and function of each strand are stated, not left implicit
Many mixed-methods submissions leave the reader to infer which strand is doing the work. This one commits: the qualitative component is declared primary and the survey's role is scoped as contextualising. That declaration is what makes the later integration failure diagnosable rather than merely murky, and it is the right way round for the research question asked.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.1 "The qualitative component was primary" and "the survey served to contextualize the interview findings"

### S2: Site selection follows a defensible variation logic
Sampling three contrasting institutional types is a sensible maximum-variation strategy for a question about how external requirements are internalised, and it is what makes the §4.4 institutional-type observation worth pursuing in a properly powered follow-up.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.2 "one large public research university, one mid-sized public teaching-focused university, and one private university"

### S3: Interview logistics are reported at a usable level of detail
Mode, duration range, recording, and transcription are all specified. This is the one part of the qualitative apparatus a reader can actually evaluate, and it establishes that the corpus is substantial enough to support depth claims if the analysis were reported.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.3 "lasting between 45 and 70 minutes, and were audio-recorded and transcribed"

### S4: A disconfirming quantitative result is reported rather than buried
The authors state plainly that the survey ran against their expectations. That candour is a real methodological virtue and gives the revision an honest foundation to build the integration section on, notwithstanding that the same paragraph then mislabels the result as corroboration.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture"

### S5: An ethics framework is declared
Approval and informed consent are both asserted, which means the governance structure exists and the §4.1 problem is one of reporting discipline within that structure rather than an unapproved study.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 "The study received institutional ethical approval. All participants gave informed consent"

### W1: Disconfirming cases were excluded by the thematic structure they should have tested
Dissenting participants were removed because they did not fit the three themes, in the same paragraph that claims the full range of views was captured. This inverts the logic of thematic analysis: the structure becomes unfalsifiable, every prevalence claim in §4.1–§4.3 is conditioned on an undisclosed exclusion, and the "balanced representation" claim is contradicted by its own next sentence. Uncorrected, the three-theme finding — the paper's core empirical claim — cannot stand, and the fix is re-analysis with the negative cases restored and their bearing on each theme reported, not a note about space.
**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views" and "these were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure"
**Confidence**: 5 — I teach negative-case analysis and reflexive TA trustworthiness criteria at doctoral level

### W2: The qualitative analytic procedure is not reportable from the text
No codebook or coding framework, no statement of whether coding was inductive or template-based, no number of coders, no reconciliation process, and no trustworthiness strategy of any kind. "Iteratively… until a stable structure was reached" describes no decision rule that another analyst could follow or contest. For a QUAL-dominant study this alone makes the findings unverifiable in principle, and it is the reason I could not treat the paper as assessable on its qualitative merits.
**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.4 Analysis — expected a codebook or coding framework, a statement of inductive versus template coding, the number of coders and their reconciliation procedure, and named trustworthiness strategies; checked §3.4, §3.1, §4.1 through §4.3, and the absent appendices
**Confidence**: 5 — standard reporting expectations for thematic analysis in this literature

### W3: Participant descriptors are deductively identifying, contradicting the anonymisation guarantee
Naming "the quality director of the largest private university in the region," and elsewhere the associate dean for quality at the public research university, identifies single post-holders to any reader who knows the system. §3.5 promises that no individual could be identified. Consent was obtained on that basis, so this is a participant-protection failure rather than a stylistic lapse, and it is not publishable as written; redaction of role-plus-institution descriptors is required throughout §4.
**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.1 "the quality director of the largest private university in the region"
**Confidence**: 5 — routine confidentiality assessment for small-n elite interview samples

### W4: The interview sample size is reported inconsistently
The abstract says fourteen administrators were interviewed; §3.2 says twelve, and repeats it as "(n=12)". The reader cannot tell which corpus produced the findings, which in turn makes every "across every interview" claim uncheckable. This needs correction and a confirmation that the corrected N is the one all analysis rests on.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth" and §3.2 "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct textual comparison

### W5: The inferential subgroup claim has no supporting apparatus
"A statistically significant difference (p<.05)" names no test, reports no test statistic or degrees of freedom, no effect size, and no confidence interval, and rests on n=9 versus n=11 with no indication of distributional assumptions or of whether this comparison was one of several examined. On what is reported, the claim is not evaluable and the moderator suggestion is not licensed. Either supply the full analysis — including how many comparisons were run — or present the contrast descriptively without an inferential verdict.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "we found a statistically significant difference (p<.05) in perceived quality culture"
**Confidence**: 5 — I have published on significance testing misuse in small-n institutional survey work

### W6: The survey instrument is entirely undocumented
No provenance or citation, no item wording, no scale range or anchors, no dimensional structure, and no internal-consistency estimate. Consequently the abstract's headline M=3.9 (SD=0.6) cannot be interpreted, and "moderately positive" is an authorial gloss rather than a reading of a defined scale. Instrument reporting or an appendix is a minimum condition for the survey to carry any weight.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.3 Data collection — expected survey instrument provenance, item wording, response scale range and anchors, and an internal consistency estimate for the quality culture score; checked §3.3, §3.4, §4.4, the abstract, and the reference list for an instrument citation
**Confidence**: 5 — standard instrument-reporting requirements

### W7: The survey sample cannot be reconstructed or reconciled
Forty-eight respondents are asserted with no sampling frame, no recruitment route, no eligibility definition, no response rate, and no per-institution breakdown. The two subgroups account for 20, leaving 28 unlocated and no statement of whether the third site contributed at all to the compared analysis. Without this, neither the aggregate mean nor the subgroup contrast can be attributed to a describable population.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.2 Participants and setting — expected the survey sampling frame, recruitment route, response rate, and the per institution breakdown reconciling 9 and 11 against the total of 48; checked §3.2, §3.3, §4.4, and the abstract
**Confidence**: 4 — inference from the reported totals, with the possibility that omitted detail exists

### W8: A genuine divergence between strands is reframed as convergence
§4.4 opens by declaring corroboration and then concedes that the qualitative accounts predicted the opposite. Nothing in the section performs integrative work: no explanation is offered for why administrator and staff readings differ, no joint display, no reconciliation strategy. As written the survey is decorative, and the more interesting finding — a level-dependent divergence in how quality culture is experienced — is discarded by the framing. The revision should name the divergence and analyse it.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "The survey corroborated the qualitative picture"
**Confidence**: 5 — integration is the specific problem I review for at *JMMR*

### W9: Conclusions generalise well beyond the sampling frame
Twelve (or fourteen) administrators at three institutions in one national system cannot license "universities across the sector," "the higher education sector as a whole," "administrators everywhere," or §6's "first comprehensive account." The design supports a contextualised account of three sites; the claims as stated are not recoverable from it, and the priority claim is additionally undermined by the exclusion documented in W1. Rescoping fixes this without loss of interest.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "these findings demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment" and "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct comparison of claim scope against the stated sampling frame

### W10: The design recommendation rests on a source the paper elsewhere reports as arguing the opposite
§5 aligns the participation recommendation with Delacroix (2018) as recommending consultation be treated as "the central mechanism" for quality culture, while §2 and the manuscript's own reference annotation record Delacroix as cautioning that participatory rhetoric becomes a compliance ritual. The third of three discussion findings therefore rests on a premise the paper itself refutes, and the internal contradiction has to be resolved before the recommendation can be assessed.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture"
**Confidence**: 5 — the contradiction is internal to the manuscript and its own annotation

### W11: The stated basis for cross-institutional confidence misdescribes the design
§4.1 attributes systematic emergence to a "structured protocol," but the design is semi-structured by the authors' own account in §3.1 and §3.3, and no protocol appears in the manuscript or an appendix. The sentence functions as the warrant for the theme's robustness across sites, so either the protocol must be supplied and the design description corrected, or the warrant must be withdrawn.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.1 "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct comparison of §4.1 against §3.1 and §3.3

### W12: Prevalence and saturation language is not calibrated to any reported evidence
"Recurred across every interview" and "recurred repeatedly" are checkable claims, but no coding counts, case-by-theme matrix, or saturation assessment is reported, and the excluded dissenting cases make universality statements unsafe. Either report prevalence per case or soften to "commonly" and "in most accounts."
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.2 "This relational, distributed understanding of leadership recurred across every interview"
**Confidence**: 4 — judgement about claim calibration rather than a verifiable error

### W13: No reproducibility affordances of any kind
There is no data, protocol, or codebook availability statement, no appendix, and no indication of where materials could be obtained. Even accepting the confidentiality constraints on transcripts, the interview guide, coding framework, and survey instrument are all shareable, and their absence means nothing in the study can be independently checked or reused.
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: whole manuscript — expected a data, protocol, or codebook availability statement and any appendix supporting independent replication; checked §3 in full, §4.4, §6, and the position where appendices would appear
**Confidence**: 5 — exhaustive check of the submitted text

A closing note on my own disposition. I came to this manuscript inclined to read missing procedure as a write-up failure, on the assumption that the codebook and reconciliation exist offstage. §4.3 removed that assumption: a paragraph that reports excluding data for not fitting the analysis is reporting an analytic decision, not omitting a description. I have scored accordingly, and I would want the revision to answer W1 substantively before the reporting gaps in W2, W6, and W7 are addressed, since restoring the negative cases may change what the themes are.
