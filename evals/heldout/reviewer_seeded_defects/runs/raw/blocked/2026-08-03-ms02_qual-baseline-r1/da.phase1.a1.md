## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** The contract asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical or analytic reporting, and reproducibility affordances would survive scrutiny at the ordinary peer-review bar of its field. Read adversarially, this means the burden is on the manuscript to demonstrate rigour, not on the reader to assume it: an unspecified sampling frame, an unreported analytic procedure, absent instrument details, or claims of pattern without any traceable path from raw material to reported result are all failures of the dimension rather than stylistic omissions. Because it is mandatory, a sufficiently severe defect here is decision-determining, so a challenger should ask what a replicator would actually need and whether the manuscript supplies it.

**D2 — domain_accuracy.** This dimension demands that substantive claims match the current state of domain evidence, that prior work is characterised as it actually is rather than as a convenient foil, and that field-specific terminology and reported results contain no factual errors. The adversarial reading is that misrepresentation can be silent: a term used in a locally invented sense, an over-generalised attribution to a literature, or a stale consensus asserted as current all count. The dimension is mandatory, so accuracy failures are not offset by elegance elsewhere.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** The contract requires that the central thesis hold together internally, that the evidence actually offered be the evidence the claims require, and that no fallacy sit at a load-bearing point in the argument. Approached as a challenge, the question is not whether the paper contains reasonable sentences but whether the chain from premises to conclusion survives a hostile trace: whether stated scope matches concluded scope, whether descriptive findings are quietly converted into causal or prescriptive claims, whether counter-explanations are acknowledged, and whether the conclusion would still stand if its weakest supporting link were removed. This is the dimension I own, and it is mandatory.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** The manuscript must be framed, defined, and interpreted in a way that an intelligent reader from an adjacent field can follow, and any claim that reaches across disciplinary boundaries must be substantiated rather than gestured at. Adversarially, the test is whether jargon is load-bearing without definition and whether borrowed constructs from neighbouring fields are used with their proper commitments intact or merely as rhetorical borrowing. This is a high-priority dimension owned by another role.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** Organisation, clarity of exposition, quality and necessity of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's conventions are assessed here. The sceptical reading is that poor structure often conceals rather than merely inconveniences: sections that do not do the work their headings promise, tables that assert what the text never derives, or missing standard sections can hide substantive gaps. This is a normal-priority dimension owned by the editor role.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution.** The manuscript must belong at the configured venue and must offer something original and significant to that readership rather than restating settled material or reporting an exercise of purely local interest. Read adversarially, novelty claims are the ones most likely to be asserted rather than demonstrated, and the challenge is to ask what specifically is new and against which prior baseline. This is mandatory and owned by the editor role.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: A traceable chain from stated research question through the evidence actually presented to each concluding claim, checking scope alignment (does the conclusion generalise beyond what was studied), inferential type alignment (are descriptive or perceptual data converted into causal, effectiveness, or prescriptive claims), internal consistency across sections (do the abstract, findings, and discussion assert the same thing), treatment of rival explanations and disconfirming evidence, and whether hedged qualifications in one section are silently dropped in another.
what_triggers_block: The central thesis rests on a load-bearing inferential defect — for example the headline conclusion is causal or prescriptive while the evidence supports only description or self-report, or a key claim is contradicted by the paper's own reported material — such that the argument cannot be repaired without new analysis or a substantial retraction of scope.
what_triggers_warn: The core argument survives but contains localised coherence defects — unhedged over-generalisation in the abstract or conclusion relative to the body, an unaddressed obvious rival explanation, circular or definitional support for a secondary claim, or a stated limitation that is not carried through into how the findings are worded — each fixable by rewording, added caveats, or explicit acknowledgement.
what_triggers_fatal: The manuscript has no recoverable argument to evaluate — the thesis is absent, mutually contradictory across its own statements, or the stated conclusions are asserted with no offered supporting evidence of any kind, so that no revision short of rewriting the paper's argumentative core could make the claims follow.
