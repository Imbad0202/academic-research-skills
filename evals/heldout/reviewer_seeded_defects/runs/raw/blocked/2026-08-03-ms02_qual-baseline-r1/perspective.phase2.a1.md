## Review Body

I read this manuscript as an organizational sociologist who works on accountability regimes, and I want to open with what is genuinely valuable here, because my recommendation is not that the paper be abandoned. The empirical object is well chosen, the prose is clean, and one passage in §4.3 is better than most of the published literature on quality professionals. My problem is that the paper describes a phenomenon with a fifty-year theoretical literature, does not name that literature, and then substitutes a claim about *scope* ("the sector as a whole") for the claim about *mechanism* that the data could actually support. That substitution is what drives my block on D4.

**The unnamed theory.** "We produce beautiful evidence portfolios that nobody reads except the panel. It's theater, and everyone knows it's theater" is not an unexplained curiosity. It is a near-canonical statement of ceremonial conformity and means–ends decoupling: formal structure elaborated for an external audience while remaining loosely connected to core technical work. The relevant vocabulary — Meyer and Rowan's institutionalized-myth argument, Bromley and Powell's distinction between policy–practice and means–ends decoupling, Power's audit society, Espeland and Sauder's reactivity work, Hallett's recoupling — is entirely absent, and §5 uses "decouple" as a plain verb rather than as a theoretical claim. This matters practically, not just decoratively. A named mechanism has scope conditions and testable variation: decoupling is predicted to be stronger where external demands are ambiguous and unenforceable, where the evaluating body cannot observe technical work, and where reputational stakes are high but sanctions are weak. Naming it converts three institutions from a weak population sample into a legitimate site for analytic generalization about *when* ceremonial conformity forms. Without it, the paper can only gesture at breadth. The same holds, more briefly, for the other two themes: "distributed leadership" is used as a label with no contact with the leadership-studies tradition (Gronn, Spillane, and Bolden's higher-education review), and "identity work" is used with no contact with the identity-work literature (Sveningsson and Alvesson, Ybema and colleagues), even though §4.3's finding is precisely an identity-work finding.

I flag my own bias: I am the reviewer most likely to over-prescribe theory, and if the target outlet is a practitioner-facing quality-assurance journal, a full neo-institutional apparatus would be the wrong prescription. What I am asking for is minimal — roughly two paragraphs in §2 and a reframing of §5's second finding. The synthesizer should feel free to scale my recommendation to the venue; it should not scale it to zero, because the mechanism claim is where the paper's contribution actually lives.

**Level of analysis and the absent parties.** The administrator-versus-staff divergence in §4.4 is, from an organizational standpoint, the most interesting result in the paper, and it is disposed of as "a divergence worth pursuing in future work." Decoupling theory offers an immediate candidate explanation: the actors who staff the interface with the external body absorb the ceremonial labour, which is exactly why they perceive ceremony, while staff further from the interface are buffered from it and report ordinary working conditions. That is a testable proposition the existing data speak to. Relatedly, §6 concludes that quality culture is negotiated "among administrators, academics, and external bodies" when only one of those three parties was interviewed. Two-thirds of the asserted negotiating field is absent from the dataset.

**Participant protection.** I served on a research-ethics committee that reviewed qualitative work on identifiable professional elites, and §3.5 and §4.1 cannot both stand. Attributing a professionally damaging quotation to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region," in a study whose sampling frame is three named institutional types in one national system, identifies a specific person to any reader inside that system. The same applies to "the associate dean for quality at the public research university." This requires either a revised attribution convention (drop institutional identifiers, or use randomly assigned role labels decoupled from institution) or an explicit statement of what consent covered regarding attributed quotation.

**The inconvenient survey.** §4.4 reports a result that ran against expectation and labels it corroboration in the same breath. That is worth naming plainly, because the honest reading strengthens the paper: staff sentiment being positive while administrators report theatre is precisely what decoupling predicts, and reframing it as a confirmed prediction rather than an awkward footnote would be both more accurate and more interesting.

### S1: The animating tension is set up with genuine analytic bite

§2 does not simply stack citations. It stages a real disagreement between the cultivation position (Ferreira and Lund; Nakamura) and the position that participation itself becomes ritual (Delacroix), and identifies that disagreement as the study's motivating puzzle. An adjacent-field reader can recognize this as a live problem without any quality-assurance background, and it is the correct puzzle to have chosen.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 "This tension — between QA as participatory cultivation and QA as sophisticated performance — animates the present study."

### S2: §4.3's refusal to resolve the tension is the paper's best contribution

The observation that administrators do not resolve the compliance/meaning contradiction but inhabit it, locating professional identity in the gap itself, is a sharp and non-obvious analytic move. It is also the finding that travels furthest outside higher education: the same structure appears among compliance officers, hospital accreditation leads, and audit staff generally. This passage should be the paper's headline, not its third theme.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "they held it open, treating the gap itself as the space in which their professional identity was located"

### S3: The gap statement identifies the right empirical actor

Framing administrators as hinge actors between external requirement and internal practice is a well-motivated site selection, stated in field-neutral language that an organizational or public-administration reader will immediately recognize as an intermediary-role problem.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 "Missing from this body of work is sustained attention to the administrators who occupy the hinge between external requirement and internal practice."

### W1: Sector-wide generalisation asserted from three institutions in one system

§5's second finding escalates from three institutions in a single national system to "universities across the sector" and to "administrators everywhere," with no comparative data, no sampling logic for representativeness, and no argument for why this system's regulatory configuration would hold elsewhere. Nothing in §3.2 supports population-level inference. Decision impact: the paper's stated headline contribution is a claim it did not test, so the Discussion must be rewritten to a mechanism claim with stated scope conditions rather than a population claim; the underlying empirical material survives that rewrite.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 Discussion "these findings demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment" and "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension"
**Confidence**: 5 — assessing whether a claim is sized to its sampling frame is core to my own empirical practice.

### W2: The three-party negotiation claim rests on data from one party

The conclusion's central formulation names administrators, academics, and external bodies as the parties to the negotiation. Only administrators were interviewed; academics were never sampled (the survey covers mid-level staff, whose composition is never specified as academic), and no accreditor, agency, or panel member was studied or documented. Decision impact: the conclusion's core theoretical claim exceeds the data by two of three constituent actors, requiring either rescoping to an administrator-centred claim or new data collection; the interview findings themselves remain intact.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §6 Conclusion "an ongoing negotiation among administrators, academics, and external bodies"
**Confidence**: 5 — the mismatch between asserted actors and sampled actors is directly checkable against §3.2.

### W3: The central mechanism is described but never named or theorized

The ritual-compliance theme is a description of ceremonial conformity and means–ends decoupling, and §2's first strand explicitly invokes audit and accountability framing ("governance technology," "steering at a distance," "the audit university"), yet no organizational-theory source appears anywhere in the paper or its twelve references. Because the mechanism is unnamed, the paper cannot state its scope conditions, cannot predict its variation across institutional types, and cannot say what it adds to an existing account. Decision impact: this is what confines the paper to restatement of a known phenomenon and forces the over-reach in W1; correcting it requires substantive rewriting of §2 and §5, after which the empirical contribution stands on a defensible analytic footing.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §2 Literature Review and §5 Discussion — expected citation of neo-institutional decoupling, ceremonial conformity, and audit-society scholarship that names the mechanism the findings describe; checked all three strands of §2, the first two findings in §5, and the 12-item reference list
**Confidence**: 5 — this is my primary research literature.

### W4: "Quality culture" is the central construct yet never operationally defined, and M=3.9 is uninterpretable

The construct is glossed conceptually in §1 but never operationalized. §3.3 describes only "a short survey," §3.4 says responses were "summarized descriptively," and §4.4 reports M=3.9 (SD=0.6) with no response-scale range, no item count, no instrument provenance, and no reliability information. An adjacent-field reader cannot tell whether 3.9 sits on a five-point or seven-point scale, what "moderately positive" means, or what was measured. Decision impact: a reported quantitative result central to the mixed-methods claim cannot be interpreted at all as printed, requiring the instrument and its psychometrics to be supplied and the survey section rewritten.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.3 and §4.4 — expected the survey instrument, its item count, response-scale range, and provenance for the reported M=3.9; checked §3.3 data collection, §3.4 analysis, §4.4 survey findings, and the abstract
**Confidence**: 4 — survey instrumentation is adjacent to rather than central to my own method, but the reporting minimum is standard across fields.

### W5: Stated anonymization commitment conflicts with role-plus-institution attribution of sensitive quotations

§3.5 commits to full anonymization such that no individual could be identified. §4.1 then attributes "It's theater, and everyone knows it's theater" to the quality director of the largest private university in the region, and a second critical quotation to the associate dean for quality at the public research university; §4.2 uses "one registry head." With twelve post-holders across three distinguishable institutional types, role plus institution is functionally identifying within the system, and the content is career-relevant. Decision impact: an unresolved conflict between a declared ethical commitment and the reporting practice, involving identifiable elites and professionally damaging speech, makes publication in the present form untenable regardless of the paper's other merits; it requires revised attribution conventions or documentation of what consent covered.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 and §4.1 "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified in reported findings" and "the quality director of the largest private university in the region"
**Confidence**: 5 — I have reviewed exactly this problem as a research-ethics committee member.

### W6: Dissenting participants excluded while claiming full-range representation

§4.3 asserts balanced representation capturing the full range of administrator views, then states in the following sentence that more skeptical or dissenting participants were excluded "for space" because they fell outside the three-theme structure. This is disconfirming-case exclusion justified by the analytic structure the cases would have disturbed, paired with an explicit representativeness claim the exclusion falsifies. Decision impact: the thematic result as reported cannot be credited, because the reader has been told that cases inconsistent with it were removed and simultaneously told that none were; no reanalysis of the reported themes can proceed until the excluded material is reinstated and reported.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views on QA implementation" and "these were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure"
**Confidence**: 4 — qualitative analytic standards, judged from the paper's own two adjacent statements.

### W7: Delacroix (2018) is cited in §5 for the opposite of what §2 and the reference list say he argues

§2 and the annotated reference both state that Delacroix argues *against* treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of quality culture, warning that consultation becomes another artifact for the panel. §5's third finding then cites Delacroix as recommending broad consultation as the central mechanism for building quality culture, and calls consultation "the engine of internalization." Decision impact: the paper's principal design recommendation is anchored to an inverted reading of its own cited source, and the correct reading points the other way, so the practical implications section requires substantial rewriting rather than a citation fix.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture" versus §2 "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual"
**Confidence**: 4 — the contradiction is fully verifiable within the manuscript, though I have not consulted the source itself.

### W8: A disconfirming survey result is labelled corroboration

§4.4 opens by stating the survey corroborated the qualitative picture, then concedes that the interviews had led the authors to anticipate a more skeptical picture. A result that contradicts the expectation derived from the primary data is not corroboration. The supporting statistics compound the problem: a significance claim at p<.05 is made without naming a test, reporting a statistic, effect size, or confidence interval, on subgroups of nine and eleven, and the two compared subgroups account for only twenty of forty-eight respondents with the private institution's staff unreported. Decision impact: the mixed-methods integration claim rests on a mischaracterization of what the quantitative strand showed, requiring the survey section and its role in the argument to be rewritten; the honest reframing is available and would strengthen the paper.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "The survey corroborated the qualitative picture" and "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture"
**Confidence**: 4 — reasoning about evidential direction and small-sample subgroup claims is standard, if not my primary technique.

### W9: The administrator/staff divergence, the paper's most informative result, is deferred rather than analyzed

§5 sets the cross-level divergence aside as a matter for future work. Yet the paper's conclusion asserts a cross-level negotiated character for quality culture, and this divergence is the only cross-level evidence collected. A straightforward candidate explanation is available from the mechanism the paper declines to name: interface actors absorb ceremonial labour and therefore perceive ceremony, while those buffered from the interface do not. Decision impact: the central interpretive claim is left resting on one organizational level's accounts while the available cross-level evidence goes unexamined, requiring substantive re-analysis of existing data before the negotiation framing can be sustained.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "a divergence worth pursuing in future work"
**Confidence**: 4 — level-of-analysis discipline is central to my field; the specific explanation I propose is a hypothesis, not a certainty.

### W10: Participant count contradicts itself between abstract and methods

The abstract reports fourteen administrators interviewed; §3.2 reports twelve, stated twice as both a word and a numeral. A reader cannot determine the size of the primary evidence base. Decision impact: an authoritative correction and a re-check of any count-dependent statement are needed, but no core claim changes once the correct figure is stated. If the discrepancy instead reflects post-hoc removal of participants, the consequential impact is carried by W6 rather than by this reporting inconsistency.

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract and §3.2 "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth" versus "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct arithmetic comparison.

### W11: Priority claim contradicted by the paper's own literature review

§6 claims the first comprehensive account of administrator experience of quality-assurance implementation, while §2 cites Pettersen (2022) on distributed leadership in the quality office, Rahman (2020) on identity and vocation among quality professionals, and Silva and Tan (2021) on administrators as quality intermediaries. Decision impact: deleting or qualifying the novelty claim costs the paper nothing substantive, so this is a clarity and accuracy repair rather than a change to any finding; the venue-fit reviewer may weigh the contribution claim more heavily than I do.

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §6 "This paper has offered the first comprehensive account of how university administrators experience and enact QA implementation"
**Confidence**: 4 — based on the manuscript's own citations rather than an independent literature search.

### W12: Method description contradicts itself, and cross-institution recurrence is treated as ruling out local artifact

§4.1 attributes theme emergence to "the structured protocol," while §3.1 and §3.3 describe semi-structured interviews; the two are different instruments with different implications for how themes arise. The same sentence infers from cross-institution recurrence that the pattern is not an artifact of local circumstances, which does not follow when all three institutions sit in one national regulatory system that could itself produce the shared pattern. Decision impact: fixing the terminology and softening the inference improves precision without altering any reported finding.

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.1 "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol" versus §3.1 "semi-structured"
**Confidence**: 4 — interview-method terminology and the shared-environment confound are both familiar territory.
