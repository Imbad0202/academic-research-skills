## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data handling, analytic procedure, and reporting of uncertainty would survive scrutiny from a competent methodologist in the paper's own field, and whether enough procedural detail is exposed that another team could repeat the work. Read from a cross-disciplinary vantage point, the same requirement carries an extra burden: methods borrowed from a neighbouring discipline must be described in terms that a reader trained elsewhere can evaluate rather than merely accept on authority, and the epistemic limits of the chosen design must be stated rather than implied. I do not own this dimension and will not score it, but I note that opacity in method is also an accessibility failure, and where I see it I will route the observation to the methodology reviewer instead of scoring it myself.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role).** This dimension covers factual fidelity to the current state of evidence in the paper's home field: correct use of technical vocabulary, accurate characterisation of prior studies and frameworks, and no misstatement of established results. For an adjacent-field reader, accuracy and intelligibility are entangled, since a term used loosely can read as either an error or a translation choice, and only a domain specialist can adjudicate which. Ownership sits with the domain reviewer; my role is to flag terminology that a neighbouring discipline would reasonably misread and hand it over rather than assign a score.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, devil's-advocate role).** Here the question is whether the central thesis holds together internally, whether the evidence actually bears the weight the argument places on it, and whether any inferential leap or fallacy would collapse the paper's core claim. Cross-disciplinary framing interacts with this: arguments that travel across fields often rest on an unstated bridging premise, and an argument can look coherent inside one disciplinary idiom while the bridge to another field is missing. The devil's-advocate reviewer owns the scoring; my contribution is limited to noting where a bridging premise appears to be assumed rather than argued.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, my dimension).** This is the dimension I own and the only one I will score. It asks whether the manuscript's framing, definitions, and stated implications are usable by readers from adjacent fields, and whether any claim the paper makes about relevance beyond its home discipline is actually substantiated rather than asserted. Concretely, this means: technical constructs are defined at first use in field-neutral language; the problem is motivated in a way an outsider can recognise as a problem; the paper's claimed reach beyond its own literature is backed by cited engagement with that other literature rather than a decorative gesture; and the implications section does not promise transferability that the evidence base cannot support. Both directions of failure matter to me — a paper too insular to be read outside its niche, and a paper that over-claims generality it did not earn.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, editor role).** This dimension concerns organisation, clarity of exposition, quality and legibility of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's conventions. From a cross-disciplinary standpoint, structure is a substantial part of accessibility, because an outside reader depends heavily on signposting, a clear statement of contribution, and self-contained captions to navigate unfamiliar material. Scoring belongs to the editor-in-chief role; I will confine myself to reporting structural issues that specifically impede an outsider's comprehension.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, editor role).** This dimension evaluates whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether its contribution is original and significant enough for that readership. Cross-disciplinary reach and venue fit can pull in opposite directions, since breadth that serves adjacent fields may dilute the specificity a specialist venue expects, and my assessments of accessibility should not be mistaken for a verdict on fit. The editor-in-chief owns this judgement; I will supply evidence about who can read the paper, not a conclusion about whether it belongs.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Field-neutral definitions of core constructs at first use, an introduction that motivates the problem in terms recognisable outside the home field, self-contained figure and table captions, explicit statement of which findings are context-bound versus potentially transferable, and citations to any adjacent literature the paper claims to speak to.
what_triggers_block: Central interdisciplinary or generalisation claims are asserted with no supporting evidence or engagement with the other field's literature, or core constructs are used throughout without any definition, such that an adjacent-field reader cannot determine what was studied or how far the conclusions are meant to reach.
what_triggers_warn: Recurrent undefined jargon, thin or one-sided citation of the adjacent literature the paper invokes, implications stated more broadly than the reported evidence supports, or captions and framing that require home-field background to interpret, while the main claim remains legible overall.
