## Review Body

This manuscript studies something worth studying. The administrator who sits between the review panel and the department is genuinely under-examined empirically, and the paper's own literature review correctly identifies why: Silva and Tan (2021) theorised that intermediary position without grounding it in administrators' accounts. The interview material delivers on that, and §4.3's observation that administrators hold the compliance/meaning gap open rather than resolving it is a real refinement of how the field usually writes about coping. I want that signal preserved through revision, because most of what follows is corrective and it would be easy to lose.

The blocking problem is a source inversion at exactly the point where the paper builds its practical recommendation. §2 characterises Delacroix (2018) accurately and at some length: participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual, and consultation is not sufficient evidence of a healthy quality culture. The reference-list annotation says the same. §5 then reverses him, presenting Delacroix as recommending consultation as *the central mechanism* for building quality culture, and claims alignment with him. Both readings cannot stand. On my reading of the literature the §2 version is the correct one, which means the paper's third Discussion finding — its main design recommendation, carried forward into §6 — currently rests on an inverted source, and the correction runs against the recommendation rather than merely trimming it. Worse for the paper's argumentative arc, Delacroix's actual position is *supported* by this study's own strongest finding. If administrators experience QA as theatre in which artifacts are produced for panels, then consultation is precisely the kind of practice that gets absorbed into the artifact stream. The ritual-compliance theme is evidence for Delacroix, not against him. The paper announces this tension in §2 as the thing that "animates the present study" and then drops it: §5 resolves toward cultivation without ever testing the performativity horn against its own data. Engaging it properly would strengthen the paper substantially, which is why I score this repairable rather than fatal.

I considered fatality and rejected it. My Phase 1 fatal condition requires fabricated or invented sources, an invented regulatory description, or a pattern indicating the author is not working from the real evidence base. That is not what I see. Aoki, Montero, Nakamura, Iversen, Okonkwo, Pettersen, Rahman and Silva and Tan are each described in ways internally consistent with their stated titles and positions, and §2's rendering of the cultivation/performativity fault line is sophisticated. The Delacroix problem reads as a drafting failure between literature review and discussion, not as invention. I note separately (W10) that all twelve DOIs sit on the reserved 10.5555 test prefix and therefore do not resolve; I treat that as a verification obstacle consistent with anonymisation or typesetting, and I explicitly decline to infer fabrication from it, but a specialist reviewer cannot check fidelity to sources that cannot be retrieved, and the editor should require resolvable identifiers.

The second substantive domain problem is that the argument was not built from the field's literature. Every reference dates from 2018–2022, and §2 describes the quality-culture strand as "still emergent." That is factually wrong about the state of the field. The EUA Quality Culture Project ran across the mid-2000s and produced the structural/managerial versus cultural/psychological distinction that any operational definition of quality culture now has to engage; Harvey and Green's multi-dimensional account of quality predates it by a decade; Harvey and Stensaker's typology already sorts institutions by degree of internalisation and group cohesion; Newton documented academics' lived responses to QA, including impression management and ritual compliance, around the turn of the century; and the audit-culture tradition the paper reaches for through Harlow (2019) has its own canonical statements. This is not a bibliography-padding request. Three consequences follow. Quality culture is never operationally defined, so the paper slides between quality culture, quality assurance, compliance behaviour and institutional culture — a slide the EUA distinction exists to prevent. The survey then reports quality culture as a single scalar (M=3.9, SD=0.6, scale range unstated, instrument provenance unstated), which is inconsistent with the multi-dimensional conceptualisation the field settled on. And the three themes each map onto a source the paper itself cites — documentation displacing reflection (Iversen; Okonkwo), distributed leadership in the quality office (Pettersen), identity and vocation among quality professionals (Rahman) — with ritual compliance additionally being Newton's decades-old finding. §6's "first comprehensive account" is therefore not defensible on the paper's own bibliography. The genuinely novel element is narrower and more interesting than the claim made: administrators' accounts of *holding* the tension, and the level-divergence hinted at by the survey. Reformulate the contribution around that.

Two further accuracy failures matter. §5 generalises from three institutions in one unnamed national system to "the higher education sector as a whole," which contradicts the comparative evidence that quality-culture formation is conditioned by regulatory regime and institutional type — and contradicts §6's own call to test across systems. And §4.3 asserts balanced representation and "the full range of administrator views" in the sentence immediately before disclosing that dissenting participants were excluded. Beyond the self-contradiction, the excluded category is the diagnostically important one: resistance and outright rejection are constitutive cells in the field's quality-culture typologies, and removing them pre-shapes the finding that administrators recover meaning from QA. I also flag, for the methodology and editorial seats rather than as claims on my own dimension, the fourteen-versus-twelve participant discrepancy, the unnamed significance test on twenty of forty-eight respondents, the "demonstrate" verb applied to a survey the Methods section calls supplementary, and the identifying descriptor in §4.1 that sits against the §3.5 anonymisation claim.

### S1: The empirical gap is correctly identified and correctly attributed
The paper locates its contribution against a specific, accurately characterised limitation in the nearest prior work rather than against a vague "little is known" gesture, and the follow-on from a conceptual account to administrators' own words is the right move.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 "Silva and Tan (2021) describe this intermediary position as structurally uncomfortable, yet their account remains largely conceptual"

### S2: §2's rendering of the cultivation-versus-performativity fault line is accurate and non-trivial
The literature review states the strongest version of the sceptical position, including the mechanism by which consultation becomes an artifact. A specialist would recognise this as a faithful account of a real disagreement in the field, which makes the §5 reversal all the more repairable — the correct material is already in the manuscript.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 "an institution can hold elaborate consultations and still lack any genuine ownership of standards"

### S3: The identity-work finding refines rather than restates the coping literature
Framing the compliance/meaning gap as the site where professional identity is located, rather than as a tension administrators resolve, is a more precise formulation than the vocational-buffering account the cited work supports, and it is the most promising thing in the findings.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "they held it open, treating the gap itself as the space in which their professional identity was located"

### W1: §5 inverts Delacroix (2018), and the central practical recommendation rests on the inversion
§2 and the reference annotation both state that Delacroix argues *against* treating consultation as evidence of quality culture; §5 presents him as recommending consultation as the central mechanism and claims alignment. This is not a citation slip at the margin — it is the load-bearing citation for the paper's design recommendation and for §6's closing claim. Correcting it does not merely soften the recommendation; Delacroix's actual argument, that participation is co-optable into compliance performance, is directly supported by this study's own ritual-compliance theme, so the paper's data currently argue against its own recommendation. Revision must either defend consultation against the performativity objection using the interview material, or reformulate the recommendation. The §2 tension must be carried through §4 and §5 rather than announced and abandoned.
**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "Here our findings align with Delacroix (2018), who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism" vs §2 "argues *against* treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of a healthy quality culture"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct internal comparison within the manuscript, plus close familiarity with the cultivation/performativity debate this citation stands in for.

### W2: Quality culture is never operationally defined, then reported as a single scalar
The paper's central construct is introduced as "shared values, practices, and commitments" and thereafter used interchangeably with quality assurance, compliance behaviour and institutional culture. §4.4 then reports an "overall institutional quality culture score" as one mean, with no scale range, no instrument source, and no indication of which dimensions the score aggregates. The field's established conceptualisations are explicitly multi-dimensional and distinguish structural/managerial elements from cultural/psychological ones; a unidimensional aggregate is not commensurable with them, and the level-divergence interpretation in §5 cannot be evaluated without knowing what was measured. Supply an operational definition tied to a named framework, state the instrument and scale, and report dimensions rather than a single figure.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "respondents' overall institutional quality culture score was moderately positive (M=3.9, SD=0.6)"
**Confidence**: 4 — construct usage is verifiable in the text; the psychometric adequacy of any replacement instrument is the methodology seat's call.

### W3: The field's constitutive literature is absent, and its maturity is misdescribed
Calling the quality-culture strand "still emergent" misstates a literature that has been active for roughly two decades and that already contains the definitional apparatus this paper lacks: the EUA Quality Culture Project's structural/cultural distinction, Harvey and Green on quality as multi-dimensional, Harvey and Stensaker's quality-culture typology, Newton on academics' lived responses to QA, and the audit-culture tradition invoked through Harlow (2019) without naming its sources. §1's "past two decades" framing similarly understates the institutionalisation of QA. The problem is not a missing citation list; it is that the paper's conceptual scaffolding, novelty claim and theme structure were assembled without the works that would have constrained them. Positioning must be rebuilt, not appended to.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §2 quality-culture strand and reference list — expected engagement with the field's constitutive works (EUA Quality Culture Project; Harvey and Green 1993; Harvey and Stensaker 2008; Newton 2000 and 2002; the audit-culture/audit-society tradition); checked all 12 references, §1, §2 paragraphs 1 to 3, §5
**Confidence**: 5 — this is my primary research literature and I know its chronology and canonical statements directly.

### W4: The novelty claim is contradicted by the paper's own bibliography
Each of the three themes corresponds to a position the literature review already attributes to a cited source: ritual compliance and documentation displacing reflection to Iversen (2021) and Okonkwo (2018), distributed leadership in the quality office to Pettersen (2022), identity and vocation to Rahman (2020). Ritual compliance is additionally a long-established finding in work the paper does not cite. "First comprehensive account" is therefore not sustainable, and §4.1's assertion that systematic emergence from a structured protocol gives confidence the pattern is not local does not bear on originality. The defensible contribution is narrower: administrators' first-person accounts of sustaining the compliance/meaning gap, and the administrator/staff perceptual divergence. Reformulate the claim to what the data add beyond the cited positions.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §6 "This paper has offered the first comprehensive account of how university administrators experience and enact QA implementation"
**Confidence**: 4 — the mapping to cited sources is checkable in the manuscript; the final originality judgement belongs to the editorial seat.

### W5: Sector-wide generalisation from three institutions in one unnamed system
§5 escalates from three cases to "universities across the sector" and to a general account of the sector "as a whole," asserting that administrators everywhere face the same tension. Comparative evidence in this field shows the opposite: the form QA takes, and the conditions under which it is internalised, vary with regulatory regime, review level (institutional versus programme), stakes attached to outcomes, and institutional type — a variation the paper's own §4.4 subgroup contrast gestures at and §6 explicitly concedes by calling for tests across systems and types. The claim should be withdrawn and replaced with a bounded transferability statement naming the regime studied.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "these findings demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment rather than a managerial output" and "can be read as a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture"
**Confidence**: 4 — grounded in comparative QA scholarship; the inferential-validity aspect overlaps the coherence seat's remit.

### W6: Dissenting participants excluded while claiming full-range coverage
§4.3 claims balanced representation and the full range of administrator views, then states that more sceptical or dissenting participants were excluded because they fell outside the three-theme structure. The claim is false on the manuscript's own account, and the exclusion is not evidentially neutral: outright rejection of the QA enterprise is a distinct position in the field's quality-culture typologies and one of the diagnostic categories for judging whether internalisation has occurred. Removing it makes the finding that administrators recover meaning from ritual partly an artifact of case selection at the analytic stage. Either report the dissenting accounts as a fourth position or state plainly that the analysis characterises a subset and revise the interpretive claims accordingly.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views" and "these were excluded for space"
**Confidence**: 4 — the contradiction and its substantive consequence are visible in the text; sampling adequacy itself is the methodology seat's judgement.

### W7: Participant count reported inconsistently (fourteen versus twelve)
The abstract reports fourteen administrators interviewed; §3.2 reports twelve, with "(n=12)" restated parenthetically. A reader cannot tell which figure is correct or whether two interviews were excluded, and the discrepancy sits next to the §4.3 exclusion disclosure in a way that invites the inference that dropped cases were never reconciled with the abstract. Correct the count and, if cases were excluded, say so and where.
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth" and §3.2 "Twelve senior administrators (n=12)"
**Confidence**: 5 — a direct textual comparison requiring no domain judgement; I note the methodology seat may weigh a sample-size discrepancy more heavily than I do.

### W8: The national QA regime is never identified, so the described machinery cannot be checked
The study reports "three universities in a single national system" without naming the system or its review framework. Evidence portfolios, panels, cyclical review and action plans mean materially different things across regimes, and a domain reviewer cannot assess whether the paper's account of QA requirements is accurate without knowing which regime generates them. Anonymity does not require silence here: naming the regime type and review framework would be sufficient and is standard practice.
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.2 setting description — expected identification of the national QA regime and its review framework at the level of regime type (for example an ESG-aligned register-listed system, a US regional accreditor, or an Australian-style national regulator); checked §1, §2, §3.1 to §3.3, §5, §6
**Confidence**: 3 — the omission is certain; how much anonymity the venue permits to be relaxed is an editorial matter.

### W9: The anonymisation claim is contradicted by an identifying descriptor
§3.5 states that all data were fully anonymised so that no individual could be identified; §4.1 then attributes a quotation to the quality director of the largest private university in the region. Given a three-institution design including one private university, that descriptor identifies both institution and postholder. The statement in §3.5 is therefore inaccurate as written. Redact the descriptor to a role-level identifier and restate the anonymisation claim to what the reporting actually achieves.
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified" vs §4.1 "the quality director of the largest private university in the region"
**Confidence**: 4 — the contradiction is plain; consent and ethics-compliance implications belong to the methodology seat, which may band this higher.

### W10: All twelve DOIs use the reserved test prefix and do not resolve
Every reference carries a DOI on the 10.5555 prefix, which is reserved for testing and resolves to nothing. I take this as a production or anonymisation artifact rather than evidence about the sources themselves, and I have deliberately not treated it as grounds for a fatality finding. It does, however, prevent any reviewer from verifying the source fidelity that W1 makes central. Supply resolvable identifiers before the fidelity of the Delacroix, Iversen, Okonkwo, Pettersen and Rahman characterisations can be independently confirmed.
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: References "https://doi.org/10.5555/1042003"
**Confidence**: 3 — the prefix status is a matter of fact; the reason for its use here is not determinable from the manuscript.
