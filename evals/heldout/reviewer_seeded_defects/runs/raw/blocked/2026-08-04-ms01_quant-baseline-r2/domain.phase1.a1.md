## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design and execution would survive scrutiny from a competent peer reviewer in the manuscript's own field: is the design appropriate to the question, is the data provenance and handling described well enough to be believed, are statistical analyses correctly specified and completely reported (effect sizes, uncertainty, assumptions, missing data), and could another researcher plausibly reproduce the work from what is written. It is owned by the methodology reviewer, not by me.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role).** This is my dimension. It asks whether every substantive claim in the manuscript is consistent with what the field currently knows and accepts: whether technical terms are used with their established meanings, whether reported quantities and constructs are named and interpreted correctly, whether cited prior work is characterised faithfully rather than stretched or misattributed, and whether the paper's positioning against existing literature reflects the actual state of evidence. Errors here include misdefined domain constructs, claims that contradict well-established findings without acknowledgment or argument, misreported results from cited sources, and overreach beyond what the domain evidence base supports.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, DA role with methodology eligibility).** This concerns whether the central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether the stated conclusions actually follow from the evidence presented, whether there are internal contradictions between sections, and whether any reasoning defects (equivocation, causal claims from correlational designs, selection effects treated as findings) are severe enough to undermine the paper's core argument rather than merely a peripheral point. Owned by the devil's-advocate reviewer.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, perspective role).** This asks whether a reader from an adjacent discipline can enter the paper: are key terms defined rather than assumed, is the framing legible outside the authors' immediate subfield, and where the paper reaches across disciplinary boundaries, are those interdisciplinary claims actually supported rather than gestured at. Owned by the perspective reviewer.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, EiC role).** This covers craft rather than substance: whether the manuscript is organised so the argument is easy to follow, whether exposition is clear, whether figures and tables are legible, correctly labelled, and carry their own weight, and whether the submission observes the target venue's formatting and structural conventions. Owned by the editor-in-chief reviewer.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, EiC role).** This asks whether this paper belongs in this venue and whether it advances knowledge enough to warrant publication for that readership: originality relative to existing work, significance of the increment, and topical match to the venue's declared scope. Owned by the editor-in-chief reviewer.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Correct and conventional use of field-specific terminology and constructs; accurate characterisation of every cited prior study's design, population, and finding; reported outcome measures and metrics defined consistently with established field usage; claims about the state of the evidence base that match what the literature actually shows; conclusions whose scope stays within what the cited domain evidence can support; and no substitution of a superficially similar construct or measure for the one the paper claims to be discussing.
what_triggers_block: A load-bearing domain error that the paper's conclusions depend on — for example, a central construct or metric defined or operationalised incorrectly for the field, a cited work whose findings are materially misrepresented in support of a key claim, or a headline conclusion that contradicts well-established domain evidence with no acknowledgment or countervailing argument.
what_triggers_warn: Domain inaccuracies that are real but non-load-bearing — imprecise or non-standard terminology that does not change the analysis, an incompletely or one-sidedly characterised literature base, overstated generalisation beyond the studied setting or population, or citation of prior work that is dated or only loosely relevant to the claim it is attached to.
what_triggers_fatal: Pervasive domain unreliability such that the manuscript's factual base cannot be trusted at all — e.g. fabricated, non-existent, or systematically misattributed references; reported figures or results from cited sources that do not exist as characterised; or a fundamental misunderstanding of the domain that invalidates the research question itself, so that no revision short of a new study could repair it.
