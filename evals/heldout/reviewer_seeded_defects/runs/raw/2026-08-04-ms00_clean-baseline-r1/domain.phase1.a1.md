## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design and execution would survive scrutiny from a competent methodologist in the paper's own field: whether the sampling frame, instrument, data-handling steps, and statistical reporting are described completely enough that a reader could judge their validity, and whether enough material (instrument items, analysis specification, data availability) is exposed for another team to reproduce the work. From my vantage point as the domain-accuracy reviewer, this is not mine to score, but I note that a domain claim can only be as trustworthy as the procedure that generated it, so my own findings may reference procedural facts without adjudicating them.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role — mine).** This is the dimension I own. It asks whether every substantive assertion the manuscript makes about its subject matter is consistent with what the field's evidence base currently supports; whether the constructs, instruments, models, and technical vocabulary of the field are used with their accepted meanings rather than loosely or idiosyncratically; whether cited prior work is characterised the way those sources actually stand (correct findings, correct scope, correct direction of effect, correct attribution); and whether the numbers, effect descriptions, and interpretive statements reported inside the paper are internally correct and correctly labelled. It also covers the sin of omission that is specific to domain review: a literature framing that presents the field as settled or as a gap when the established evidence says otherwise, or that ignores a body of directly contradicting results.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, da/methodology roles).** This dimension concerns whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether the stated conclusions actually follow from the evidence presented, whether inferential leaps (correlation to causation, sample to population, measured proxy to target construct) are licensed or merely asserted, and whether the paper contains internal contradictions or reasoning defects severe enough to undermine the main claim. I do not score this, though domain errors and argumentative defects frequently co-occur.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high, perspective role).** This dimension asks whether a reader from a neighbouring discipline can follow the framing, whether field-specific terms are defined at first use, and whether any claim reaching beyond the paper's home discipline is actually backed by evidence rather than gestured at. It is scored by the perspective reviewer, not by me.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal, eic role).** This dimension covers manuscript craft: section organisation and flow, clarity of exposition, whether figures and tables are legible, correctly labelled, and self-contained, and whether the submission observes the target venue's formatting and reporting conventions. It belongs to the editor-in-chief role.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, eic role).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue at all and whether it advances the field enough to warrant the space — originality relative to what is already published, and significance calibrated to the readership's expectations. It is scored by the editor-in-chief, though my assessment of how the paper represents prior work may inform that judgement.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether field-standard constructs, instruments, and technical terms are used with their accepted definitions; whether every citation is characterised in a way that matches what the cited source actually reports (finding, direction, scope, population); whether reported statistics, measures, and interpretive labels inside the paper are correct and consistently applied; whether the literature framing represents the current evidence base fairly, including directly contradicting or superseding findings; and whether claimed novelty or claimed gaps are accurate against what the field has already established.
what_triggers_block: A substantive domain claim that contradicts well-established evidence in the field with no acknowledgement or defence, a core construct or instrument used with a definition materially different from its accepted meaning in ways that change what the results mean, a cited source materially misrepresented on a point the argument depends on, or an internally incorrect reported result that alters the paper's substantive conclusion.
what_triggers_warn: Localised terminological imprecision, an outdated or incomplete citation for a claim that is nonetheless correct, an overstated novelty or gap claim that the literature partly contradicts, or minor inconsistencies in reported figures or labels that do not change the substantive conclusion.
what_triggers_fatal: Pervasive domain error such that the paper's central subject-matter premise is false as stated in the field, fabricated or non-existent sources supporting load-bearing claims, or systematic misrepresentation of prior work such that no revision short of a different paper could repair the evidence base.
