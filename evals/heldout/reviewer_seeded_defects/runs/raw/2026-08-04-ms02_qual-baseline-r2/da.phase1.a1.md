## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** The contract asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical or analytic reporting, and reproducibility affordances would survive the ordinary peer-review bar of its field. Read adversarially, this dimension is a demand that the manuscript expose its machinery rather than assert it: who was sampled and why, how data were collected and coded, what was measured, what was discarded, and whether an independent reader could in principle repeat the procedure and arrive at comparable output. A challenger's posture here is to assume the reported method is the most flattering possible account of what actually happened, and to ask what the manuscript would look like if the omitted details were unflattering. Ownership of this dimension sits with the methodology reviewer, so my job is not to score it but to note where argumentative weaknesses I find are in fact symptoms of a method gap that the owning reviewer should adjudicate.

**D2 — domain_accuracy.** This dimension requires that the substantive claims track the current state of evidence in the field, that prior work is represented as its authors would recognise it, and that domain terminology and reported results contain no factual errors. The adversarial reading is that citation is cheap and misattribution is easy: a paper can be internally tidy while quietly mischaracterising the literature it leans on, borrowing a term of art with a settled technical meaning and using it loosely, or presenting a contested finding as consensus. Because the domain reviewer owns this dimension, my role is to flag places where an argument's load-bearing premise is an empirical or definitional claim about the domain that I cannot verify, and hand that premise over for verification rather than score it myself.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** This is the dimension I own. It asks whether the central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether premises actually entail the conclusions drawn, whether the evidence presented is the kind of evidence that could bear on the claim being made, and whether any reasoning defects are severe enough to undermine the paper's core argument rather than merely decorate its margins. Adversarially, I treat the manuscript as a defendant: I will look for conclusions that outrun their evidence, scope creep between what was studied and what is claimed, causal language resting on non-causal design, circularity where a construct is defined by the very outcome it is said to explain, selective engagement with disconfirming possibilities, equivocation on key terms between sections, and abstract/discussion claims that the results section never earned. The distinction I must hold carefully is between a *fixable* overstatement and a *structural* incoherence: the former is a revision instruction, the latter means the paper's thesis cannot be rescued without a different study. I must also resist the temptation to convert stylistic thinness or my own disagreement with the conclusion into a coherence finding, since that would inflate severity and corrupt the panel's aggregation.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** The contract requires that framing, definitions, and implications be intelligible to readers in adjacent fields, and that any interdisciplinary claim be substantiated rather than gestured at. Read adversarially, this is a test of whether the paper's reach is earned: borrowing vocabulary or authority from a neighbouring discipline while ignoring that discipline's evidentiary standards is a common failure. The perspective reviewer owns this; where I find that an argument only appears coherent because a key term is left undefined and thereby elastic, that is simultaneously a coherence problem for me and an accessibility problem for them, and I should say so without scoring their dimension.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** This dimension covers organisation, clarity of exposition, quality of figures and tables, and conformity with venue conventions. The adversarial concern is that prose quality can mask or manufacture argument quality in both directions: fluent writing can smuggle a non-sequitur past a reader, and disorganised writing can make a sound argument look broken. The EiC owns this, and the discipline required of me is to keep my own findings on the argument side of that line — I should not downgrade coherence because a paper is badly signposted, nor forgive an invalid inference because it is elegantly phrased.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution.** The manuscript must belong at the configured venue and must offer something original and significant to that readership. Adversarially, this asks whether the paper adds knowledge or merely restates what practitioners in the area already assume, and whether its stated contribution is the contribution actually delivered. Contribution claims are argument claims in disguise, so my coherence findings are relevant evidence for the EiC who owns this dimension: if the discussion's novelty claim is not entailed by the results, that is both an inference defect I score and a contribution question I should surface for them.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether the stated thesis is traceable through explicit premises to conclusions — checking that each headline claim in abstract, discussion, and conclusion is entailed by evidence actually presented in the body; that key constructs keep one stable definition throughout; that causal, generalising, or prescriptive language matches what the design and data can support; that plausible alternative explanations and disconfirming cases are engaged rather than ignored; and that no load-bearing step is circular, question-begging, or supported only by appeal to authority or restatement.
what_triggers_block: A load-bearing inference is invalid or unsupported such that the paper's central thesis does not follow from its own evidence — for example the main conclusion asserts a causal or generalisable relationship the presented material cannot establish, a core construct is defined in terms of the outcome it is invoked to explain, or the thesis contradicts a finding reported elsewhere in the same manuscript — and no re-scoping short of new evidence repairs it.
what_triggers_warn: The central thesis survives scrutiny but peripheral reasoning is defective in ways fixable by revision — overstated hedging-free claims in abstract or conclusion that the body qualifies, one or two unengaged alternative explanations, a term used loosely in one section but precisely elsewhere, or evidence cited for a claim slightly broader than it supports.
what_triggers_fatal: The manuscript's argumentative structure is irreparable within the current study design — the thesis is self-contradictory or unfalsifiable as stated, the reported evidence points against the conclusion drawn, or the argument rests on a premise the paper itself demonstrates to be false — so that no revision of the existing material could yield a coherent argument.
