### D5: writing_and_structure
score: block
trigger: "supporting evidence cannot be reliably reconstructed on a careful read"

The manuscript reads fluently and its sectioning is conventional, which is why my Phase 1 warn trigger was my first instinct. I am not applying it, because the warn trigger ends with "that a revision round could fix without changing the substance," and none of the four defects that drive this score can be fixed without changing the substance. The reported sample size contradicts itself between Abstract and §3.2 (fourteen versus twelve); the instrument contradicts itself between §3.1 ("semi-structured") and §4.1 ("the structured protocol"); the ethics statement in §3.5 contradicts the participant description in §4.1; and the entire reference apparatus carries reserved-prefix DOIs, so the literature the argument stands on cannot be checked at all. Taken together I cannot reconstruct from this text how many people were interviewed, what they were asked, which cases were dropped, or what was actually cited. There are also no exhibits of any kind — no participant table, no protocol, no coding examples, no survey instrument, no table for the survey figures — and no limitations section. This is a presentation failure of record-reliability, not of style.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution
score: block
block_class: repairable
trigger: "The contribution as delivered is substantially thinner than claimed"

Scope fit is not in question: internal QA implementation and quality culture sit at the centre of this venue's remit, and the paper even cites the journal's own back catalogue. The contribution is the problem. §6 claims "the first comprehensive account" of administrator experience of QA implementation, and each of the three "dominant themes" is pre-named in the manuscript's own bibliography — ritual compliance in Okonkwo (2018) and Montero (2021), distributed leadership in the quality office in Pettersen (2022), identity and vocation among quality professionals in Rahman (2020). The genuinely defensible increment is narrower and is stated correctly in §2: a grounded empirical extension of Silva and Tan's (2021) conceptual account of the intermediary position, in administrators' own words. That smaller claim would clear this journal's contribution bar. It is not delivered here at a standard the readership can use, because the national system is unnamed, no QA regime, agency or review cycle is described, no fieldwork period is given, the evidence base is reported in on the order of 2,600 words with two or three quotations per theme, dissenting cases are disclosed as excluded, and §5 inflates three anonymous institutions into "the higher education sector as a whole." This is not fatal — the topic is squarely in scope and there is new interview material — so I bind it to the block trigger, not to my Phase 1 fatal trigger, which requires out-of-scope framing or no identifiable original contribution.

## Review Body

I read this as the journal-fit seat: what the paper adds to a corpus this journal has already published, whether an international readership can situate it, and whether the empirical base and its reporting meet the venue's standards. I do not score methodology, domain accuracy, argumentative coherence, or cross-disciplinary reach, and where my observations touch those dimensions I flag them for the owning seats rather than pressing a verdict.

On fit and novelty, my judgment is that the increment claimed in §6 and the increment delivered in §4 are different papers. The three themes are competently rendered but they are the three themes this literature already has names for, and the manuscript supplies those names itself in §2. What is new is the direct administrator voice on the hinge position that Silva and Tan (2021) theorised — a real contribution, and one this journal would want, provided the authors claim it accurately and evidence it properly. The Conclusion's "first comprehensive account" is not defensible against the paper's own reference list and should be replaced by the narrower claim. I would also ask the authors to reconsider §5's third finding, which builds its central design recommendation on Delacroix (2018) as though Delacroix endorsed stakeholder consultation as the engine of quality culture. §2 characterises Delacroix correctly as arguing the opposite, and the reference list's own annotation confirms it. Whatever the cause, the practical implication that the paper advances for the field currently rests on an inverted source.

On international readability, the anonymisation has been taken past the point of diminishing returns. §3.2 gives "three universities in a single national system" and nothing more: no regulatory regime, no agency, no review cycle length, no dates. Comparative QA research travels because readers can map a regime onto their own; here they cannot. Nothing in the findings requires the system to be unnamed — the institutions can stay anonymous while the system, the regime, and the fieldwork window are named. Meanwhile the anonymisation that does matter has failed in the other direction: §4.1 identifies a participant as the quality director of the largest private university in the region, which, with §3.2's three institution types, is close to a name, and directly contradicts §3.5's assurance that no individual could be identified.

On empirical sufficiency at this length: this is a Major Revision candidate rather than a below-threshold submission, and I want to be specific about why. The interview material is genuinely good — the quotations do analytic work rather than decorate — and a defensible paper is recoverable. But the reporting is currently below what this journal expects of a mixed-methods study: no protocol, no participant table, no coding evidence, no survey instrument, no limitations section, an unresolved discrepancy in the number of interviews, and a survey subgroup contrast that accounts for 20 of 48 respondents without naming the test, the group means, or an effect size. The methodology seat should judge the technical adequacy of that last item; my point is narrower and editorial — as reported, it cannot be evaluated by anyone.

The paragraph closing §4.3 is the most serious thing in the manuscript from any seat's point of view. It asserts balanced representation and the full range of administrator views, and in the next sentence discloses that dissenting participants were excluded because they fell outside the three-theme structure. That is theme-driven exclusion of disconfirming cases, stated by the authors. It cannot be repaired by rewriting the sentence; it requires the excluded accounts to be reintegrated and the theme structure to be re-derived against them.

Finally, two matters I will not let pass as production issues. First, the construct: "quality culture" is used as an interview outcome, as a survey score, and as a negotiated process, and is never defined or operationalised anywhere, nor distinguished from quality assurance, accreditation, or audit. For this readership that conflation is not a wording preference. Second, every reference carries a DOI on the 10.5555 reserved test prefix in a sequential block (1042001–1042012), and one entry still contains an inline editorial annotation. I am aware that a reviewer in my seat is tempted to send that to copy-editing. I am not doing so. Until the authors supply verifiable identifiers, no editor can confirm that the corpus this paper positions itself against exists as described, and the resolution of that query could be disqualifying rather than corrective. I would hold any recommendation on this manuscript until the reference list is verified, independently of the dimension scores above.
