## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data collection, analytic procedures, and statistical or qualitative reporting would survive scrutiny by a competent peer reviewer in the field, and whether enough procedural detail is supplied that another researcher could in principle repeat the work. From a domain-accuracy standpoint I note that rigor and factual correctness are separable: a study can use impeccable procedures while still misdescribing the substantive field, or vice versa. Scoring this dimension is not my responsibility under this contract, and I will not assign it a score.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by me as the domain reviewer).** This is my dimension. It asks whether the manuscript's substantive claims are consistent with the current state of evidence in higher education policy and quality assurance; whether prior literature, frameworks, standards, and agencies are represented as they actually are rather than as caricature or paraphrase-drift; and whether field-specific terminology, regulatory constructs, and reported results are used with technical correctness. In this field that means, concretely, correct handling of terms of art (quality culture, quality assurance versus quality enhancement, internal versus external QA, accreditation versus audit versus evaluation), correct attribution of frameworks and standards to their actual sources and scopes, correct characterisation of national or supranational QA regimes and their jurisdictional limits, and no assertion of an empirical regularity that the cited evidence base does not support. It also covers whether generalisations drawn from one system are illegitimately transported to another without acknowledging institutional and governance differences.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the devil's-advocate reviewer).** This concerns whether the central thesis holds together internally, whether the evidence presented actually bears on the claims it is recruited to support, and whether inferential moves are free of fallacies severe enough to undermine the argument. It overlaps with my dimension where a domain misstatement is load-bearing for the argument, but the primary judgement of logical structure belongs to the DA and methodology roles. I will not score it.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by the perspective reviewer).** This asks whether framing, definitions, and stated implications are intelligible and useful to readers from adjacent fields, and whether any interdisciplinary claims the paper makes are actually substantiated rather than gestured at. It is an accessibility-and-reach judgement rather than a correctness judgement, and it is outside my dispatch role.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief).** This covers organisation, expositional clarity, quality and legibility of figures and tables, and conformity with the target venue's formatting and reporting conventions. Presentation defects are distinct from factual defects; a clearly written manuscript can still be domain-inaccurate. Not mine to score.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief).** This asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it makes an original and significant contribution for that readership. Originality assessment leans on knowing what already exists in the literature, which touches my remit, but the contract assigns the scoring decision to the EiC and I will respect that boundary.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether QA-specific terms of art are used with their established technical meanings; whether named frameworks, standards, agencies, and national or regional QA regimes are attributed to the correct source, scope, and jurisdiction; whether cited prior work is characterised as its authors actually argued rather than drifted or straw-manned; whether reported empirical claims about quality culture, administrator behaviour, or implementation outcomes are consistent with the current published evidence base; and whether findings from one governance context are transported to another with explicit acknowledgement of system differences.
what_triggers_block: A load-bearing factual error in the domain — for example a named QA framework, standard, or agency described with the wrong scope, mandate, or content; a cited study's findings reversed or materially misrepresented; or a central substantive claim asserted as established when the field's evidence does not support it — such that a domain-competent reader would consider a principal conclusion unsound until corrected.
what_triggers_warn: Localised inaccuracies or imprecision that do not overturn a principal conclusion — for example loose or inconsistent use of a term of art, an outdated characterisation of a standard superseded by a later revision, an overreaching generalisation across national systems stated without qualification, or a citation used to support a claim broader than the source establishes.
what_triggers_fatal: Pervasive domain misrepresentation such that the manuscript's account of the field is not correctable by revision — for example fabricated or non-existent standards, agencies, or citations; systematic invention of prior findings; or a factual foundation so wrong throughout that every substantive claim would have to be rebuilt from scratch.
