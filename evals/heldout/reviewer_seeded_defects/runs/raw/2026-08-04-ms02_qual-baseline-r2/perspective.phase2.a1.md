I record this as `block` rather than `warn` because the failure is not thinness requiring extra effort from the outsider; it is the absence of the licence for the paper's most exportable claim.

## Review Body

I read this manuscript from outside the higher education quality assurance field. I work on audit cultures, accountability infrastructures, and institutional decoupling, and I study universities only as one organizational case among hospitals, courts, and firms. My judgement about what is novel *within* higher education should be discounted accordingly and arbitrated by the domain seat. What I can judge is whether the paper's framing, constructs, and implications are usable one field over, and whether its claims about reach are licensed.

The central observation of this paper has a name, a fifty-year literature, and a well-developed set of competing explanations, none of which appear here. "Elaborate documentation exercises whose connection to actual teaching quality they found tenuous", produced for an external audience, with "producing the record" displacing "improving the thing recorded", is ceremonial conformity and policy–practice decoupling as organizational sociology has described it since the late 1970s, extended through the audit-society and reactivity literatures. The manuscript presents it as an emergent empirical theme within its own field. This is not a citation-hygiene complaint. Engaging that tradition would relocate the paper's question from *does QA decouple?* (settled, and settled outside this field) to the question the data actually bear on and which remains genuinely open: why does decoupling persist and reproduce itself even where the actors running it can see it clearly and name it as theater to a researcher's face? The manuscript has interview material addressed to exactly that question and does not ask it. The same applies to "identity work", which is a specific construct with a specific analytic content in organizational studies, deployed here as a chapter heading.

Relatedly, and to the paper's credit, its strongest sentence is the one it develops least. §4.3's claim that administrators do not resolve the tension between compliance and meaning but "held it open, treating the gap itself as the space in which their professional identity was located" is a non-obvious and, in my judgement, exportable claim about how actors inhabit decoupled systems. It would be legible and interesting to someone studying clinical governance or judicial performance measurement. It receives one paragraph, is not carried into §5's three findings, and is not present in the abstract at all, while the two claims the paper foregrounds (sector-wide generality, and a participation recommendation resting on a reversed citation) are the two that will not survive review.

I flag the §3.5 / §4.1 anonymization contradiction as a research-governance matter for editorial attention rather than as a reviewer's stylistic preference. As a long-serving member of a university research ethics committee with specific responsibility for qualitative de-identification, my assessment is that this text cannot be published in its present form. §3.5 gives participants an unconditional guarantee; §4.1 then supplies role-plus-institution descriptors that, against a three-institution frame in which each institution has one such post, uniquely identify two senior staff. The attributed content is institutionally damaging. The remedy is not a copy-edit: it requires stripped role descriptors, re-examination of whether the consent obtained covered attributable criticism of a named-in-effect employer, and, in my view, a statement from the approving committee. I note for the panel that this exposure scales inversely with sample size, which is an uncomfortable interaction with the paper's other problem: the small-N design that limits its claims simultaneously maximizes its identifiability risk, so "recruit more institutions" is both the methodological and the governance remedy.

On usability for practitioners, which is where a cross-disciplinary reader lands: I cannot extract anything a quality office or a national agency could do differently. §5's recommendation that "processes that maximize genuine participation should be favored" is attributed to the one cited author who argues the opposite, and it is the precise position the paper's own §2 identifies as liable to become another compliance ritual. If the paper's actual finding is that administrators recover meaning through persuasion, coalition-building, and vocational framing rather than through documentation, the operational implications concern workload allocation (persuasion is uncosted labour in every QA workload model I have seen), professional development for quality staff, and how agencies specify evidence requirements so that the artifact does not displace the practice. None of these is addressed.

Finally, a structural assumption the manuscript does not examine. It treats administrators as the privileged vantage point on institutional quality culture, but administrators are precisely the actors with the strongest occupational interest in QA being meaningful, and §4.3 documents that interest explicitly before reading it as insight rather than interrogating it as a motivated account. Academics and students, whose experience the QA apparatus nominally exists to serve, appear only as objects of administrators' persuasion; the survey frame is "mid-level staff" of unspecified composition; and power relations inside the institution are absent from the design. "Institutional quality culture" is an institution-level construct, and I do not think it can be characterized from this single, interested vantage point without saying so as a boundary condition.

Three further items sit in other seats' territory and I raise them without banding, deferring to the methodology and devil's-advocate reviewers: §4.1's "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol" is inconsistent with §3.1's semi-structured design and §3.4's iterative emergent coding; §4.4 reports a significant difference at p<.05 between n=9 and n=11 subgroups without naming a test, reporting an effect size, or addressing multiplicity; and §4.4's framing of the survey as corroborating the qualitative picture is hard to reconcile with the same paragraph conceding that the survey contradicted what the interviews led the authors to anticipate.

### S1: The gap-inhabiting observation in §4.3 is a genuine, exportable contribution

This is the finding that would interest readers in any sector where audit and practice have separated. It describes actors who neither believe nor resist, but who locate their professional selfhood in the discrepancy itself, which is a mechanism for the reproduction of decoupling rather than merely evidence of it.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3, "Rather than resolving the tension between compliance and meaning, they held it open, treating the gap itself as the space"

### S2: The one core construct that is properly defined for an outside reader

§1 glosses "quality culture" in plain terms and attributes the gloss, which is why the introduction is the most legible part of the manuscript for a non-specialist. This is the standard the paper should have met for its other two theme constructs.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §1, "the shared values, practices, and commitments through which an institution takes collective ownership of its own standards"

### S3: §4.2 supplies a portable empirical observation about how compliance work is actually accomplished

The finding that the documentary work is the easy part and the persuasion is the job is concrete, quotable, and directly usable in adjacent literatures on how mandates get absorbed into organizational routines.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.2, "Half my job is convincing people this matters", "The forms are the easy part"

### W1: The stated anonymization guarantee is broken in the text, exposing two identifiable senior staff

§3.5 promises that no individual could be identified in reported findings. §4.1 then attributes quotations to the quality director of the largest private university in the region and to the associate dean for quality at the public research university. Given a three-institution frame with one such post per institution, both speakers are effectively named, and the attributed content is institutionally damaging. Uncorrected, this makes publication untenable independently of every other issue in the manuscript: it is a live risk to identifiable participants who consented on a promise the paper does not keep, and it raises the question of whether the approved protocol contemplated attributable institutional criticism.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 and §4.1, "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified in reported findings", "the quality director of the largest private university in the region", "the associate dean for quality at the public research university"
**Confidence**: 5 — long-serving ethics committee member with designated responsibility for qualitative de-identification review

### W2: Disconfirming cases were removed while the text claims balanced representation of the full range of views

§4.3 asserts that the study captured the full range of administrator views, then states in the next sentence that participants sceptical of the entire QA enterprise were excluded because they fell outside the three-theme structure. This is not a length constraint; it is the removal of the negative cases that would test the three themes, and it means the themes are partly constituted by the exclusion of evidence against them. Every prevalence claim in §4 and the "pervasiveness" claim in §5 rest on it. On its own this defect invalidates the paper's core empirical claims as stated, and the fix is not cosmetic: the excluded accounts must be reported and the theme structure re-derived with them in place.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3, "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views on QA implementation", "these were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure that organized our analysis"
**Confidence**: 4 — ethics-committee reviewer of qualitative protocols; negative-case reporting norms are cross-disciplinary, though the methodology seat should set the analytic remedy

### W3: Sector-wide and universal claims are asserted without evidence, from a study whose scope conditions are withheld

§5 converts a three-institution study into "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture" and asserts that administrators everywhere face the same tension; §6 claims the first comprehensive account. No evidence, comparative case, or citation is offered for any of this. The problem is compounded by the withholding of scope conditions an outsider needs to assess transfer at all: §3.2 places the study in an unnamed "single national system", with no description of the regulatory regime, review cycle, or consequences attached to external judgement. I cannot tell whether these findings would hold in a system with public league tables, in one with funding-linked accreditation, or in one with neither. This is the binding basis for my `block` on D4.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.2, §5, and §6, "three universities in a single national system", "can be read as a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture", "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension between external requirement and internal meaning", "the first comprehensive account"
**Confidence**: 5 — assessing whether a stated inferential reach is licensed by stated design is squarely within my competence

### W4: The paper's central finding is a named construct in organizational sociology, presented as a novel field-level discovery, with the relevant literatures uncited

"QA as ritual compliance" is ceremonial conformity and policy–practice decoupling; the documentation displacement described in §2 and §4.1 is the reactivity and audit-culture literature; "identity work" is a specific construct from organizational studies. None of these traditions is cited, engaged, or explicitly ruled out. The consequence is not only a novelty overclaim that any reader from an adjacent field will catch immediately, but a lost analytic opportunity: engaging the tradition would let the authors ask why decoupling persists among actors who can see and name it, which is what their §4.3 data actually address. Repair requires either substantive engagement (with attendant reframing of the contribution) or an explicit argument for why the tradition does not apply to this case.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §2 Literature Review and the reference list — expected engagement with the institutional-decoupling, ceremonial-conformity, audit-culture, and reactivity literatures that name this paper's central finding; checked §2 three-strand framing, §4.1, §5 first finding, and all reference entries
**Confidence**: 5 — this is my primary research literature

### W5: The single practical recommendation rests on a citation reversed against the paper's own reading of the source

§2 correctly reports that Delacroix (2018) cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual and argues against treating consultation as sufficient evidence of quality culture; the reference-list annotation says the same. §5 then aligns its participation recommendation with Delacroix as recommending that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building quality culture. Because this reversal carries the manuscript's only actionable claim, correcting it does not merely fix a sentence: it removes the support for the recommendation and requires the implications section to be rebuilt, presumably against Delacroix's actual position, which the paper's own findings tend to support.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 and §5, "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual", "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture"
**Confidence**: 5 — the contradiction is internal to the manuscript and verifiable without access to the cited source

### W6: An institution-level construct is characterized from a single interested vantage point, with power relations and the served populations absent

The paper claims to illuminate "institutional quality culture" but collects accounts only from the actors with the strongest occupational stake in QA being meaningful. §4.3 documents that stake and then treats the vocational framing as insight rather than examining it as a motivated account. Academics and students appear only as objects of persuasion; the survey frame is unspecified as to role composition, seniority, or relation to QA work; and no attention is paid to the authority relations within which administrators' persuasion operates. Either additional vantage points are needed, or the construct and the claims must be rescoped to administrators' accounts of quality culture, which is a different and much narrower object.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.2 and §4 — expected accounts from academics, students, or governance actors alongside the occupational composition of the 48-respondent survey frame; checked §3.1 design, §3.2 participants, §3.3 data collection, §4.4 survey findings
**Confidence**: 4 — standard vantage-point critique in organizational research; the domain seat should judge whether field convention treats administrator-only designs as sufficient for this construct

### W7: The stated implications for practice offer no operational lever, while the most transferable finding is left undeveloped

The abstract promises implications for designing QA processes that are meaningful rather than performative, and §6 repeats the promise. What is delivered is a preference for participation (itself resting on W5) and an injunction to create room for meaning-making. Nothing here tells a quality office or a national agency what to change: not how the persuasion labour documented in §4.2 should be recognized in workload, not what professional development the identity work in §4.3 implies, not how evidence requirements might be specified so the artifact stops displacing the practice. The paper's own strongest observation is the natural source of these implications and receives a single paragraph.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §5 third finding and §6 — expected at least one operational lever for quality offices or national agencies, such as workload allocation, professional development, or evidence-requirement design; checked Abstract implications sentence, §5 paragraphs three and four, §6 conclusion
**Confidence**: 4 — practitioner-facing usability judgement; I am not a QA practitioner and the domain seat may know levers the field would consider implicit

### W8: The interview sample size is reported inconsistently between the abstract and the methods

The abstract states fourteen administrators were interviewed; §3.2 states twelve, with an explicit n=12. An outside reader cannot reconstruct the evidentiary base from the paper as written, and the discrepancy also bears on W1, since identifiability turns on how many post-holders sit behind each role descriptor. I band this by my own decision impact: whichever figure is correct, the study remains small-N and no core claim changes, so this is a correction rather than a re-analysis. The methodology seat may reasonably band it higher if it indicates a data-handling problem rather than a typographical one.

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract and §3.2, "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth", "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct textual comparison
