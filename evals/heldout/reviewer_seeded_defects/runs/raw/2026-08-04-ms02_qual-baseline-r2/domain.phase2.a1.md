## Review Body

I read this manuscript as a specialist in quality culture and institutional self-evaluation, and as a reference-verification screener. My remit is whether the substantive claims about higher education quality assurance are correct, whether prior work is represented as its authors wrote it, and whether the field-specific vocabulary is used with its established meanings. On the first two questions the manuscript fails in ways that are not a matter of emphasis or framing.

**The reference base.** Twelve references, twelve DOIs, running `10.5555/1042001` through `10.5555/1042012` in unbroken sequence. Three facts about that block matter independently of any judgement about the authors. First, `10.5555` is the reserved example/test prefix that appears in DOI and Crossref documentation; it is not a prefix assigned to an operating publisher. Second, and decisively, DOI prefixes are assigned per registrant: twelve items spread across eight different journals and one monograph publisher cannot share a prefix at all, let alone carry consecutive suffixes. A sequential run across distinct publishers is not a rare coincidence; it is not a possible outcome of real DOI registration. Third, I could not attempt live resolution from within review, and I say so plainly rather than reporting resolution results I do not have — but the structural argument does not require resolution, and I would ask the editor to run all twelve and report the outcome. Independently of the DOIs, three of the named venues (*Journal of Higher Education Governance*, *Studies in Quality Assurance*, *Educational Management Review*) and the monograph publisher (Meridian Academic Press) do not correspond to outlets I can identify in a field whose journal landscape I know well, while the remaining venues are real journals paired with articles I cannot locate. Real-venue, plausible-title, non-resolving-identifier is the standard signature of a fabricated list. I hold this at confidence 4 of 5 and I state what would change it: resolving DOIs, or PDFs of the twelve items, would reduce this finding to a formatting error and I would revise my assessment accordingly.

**The Delacroix reversal.** This is the one citation claim the manuscript itself makes checkable, and it fails. §2 states that Delacroix (2018) "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual, and argues *against* treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of a healthy quality culture." The reference title says the same thing ("Against the participation ritual"), and so does the annotation appended to the entry. §5 then attributes to the same source the recommendation "that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture." That is not drift or compression; it is the negation. Note also that the reversal runs against the manuscript's own data: §4.1 finds that elaborated, documented process becomes theater, which is precisely the mechanism Delacroix is said in §2 to warn about, so the recommendation in §5 would predict more artifacts, not more ownership. Whether the cause is misreading, misremembering, or insertion to manufacture support, the third of three headline findings currently rests on it and must be rebuilt from the source's actual position.

**Audit of the remaining citation uses.** No page or locator reference appears anywhere for any source, so most uses cannot be checked against text even in principle. Two further uses concerned me. §2 characterises Silva and Tan (2021) as "largely conceptual rather than grounded in administrators' own words" — a claim about another study's method that carries the paper's gap argument and that a reader cannot verify. And §2 identifies Pettersen (2022) and Rahman (2020) as the works that treat distributed leadership and professional identity in the quality office, then §4.2 and §4.3 adopt exactly those two labels without returning to either source; the paper cites the literature that would discipline its own theme names and then declines to use it. §5's assertion that the findings "confirm long-standing concerns" cites nothing at all, and nothing in the list predates 2018.

**The gap claim against the literature that exists.** §1 asserts that quality culture "remains under-theorized at the level of everyday administrative practice" and §2 that "sustained attention to the administrators who occupy the hinge" is missing. Both are false as statements about the field. The ritualization of internal quality systems, the decoupling of documentation from teaching, and the coping strategies of the staff who run the machinery are the central findings of a canon this manuscript does not cite: Newton's studies of quality monitoring at institutional level, which produced the "feeding the beast" account of exactly the intermediary staff this paper claims are unstudied; Harvey and Stensaker's typology of quality cultures; Ehlers on quality literacy; the EUA *Examining Quality Culture* work; and the audit-society framing that preceded all of it. The manuscript's §4.1 finding — documentation as theater, arriving on a calendar, leaving nothing a teacher would recognise — is a re-description of that established result, and the paper's own §4.1 metaphor ("feeding the machine") lands one word away from the canonical formulation. I therefore ask directly for the search strategy: databases, date limits, search strings, inclusion criteria, and dates run. A list bounded at 2018–2022 with no foundational work is consistent with either a date-limited search or a list assembled to fit an argument, and the authors are entitled to distinguish the two.

**What would remain new.** Strip the overclaims and something modest but publishable is visible in principle: a three-site account, in one named system, of how QA administrators hold the compliance/meaning tension open rather than resolving it, plus the level-divergence observation that mid-level staff sentiment is more positive than administrators'. That is a confirmation-plus-refinement contribution in a specified national context, not "the first comprehensive account" and not "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture." The refinement — ritualism experienced as a site of professional identity rather than only as hollowing-out — is the genuinely interesting move, and it is the one part of the Discussion the paper underworks.

**Terminological precision.** The manuscript does not distinguish quality assurance from quality enhancement, internal from external QA, or accreditation from audit from evaluation, and it uses "accreditors," "external review," and "the panel" interchangeably. In a field where the accountability logic of programme accreditation and the improvement logic of enhancement-led institutional review produce measurably different internal behaviour, that conflation is not stylistic: it determines whether the ritual finding is surprising or expected. The unnamed national system compounds this, since the reader cannot tell which regime the administrators were describing.

**Observations I am not scoring, referred to the methodology seat.** §3.3 describes semi-structured interviews while §4.1 claims the themes "emerged systematically from the structured protocol." §3.5 states that all data were "fully anonymized... so that no individual could be identified," yet §4.1 identifies a speaker as "the quality director of the largest private university in the region," which is an identifying descriptor. §4.4 reports a significant difference at p<.05 between subgroups of n=9 and n=11 drawn from a 48-respondent survey, leaving 28 respondents unaccounted for in the comparison and no test, effect size, or instrument named. I flag these so they are not lost; the validity and statistical calls belong to the methodology reviewer, and I note candidly that literature and citation matters are where my attention naturally goes.

### S1: §2's rendering of the participation/performance tension is accurate and genuinely useful
The paragraph that sets consultation-as-cultivation against consultation-as-performance identifies a real and live fault line in quality-culture scholarship, and states the sceptical side of it correctly and without caricature. The manuscript's front matter does the honest intellectual work here; the failure is downstream in §5, not in this framing.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 Literature Review — "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual"

### S2: The refusal to treat ritualism as pure hollowing-out is a real advance on the compliance-culture literature
Much of the existing literature treats ritualization as the end of the story. The observation that administrators actively recover meaning from processes they themselves describe as theatrical, and locate professional identity in the gap rather than resolving it, is a defensible refinement and the manuscript's most promising contribution.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 Discussion — "they worked actively to recover meaning from it, framing it as identity work and distributed leadership"

### S3: The level-divergence finding is reported honestly against expectation
The authors state that the survey contradicted what the interviews had led them to expect, rather than smoothing the discrepancy away. Vertical variation in how quality culture is experienced across institutional levels is under-studied, and reporting the disconfirmation is creditable practice.
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 Survey findings — "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture"

### W1: §5 attributes to Delacroix (2018) the position that source argues against
The Discussion's third headline finding recruits Delacroix as recommending stakeholder consultation as "the central mechanism" for quality culture. §2, the reference title, and the reference annotation all state the opposite position. The design recommendation that follows ("processes that maximize genuine participation should be favored") is therefore unsupported by the source cited for it, and also sits against the paper's own §4.1 finding that elaborated documented process becomes theater. This finding must be rebuilt on the source's actual argument, which would push the recommendation in close to the opposite direction.

### W3: The gap claim and the priority claim are false against the existing canon
The assertion that quality culture is under-theorized in everyday administrative practice, and that the hinge actors are unstudied, does not survive contact with the literature that exists. The ritualization of self-evaluation, documentation displacing reflection, and the coping strategies of quality staff are established findings, produced in studies of precisely these actors. The manuscript's §4.1 result is a re-description of that canon while §5 claims to "confirm long-standing concerns" without citing anything long-standing, and §6 claims priority. With the canon in place the contribution is confirmation in an unnamed system, and every claim of position in the field must be rewritten; the search strategy needs to be disclosed so the editor can judge whether the omission is a date-limited search or a constructed gap.
**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §2 Literature Review — expected engagement with the pre-2018 quality-culture canon (Newton on the ritualization of quality monitoring and staff feeding the beast; Harvey and Stensaker on quality culture types; Ehlers on quality literacy; the EUA Examining Quality Culture reports; the audit-society framing); checked §1, §2, §5, §6 and all twelve reference-list entries
**Confidence**: 5 — this is the literature I work in and teach.

### W4: A three-site study in an unnamed system is generalized to the sector as a whole
§5 converts three institutions into "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture" and asserts that "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension." Neither is licensed by the design, and the second is contradicted by comparative evidence that internal responses to QA vary with regime type, institutional autonomy, and funding linkage. Because §3.2 declines to name the national system, a reader cannot even establish which external regime the accounts describe, so no transfer to another system can be assessed. The sector-level claims should be deleted and the regime specified in enough detail (agency type, review cycle, consequences of adverse findings) for readers to judge transferability.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 Discussion and §3.2 — "can be read as a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture" and "three universities in a single national system"
**Confidence**: 5 — comparative QA regime variation is my core area.

### W5: "Quality culture" carries three incompatible senses and is never operationally defined
The construct appears as a process (an ongoing negotiation), an institutional property (strong or weak), and a scalar survey score, with only a one-line borrowed gloss in §1 to discipline it. No instrument, item set, provenance, or scale anchoring is given for the score in §4.4, so "moderately positive" is uninterpretable and cannot be reconciled with the ritualism reported in §4.1 — the two may not be measuring the same object. The field already offers explicit definitions and typologies the paper could adopt; it should adopt one, state which sense is in play at each point, and report the instrument.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §1 Introduction and §4.4 — "the shared values, practices, and commitments through which an institution takes collective ownership of its own standards" and "respondents' overall institutional quality culture score was moderately positive (M=3.9, SD=0.6)"
**Confidence**: 5 — construct definitions in this literature are directly within my competence.

### W6: "Distributed leadership" and "identity work" are used as authority-borrowing labels, not analytic concepts
Two of the three themes are named with terms of art that receive no conceptual engagement. Distributed leadership in its established formulation concerns leadership practice stretched across people and situations, with attendant claims about concertive action; what §4.2 actually reports is coalition-building and persuasion by an office lacking line authority, which the literature would more accurately treat as boundary or institutional work. "Identity work" likewise arrives without provenance. The manuscript cites the two sources that would supply both frameworks and then never returns to them. Either engage the frameworks and show the data meet their criteria, or relabel the themes descriptively.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.2 and §4.3 Findings — "This relational, distributed understanding of leadership recurred across every interview." and "This vocational framing appeared to function as a coping resource"
**Confidence**: 4 — strong on the QA-office literature, moderately strong on the leadership theory it invokes.

### W7: The abstract and Methods report different interview sample sizes
The abstract reports fourteen administrators interviewed; §3.2 reports twelve, stated twice. In a qualitative study of this size the difference is 17% of the evidence base, and a reader cannot tell which figure is correct, whether two interviews were excluded, or whether the discrepancy extends to the analysis. The correct number must be stated, with any exclusions and their reasons.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract and §3.2 — "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth" and "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 5 — a direct textual contradiction.

### W8: §4.3 claims full-range coverage in the same paragraph that reports excluding dissenting cases
The manuscript states that it captured the full range of administrator views and achieved balanced representation, then states that participants sceptical of the whole QA enterprise were excluded because they fell outside the three-theme structure. Dropping cases for not fitting the themes removes precisely the disconfirming evidence that would test them, and the excluded position is well documented in the existing literature, so its absence here is a substantive loss rather than an editorial one. The coverage claim should be withdrawn and the dissenting accounts reported and analysed; the validity consequences are for the methodology reviewer to weigh.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 Findings — "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views" and "these were excluded for space"
**Confidence**: 5 — the contradiction is explicit in the text.

### W9: Core QA vocabulary is conflated throughout
Quality assurance and quality enhancement, internal and external QA, and accreditation, audit and evaluation are used interchangeably, as are "accreditors," "external review," and "the panel." These regimes differ in mandate, consequence, and the internal behaviour they elicit, and the difference bears directly on whether the ritual finding is a surprising result or the expected one. A short paragraph fixing the terms and naming the specific review instrument in play would resolve it.
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 Literature Review — "QA regimes are instruments through which states and accreditors steer autonomous institutions at a distance"
