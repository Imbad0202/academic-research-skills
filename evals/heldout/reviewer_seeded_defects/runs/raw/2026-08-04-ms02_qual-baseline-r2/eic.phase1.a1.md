## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** From the editorial chair's seat, this dimension asks whether the study's design and execution would survive scrutiny by a competent methods referee in this field: is the sampling or case-selection defensible, are data collection and handling described well enough that a reader could judge their soundness, are analytic procedures and any statistics reported completely rather than gestured at, and are there enough reproducibility affordances (instruments, coding schemes, data availability statements) that the findings are checkable rather than merely asserted. Ownership sits with the methodology reviewer; my role is to register the outcome, not to substitute my own methods verdict.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role).** This covers whether the manuscript's substantive claims are consistent with the current state of evidence in its own field, whether prior literature is characterised faithfully rather than strawmanned or cited loosely, and whether field-specific terminology, frameworks, instruments, and reported results are used correctly. Editorially, this is the dimension where a misstatement is not a stylistic irritation but a correctness failure that could propagate into the record; the domain reviewer owns the determination.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, da/methodology roles).** Here the question is internal: does the central thesis hold together from premise to conclusion, does the evidence actually bear the weight of the claims attached to it, and are there inferential leaps, equivocations, circularities, or other fallacies that would collapse the argument if named aloud. This is distinct from whether the facts are right (D2) or the methods sound (D1) — a paper can be accurate and well-designed yet still argue badly. The devil's-advocate reviewer owns it, with methodology as a secondary eligible voice.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, perspective role).** This asks whether a well-informed reader from an adjacent field can enter the manuscript: are framings and definitions supplied rather than presupposed, is jargon either avoided or explained, and — critically — where the paper reaches across disciplinary boundaries to make a claim, is that reach actually substantiated rather than decorative. It is high priority rather than mandatory, meaning failure here escalates the decision but cannot by itself trigger rejection. The perspective reviewer owns it.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, EiC role).** This is mine. It concerns whether the manuscript is organised so that a reader can follow the argument's architecture, whether exposition is clear and appropriately economical, whether figures and tables are legible, correctly labelled, and genuinely informative rather than ornamental, and whether the submission observes the conventions of the target venue (sectioning, referencing, abstract structure, length norms). Normal priority means deficiencies here shape revision requests rather than acceptance in principle.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, EiC role).** Also mine, and the heavier of the two. This asks two linked questions: does the manuscript belong in this venue given its scope and readership, and does it advance something original and consequential enough to justify the space it would occupy. Contribution is judged relative to what the field already knows — restatement, incremental confirmation without added leverage, or a topic outside the journal's remit all count against it. Because this dimension is mandatory, a severe failure here can drive the panel to rejection independent of the other reviewers' findings.

## Scoring Plan

### D5: writing_and_structure

dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: A discernible organising logic across sections; claims located where a reader would expect them; defined terms before use; figures and tables that are self-describing, correctly captioned, and referenced in text; consistent and complete referencing; and conformance to the venue's structural and length conventions for the manuscript type.
what_triggers_block: Structure or exposition is disordered enough that the paper's central claim and supporting evidence cannot be reliably reconstructed on a careful read — for example, missing or empty core sections, findings presented with no traceable link to any stated question, or figures/tables that are uninterpretable, uncaptioned, or contradict the surrounding text.
what_triggers_warn: The argument is followable but the presentation costs the reader effort — repetitive or misordered passages, undefined jargon, thin or poorly labelled exhibits, inconsistent citation formatting, or noticeable departures from venue conventions that a revision round could fix without changing the substance.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution

dimension_id: D6
what_to_look_for: An explicit statement of what is new and why it matters, positioned against identifiable prior work; a topic and readership that match the configured venue's remit; evidence that the claimed contribution is actually delivered in the body rather than only promised in the abstract; and scope commensurate with the manuscript's length and depth.
what_triggers_block: The contribution as delivered is substantially thinner than claimed — the novelty reduces to a restatement or minor confirmation of established findings, or the framing is plausible for the venue but the paper does not supply enough substance to be worth the readership's attention without significant additional work.
what_triggers_warn: The contribution is real but its significance is under-argued or its positioning against prior work is incomplete, or the venue fit is adjacent rather than central such that the framing needs sharpening to reach this readership.
what_triggers_fatal: The manuscript falls outside the configured venue's scope altogether, or offers no identifiable original contribution — presenting only summary, opinion, or previously published material with no new claim, evidence, or synthesis that the field could act on.
