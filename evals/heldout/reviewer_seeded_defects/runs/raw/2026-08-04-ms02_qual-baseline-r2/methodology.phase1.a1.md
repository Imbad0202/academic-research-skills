## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, my ownership).** From a methods-rigor standpoint, this dimension asks whether the study's design actually licenses the inferences drawn from it: whether the research question is matched to an appropriate design, whether sampling and recruitment are described well enough to judge who is represented and who is excluded, whether data collection instruments and their administration are documented, whether analysis procedures (statistical or qualitative-analytic) are specified rather than gestured at, whether uncertainty and variability are reported alongside point estimates or themes, and whether an independent researcher would have enough procedural detail — instruments, coding schemes, data availability, ethics approval, consent handling — to attempt a replication or at least a critical audit. It is the dimension I own, and the one where absence of information is itself evidence: undocumented method is unassessable method.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain reviewer's ownership).** This dimension concerns whether the manuscript's substantive claims are consistent with the current state of evidence in its field, whether prior literature is characterised faithfully rather than strawmanned or over-credited, and whether field-specific terminology, frameworks, and reported results are used with technical correctness. I do not score it, but I note that methodological weaknesses and domain misstatements often co-occur, and I will flag any factual concern I encounter to the owning reviewer rather than scoring it myself.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, discourse-analysis reviewer owns; I am eligible).** Here the question is whether the central thesis holds together internally and whether the chain from evidence to conclusion is unbroken. Read through a methodology lens, this means checking for inferential over-reach — conclusions that outrun what the design can support, causal language attached to associational or descriptive evidence, generalisations beyond the sampled population, findings that contradict each other or contradict the stated limitations, and reasoning moves (affirming a hypothesis from absence of disconfirmation, treating self-report as behavioural fact) that would not survive scrutiny. My contribution is the evidence-to-claim link specifically, complementing the owner's broader rhetorical analysis.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, perspective reviewer's ownership).** This dimension evaluates whether readers from adjacent fields can enter the argument: whether constructs are defined rather than assumed, whether jargon is unpacked, whether implications are framed so that their reach beyond the immediate subfield is visible, and whether any interdisciplinary claims are actually backed by engagement with the borrowed discipline rather than decorative citation. Not mine to score.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, editor-in-chief's ownership).** This covers manuscript craft: whether the organisation guides a reader, whether exposition is clear, whether figures and tables are legible, correctly labelled, and self-sufficient, and whether venue formatting and reporting conventions are respected. Not mine to score, though I recognise that unreadable methods sections create genuine ambiguity between "poorly written" and "not done."

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, editor-in-chief's ownership).** This dimension asks whether the paper belongs in the configured venue and whether it advances the field in a way that matters to that readership — originality relative to existing work, significance of the increment, and appropriateness of scope and depth. Not mine to score; my methods assessment may inform it, since a contribution claim rests on the credibility of the evidence behind it.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor

dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: Explicit statement of design and its fit to the research question; sampling frame, recruitment, sample size, and participant/site characteristics; data collection instruments and procedures; a specified analytic strategy (statistical tests with assumptions, or qualitative coding/analysis procedure with trustworthiness measures); reported uncertainty, variability, or negative/disconfirming cases; ethics approval and consent; and reproducibility affordances such as available instruments, codebooks, protocols, or data/materials statements.
what_triggers_block: Core analytic or sampling procedure is unspecified or non-reconstructible — for example, results reported with no stated analysis method, no described sample or selection process, or conclusions resting on data whose provenance is never given — so that the reported findings cannot be verified even in principle.
what_triggers_warn: Method is present and broadly reconstructible but has identifiable gaps that weaken confidence — missing effect sizes or dispersion measures, unaddressed selection or self-report bias, absent inter-coder or reliability checks, thin instrument documentation, or no data/materials availability statement — none of which alone invalidates the findings.
what_triggers_fatal: Evidence of a defect that irreparably invalidates the study as reported and cannot be fixed by revision — such as internally contradictory or arithmetically impossible reported numbers, an analysis applied to data structurally incapable of supporting it, missing ethics approval for human-subjects data, or signs of fabricated, duplicated, or undisclosed-source data.

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Traceability from each substantive claim back to a specific reported result; alignment between the strength of claim language (causal, prescriptive, generalising) and the strength the design can bear; consistency among findings, discussion, abstract, and stated limitations; acknowledgement of alternative explanations and disconfirming evidence; and conclusions whose scope matches the population and setting actually studied.
what_triggers_block: The paper's central conclusion is not supported by the evidence presented — causal or policy-prescriptive claims drawn from descriptive or associational data, generalisation to populations never sampled, or a thesis that a reported result actually contradicts — such that the main argument fails on its own terms.
what_triggers_warn: The core argument survives but contains localised inferential slippage — over-strong wording in the abstract or discussion, secondary claims without an evidentiary anchor, limitations acknowledged in one section yet ignored when conclusions are drawn, or alternative explanations left unconsidered.
what_triggers_fatal: The argument is structurally self-defeating and unrecoverable by revision — the thesis is internally contradictory, or it rests on a premise the manuscript's own data disconfirm, so that no rewriting short of a different study could rescue the central claim.
