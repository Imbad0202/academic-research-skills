## Review Body

This manuscript asks a worthwhile question and has, buried inside it, a defensible small qualitative study of QA administrators at three institutions. What it currently presents, however, is a set of claims whose scale the reported method cannot carry. My assessment concentrates on three linked problems: the analytic procedures for both strands are described too thinly to be audited or reproduced; the sample is reported inconsistently and incompletely; and the paper's own text documents the removal of disconfirming cases while simultaneously asserting comprehensiveness and universality.

On D1 I score `block`. The decisive item is §4.4, where a significance claim is asserted with no named test, no per-group descriptive statistics, no effect size, no interval estimate, and no assumption checks — results reported with no stated analysis method, in the plain sense. The qualitative strand is close behind: "iterative" coding "until a stable structure was reached" is a description of an outcome, not of a procedure, and there is no coding framework, coder count, intercoder or member-checking step, positionality statement, or saturation argument that would let a reader judge how the three themes were produced.

I want to be explicit about why I did **not** escalate this to a fatal block, since my scoring plan lists "internally contradictory ... reported numbers" among fatal indicators and the abstract's "Fourteen" against §3.2's "Twelve" is exactly such a contradiction. That clause is governed by its own umbrella condition: the defect must be irreparable by revision. Here it is not. There is no sign of fabrication, ethics approval and consent are reported, and no figure is arithmetically impossible (n=9 and n=11 are simply an unexplained subset of 48). The authors can state which N is correct, report what they actually did, and reanalyse. That is a substantial revision, not an unrecoverable study, and I have classed the block accordingly.

On D3, which I score but do not own, my contribution is confined to the evidence-to-claim link. Two moves fail on their own terms. The first is §5's leap from three institutions in one national system to what "universities across the sector" do and to "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture" — generalisation to populations never sampled, stated not as speculation but as the paper's second finding. The second is the collision between §4.3's admission that dissenting participants were dropped and the same paragraph's claim of "the full range" of views, plus §4.2's "recurred across every interview." A universality claim cannot be built on a corpus from which non-conforming cases were removed. The remedy is available — reanalyse the negative cases or withdraw the universality claims — so I again class the block as repairable rather than fatal.

Two smaller observations for the authors' attention. First, divergence between strands is a legitimate and often the most interesting mixed-methods result; §4.4 has one and misreports it as corroboration in the same breath as conceding it. Second, the honest version of this paper — three sites, twelve or fourteen administrators, an exploratory survey with descriptive statistics only, and negative cases restored — is a smaller paper than the one drafted, but it is a publishable one. I flag one item outside my seat for the domain reviewer: the characterisation of Delacroix (2018) in §5 should be checked against that entry's reference-list annotation.

### S1: Design priority between strands is explicitly declared
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.1 "The qualitative component was primary; the survey served to contextualize the interview findings"
Many mixed-methods submissions leave strand priority implicit. Declaring the qualitative component as primary and the survey as contextualising is the correct reporting move and gives a reader a starting point for judging the integration claims (which, separately, are not made good on).

### S2: Site selection reflects a defensible variation logic
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.2 "one large public research university, one mid-sized public teaching-focused university, and one private university"
Sampling across mission type and sector, and across quality directors, associate deans, and registry heads, is a sensible maximum-variation strategy for an exploratory study of this kind. The rationale is never articulated, but the structure itself is sound and worth preserving in revision.

### S3: Interview collection parameters are reported concretely
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.3 "lasting between 45 and 70 minutes, and were audio-recorded and transcribed"
Mode, duration range, recording, and transcription are all stated. This is the one part of the methods where a reader can reconstruct what happened, and it should be the model for the rest of §3.

### S4: Ethics approval and consent are stated rather than assumed
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 "The study received institutional ethical approval. All participants gave informed consent"
The presence of an explicit ethics subsection is a genuine reporting strength, and it removes the most serious class of fatal concern from this review. It is undercut by the identifiability problem in W12, but the statement itself is correctly placed and correctly framed.

### W1: The §4.4 significance claim is asserted without any analysis to inspect
**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "we found a statistically significant difference (p<.05) in perceived quality culture" and "points to institutional type as a possible moderator"
No test is named. There are no group means or SDs, no effect size, no confidence interval, no assumption checks, no statement of how many comparisons were available or examined, and no adjustment for having selected one contrast among several possible institution and role pairings. With n=9 against n=11, the sampling distribution assumptions matter acutely and are simply unaddressed. As reported, the reader cannot verify the claim even in principle, and the moderator sentence is not supportable at any strength; it should be deleted unless the full analysis is reported. Uncorrected, this defect alone makes the quantitative strand unassessable, which is why I treat it as rejection-level on its own rather than as an accumulation with W7 and W8.
**Confidence**: 5 — routine statistical screening of small-N educational survey analyses.

### W2: Disconfirming cases were removed, and two comprehensiveness claims depend on their absence
**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.2–§4.3 "these were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure that organized our analysis" and "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views" and "This relational, distributed understanding of leadership recurred across every interview"
The stated ground for exclusion is that the cases did not fit the coding structure. That inverts the relationship between data and framework: in thematic analysis, cases that resist the structure are the primary evidence that the structure needs revision, and negative-case analysis is a core trustworthiness procedure in the Braun and Clarke lineage the paper implicitly works within. "For space" is not an analytic justification, and the exclusion directly falsifies the "full range" claim in the same paragraph and the "every interview" claim one section earlier. This single decision, uncorrected, invalidates the comprehensiveness on which the three-theme finding rests, independently of every other problem I list. Required: analyse and report the dissenting accounts, revise the theme structure if they do not fit, or withdraw both universality claims.
**Confidence**: 5 — publishes on thematic analysis reporting standards and trustworthiness procedures.

### W3: Sector-wide conclusions are drawn from three institutions in one national system
**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "these findings demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment rather than a managerial output" and "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension"
This is presented as the paper's second finding, not as a speculative extension, and the language is at maximum strength ("demonstrate", "the sector as a whole", "everywhere"). Three purposively selected sites in one system, with participants recruited through institutional QA offices, cannot license any claim about a sector, still less about administrators universally. The Conclusion compounds it with "the first comprehensive account". The finding that survives is a claim about how twelve or fourteen administrators at three institutions construct quality culture, which is worth publishing on its own terms. The core qualitative material survives the correction, but the claim as written is a stated finding the design cannot bear at all, which places it above the Major band.
**Confidence**: 5 — external validity assessment for small-sample qualitative designs.

### W4: The interview sample size is reported inconsistently and never reconciled
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract and §3.2 "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth" and "Twelve senior administrators (n=12)"
The abstract and the methods disagree about the size of the primary dataset, and nothing in the manuscript resolves which is correct. This is not merely cosmetic: every theme-prevalence statement, and the per-institution distribution the paper never provides, depends on knowing the denominator. The authors should state the correct N, explain how the discrepancy arose, and check whether the two dropped or added cases relate to the exclusions described in W2.
**Confidence**: 5 — direct textual comparison.

### W5: The thematic analysis procedure is described as an outcome, not as a method
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.4 Analysis — expected a stated coding framework, number of coders, intercoder or member-checking procedure, positionality statement, and saturation criterion; checked §3.1, §3.3, §3.4, §4.1 through §4.3, and the reference list for an appended protocol
"Coding proceeded iteratively ... until a stable structure was reached" tells a reader that themes were produced but not how. Which analytic tradition, inductive or deductive coding, how many analysts, how disagreements were handled, whether transcripts were returned to participants, and on what basis coding stopped are all absent. Under COREQ or SRQR this section would fail on the majority of items. The findings may well be sound; as written they are unauditable, and the fix is reporting what was actually done rather than new data.
**Confidence**: 5 — writes on reporting standards for thematic analysis.

### W6: The survey has no sampling frame, no response rate, and an unreconciled respondent count
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: survey reporting in §3.2 and §3.3 — expected a sampling frame, invitation and response rates, and per-institution respondent counts that reconcile the n=9 and n=11 subgroups with the 48 stated respondents; checked §3.2, §3.3, §3.4, §4.4, and the abstract
Who was eligible, how many were invited, how many responded, and how the 48 distribute across the three sites are all unstated, so non-response and self-selection bias cannot be assessed. The two subgroups in §4.4 account for 20 respondents; the disposition of the other 28, including whether the private university contributed any, is never given. Since the paper's one inferential claim turns on institution type, the missing per-institution breakdown is not a bookkeeping gap but a precondition for reading the result.
**Confidence**: 5 — survey methods screening.

### W7: The reported subgroup analysis is not the analysis that was planned
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.4 and §4.4 "subgroup comparisons were examined where role categories permitted" and "Comparing staff at the research university (n=9) with staff at the teaching-focused university (n=11)"
The analysis plan specifies comparison by role category; the result reported is a comparison by institution type. Either the role comparison was run and not reported, or the institution comparison was unplanned, or both. Each possibility bears on how the p-value should be read, and the manuscript gives the reader no way to tell which obtains. The authors should state all comparisons conducted and report them, planned or otherwise.
**Confidence**: 4 — analysis-plan deviation is evident from the text, though the authors' intent is not.

### W8: The quality culture score is uninterpretable as reported
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: instrument reporting for the quality culture score — expected response scale anchors, item count, instrument provenance, and an internal-consistency estimate for M=3.9, SD=0.6; checked §3.3, §3.4, §4.4, §5, and the reference list
A reader cannot tell whether 3.9 sits on a five-, six-, or seven-point scale, how many items were aggregated, whether the instrument was adapted from published work or written for this study, or whether the composite is internally consistent. Without anchors, "moderately positive" is an assertion rather than a reading, and the same is true of the SD and of any between-group difference computed from these values. This is recoverable by reporting the instrument, ideally as an appendix.
**Confidence**: 5 — instrument documentation is a standard screening item.

### W9: A divergent result is narrated as convergence, and no integration strategy is stated
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "The survey corroborated the qualitative picture." and "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture"
These two sentences, two apart, cannot both stand. The survey did not corroborate the interviews; it diverged from what the interviews predicted, which the authors then notice and reframe as a level-of-institution effect in §5 without retracting "corroborated". Separately, the manuscript states no integration strategy, no design typology, no joint display, and no point of interface between strands, so the survey's contribution to the qualitative claims is asserted rather than shown. Divergence is a legitimate and interesting finding here and should be reported as one.
**Confidence**: 5 — publishes on QUAL to quan integration quality.

### W10: There is no limitations section
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §5 and §6 — expected a limitations statement covering three-site scope, single national system, recruitment through institutional QA offices, and small subgroup sizes; checked §1, §3.1 through §3.5, §5, §6, and the abstract
The absence is consequential rather than formal: it is precisely what allows §5 to scale from three sites to a sector without friction, and it leaves gatekeeper-mediated recruitment through QA offices, which plausibly selects for administrators invested in QA, entirely unexamined. A limitations section that names these constraints would also make the narrower claims in W3 the natural ones to write.
**Confidence**: 5 — standard reporting element for empirical work.

### W11: Protocol structure is offered as a control for site effects, and the design is described two ways
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.1 and §3.1 "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol, giving us confidence that the pattern was not an artifact" and "semi-structured interviews"
Uniformity of questioning cannot rule out that a pattern is local to one institution; only a per-institution distribution of themes can speak to that, and none is reported. The inference is a non sequitur regardless of whether the finding is true. The sentence also contains a second problem: themes that "emerged" describe inductive analysis, while a "structured protocol" describes the opposite, and §3.1 in any case says semi-structured. The authors should report how themes distributed across the three sites and align their design vocabulary.
**Confidence**: 5 — qualitative design specialisation.

### W12: A quoted participant is identifiable, contradicting the anonymisation claim
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.1 and §3.5 "As the quality director of the largest private university in the region said" and "no individual could be identified in reported findings"
The study includes exactly one private university, and the attribution names a unique post at a uniquely specified institution. The same applies, more weakly, to the associate dean at "the public research university". As a methods matter this is a direct internal contradiction with §3.5 and requires either generic attributions or documented participant approval for the identifying detail. I note that the participant-protection dimension of this issue extends beyond my seat and defer to the reviewer covering it.
**Confidence**: 4 — the identifiability follows from the reported site structure, though local context is unknown to me.

### W13: No reproducibility affordances are provided
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: manuscript-wide reproducibility affordances — expected a data availability statement, the interview guide, the coding scheme, and the survey instrument as appendices or deposited materials; checked §3.3, §3.4, §3.5, §6, and the reference list
Beyond the procedural gaps in W5 and W8, nothing in the manuscript is shareable: no interview guide, no codebook, no survey instrument, no statement about whether de-identified transcripts or item-level survey data can be obtained. For an interview study, full data sharing is often inappropriate, but a materials statement and appended instruments are achievable and would let readers judge the instrument-level decisions the current text obscures. This does not change any core claim but materially affects the paper's auditability.
**Confidence**: 5 — standard reproducibility checklist.
