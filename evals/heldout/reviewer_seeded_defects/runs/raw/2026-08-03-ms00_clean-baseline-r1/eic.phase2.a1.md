## Review Body

I read this as the editor who decides whether a submission enters review, under which article type, and whether the topic is already saturated. My verdict on the two questions in my remit is: fit yes, sufficiency no — and the sufficiency problem is specific and fixable rather than terminal.

**The article-type judgment, stated explicitly.** This manuscript is submitted with the full apparatus of a research article — literature review, methods with four subsections, separate Discussion, separate Limitations, Conclusion — around a payload of one Pearson correlation and one Spearman robustness check on n = 214 from one site. That is a research note or short report, not a full paper. I say this as an article-type routing decision, not as a euphemism for rejection: the reporting discipline here is above the median of what crosses my desk, and a well-positioned short report would be a defensible use of journal space. What is not defensible is the current mismatch between format claim and evidentiary mass, which the paper never addresses because it never states which article type it is targeting or why a full-length treatment is warranted.

**The saturation test.** This is where the manuscript fails my screen most cleanly. The acceptance-model record already contains pooled perceived-usefulness-to-use associations across many samples and instruments. A single-site r = .42 is informative only in relation to that pooled distribution: does it sit inside the interval, at the low end, at the high end, and if the pooled estimate is already narrow, what does one more point inside it buy the reader? The manuscript raises exactly the right frame — Song (2018) is cited for the observation that any single-site estimate is "one point in a distribution" — and then declines to use it. No pooled estimate is named, no comparison interval is given, no moderator is proposed that would make this site diagnostic of anything. The paper asks to be read as "comparable with prior work" while supplying no comparison. This is a repairable analytic-framing gap, not a data problem; the authors already have everything they need except the literature work.

**On restraint: virtue or device.** My configured concern was whether the paper's refusal to overclaim is genuine rigor or thinness dressed as rigor. My honest answer is: both, at different layers, and they should be scored separately. At the level of *inferential* language the restraint is real and creditable — the reverse-causation pathway is named rather than buried, the self-report/behavioural-log distinction is imported from Vasquez (2020) and applied to the paper's own measure, and no causal verb slips into the Discussion. That is not rhetoric; that is a correctly disciplined author. At the level of *contribution* language the restraint becomes load-bearing in a way it should not be. "Modest, design-bounded evidence," "incremental data point," "one point in a distribution" — these phrases perform the function that a specification of the increment should perform. Modesty about a claim's strength is a virtue; modesty deployed so that the size of the contribution never has to be stated is a substitute for the argument I need in order to allocate space. The fix is unglamorous and entirely available: say what the increment is, in numbers, against the record.

**Reference base.** Six sources, all apparently single-study, none synthetic or meta-analytic, for a topic with three decades of accumulation. I treat this as fixable in revision rather than as proof of a study designed in isolation, because the six that are present are used precisely and non-decoratively — each is cited for a specific claim it can actually carry, which is more than many longer reference lists manage. But it is below the floor for a full research article at any indexed venue, and it is causally connected to the saturation failure above: a paper that has not read the synthesis literature cannot benchmark itself against it.

**Scope limits of this review.** I did not audit the statistical treatment of the single-item ordinal outcome, the adequacy of α = .88 as instrument evidence for a six-item adapted scale, or the arithmetic of the prospective power statement. Those belong to the methodology seat, and my D6 block should be read as independent of and non-substitutable for whatever that seat finds. If the measurement critique is severe, it stands on its own; a favourable journal-fit path (re-type as research note, benchmark, expand references) does not discharge it.

### S1: Inferential restraint is genuine and correctly targeted

The reverse-causation pathway is stated as an equally supported reading of the same data rather than gestured at, and the self-report-versus-log distinction is applied reflexively to the paper's own measure rather than only to others' work. This is the standard I wish more acceptance-model submissions met.

- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §5 Discussion "the reverse pathway, in which more frequent use raises perceived usefulness, is equally consistent with the data"`

### S2: Complete inferential reporting package

The correlation is reported with confidence interval, p value, and n together; a Spearman check is reported with its own coefficient rather than asserted as "consistent"; a prospective sensitivity statement is present. I credit the completeness of the reporting surface without endorsing the underlying computations, which are not mine to verify.

- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §4 Results "r = .42, 95% CI [.30, .52], p < .001, n = 214"`

### S3: Ethics and consent reporting is crisp and complete

Approval body, voluntariness, absence of incentive, consent mechanism, and non-linkability are each stated in one pass with no filler. This clears the venue's requirements without an editorial query.

- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §3.3 "The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the university's research ethics committee"`

### S4: Transparent response-flow accounting

The path from received to analysed responses is itemised by exclusion reason rather than reported only as a final n, which lets a reader reconstruct the analytic sample.

- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §3.1 "A total of 233 responses were received; 14 incomplete submissions and 5 duplicate entries were removed"`

### W1: The contribution is never benchmarked against the existing pooled estimate

The paper claims comparability with prior technology-acceptance work but supplies no pooled or meta-analytic estimate to compare against, no interval within which .42 can be located, and no statement of what the reader knows after this study that they did not know before. Without that, a single-site replication of a well-established association has no stated increment, and I cannot evaluate whether the space is warranted. Remedy: name the pooled estimate(s) from the synthesis literature, locate .42 relative to them, and state the increment explicitly — substantial rewriting and literature work, but no new data.

- **Severity**: Major
- **Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §2 Literature Review and §5 Discussion — expected quantitative benchmarking of r = .42 against pooled acceptance-model meta-analytic estimates; checked Abstract, §2, §4, §5, §7, References`
- **Confidence**: 5 — editorial familiarity with the acceptance-model literature stock and with what benchmarking against it looks like when done.

### W2: Reference base of six sources is below the floor for a full research article

The literature review invokes a decades-long research tradition while the reference list contains six entries, none of them a synthesis or meta-analysis. This is simultaneously a formal shortfall for the article type and the proximate cause of W1: a paper that has not engaged the synthesis literature has no instrument for positioning itself within it. I judge this fixable in revision rather than disqualifying, because the six cited works are used precisely rather than ornamentally, but it must be fixed substantively rather than by padding.

- **Severity**: Major
- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §2 ¶1 "Research on technology acceptance has long proposed that perceived usefulness"`
- **Confidence**: 5 — routine desk screen; reference-base adequacy for indexed venues is a judgment I make weekly.

### W3: Modesty framing substitutes for a contribution argument, and article type is never declared

"Modest, design-bounded evidence," "incremental data point," and "offered as an incremental, design-bounded contribution" recur across Abstract, §2, and §7 without a single specification of what the increment is. The effect is that self-limitation reads as a rigor signal while doing the work that a contribution statement should do, and the mismatch between full-article format and one-correlation payload is never confronted. Independent of W1: even a fully benchmarked version of this study is a research note, and the paper needs to say so and argue for its chosen type.

- **Severity**: Major
- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: Abstract "The findings offer modest, design-bounded evidence that perceived usefulness tracks with LMS engagement among undergraduates"`
- **Confidence**: 4 — sufficiency and article-type calls are venue-relative, and another venue's threshold could differ.

### W4: No tables or figures at all, including the scatterplot the analysis relies on

A quantitative submission arrives with zero exhibits. Most consequentially, §3.4 asserts an assumption check based on scatterplot inspection while the scatterplot is never shown, so the reader is asked to accept an evidentiary step on description alone. A descriptive table (item-level means, use-category distribution, year-level composition) would also let readers assess sample composition, which is currently asserted only as spanning four year levels with no distribution.

- **Severity**: Minor
- **Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §4 Results and §3.4 Analysis — expected at least one table or figure, including the scatterplot cited for the linearity and outlier check; checked Abstract through §7 and all section bodies`
- **Confidence**: 5 — direct inspection of the submitted text for exhibits and callouts.

### W5: Shared variance is described verbally instead of reported

The Results section characterises the proportion of shared variance as "modest" and leaves it at that, when the number (≈18%) is one line of arithmetic and is the quantity most readers will want in order to judge practical importance. Verbal substitution for an available statistic is a reporting-hygiene lapse in an otherwise well-reported paper.

- **Severity**: Minor
- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §4 Results "The proportion of variance shared by the two measures was accordingly modest"`
- **Confidence**: 4 — reporting-convention check against standard quantitative expectations.

### W6: Discussion and Limitations duplicate each other, and the finding is restated four times

The cross-sectional caution, the self-report caution, and the single-site caution each appear in both §5 and §6; r = .42 is stated in Abstract, §4, §5, and §7. In a manuscript this short, restatement occupies space that the missing benchmarking and exhibits should occupy. Merging §6 into §5 or reserving §5 strictly for interpretation would tighten the paper without losing content.

- **Severity**: Minor
- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §6 Limitations "Third, the cross-sectional design precludes any causal or temporal inference."`
- **Confidence**: 4 — structural read of section functions and overlap.
