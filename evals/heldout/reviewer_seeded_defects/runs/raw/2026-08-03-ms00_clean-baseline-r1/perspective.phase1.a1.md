## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data handling, statistical reporting, and reproducibility supports would survive scrutiny by competent peers in the paper's own field. From where I sit as the cross-disciplinary reader, methodological rigour matters because a reader from an adjacent field cannot independently verify craft they cannot see: if the procedures are underspecified, the outsider has no way to judge how far the findings travel. I do not score this dimension; I note only that its outcome constrains how confidently any interdisciplinary implication can be stated.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by the domain reviewer).** This dimension checks that the manuscript's claims are consistent with current evidence in its home domain, that prior literature is characterised faithfully, and that domain-specific terminology, constructs, and reported results contain no factual errors. For adjacent-field readers this is the substrate of trust: a misdescribed construct or a misattributed prior finding is far more damaging when it is imported across a disciplinary boundary, because the borrower usually lacks the local knowledge to catch it. Scoring belongs to the domain specialist, not to me.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the discourse-analysis reviewer, also open to methodology).** This dimension concerns whether the central thesis holds together internally, whether the evidence presented actually licenses the conclusions drawn, and whether any reasoning defect is serious enough to undercut the main argument. Coherence is the mechanism by which an argument becomes portable: a reader outside the field follows the logic before the local detail, so an unsupported inferential leap is what an outsider notices first and trusts least. I do not hold this scoring pen either.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, my dimension).** This is the dimension I own. It asks whether the paper's framing, its definitions of key constructs, and its stated implications are genuinely accessible to a competent reader from a neighbouring discipline, and whether any claim the paper makes about relevance beyond its own subfield is actually substantiated rather than asserted. Two failure modes sit here in tension. One is opacity: unglossed jargon, undefined constructs, an introduction that presumes the reader already inhabits the literature, or findings whose scope conditions are never made explicit, so an outsider cannot tell what the result means or where it applies. The other is overreach: sweeping claims of transferability, policy relevance, or generality that the study's own evidence base cannot carry. My job is to judge accessibility and substantiation together, and to say plainly whether a reader in an adjacent field could both understand and appropriately use this work.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief).** This dimension covers organisation, clarity of exposition, quality and legibility of figures and tables, and conformity with the venue's conventions. It overlaps with my concern for accessibility but is distinct from it: a paper can be cleanly written yet still hermetic to outsiders, or awkwardly written yet conceptually portable. Scoring rests with the editorial role; I confine myself to accessibility of framing rather than prose quality per se.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it advances something original and significant for that readership. Fit and cross-disciplinary reach are related but not identical: a paper can be a perfect fit for a narrow specialist venue while remaining opaque to adjacent fields, or broadly legible while contributing little that is new. The editorial role scores this; my findings on relevance and substantiation are inputs to that judgement, not substitutes for it.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether key constructs are explicitly defined rather than assumed, whether the introduction and discussion state the problem and its stakes in terms a competent adjacent-field reader could follow, whether measurement of central concepts is described well enough for an outsider to know what was actually captured, whether scope and boundary conditions are stated, and whether every claim of relevance beyond the home subfield is tied to specific evidence in this study rather than asserted or borrowed from citation alone.
what_triggers_block: Core constructs are used without operational definition, or the paper's headline implications for other fields, practice, or policy are asserted with no evidentiary chain back to its own data, such that an adjacent-field reader would be unable to determine what the findings mean or would be actively misled about their reach.
what_triggers_warn: The work is broadly followable and its main claims are anchored, but accessibility gaps remain, such as unglossed field-specific jargon, a construct defined only implicitly through its measure, generalisation beyond the sampled population stated without explicit scope caveats, or interdisciplinary implications that are plausible yet argued thinly rather than substantiated.
