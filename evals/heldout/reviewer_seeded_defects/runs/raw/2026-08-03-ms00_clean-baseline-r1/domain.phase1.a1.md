## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role):** This dimension asks whether the study's design and execution would survive scrutiny from a competent methodologist in the field: is the sampling frame defensible, are the measures described well enough to be reproduced, are statistical procedures reported with the estimates, uncertainty, and assumption checks the field expects, and is enough material (instrument, data, analysis code) disclosed that another team could re-run the work? As a domain reviewer I read this dimension as owned by the methodology reviewer, so I will not score it, but I note that domain-accuracy judgements can be entangled with it — for example, a construct being measured in a way the domain literature has abandoned. Where I encounter such overlap I will keep my own scoring confined to the substantive-claims question and flag the design concern narratively rather than as a D1 score.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role):** This is my dimension. It asks whether every substantive claim the manuscript makes about its subject matter is consistent with the current state of evidence in that field; whether the prior literature it invokes is described as those sources actually describe themselves, rather than as convenient paraphrase or citation-by-reputation; and whether field-specific vocabulary, constructs, instruments, and reported quantities are used with their accepted technical meanings. Failures here include misattributed findings, models or frameworks described with the wrong provenance or the wrong content, terminology used loosely in ways that change the claim, numeric or unit errors in domain results, engagement with a literature that stops well short of the live debate, and inferential claims about mechanism or effect that the cited domain evidence does not license. The bar is factual correctness and faithful representation, not agreement with the authors' position.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, devil's-advocate and methodology roles):** This dimension asks whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together on its own terms — whether stated premises actually entail the conclusions drawn, whether evidence introduced is the evidence the argument needs, and whether there are gaps, equivocations, or reasoning errors serious enough to undermine the main claim rather than merely blemish it. It is a structural question about the chain of reasoning rather than a question about whether each individual factual link is true. It is not open to my role, so I will not assign it a score; if I find that a domain misstatement also breaks an inference, I will describe the domain error and leave the coherence verdict to the reviewers who own it.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high, perspective role):** This dimension asks whether a reader from a neighbouring field could enter the manuscript successfully: are framings and definitions supplied rather than assumed, is jargon either avoided or unpacked, are the stated implications legible outside the immediate specialty, and — crucially — where the manuscript reaches into another discipline's territory, are those borrowed claims actually supported rather than gestured at? The owner is the perspective reviewer and it is outside my eligibility, so no score from me. My contribution to it is indirect: if a claim reaching across a disciplinary boundary is factually wrong in the domain I am assessing, that belongs in my D2 finding.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal, editor-in-chief role):** This dimension covers craft and presentation: whether the manuscript is organised so a reader can follow the argument's development, whether exposition is clear at the sentence and paragraph level, whether figures and tables are legible, correctly labelled, and genuinely informative, and whether the submission follows the conventions of the venue it targets. It is scored by the editor-in-chief role and is not eligible for mine, so I will not score it. If prose is so imprecise that I cannot determine what domain claim is being made, I will report that as an obstacle to my own assessment rather than as a writing score.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, editor-in-chief role):** This dimension asks two joined questions: does the manuscript belong in the configured venue given that venue's scope and readership, and does it add something original and consequential enough to warrant the space? Novelty here is relative to what the field already holds, and significance is relative to what this readership needs. The editor-in-chief owns it and I am not eligible to score it. My domain reading can inform it — for instance, if the contribution is presented as new but the domain literature already establishes it — and where that happens I will document the specific prior work as a domain-accuracy finding and leave the fit-and-contribution verdict to its owner.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether each substantive domain claim matches current field evidence, whether cited prior work is represented as those sources actually state it, whether field-specific constructs, instruments, and terminology carry their accepted technical meanings, and whether reported domain quantities, units, and effect descriptions are internally and externally correct.
what_triggers_block: One or more central claims — claims the manuscript's contribution rests on — are inconsistent with well-established domain evidence, or a key cited source is materially misrepresented, such that correcting the record would require substantive rework of the paper's substantive argument.
what_triggers_warn: Localised domain problems that do not unseat the main claim: imprecise or non-standard use of a field term, an overstated generalisation beyond what the cited evidence supports, a citation that is loosely rather than falsely characterised, or omission of directly relevant recent domain work that should be engaged.
what_triggers_fatal: Fabricated or non-existent sources, invented or unverifiable domain results presented as established, or domain errors so pervasive across the manuscript that no revision short of a different study could make the substantive account trustworthy.
