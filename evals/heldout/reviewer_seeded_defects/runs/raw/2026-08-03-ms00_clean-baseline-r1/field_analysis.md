**Verification flag for the panel (factual, must be checked before any recommendation is issued):** all six references carry DOIs under the `10.5555` prefix, which is widely used as a placeholder/test prefix in DOI documentation rather than as a live registrant prefix, and none of the six journal titles matches a readily recognizable indexed journal. This may be an artifact of manuscript anonymization or of placeholder drafting. Either way it is checkable and it outranks every other finding in the review: if the citation base cannot be verified, the paper's entire claim to being "consistent with prior work" is unevaluable. Assigned to Peer Reviewer 2 below.

## Recommended Target Journals (Top 3)

1. **Research in Learning Technology** (ALT, open access) — Best structural fit. It publishes short original-research articles and does not require theoretical novelty as an entry condition; a bounded single-site correlational finding is a recognized article type there. Lowest reframing cost.
2. **Journal of Information Technology Education: Research** — Routinely publishes single-institution, TAM-adjacent survey work at roughly this scale. Tolerant of incremental contribution provided instrumentation is documented, which is currently the paper's weakest reporting area.
3. **Education and Information Technologies** (Springer) — Honest stretch target. High publication volume and receptive to survey work, but the novelty bar would require at least one addition: a second construct, a subgroup comparison, or a log-data validation subsample. Submitting as-is risks a "too limited to warrant publication" rejection rather than a revise decision.

Explicitly not recommended at current scope: *Computers & Education*, *British Journal of Educational Technology*, *International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education*, *Online Learning*.

## Reviewer Configuration Cards

### Reviewer Configuration Card #1

**Role**: EIC
**Display role**: Journal-Fit Reviewer
**Identity Description**: Senior Editor of *Research in Learning Technology*, background in higher education digital learning and academic development; handles a high monthly volume of LMS/technology-acceptance survey submissions and is the person who decides whether a manuscript enters peer review at all, which article type it enters as, and whether the topic is already saturated.
**Review Focus**:
  1. **Sufficiency, not validity.** Determine whether one bivariate correlation from one site clears the journal's contribution threshold, and if so, under which article type — full paper versus research note / short report. State the article-type judgment explicitly rather than leaving it implicit in a revise/reject verdict.
  2. **Saturation test against the existing literature stock.** Pooled perceived-usefulness–to-use associations already exist in the acceptance-model meta-analytic record. Assess whether the manuscript tells the reader what its r = .42 adds beyond confirming a known pooled estimate, and whether the paper benchmarks itself against that record (it currently does not).
  3. **Reference base adequacy as an editorial screen.** Six references for a topic with a thirty-year literature is below the floor for a full research article at any indexed venue. Judge whether this is fixable in revision or signals that the study was designed without full engagement with the field.
**Will particularly care about**: Whether the manuscript's disciplined refusal to overclaim is a genuine scientific virtue or a rhetorical device that converts thinness into apparent rigor — i.e. whether "we make only a modest claim" is doing work that "we have little to report" should be doing.
**Possible blind spots**: Will not audit the statistical treatment of an ordinal single-item outcome, and may accept the reported α = .88 as sufficient instrument evidence. May also undervalue the paper's genuine reporting hygiene because the topic feels routine. Synthesizer should ensure the Journal-Fit verdict does not overwrite R1's measurement findings.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #2

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 1
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 1
**Identity Description**: Quantitative survey methodologist with a psychometrics background, specializing in self-report measurement quality and correlational designs in education research; teaches survey sampling and routinely reviews manuscripts where a single-item measure carries the outcome variable.
**Review Focus**:
  1. **Single-item ordinal outcome against a Pearson estimate.** A five-point frequency item as the dependent variable imposes a ceiling on the observable correlation through coarse categorization and unmodeled measurement error; r = .42 is therefore an attenuated estimate of an unknown latent association. Assess whether the paper acknowledges attenuation (it does not) and whether the Spearman check (ρ = .40) addresses the parametric assumption while leaving the coarseness problem untouched.
  2. **Sampling and the missing denominator.** "All enrolled undergraduates were eligible" with 233 received responses, but total enrollment is never reported, so no response rate can be computed and the extent of nonresponse bias is unassessable. The voluntary-response limitation is acknowledged in prose but not quantified.
  3. **Internal contradiction in the anonymity/deduplication account.** Section 3.3 states no identifying information was collected and responses could not be linked to individuals; Section 3.1 states 5 duplicate entries were removed. Ask by what mechanism duplicates were identified under those conditions. This is a concrete, answerable question, and the answer determines whether the ethics statement or the data-cleaning statement requires correction.
  4. **Instrument adaptation is undocumented.** The scale is described as "adapted from Costa and Wren (2019)" with no report of what was changed, why, or whether the adapted six items were checked for dimensionality. Cronbach's α establishes internal consistency, not structural validity, and cannot substitute for it.
  5. **Power statement framing and unused sample capacity.** With n = 214 the sensitivity claim (detecting r ≥ .19) is credible, but the paper reports no descriptive breakdown by year level despite noting all four levels are represented, and runs no covariate or subgroup analysis. Assess whether the analysis extracts what the sample can support.
**Will particularly care about**: Whether the reported coefficient is presented with the measurement caveats that would let a reader calibrate it, rather than as a clean point estimate that happens to be flanked by a general limitations section.
**Possible blind spots**: Will not judge whether the finding matters to the educational technology field, and may not notice that the acceptance-model literature is cited without its canonical sources. Tends to recommend adding measurement machinery to a paper whose declared scope may not warrant it — synthesizer should weigh this against the deliberate narrowness of the research question.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #3

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 2
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 2
**Identity Description**: Senior educational technology researcher working in the technology-acceptance tradition, with a decade of work on LMS adoption in higher education and prior involvement in meta-analytic synthesis of acceptance-model predictors; knows the canonical instrument lineage and the post-2021 shift in baseline LMS use.
**Review Focus**:
  1. **Reference verification as first-order business.** All six DOIs sit under the `10.5555` prefix, commonly used as a documentation placeholder, and the cited journal titles are not recognizable indexed venues. Verify each reference before assessing anything else. If the citation base is unverifiable, every claim of consistency with prior work collapses and this finding dominates the review.
  2. **Construct precision within the acceptance tradition.** In the canonical formulation, perceived usefulness predicts behavioral intention, with use as a downstream outcome; the paper collapses this into a direct perception–use association without naming or justifying the shortcut. Assess whether the theoretical omission is defensible under a purely correlational framing or whether it misrepresents the tradition being invoked.
  3. **Literature currency and coverage.** Six sources, none earlier than 2018, no foundational acceptance literature, no UTAUT-family work, no post-2021 studies, and no engagement with the learning-analytics literature on LMS behavioral measures — despite the paper's own limitations section relying on precisely that literature's central finding.
  4. **Benchmarking against known effect sizes.** The paper says its finding is "consistent with prior technology-acceptance research" but never states what the prior pooled estimate is, so the reader cannot tell whether r = .42 is typical, high, or low. Without that anchor, "incremental data point" is an assertion rather than a demonstrated position in a distribution — a gap made sharper by the paper's own citation of Song (2018) on cross-institutional variability.
**Will particularly care about**: Whether the manuscript's contribution claim survives contact with what the field already knows, and whether "one more data point" is a real contribution when the paper does not locate the point on any existing map.
**Possible blind spots**: May under-weight the practical value of the study for institutional platform administrators, and may push toward theoretical elaboration that would break the paper's deliberately narrow frame. Likely to overlook the anonymity/deduplication contradiction, which sits in R1's territory.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #4

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 3
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 3
**Identity Description**: Director of a university Centre for Teaching and Learning who also leads institutional learning-analytics work — runs the LMS platform, owns its clickstream data, designs student onboarding programmes, and is the person the paper's practical recommendation is nominally addressed to. Not an acceptance-model researcher and not a methodologist; evaluates the paper by whether it could change a decision.
**Review Focus**:
  1. **The unused data that was sitting on the same server.** The study surveyed students about their use of a platform that logs their every access, at their own institution, on the same population, in the same window. The paper flags self-report divergence as a limitation without explaining why log data was not obtained or why a validation subsample was infeasible. From an institutional standpoint this is a data-governance and ethics-approval question with known answers, not an insurmountable barrier — and if a barrier existed, name it.
  2. **Actionability of the onboarding implication.** Ask what institutional decision changes at r = .42 that would not change at r = .20 or r = .60. The onboarding recommendation is a plausible intuition that the correlation neither supports nor tests; the paper concedes this in a clause and then keeps the recommendation in the Discussion and Abstract anyway.
  3. **Omitted variables that practitioners know dominate LMS use.** Whether assignment submission is gated through the LMS, whether instructors require it, whether assessment schedules cluster, whether a mobile app exists, and course modality — these determine LMS access frequency far more than perception does. The paper gestures at "course requirements and assessment schedules" in Results but measures none of them, which is precisely why a single-site estimate cannot transfer.
  4. **Missing transfer parameters.** Neither the platform (Canvas / Moodle / Blackboard / other) nor the point in the academic term when the three-week survey window fell is reported. Both are necessary for any other institution to judge whether the finding applies to them; without them the "single-institution" limitation is unremediable rather than merely acknowledged.
**Will particularly care about**: Whether a practitioner reading this paper could do anything differently on Monday morning, and whether the paper is honest that the answer is probably no.
**Possible blind spots**: Will push for scope expansion (log data, contextual variables, multi-site) that a deliberately bounded descriptive note is not obliged to carry, and may undervalue the paper's methodological restraint as timidity. Has no view on instrument validity or citation adequacy.

## Review Strategy Recommendations

**Special characteristics requiring particular attention**

- **The central question for this panel is sufficiency, not validity.** Unusually, the paper's own limitations section pre-empts the four critiques a reviewer would normally lead with: causality, generalizability, self-report validity, and volunteer bias. All four are correctly stated. Reviewers must therefore not spend their findings restating limitations the authors already conceded; the unresolved question is whether a correctly-executed study this small constitutes a publishable contribution, and if so in what article format. Any reviewer whose findings duplicate Section 6 has added nothing.
- **Restraint is not a substitute for contribution, and reviewers must not treat it as one.** The manuscript's calibrated language is genuinely good practice and should be credited as such. It also functions to make thinness look like discipline. Both readings need to be on the table, and the panel should not let the paper's good epistemic manners buy it a verdict its substance does not support.
- **Two concrete, checkable defects sit outside the acknowledged limitations** and should carry disproportionate weight because they are factual rather than interpretive: (a) the anonymity claim versus the removal of 5 duplicates is an internal contradiction with only a small number of possible resolutions, one of which requires correcting the ethics statement; (b) the reference DOIs and journal titles cannot be verified as they stand. Item (b) is potentially decision-determining and must be resolved before any recommendation is finalized.
- **The missing denominator is the quietest serious gap.** "All enrolled undergraduates were eligible" plus 233 responses yields no response rate, which means the voluntary-response limitation the authors acknowledge cannot be bounded even approximately. This is a two-sentence fix that materially changes what the sample can be said to represent.

**Complementarity and tension across reviewers**

- **Coverage is deliberately partitioned to avoid overlap**: R1 owns measurement and sampling internals; R2 owns literature, construct fidelity, and reference verification; R3 owns data-source choice, contextual confounds, and practical actionability; the Journal-Fit Reviewer owns article-type and threshold judgment. No two cards claim the same finding, and each card names the territory it does not enter.
- **Expect a three-way expansion conflict.** R1 will want more measurement apparatus, R2 more theory and literature, R3 more data sources and context variables. Each recommendation is individually reasonable; together they would convert a bounded correlational note into a different and much larger study. The synthesizer must not simply concatenate the three, and the Journal-Fit Reviewer's article-type judgment is the arbitration mechanism: if the paper is repositioned as a research note, most expansion demands become out of scope and the reporting fixes (denominator, deduplication mechanism, instrument adaptation, platform and timing, effect-size benchmark) become the actionable set.
- **Anticipated axis of disagreement on the verdict**: R1 is likely to find the study internally sound but under-reported and therefore fixable; R2 and the Journal-Fit Reviewer are likely to question whether the contribution justifies publication at all; R3 is likely to find the practical claim unsupported while sympathizing with the modest framing. A split verdict here is informative rather than a failure of the panel, and the synthesizer should preserve it rather than average it away.
- **Register**: standard rigorous review. The manuscript is a polished pre-submission draft, not a first draft, so developmental-feedback register is not indicated. Tone stays professional and non-punitive; the verdict tracks the evidence against the criteria regardless of how constructively it is worded.
