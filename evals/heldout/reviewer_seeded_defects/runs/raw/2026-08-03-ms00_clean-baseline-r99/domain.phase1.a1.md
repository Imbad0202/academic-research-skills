## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design and execution would survive scrutiny by a competent methodologist in the manuscript's field: whether the sampling and data-collection strategy can bear the weight of the inferences drawn, whether measurement instruments are described well enough to be judged, whether statistical procedures are reported with the estimates, uncertainty, and assumption checks the field expects, and whether enough procedural detail (instrument wording, analytic steps, data availability) exists for another researcher to attempt a replication. From my seat as the domain reviewer I read but do not score this dimension; it belongs to the methodology reviewer, and I will confine myself to flagging any domain-side observation that might inform their judgement rather than issuing a methodological verdict of my own.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role — mine).** This is the dimension I own. It asks whether every substantive claim the manuscript makes about its subject matter is consistent with the current state of evidence in that field; whether cited and paraphrased prior work is characterised faithfully rather than stretched, flattened, or attributed with findings it does not contain; whether field-specific constructs and terminology are used with their accepted technical meanings rather than as loose synonyms; and whether reported results, quantities, and definitions are internally and externally factually correct. Failure here includes both commission (asserting something the domain literature contradicts, or misdescribing a named theory, model, or instrument) and consequential omission (presenting a claim as settled or novel when the domain's established evidence base says otherwise). My judgement is about factual and interpretive fidelity to the field, not about whether the argument is elegant or the writing is clean.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, discourse-analysis and methodology roles).** This dimension concerns the internal logic of the manuscript: whether the central thesis remains the same thesis from abstract to conclusion, whether the evidence actually adduced is of the type and strength that the stated conclusions require, and whether there are reasoning defects — conflation of association with causation, equivocation on key terms, unsupported generalisation beyond the studied population, circularity — severe enough to undermine the core argument rather than merely blemish a passage. I am not an eligible scorer for this dimension and will not assign it a rating; where a domain-level factual problem also happens to break an inferential chain, I will note the domain fact and leave the coherence verdict to the roles that own it.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, perspective role).** This dimension evaluates whether a reader from an adjacent discipline can enter the manuscript successfully: whether framing establishes why the problem matters outside its immediate specialty, whether specialised constructs are defined at first use rather than assumed, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplinary boundaries is backed by evidence from the discipline it reaches into rather than asserted by analogy. It is owned by the perspective reviewer and is outside my scoring remit.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, editor-in-chief role).** This dimension covers craft and presentation: whether sections are ordered and proportioned so the argument unfolds legibly, whether exposition is clear at sentence and paragraph level, whether figures and tables are self-sufficient, correctly labelled, and genuinely informative, and whether the manuscript observes the target venue's structural and stylistic conventions. The editor-in-chief role scores it; I do not.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, editor-in-chief role).** This dimension asks the editorial question of whether the work belongs in the configured venue at all and whether it advances knowledge enough to justify the space: originality relative to what is already published, significance for the venue's actual readership, and alignment of scope and depth with what that venue publishes. It is a mandatory dimension held by the editor-in-chief; my domain findings may bear on the originality half of that judgement, but the score is not mine to give.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Each substantive domain claim traced to whether it is supported, contradicted, or unaddressed by current evidence in educational technology and higher education; fidelity of every characterisation of prior work and of any named theory, model, framework, or measurement instrument to what those sources actually say; correct and consistent technical use of field constructs and their operational definitions; and factual correctness and internal consistency of reported domain quantities, definitions, and stated findings.
what_triggers_block: A central claim of the manuscript is contradicted by well-established evidence in the field, or a named prior work, theory, or instrument is misrepresented in a way that materially props up a headline conclusion, such that the domain contribution cannot stand without substantial rework.
what_triggers_warn: Localised domain inaccuracies that do not carry a headline conclusion — imprecise or drifting use of a field construct, an over-general or under-qualified characterisation of the prior literature, a stale or incomplete evidence base on a secondary point, or a terminological slip correctable by revision.
what_triggers_fatal: Fabricated or non-existent domain evidence, citations that do not support and cannot be reconciled with the assertions attached to them, or reported domain results that are internally impossible or contradict the manuscript's own stated data, indicating the factual record is unreliable rather than merely mistaken.
