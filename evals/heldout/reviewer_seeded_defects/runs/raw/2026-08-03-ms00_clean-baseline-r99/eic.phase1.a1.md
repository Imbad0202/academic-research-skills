## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer):** This dimension asks whether the study's design and execution would survive scrutiny from a competent methodologist in the paper's own field. It covers how the sample was obtained and justified, how the data were collected and cleaned, whether the analytic choices match the design, whether statistics are reported completely enough to be interpreted rather than merely asserted, and whether a reader has enough procedural detail (instruments, code, data availability, analysis steps) to attempt reproduction. As editor I note that this dimension is mandatory, so a fatal defect here maps straight to rejection under F1, and it is not mine to score — it belongs to the methodology role.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by the domain reviewer):** This dimension governs factual fidelity to the field: whether the substantive claims are consistent with the current evidence base, whether cited prior work is characterised accurately rather than conscripted, and whether field-specific terminology, constructs, instruments, and reported results are used correctly. Misattribution of an established finding, or use of a technical term in a sense the field does not accept, lives here. It is a mandatory dimension scored by the domain specialist, not by editorial oversight.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the discourse-analysis reviewer, methodology also eligible):** This dimension tests whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together on its own terms — that the stated conclusions are the ones the presented evidence can bear, that intermediate steps do not quietly change the claim's scope, and that no reasoning defect (circularity, equivocation, causal language attached to non-causal evidence, unstated load-bearing assumptions) undermines the core argument. Because it is mandatory, coherence failures escalate the decision even when the data themselves are sound. Editorial oversight does not score it.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by the perspective reviewer):** This dimension asks whether a reader from an adjacent discipline can enter the paper: whether framing situates the work in terms outsiders recognise, whether constructs and jargon are defined at first use, and whether any claim that the work matters beyond its home subfield is actually backed by argument or evidence rather than asserted in a closing paragraph. It is high priority rather than mandatory, so a block here triggers major revision via F4 without being a rejection trigger. It is scored by the perspective role.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by editorial oversight):** This is mine. It concerns craft and presentation: whether the manuscript's organisation lets a reader follow the argument in the order it is given, whether exposition is clear and free of ambiguity that changes meaning, whether figures and tables are legible, self-labelled, and genuinely informative rather than decorative or redundant with the text, and whether the submission observes the conventions expected at the configured venue (sectioning, abstract, reporting format, reference style, length norms). It is normal priority, so problems here shape revision requests rather than acceptance in principle; there is no fatal tier for me to apply.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by editorial oversight):** Also mine, and the heavier of my two. It asks two linked questions: does this manuscript belong in this venue for this readership, and does it add something the literature did not already have? That means judging originality against existing work, the significance and scope of the increment, whether the paper's genre and depth match what the venue publishes, and whether the stated contribution is the contribution actually delivered. Because it is mandatory, a fatal determination here routes to rejection under F1 and a block routes to major revision under F2.

## Scoring Plan

### D5: writing_and_structure

dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: Whether sections appear in an order that supports the argument, whether abstract/introduction/methods/results/discussion each do their assigned job, whether prose is unambiguous at the sentence level, whether every figure and table is numbered, captioned, self-interpretable and referenced in text, and whether reference style, reporting format, and length conform to the configured venue's conventions.
what_triggers_block: Presentation defects severe enough to obstruct assessment — for example results reported only in prose with no tabulation of the numbers being discussed, figures or tables that are absent, unlabelled, or inconsistent with the text, missing required sections, or organisation so disordered that the reader cannot reconstruct what was done in what order.
what_triggers_warn: Localised presentation weaknesses that do not obstruct assessment — redundancy between text and tables, undefined abbreviations, uneven section depth, an abstract that under-reports the actual findings, inconsistent reference formatting, or captions that require the body text to be intelligible.
### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution

dimension_id: D6
what_to_look_for: Whether the topic, genre, and depth of treatment match what the configured venue publishes for its readership, whether the claimed contribution is stated explicitly and is positioned against the specific prior work it advances beyond, whether the increment is substantive rather than a re-instantiation of settled findings in a new local setting, and whether the contribution actually delivered matches the contribution advertised in title and abstract.
what_triggers_block: A contribution that is real but too thin or too narrowly scoped for the venue's readership as submitted — no articulated advance over existing literature, positioning that omits the closest prior work, or a scope/depth mismatch with the venue that would need substantive additional work rather than rewriting to remedy.
what_triggers_warn: Fit and contribution are defensible but under-argued — the novelty claim is present yet asserted rather than demonstrated against named prior work, implications for the venue's readership are left implicit, or the framing undersells or overreaches slightly relative to what the paper delivers.
what_triggers_fatal: No admissible contribution for this venue at all — the work is substantively duplicative of existing published findings, or the subject matter falls outside the venue's scope such that no revision within the manuscript's design could make it publishable here.
