## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology-owned).** From a methods-rigor standpoint, this dimension asks whether the study's design can actually bear the weight of the inferences drawn from it. I read it as covering: whether the research question is matched to an appropriate design; whether sampling, recruitment, and eligibility are specified well enough to know who the results describe; whether measurement instruments are documented with evidence of reliability and validity; whether data handling (missingness, exclusions, transformations) is disclosed rather than implied; whether the statistical reporting includes the quantities a reader needs to judge precision and uncertainty rather than significance alone; and whether the reproducibility affordances (analysis specification, materials, data or code availability, ethics/consent statements) reach the bar the field expects. This is a mandatory dimension, so failures here can be decision-determining rather than merely advisory.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain-owned).** This dimension governs whether substantive claims are faithful to the current evidence base of the field, whether cited prior work is characterised as those sources actually argue, and whether field-specific constructs, terminology, instruments, and reported results are used correctly. I do not score this dimension; it is owned by the domain reviewer, and I will not substitute my methodological reading for their substantive verdict. Where a methodological concern of mine happens to touch a domain fact, my obligation is to flag it as a methods observation within my own dimensions rather than to encroach on D2.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, DA-owned, methodology-eligible).** This dimension concerns whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether each claim is actually licensed by the evidence offered for it, whether the chain from data to conclusion contains no missing or reversed steps, and whether there are inferential fallacies serious enough to undermine the core argument. My eligible angle on it is narrower than the DA reviewer's: I assess coherence as a question of inferential warrant — whether the strength, scope, and direction of the stated conclusions are proportionate to what the described design and analysis can support, and whether limitations acknowledged in one section are quietly abandoned in another. The dimension is mandatory, so an argument whose core inference does not follow is a decision-level problem.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, perspective-owned).** This dimension asks whether framing, definitions, and stated implications are intelligible and defensible to readers from adjacent fields, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplinary boundaries is substantiated rather than asserted. It is owned by the perspective reviewer and is outside my scoring remit. I note only that it carries high priority, meaning a block here can itself drive a revision outcome even without a mandatory-dimension failure.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, EiC-owned).** This dimension covers manuscript organisation, clarity of exposition, the quality and self-sufficiency of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's presentational conventions. It belongs to the editor-in-chief role. I will not score it, and I will resist the common temptation to convert presentational weaknesses into methodological findings; if something is genuinely unreportable rather than merely poorly written, that belongs in D1 as a reporting-completeness issue, and I must say so explicitly rather than blur the two.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, EiC-owned).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it delivers an original and significant enough contribution for that readership. It is mandatory but EiC-owned, so I neither score nor lobby on it. My methodological findings may be inputs an editor uses when weighing contribution, but the judgement of sufficiency and fit is not mine to make in this dispatch.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor

dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: Explicit design–question match; sampling frame, recruitment route, response/participation accounting, and eligibility criteria; full instrument documentation with reliability and validity evidence; disclosure of missing data, exclusions, and any derived or transformed variables; analysis specification stated before results (including whether any test was pre-specified or exploratory); effect estimates with uncertainty intervals and exact test statistics rather than significance labels alone; ethics approval and informed-consent statements; and reproducibility affordances such as available instruments, data, or analysis code.
what_triggers_block: A core reported inference cannot be reconstructed or evaluated because a load-bearing methodological element is absent or internally inconsistent — for example no usable description of who was sampled and how, an outcome or predictor whose operationalisation is never defined, or reported statistics that are mutually incompatible or lack the uncertainty information needed to judge them.
what_triggers_warn: The design and analysis are reconstructable and the reported numbers hang together, but rigor is materially thinned by fixable reporting gaps — for example missing reliability evidence for an instrument, undisclosed handling of missing responses, absent effect sizes or intervals alongside otherwise complete tests, no ethics or consent statement, or no availability statement for materials, data, or code.
what_triggers_fatal: The methodological foundation is unsalvageable within a revision cycle because the design cannot in principle support the class of claim being made, or because the reporting shows evidence of a fundamental integrity failure — for example results that cannot arise from the stated data structure, analyses reported on a sample that does not exist as described, or outcome definitions that appear to have shifted to fit the findings.

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether the stated conclusions stay within the inferential ceiling of the described design; whether causal, temporal, directional, or generalising language is licensed by what was actually measured; whether every claim in the abstract and discussion traces back to a specific reported result; whether limitations conceded in one section are honoured in the conclusions rather than quietly dropped; and whether the argument's intermediate steps are all present rather than assumed.
what_triggers_block: The central claim rests on an inferential leap the design cannot support — for example causal or directional conclusions drawn from a structure that fixes no temporal order, or headline conclusions that are broader in population or magnitude than any reported result licenses — such that the thesis as written is not established even though a defensible narrower claim exists.
what_triggers_warn: The central thesis survives scrutiny but coherence is locally strained — for example over-strong verbs in the abstract relative to a hedged results section, a discussion claim without an identifiable supporting result, or a stated limitation that is not carried through into the concluding language.
what_triggers_fatal: The argument is self-defeating at its core, with the manuscript's own reported evidence contradicting its central thesis, or with two load-bearing claims that cannot both be true, so that no revision short of a different argument could rescue the paper's stated conclusion.
