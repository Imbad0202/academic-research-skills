### D3: argumentative_coherence
score: warn
trigger: "Localised inferential overreach, drifting or inconsistently used construct definitions"

The central correlational claim — that in this sample perceived usefulness and self-reported LMS use covary at r = .42 — is stated at the strength the design licenses, reported with a confidence interval, checked with a rank-order coefficient, and repeatedly insulated from causal reading. That claim survives adversarial pressure and I do not block it. What does not survive is the surrounding layer of claims the manuscript builds on top of it: the abstract restates the finding in behavioural terms ("engagement") that no measure in the paper indexes; the "consistent with prior work" claim that constitutes the paper's stated contribution is never given numeric content and, as framed against acknowledged between-instrument and between-site variability, could not have been falsified by any plausible result; the research question is posed at population level while the evidence supports only respondent-level description; and the interpretation of the headline coefficient depends on an unargued premise that two self-reports collected in a single instrument from a single respondent are independent measurements. Each is repairable by qualification, added comparison, and disclosure rather than by a different study, so the score is warn and not block.

## Review Body

I read this manuscript as a deliberately modest paper that has largely earned the right to its modesty, and my job is to test whether the modesty is real or performed. It is mostly real. The Literature Review anticipates the two attacks that would ordinarily end a paper like this — reverse causation (Delgado) and self-report/log divergence (Vasquez) — and the Discussion and Limitations do not merely name them, they carry them into the wording of the conclusion. That is more discipline than is common, and I want it on the record before the criticisms.

The criticisms concentrate at the seams where the paper hands its result to a reader. The abstract's closing sentence is the clearest failure: after a paper that carefully says "self-reported use" everywhere else, the take-home line says perceived usefulness "tracks with LMS engagement." Engagement is a behavioural construct; the paper measured a single ordinal perception item and told us in §2 to read it as "perceived rather than actual engagement." The sentence readers will cite therefore asserts what §2 disclaims. This is not a stylistic quibble — it is the hedged-then-unhedged restatement pattern, and it recurs in §2's own summary of prior work ("associations between students' perceptions of an LMS and their engagement with it"), which suggests the construct slippage is habitual rather than a one-off slip.

The second attack goes at the paper's stated contribution rather than its measurement. §2 and §7 define the contribution as an incremental data point "comparable with prior work," and §5 and the abstract assert that the result is "consistent with prior technology-acceptance research." No prior coefficient appears anywhere in the manuscript. Worse, the paper has already told us that effect sizes "vary across samples and instruments" (§2) and that association strengths "varied by institution" (Song). Under those two premises, almost any positive coefficient would have been declared consistent, which makes the consistency claim close to unfalsifiable as constructed. Since comparability is the entire warrant for the paper's contribution, this is where the argument is thinnest: the empirical claim is well warranted, but the claim about what the empirical claim is *for* is not.

The third attack is the one the paper does not see at all. Both variables are self-reports elicited from the same respondent in the same instrument at the same sitting. The Vasquez citation is deployed only as a validity caveat about the use measure, never as a threat to the coefficient itself; nothing in §3.4 or §6 addresses acquiescence, consistency motif, or shared method variance, any of which inflates r between two attitudinal self-reports. The manuscript's interpretive move — treating .42 as an estimate of a substantive association comparable with prior estimates — presupposes that this inflation is negligible. That premise is neither stated nor defended, and it is precisely the premise a hostile reader attacks first.

Related, and partly a design question but argumentatively load-bearing: recruitment ran "through the institution's course-announcement channel." The manuscript never says whether that channel is inside the LMS or reached through it. If it is, respondents were sampled conditional on the very behaviour that forms the dependent variable, the low end of the frequency scale ("rarely or never") is structurally under-sampled, and the reported coefficient is an estimate within a range-restricted subpopulation. §6's fourth limitation gestures at this ("students who engage more with institutional channels may be overrepresented") but does not connect it to the specific possibility that the recruitment channel and the outcome measure are the same system — and with no population denominator and no response rate anywhere in §3.1, the reader cannot bound the threat even qualitatively. This matters for coherence because §1 poses the question at population level ("among undergraduates at one university"), whereas the evidence supports only a statement about the 214 people who answered.

Two internal-consistency problems remain. §3.1 reports removing five duplicate entries; §3.3 states that no identifying information was collected and that responses "could not be linked back to individual students." At least one of these sentences is inaccurate as written — duplicate detection requires some persistent identifier or heuristic, and if one existed the anonymity statement needs correction, while if none existed the deduplication claim needs explanation. And the abstract describes the instrument as "adapted, previously validated," which attributes to the adapted six-item version a validation the manuscript only reports for Costa and Wren's original. The α of .88 establishes internal consistency, not that the adaptation measures the same construct; the item stems are not reproduced, so a reader cannot judge how far the adaptation travelled.

Two lesser points that do not reach Major but should be fixed. First, the practical implication in §5 recommends onboarding that helps students "see concrete usefulness," which presupposes the perception-to-use direction that the same paragraph declares indistinguishable from its reverse; the hedge "suggested by, not proven by" softens the tone without repairing the logic, and the sentence should be made explicitly conditional on a direction the design cannot fix. Second, §4 describes the shared variance as "modest" without ever stating r² ≈ .18, which leaves the paper's most reader-relevant magnitude statement qualitative when it could be exact. I also note, for the seats that own contribution and methodology, that my Major issue on unevidenced comparability bears directly on D6, and that the absent denominator and unreproduced items bear on D1.
