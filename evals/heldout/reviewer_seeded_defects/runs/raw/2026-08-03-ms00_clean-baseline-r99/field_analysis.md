## Field Analysis

| Dimension | Analysis Result |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Primary Discipline** | Educational technology in higher education (learning-technology adoption / LMS engagement) |
| **Secondary Disciplines** | (1) Information systems — technology acceptance tradition (TAM/UTAUT lineage); (2) Educational measurement & psychometrics — instrument adaptation, self-report validity; (3) Learning analytics / institutional research — behavioral log data as the alternative measurement route the paper explicitly declines to use |

| **Target Journal Tier** | **Q3 realistic; Q4 floor; Q2 only with substantial strengthening.** Rationale: the *writing* is Q2-capable — the claim register is disciplined, the limitations section is unusually honest, and the causal restraint is maintained consistently from abstract to conclusion. The *evidentiary content* is not: one bivariate correlation from one institution, a single-item dependent measure, six references, no canonical technology-acceptance sources, no meta-analytic benchmarking, no tables. Q1 outlets (*Computers & Education*, *BJET*, *ETHE*) would desk-reject on novelty threshold, not on quality of execution. The manuscript's natural home is a short-report or research-note channel |
| **Paper Maturity** | **Split verdict — "pre-submission in form, early draft in substance."** Structure is complete (IMRaD + ethics + limitations + conclusion), prose is polished, citation formatting is consistent, and the argument does not overreach. But the scholarly content is under-developed: no descriptives or correlation table, no demographic breakdown, no instrument appendix, no data-availability statement, and a reference base too thin to situate the contribution. This is not a rough draft that needs cleaning — it is a clean draft that needs substance |

**Note on register (Edge Case 4 adjacent):** the manuscript is not low-quality writing; it is high-quality writing around a thin evidentiary core. Reviewers should adopt a **developmental register** — but the register governs wording only. The recommendation must still follow the evidence against the criteria, and the criteria question here is genuinely open: *is a well-reported below-threshold increment publishable?* Do not let the paper's admirable honesty about its limits function as immunity from the limits.

## Recommended Target Journals (Top 3)

1. **Research in Learning Technology** (ALT, open access, Q3) — Explicitly accepts short empirical papers and replication-style contributions; a bounded single-site association with transparent reporting fits its scope without requiring the paper to inflate its claims. Best realistic match.
2. **Contemporary Educational Technology** or **Journal of Information Technology Education: Research** (Q2/Q3) — Mainstream ed-tech outlets that publish survey-based acceptance work; both would require the canonical TAM literature to be engaged and at least one table of results, but neither demands a novel theoretical model.
3. **Australasian Journal of Educational Technology** (Q1/Q2) — Stretch target. Would need the study repositioned against meta-analytic PU–use effect sizes, plus either log-data validation or a multi-site sample. Listing it as the aspirational ceiling, not as a plausible first submission.

**Not recommended as first submission:** *Computers & Education*, *BJET*, *International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education* — novelty threshold mismatch, near-certain desk rejection.

## Reviewer Configuration Cards

### Reviewer Configuration Card #1

**Role**: EIC
**Display role**: Journal-Fit Reviewer
**Identity Description**: Associate Editor of *Research in Learning Technology*, specializing in technology adoption in post-secondary settings; has run that journal's short-paper and replication track for four years and routinely triages single-site survey submissions against the journal's "incremental but verifiable" standard.
**Review Focus**:
1. **Novelty threshold as a scope decision, not a quality judgment** — Does a single bivariate PU–use correlation clear the bar for a full research article, or should this be routed as a research note / brief report? State which, and what would change the answer.
2. **Reader value** — What can a learning-technology practitioner or researcher *do* with r = .42 from one unnamed mid-sized public university? Is the paper positioned as a replication data point (in which case it must say so explicitly and be indexed against prior effect sizes) or as a standalone finding (in which case it is under-powered as a contribution, not statistically but substantively)?
3. **Framing coherence** — The abstract, discussion, and conclusion are consistently modest. Assess whether that modesty is calibration (a strength worth protecting in revision) or whether it substitutes for the missing work of situating the finding.
**Will particularly care about**: Whether the paper's central sentence — "offered as an incremental, design-bounded contribution" — is honored by actually benchmarking the increment against existing evidence, rather than merely asserting consistency with four cited studies.
**Possible blind spots**: Likely to accept the methods section at face value and to under-scrutinize measurement detail; may also treat the reference list as a scope issue rather than a verification issue. Both must be covered by R1 and R3.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #2

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 1
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 1
**Identity Description**: Quantitative survey methodologist with a psychometrics appointment in an education research faculty; research programme on single-item versus multi-item measurement, common-method variance in self-report designs, and reporting completeness in correlational education research. Frequently serves as a statistical reviewer.
**Review Focus**:
1. **Measurement adequacy of the dependent variable** — Self-reported use is a single ordinal five-point frequency item. Pearson r is reported as primary and Spearman as a "robustness check"; argue whether that ordering should be reversed. Assess attenuation from a coarse single item, absence of any reliability evidence for the DV, and whether "several times daily" versus "a few times per week" categories can support a linear correlation claim at all.
2. **Instrument adaptation and validity evidence** — The scale is "adapted from Costa and Wren (2019)" but no adaptation is documented, no items are reproduced, and no factor structure is tested in this sample. Cronbach's α = .88 establishes internal consistency, not dimensionality or construct validity. Specify the minimum evidence needed.
3. **Reporting completeness and sampling transparency** — No population denominator, therefore no response rate and no quantifiable nonresponse bias despite the paper acknowledging voluntary-response skew; no descriptives table; no correlation matrix; no demographic breakdown beyond "spanned all four year levels"; r² is characterized as "accordingly modest" rather than stated (it is ~.18). Also evaluate the power statement — whether the sensitivity claim (>.80 power for r ≥ .19) was specified a priori or reconstructed after the fact, and whether n = 214 pre- or post-exclusion was used.
4. **Common-method variance** — Both variables come from the same instrument, same respondent, same sitting. Assess whether the paper's correlational restraint adequately addresses this or only addresses causal direction.
**Will particularly care about**: Whether the reported coefficient is defensible as an estimate at all given a single-item, same-source dependent measure — separate from the (already conceded) question of causal direction.
**Possible blind spots**: Will likely say little about the adequacy of the literature base, the field-level significance of the contribution, or research-transparency concerns. May also push for analytic elaboration (covariates, SEM) that the paper's declared scope does not require.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #3

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 2
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 2
**Identity Description**: Senior higher-education researcher specializing in learning-technology adoption, with two decades tracking the TAM/UTAUT tradition in educational settings; has co-authored a meta-analysis of perceived-usefulness effects on student system use and reviews regularly for mainstream ed-tech journals.
**Review Focus**:
1. **Literature base adequacy** — Six references, none of them canonical. Davis (1989) is absent; UTAUT is absent; the several existing meta-analyses of PU–use associations in education are absent; nothing post-2021 appears. Assess whether a paper positioned as an increment to the technology-acceptance literature can be evaluated without engaging that literature's foundations and its cumulative effect-size estimates.
2. **Benchmarking the increment** — The correct comparator for r = .42 is not four individual studies but the meta-analytic distribution of PU–use correlations. Specify what the paper must do: locate its estimate within that distribution and state whether it falls near, above, or below the pooled value. Without this, "consistent with prior research" is unfalsifiable.
3. **Construct architecture** — The acceptance tradition models PU → behavioral intention → use. This paper collapses to PU → self-reported use with no justification for omitting intention and no discussion of what that omission implies for comparability with the studies it cites. Also assess whether "perceived usefulness" is theoretically distinguished from perceived ease of use and from course-mandated use — the paper itself notes assessment schedules drive engagement but does not measure that.
4. **Contribution claim** — Evaluate whether the field needs another single-site PU–use correlation, and if not, what minimum addition (log validation, multi-site, moderator analysis, preregistered replication framing) would make it worth publishing.
**Will particularly care about**: Whether the paper's contribution claim survives contact with the mature literature it is claiming to increment — and whether the authors know that literature.
**Possible blind spots**: May undervalue the genuine merit of transparently reported, non-inflated replication evidence, and may push scope creep — demanding a full acceptance model the paper explicitly and legitimately declined to test.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #4

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 3
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 3
**Identity Description**: Metascience and research-integrity researcher who also directs an institutional learning-analytics unit; serves on a journal's reproducibility and data-availability screening panel, and negotiates data-governance approvals for LMS log-data access in practice. Reviews from the intersection of *verifiability* and *institutional feasibility* — deliberately different from R1's statistical lens and R2's domain lens.
**Review Focus**:
1. **Reference verifiability — highest priority, resolve before substantive review** — All six references share the DOI prefix `10.5555`, which is a reserved example/sandbox prefix rather than a registered publisher prefix; none of the six journal titles corresponds to an indexed journal known to this reviewer; and no work by any author is independently locatable. Establish whether these sources exist. If they do not, that finding supersedes every other comment in the review and changes the recommendation category entirely. Report factually — verification outcome first, interpretation second.
2. **Protocol internal consistency** — Section 3.3 states "no identifying information was collected, and responses could not be linked back to individual students." Section 3.1 states five duplicate entries were removed. Ask how duplicates were identified under strict anonymity. This is a protocol-coherence and ethics-reporting question, not a statistics question, and it requires a direct answer rather than a sentence of hedging.
3. **Feasibility of the declined measurement route** — The literature review cites Vasquez (2020) to establish that self-report diverges from log data, then proceeds with self-report and lists that as a limitation. From an institutional-data standpoint: the university already holds LMS access logs. What actually blocked access — ethics scope, data-governance policy, technical access, or cost? Naming the barrier turns a generic limitation into information other institutions can use; leaving it unnamed makes the limitations section decorative.
4. **Transparency infrastructure** — No data-availability statement, no materials or item wording, no analysis code, no preregistration, no institutional identification beyond "one mid-sized public university." Assess the minimum open-science package required for a finding whose entire claimed value is verifiability and comparability.
**Will particularly care about**: Whether a paper whose stated contribution is "transparently reported" meets the transparency standard it invokes — including whether its own evidentiary chain (references, protocol, materials) can be independently checked.
**Possible blind spots**: May under-engage the substantive educational-technology question and the pedagogical implications; will not assess statistical adequacy in depth. Tends toward gate-keeping register, so the synthesizer should watch that developmental framing is preserved where the underlying issues are fixable.

## Review Strategy Recommendations

**Special characteristics requiring attention**

- **Honesty is not contribution.** This manuscript is unusually well-calibrated: it declines causal language consistently, pre-empts the reverse-causation objection by citing it, and lists four real limitations. That restraint is a genuine strength and reviewers should say so explicitly. It is also, structurally, a hedge that makes the paper hard to falsify — the claim is so modest that "consistent with prior work" cannot fail. All four reviewers should evaluate the calibration and the substance separately, and the synthesizer must not let the first pay for the second.
- **The reference-verification lead is potentially dispositive.** The uniform `10.5555` DOI prefix and the generic-sounding journal titles are an observable pattern that must be checked, not assumed either way. R3 owns this. If the sources are unverifiable, the review's center of gravity moves from "thin but improvable" to a source-integrity question, and R1's and R2's technical comments become secondary. Sequence the review accordingly: verification first, then substance.
- **Two internal inconsistencies worth seeding as verification leads** (not conclusions — reviewers should confirm from the text): the anonymity-versus-deduplication contradiction (R3), and the primary/robustness ordering of Pearson versus Spearman for a single ordinal item (R1). Neither is fatal; both require an authored answer rather than a hedge.
- **The r² is stated in prose but never numerically.** "The proportion of variance shared by the two measures was accordingly modest" is vaguer than the paper's own standard elsewhere. Assigned to R1.

**Complementarity and tension between reviewers**

- **Designed tension, R2 versus Journal-Fit Reviewer**: R2 will likely argue the increment is not worth publishing without more; the Journal-Fit Reviewer will likely argue it is publishable *as a short report if properly positioned*. This disagreement is productive and should be surfaced in synthesis rather than averaged away — it is the paper's real decision point.
- **Designed tension, R3 versus R2 and Journal-Fit Reviewer**: R3's verifiability findings may make the substantive debate moot. Synthesis must sequence, not blend.
- **Boundary maintained between R1 and R3**: R1 owns statistics, measurement, and reporting completeness. R3 owns protocol coherence, source verifiability, and open-science infrastructure. The anonymity/deduplication issue is assigned to R3 only, to prevent duplicate flagging of the same sentence from two directions.
- **Coverage gap to watch**: no reviewer is configured primarily as a student-experience or pedagogy voice. This is deliberate — the paper makes no pedagogical claim beyond one hedged onboarding sentence — but if revision expands the practical implications, a pedagogy angle would need to be added.
- **Register instruction for all four**: developmental wording, evidence-based verdict. Most of the identified problems are fixable within the paper's declared scope (add canonical citations, add tables, report r², reverse the correlation ordering, name the log-data barrier, document instrument adaptation). The two that are not fixable by revision — single-institution sampling and cross-sectional design — the paper already concedes, and reviewers should not spend recommendation weight re-litigating concessions already made.
