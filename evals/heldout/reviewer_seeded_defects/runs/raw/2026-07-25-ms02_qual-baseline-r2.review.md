### Reviewer Configuration Card #2

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 1 — Methodology
**Identity Description**: A mixed-methods design methodologist based in an education research faculty, trained in both interpretive qualitative analysis (Braun & Clarke reflexive thematic analysis, Saldaña coding) and applied social statistics, who teaches a doctoral seminar on integration in mixed-methods designs and serves as the standing methods reviewer for two education journals. Known for insisting that a "mixed-methods" label be earned at the point of integration, not merely by the co-presence of two datasets.

**Review Focus**:
1. **Participant accounting and sampling transparency.** Reconcile the Abstract's "Fourteen administrators were interviewed" against §3.2's "Twelve senior administrators (n=12)" and determine which figure the analysis rests on. Then examine the survey: §3.2 states n=48, but §4.4 compares n=9 (research university) with n=11 (teaching-focused university), leaving 28 respondents unaccounted for with no response-rate, sampling-frame, or distribution-by-site reporting anywhere in §3.
2. **Whether the quantitative claim is supportable at all.** Evaluate the §4.4 significance test: a p<.05 claim from a 9-vs-11 comparison, reported with no test name, no test statistic, no degrees of freedom, no effect size, no confidence interval, and no indication of whether assumptions were checked or whether this was one of several comparisons run ("subgroup comparisons were examined where role categories permitted" in §3.4 suggests undisclosed multiplicity). Assess whether "M=3.9, SD=0.6" is even interpretable given that the instrument, its scale range, its item count, its provenance, and any reliability evidence are entirely absent.
3. **Thematic analysis rigor and the credibility claims attached to it.** Section 3.4 describes coding in two sentences with no analytic tradition named, no coder count, no audit trail, no reflexivity statement. Against that thinness, scrutinize §4.1's claim that "these themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol" — the protocol is described as *semi*-structured in §3.1, and "emerged" and "structured protocol" pull in opposite epistemological directions. Likewise test §4.2's "recurred across every interview" and §4.3's "several administrators" for whether any counting was actually done.
4. **Integration — the mixed-methods claim itself.** Determine whether the two strands are integrated or merely adjacent. Note the tension in §4.4: the survey is introduced as having "corroborated the qualitative picture," then immediately described as contradicting the researchers' expectations ("the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture"). Corroboration and disconfirmation cannot both be true of the same result; decide which the data supports and whether the paper is retrofitting coherence.

**Will particularly care about**: Whether the design permits any of the causal or population-level language the paper uses — and specifically whether the disclosure in §4.5 that dissenting participants "were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure" describes analytic selection that invalidates the claim, made in the same paragraph, of "balanced representation of perspectives" capturing "the full range of administrator views." Selecting data to fit a theme structure and then claiming full-range coverage is a rigor failure, not a reporting slip, and this reviewer should say so in those terms.

**Possible blind spots**: A methods specialist may treat the literature review as out of scope and therefore miss that the Delacroix (2018) misrepresentation in §5 is not a citation-formatting problem but an argument built on an inverted source. May also under-weight how the three-institution design interacts with the confidentiality breach in §4.1 — R3 covers that consequence.

## Review Strategy Recommendations

**Special characteristics requiring particular attention**

- **This manuscript's defects are internal, not external.** Every serious problem is verifiable against the manuscript's own text without consulting a single outside source: the participant count contradicts itself (Abstract 14 vs §3.2 twelve), the anonymity claim contradicts the finding (§3.5 vs §4.1), the balance claim contradicts the exclusion disclosed in the same paragraph (§4.5), the corroboration claim contradicts the disconfirmation stated one sentence later (§4.4), and the Delacroix citation contradicts both §2 and its own reference-list annotation (§5). Reviewers should be instructed to read for internal consistency first. A reviewer who reads sequentially and charitably will pass over most of these; a reviewer who cross-checks sections against each other will find all of them.

- **Polish is doing concealment work here.** The prose is genuinely good — fluent, confident, well-structured, with references formatted cleanly and DOIs throughout. That surface competence is exactly why the maturity rating is "revised draft" rather than "pre-submission," and reviewers should be primed not to let fluency substitute for evidence. Several of the most problematic sentences are also among the best-written ones ("the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views" immediately precedes the admission that dissenting views were excluded).

- **The reference list is unverifiable by design.** All twelve DOIs are in the 10.5555 prefix, a reserved test/example range, and the sequence 1042001–1042012 is perfectly consecutive. No reviewer should attempt external verification or fabricate a verification result. Instead, the Delacroix check must be run *internally*: §2's characterization, the reference-list annotation, and §5's use are all present in the document and they do not agree. That internal check is sufficient and is the check that matters. Reviewers should state findings about citation fidelity in terms of what the manuscript says about its sources, never in terms of what the sources say — which is not knowable here.

- **Severity is not evenly distributed.** Three issues are of a different order from the rest and should not be flattened into a long list of medium-priority revisions: (1) participant identifiability against an explicit anonymity claim (§3.5 / §4.1) — an ethics matter with a real person behind it; (2) the inverted citation of Delacroix (§5) — the paper's central recommendation rests on the reverse of what its source argues; (3) the disclosed exclusion of dissenting cases alongside a claim of full-range representation (§4.5) — analytic selection presented as comprehensiveness. Everything else, including the arithmetic and the statistics reporting, is serious but repairable within a normal revision cycle. The synthesizer should preserve this ordering rather than averaging severity across reviewers.

- **Two claims are quantitatively impossible as stated and should be named as such.** The survey subgroups (9 + 11) account for 20 of 48 respondents with no explanation of the remaining 28; and the significance claim carries no test, statistic, df, or effect size at a sample size where almost nothing would reach significance legitimately. These should be flagged as "not evaluable as reported" rather than argued about.

**Complementarity and tension between reviewers**

- **Designed overlap on the integrity axis (§4.5 exclusion).** R1 and R3 will both reach the exclusion of dissenting participants — R1 as a threat to analytic validity, R3 as a question of consent and consequence. This duplication is intentional; convergence from two independent framings is stronger evidence than either alone. The synthesizer should report it as one finding with two grounds, not two findings.

- **Genuine tension expected between EIC and R2 on novelty.** The EIC will judge novelty against journal fit and may find the administrator angle sufficiently marketable; R2, reading the same three citations (Pettersen, Rahman, Silva & Tan), is likelier to conclude the gap claim is overstated to the point of misrepresenting the field. Do not resolve this by splitting the difference. The productive resolution is to distinguish *the topic* (well covered) from *the evidentiary form* (administrators' own accounts, thinly covered) and to require the manuscript to make that distinction itself.

- **Expected tension between R1 and R3 on the survey.** R1 will likely recommend either removing the quantitative component or reporting it properly. R3 has no stake in the statistics but will note that removing it does not touch the ethics problem. The synthesizer must not let a clean methodological fix ("drop the survey, resubmit as a qualitative study") create the impression that the manuscript's problems have been addressed — the identifiability, citation-inversion, and selective-reporting issues all survive that fix intact.

- **Sequencing note for the panel.** R3's identifiability finding should be surfaced early in synthesis rather than in a closing ethics section, because it constrains the decision space for every other recommendation: a manuscript that cannot report its most vivid quotation as attributed must rethink its Findings section structure, which in turn interacts with R1's demand for transparent case accounting and R2's request for a more developed theoretical frame. Treating ethics as an appendix would produce a revision roadmap that quietly conflicts with itself.

- **Tone calibration.** This is a competent author who has produced a readable, well-organized manuscript on a legitimate question, and the underlying study — administrators' accounts of QA work — is worth doing and worth publishing in some form. Reviewers should be direct about the three high-severity findings without implying bad faith; several of the contradictions are more consistent with a rushed final assembly than with intent. The correct register is serious and specific, not punitive.

## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory).** As the editor holding this manuscript against the journal's peer-review bar, I read D1 as asking whether the study's design, its handling of data, its reporting of analysis, and the affordances it offers a reader who wants to reproduce or audit the work meet what the higher-education/QA-governance field would demand of a published contribution. My seat does not adjudicate technical craft the way a methodology specialist does — Reviewer 1 owns that — but the editorial version of this dimension is real and separable: does the manuscript disclose enough about how it was done that the journal can stand behind the conclusions it prints? For an administrator-perspectives study in QA governance, that means an identifiable design (interview, survey, case, document analysis), a stated basis for who was studied and why, and enough analytic transparency that a reader can distinguish what participants said from what the authors concluded.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript's claims sit correctly within the current evidence base of higher-education quality assurance, whether the prior literature it invokes is represented as that literature actually stands, and whether field-specific terminology and reported results are used correctly. Editorially this is where I check that the paper is in conversation with the field rather than reinventing it: QA governance has a substantial and specific prior body — accreditation regimes, quality-culture constructs, accountability-versus-improvement tensions, institutional-change and isomorphism accounts — and a manuscript that misattributes, mislabels, or ignores it is a liability for the journal regardless of how well its own data were handled. I also read D2 as covering the correct and non-sliding use of loaded terms of art in this field, where "quality culture," "quality assurance," "accreditation," and "institutional effectiveness" are not interchangeable.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory).** Here I am asking whether the paper's central thesis holds together end to end: whether the research question, the evidence marshalled, and the conclusions drawn are the same argument, and whether any inferential step is doing work the evidence cannot support. This is the dimension closest to my core editorial function — the title-to-abstract-to-introduction-to-conclusion consistency check, and the over-promise/under-deliver test. In a study of administrator perspectives, the characteristic coherence risk is the slide from what administrators *report* or *perceive* to what the institution *actually does* or what QA implementation *actually causes*; whether the manuscript keeps or loses that distinction is an argument-integrity question, not a statistics question.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority).** As EIC I own the readership question, and D4 is its contract form: are the framing, the definitions, and the stated implications legible and useful to readers from adjacent fields — organisational studies, public administration, policy, management of professional organisations — rather than only to specialists already inside the QA-governance conversation, and where the manuscript makes an interdisciplinary claim, is that claim actually substantiated rather than gestured at? The QA field is unusually dependent on jurisdiction-specific vocabulary and on national regulatory architecture, so the failure mode I expect is a paper legible only to readers who already share the authors' system. This dimension is high, not mandatory, so it can degrade the decision but its failure alone should not read as a fatal defect.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority).** This covers whether the manuscript is organised so a reader can follow it, whether the exposition is clear, whether tables and figures are of publishable quality and carry their own weight, and whether the paper observes the conventions of the venue it is aimed at — including length. At 2,249 words the manuscript is far below the length of a standard empirical article in this field, and my Phase 2 reading will have to determine whether that reflects a legitimately short form (research note, commentary, short communication) executed properly, or a full-scale empirical claim compressed into a space that cannot carry it. This is a normal-priority dimension: it shapes the revision letter and can contribute to a cumulative picture, but under this contract's failure conditions it can never by itself drive the decision.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor

- `what_to_look_for`: Whether a named, identifiable research design is stated rather than implied; who the administrator participants were, how many, from what institutional types and jurisdictions, and on what basis they were selected; how data were generated (interview protocol, survey instrument, documents) and over what period; how the material was analysed and by whom, with some statement of the analytic procedure rather than an unexplained leap to themes; any reproducibility affordance at all — an instrument or protocol in an appendix, a coding scheme, a data-availability statement; ethics/consent handling appropriate to research on named institutional actors; and whether the reported results are presented in a form (quotes, counts, statistics with the reporting conventions the field expects) that a reader can check against the design as described. I will also watch whether the word budget is being spent on method disclosure or on framing.
- `what_triggers_block`: The manuscript advances empirical claims about administrators' perspectives while leaving the evidentiary basis effectively undisclosed or internally inconsistent — no ascertainable design, or participants and sampling not characterised at all, or an analytic route from data to findings that is simply absent, such that no reader (and no journal) could determine what was actually done. Also block if reported results contradict the design as described (e.g., inferential or generalisation claims from a design that cannot support them, or reported figures that do not reconcile with the stated sample), or if human-subjects research on institutional actors is reported with no ethics/consent handling where the field expects it.
- `what_triggers_warn`: The design is identifiable and the study is legible as a piece of research, but disclosure is materially thin in ways a revision could fix — sampling rationale gestured at rather than stated, analytic procedure named but not evidenced, no instrument or coding scheme available, no data-availability or limitations statement, or results reported with enough imprecision that a reader can follow the argument but cannot audit it. Thin-but-fixable disclosure is a warn; absent-or-incoherent disclosure is a block.

### D2: domain_accuracy

- `what_to_look_for`: Whether the paper's account of quality assurance, quality culture, and institutional change corresponds to how those constructs are actually used in the current literature; whether cited prior work is represented as it stands rather than as the argument would prefer; whether named external frameworks, standards, agencies, or accreditation regimes are described correctly and attributed to the right jurisdiction and the right period; whether the key terms of art hold a stable meaning across the manuscript; whether the engagement with prior work is with the actual QA-governance scholarship rather than a thin or off-field substitute; and whether any factual assertion about the state of the field (prevalence, consensus, "little research exists on…") is the kind of claim the authors are in a position to make.
- `what_triggers_block`: A factual misstatement or misrepresentation load-bearing for the paper's contribution — a foundational construct defined contrary to its established meaning, a named framework/standard/agency described incorrectly in a way the argument then relies on, prior work characterised as saying something it does not in order to open the gap the paper claims to fill, or citations that cannot be reconciled with the claims attached to them. Also block on a fabricated or unverifiable authority (a source, statistic, or standard that does not exist as described), and on a novelty or absence claim ("the first study to…", "no prior research has…") asserted as fact against a literature that plainly contains it.
- `what_triggers_warn`: Domain handling is broadly sound but shows drift or gaps that a revision can repair — key terms used loosely or with meaning that shifts between sections, an engagement with the QA literature that is real but dated or noticeably partial (a major strand of the relevant conversation unaddressed), terminology imported from an adjacent field without reconciling it to QA usage, or claims about the field stated more confidently than the cited support warrants without being outright wrong.

### D3: argumentative_coherence

- `what_to_look_for`: Whether the research question is stated and whether the conclusion answers *that* question; whether the title's promise, the abstract's claim, the introduction's framing, and the conclusion's assertions describe one consistent study; whether each substantive claim is traceable to evidence the paper actually presents; whether the perception-to-practice and association-to-causation boundaries are maintained, given that this study's data are administrator *perspectives*; whether scope conditions travel with the claims (which institutions, which system, which period) or are quietly dropped as the argument proceeds; whether counter-evidence, disconfirming cases, or alternative explanations are acknowledged where the data would raise them; and whether the recommendations or implications at the end are proportionate to what was shown.
- `what_triggers_block`: The central argument does not hold — the conclusions assert something the presented evidence cannot support at all, most characteristically by converting what administrators report or believe into claims about what institutions do, what QA implementation causes, or what works, without any warranting step. Also block on an internal contradiction the paper never resolves (the abstract or conclusion claiming a finding the body does not contain or contradicts), on a research question that the paper simply never answers while presenting itself as having answered it, or on a fallacy at the load-bearing inferential joint (circularity between the definition of quality culture and the evidence for it, or generalisation to a population the design never reached).
- `what_triggers_warn`: The core argument is sound and the conclusion does address the question, but the reasoning has repairable soft spots — hedging that is inconsistent so that some claims outrun the evidence while their neighbours do not, scope conditions stated early and dropped later, implications or recommendations pitched somewhat beyond what the findings establish, alternative explanations left unaddressed where a reader would raise them, or a title/abstract that over-promises relative to a body that delivers something narrower but real.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

- `what_to_look_for`: Whether the framing explains why an institutional-change or organisational-behaviour reader, not only a QA specialist, should care; whether jurisdiction-specific machinery (national agencies, accreditation cycles, statutory requirements, local acronyms) is defined at first use and situated so an outside reader can place it; whether the constructs are given working definitions rather than assumed; whether the stated implications reach beyond the immediate QA-practitioner audience, and whether any claim of relevance to another discipline (organisational theory, public administration, management) is actually substantiated through engagement with that field rather than merely asserted; and whether the paper's own theoretical anchoring is named, since a named anchor is what lets adjacent readers connect the findings to their own literature.

The gap asserted in §2 — "Missing from this body of work is sustained attention to the administrators who occupy the hinge between external requirement and internal practice" — is contradicted three sentences earlier by the manuscript's own account of Pettersen (2022) and Rahman (2020) on the quality office, and immediately afterwards by Silva and Tan (2021) on administrators as intermediaries. Correctly scoped, this is a useful minor contribution: a grounded account of how QA administrators construct meaning under conditions they themselves describe as theatrical. Title, Abstract, and Introduction describe one study; §5 and §6 describe a different and much larger one. **D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory).** From a methodology seat, this dimension asks whether the study's design can actually answer the question it poses, and whether a reader could tell how the answer was produced. For a piece on administrator perspectives on quality assurance implementation, that means: an explicitly named design (qualitative interview study, case study, survey, mixed methods, or conceptual analysis) rather than an unnamed "we talked to people" posture; a sampling logic tied to the inferential target (which institutions, which administrator roles, why those and not others); data collection described at a level of procedural detail another researcher could follow (instrument, protocol, duration, language, setting); an analysis procedure named and operationalised (coding approach, who coded, how disagreement was handled, or for quantitative portions the statistical model and its assumptions); and reproducibility affordances appropriate to the field — ethics/IRB record, consent, anonymisation of institutions and individuals, data or instrument availability. The field's peer-review bar in higher-education QA studies is qualitative-inclusive, so small samples and interpretive analysis are not defects per se; what is a defect is a design whose warrant is never stated. I weigh reproducibility and design–question fit above statistical sophistication here, and I apply qualitative trustworthiness criteria (credibility, transferability, dependability, confirmability) rather than importing quantitative generalisability language where it does not belong.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory).** Read through the methodology lens, this dimension is about whether the paper's methodological and technical vocabulary is used correctly and consistently with how the QA governance field uses it, and whether empirical claims attributed to prior work are represented as those studies actually found them. In this field the load-bearing terms carry precise institutional meanings — quality culture, quality assurance versus quality enhancement, internal versus external QA, accreditation versus audit versus evaluation, compliance versus improvement orientation, and the specific referents of national/regional frameworks (ESG, national agencies, institutional self-assessment cycles). Methodological terms carry their own precision requirements: purposive versus convenience sampling, thematic analysis versus grounded theory versus content analysis, saturation, triangulation, member checking, trustworthiness versus validity. My contribution is not a literature-completeness audit (Reviewer 2's remit) but a check that terms are not conflated, that instruments or frameworks are attributed to their actual sources, and that any figure, proportion, or empirical finding imported from prior work is not restated at a strength the original did not support.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory).** For me this is the chain from research question to data to analysis to conclusion, tested for breaks. The specific hazard in an administrator-perspectives study is the slide from what administrators *said* to what the institution *is* or *does*, and from association to causation — treating self-reported perception of quality culture as measurement of quality culture, or concluding that a QA mechanism *produced* cultural change from cross-sectional accounts of people who implemented it and have a stake in its success. I also watch for scope creep between sections: a question posed about one institutional type answered with claims about universities generally; a finding stated cautiously in results and asserted flatly in the discussion or abstract. Related fallacies live on my checklist — ecological inference from institutional-level accounts to individual behaviour, survivorship bias when only successfully-implemented cases are studied, confirmation bias when disconfirming accounts are absent from a dataset that surely contained tension, and circular reasoning where quality culture is defined by the very indicators used to demonstrate it. Coherence for me means the conclusion is the size and kind of claim the design can bear.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high).** My angle on accessibility is methodological legibility to adjacent-field readers — higher-education policy, organisational change, public administration, management. A reader from organisational studies should be able to tell what was done and to whom without knowing the acronyms of one national QA system. So I look for whether the study's context and units are defined rather than assumed, whether QA-specific terminology is glossed on first use, and whether design choices are justified in terms a methodologist outside QA would recognise. Where the paper reaches into adjacent literatures — organisational culture, institutional theory, change management, implementation science — I check whether it also imports the corresponding methodological obligations, or borrows the vocabulary without the design that would substantiate the borrowed claim. This is a high-priority but not mandatory dimension, and I score it on substantiation and definitional clarity, not on breadth of citation.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal).** For the methodology seat this reduces to whether the methodological content is where a reader can find it and stated once, consistently. A 2,249-word manuscript is short — roughly a research note or short communication — so I expect compression, and compression is not itself a fault. What I score is whether compression has eliminated the load-bearing method description entirely, whether the paper has a discernible methods section or equivalent, whether tables and figures that carry methodological information (participant characteristics, coding frameworks, instrument items) are legible and self-explanatory, and whether numbers reported in one place match the same numbers reported elsewhere. Venue conventions matter to me only where they touch methodological reporting: reporting standards for the declared design, ethics statements, and structural conventions of the paper type. This is the lowest-priority dimension in the contract and I score it accordingly — presentation problems that do not obscure the method are minor issues, not warnings.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
- **what_to_look_for**: An explicitly named research design and a stated rationale for it. Whether the design type matches the question implied by the title (administrator *perspectives* implies an interpretive or survey design; *implementation* claims imply something longitudinal or process-tracing). Sampling: unit of analysis, institution count and type, participant roles, recruitment route, selection criteria, and the logic (purposive/theoretical/convenience/census) with justification. Sample size adequacy against the appropriate standard for the declared paradigm — theoretical saturation and its evidence for qualitative work, a priori power analysis for quantitative work, neither imported into the other. Data collection procedure: instrument or interview protocol, whether it is reproduced or appended, pilot testing, interview length and mode, language of collection and any translation procedure. Analysis: named approach, coding process, number of coders, inter-coder agreement or negotiated-consensus procedure, software, audit trail. Qualitative trustworthiness moves — member checking, peer debriefing, negative-case analysis, thick description, researcher reflexivity and positionality (which matters acutely if the authors are themselves QA administrators). Quantitative reporting, if present: effect sizes with interpretation, confidence intervals, assumption tests, missing-data amount and handling, APA 7.0 statistical formatting. Reproducibility affordances: IRB/ethics approval with an identifiable body, informed consent, anonymisation and confidentiality procedures for institutions and named administrators, availability of data, instruments, or codebook. Word-count realism: at 2,249 words, whether methodological detail is genuinely absent or is present-but-compressed, and whether any supplementary or appendix pointer carries the remainder.
- **what_triggers_block**: No identifiable research design at all — the reader cannot determine what data exist, from whom, or how they were analysed, so no replication or appraisal is possible. Or a design that cannot in principle answer the stated research question (e.g. causal or effectiveness claims about QA implementation resting on a single cross-sectional set of self-reports with no comparison, no temporal ordering, and no acknowledgement of the gap). Or an analysis method that is fundamentally mismatched to the data type (inferential statistics on data that cannot support them; parametric tests declared on ordinal single-item measures with no acknowledgement; quantitative claims from a sample described only as "several administrators"). Or human-subjects research with no ethics/consent statement and no anonymisation of identifiable institutions or individuals. Or internally contradictory method reporting — participant counts, institution counts, or the declared design differing between sections such that no single reading of the method is true. Or evidence of a red-flag reporting pattern (undisclosed selective reporting, uncorrected multiple comparisons presented as confirmatory findings, hypotheses evidently formed after seeing results).
- **what_triggers_warn**: The design is named and broadly appropriate, but one or more load-bearing procedural elements are underspecified rather than absent: sampling logic stated without selection criteria or justification; participants described without role, institution type, or experience; interview protocol referenced but neither reproduced nor summarised; analysis named ("thematic analysis") without any account of how codes were derived, by whom, or how disagreement was resolved. Or saturation asserted without evidence of how it was determined. Or a single coder with no reflexivity statement and no alternative dependability procedure. Or an ethics statement present but thin (approval mentioned without body or protocol identifier; consent unmentioned). Or limitations acknowledged only in generic form ("the sample is small") without naming which inferences that limitation constrains. Or quantitative elements reported without effect sizes, confidence intervals, or assumption checks where the design calls for them. Or missing-data handling unreported where non-response is plausible. In short: the method is recoverable in outline and the study is appraisable, but a competent reader could not replicate the procedure without asking the authors questions.

### D2: domain_accuracy
- **what_to_look_for**: Correct and stable use of QA governance terminology — quality culture, quality assurance versus quality enhancement, internal versus external QA, accreditation versus audit versus institutional evaluation, compliance versus improvement orientation — with definitions supplied where the paper's argument turns on the distinction. Whether frameworks invoked (ESG, national agency standards, institutional self-assessment cycles, AACSB/AoL-style assurance-of-learning schemes) are described as those frameworks actually specify rather than as loose paraphrase. Whether constructs borrowed from other literatures (organisational culture typologies, institutional theory, implementation constructs) are used with their source definitions intact. Methodological terminology used precisely: purposive versus convenience versus snowball sampling; thematic analysis versus grounded theory versus qualitative content analysis, each with its actual procedural commitments; saturation, triangulation, member checking, trustworthiness. Whether empirical claims attributed to prior studies are stated at the strength those studies supported — a single-country case study not reported as an established general finding. Whether numbers, proportions, or instrument properties imported from other work (reliability coefficients, framework item counts, national statistics) are accurate and sourced. Whether the paper's own findings are described in terminology consistent between abstract, results, and discussion.
- **what_triggers_block**: A factual or terminological error at the centre of the paper's argument that changes what the study means — e.g. the paper conflates quality assurance with quality enhancement, or accreditation with audit, in a way that makes its central finding a statement about something other than what it claims to study. Or a named framework or standard is described with substantively wrong content (attributing requirements to ESG or a national agency that they do not contain) and a claim is built on that description. Or a methodological label is applied to a procedure that is demonstrably not that method (declaring grounded theory while describing a fixed a priori codebook with no constant comparison and no theoretical sampling), such that the reported analysis cannot be what the paper says it is. Or a prior study's finding is misrepresented in a way that supplies the paper's warrant for its own design or conclusion — a misattributed effect, a reversed direction, or a claim the cited work explicitly disclaimed.
- **what_triggers_warn**: Terminology is used loosely but recoverably — quality culture invoked without definition in a paper whose title makes it the central construct; "validity" applied to qualitative findings where "credibility" or "transferability" is the field-appropriate term; internal/external QA used interchangeably in places without the argument depending on it. Or a framework is cited accurately but at a level of generality that does not support the specific inference drawn from it. Or a prior finding is stated slightly stronger than its source (a context-bounded result described without its bounds), without that overstatement carrying the paper's core claim. Or a technical term drifts between sections (a construct named one way in the methods and another in the discussion) without the underlying referent being wrong. Or reliability, framework, or national statistics are cited without a source, so accuracy cannot be checked but nothing is evidently wrong.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
- **what_to_look_for**: The traceable chain research question → design → data → results → conclusion, checked link by link. Whether the stated conclusions are of the same kind and scope as the evidence: perception data yielding perception claims, not institutional-state claims; single-institution or few-institution data yielding bounded claims, not sector-wide ones. Whether causal language ("led to", "resulted in", "drives", "builds") appears where the design supports only association, co-occurrence, or participant attribution. Whether the temporal claim implicit in "implementation" and "building culture" is matched by temporally-ordered data or explicitly flagged as retrospective self-report. Whether disconfirming or tension-laden accounts appear — an administrator-perspectives dataset with no reported resistance, ambivalence, or failure is a confirmation-bias signal. Whether the paper handles the obvious counter-explanation for its findings (social desirability and role-interest of administrators reporting on QA systems they operate). Whether definitions are non-circular — quality culture not defined by the same indicators used to demonstrate its presence. Whether abstract, results, and discussion state the finding at the same strength. Whether limitations, when stated, actually constrain the conclusions drawn rather than sitting decoratively beside them. Ecological inference (institution-level accounts used to characterise individual staff behaviour) and survivorship bias (only institutions with successful implementations sampled) are on the same scan.
- **what_triggers_block**: The central conclusion is not supported by the reported evidence — the paper asserts a causal or effectiveness claim about QA implementation from a design that cannot yield one, and does so in the abstract and discussion without qualification. Or the paper's core claim contradicts its own reported data or its own stated limitation. Or the conclusion answers a materially different question from the one posed (a study framed on administrator perspectives concluding about student outcomes or institutional performance without the corresponding data). Or a fallacy carries the main argument: circular definition of the central construct, ecological inference from institutional accounts to individual conduct presented as a finding, or generalisation to the sector from a sample selected for success with the selection unacknowledged. Or the paper reports no disconfirming evidence and simultaneously claims comprehensive or balanced characterisation of the phenomenon.
- **what_triggers_warn**: Overreach that is localised rather than load-bearing — a discussion paragraph or an abstract sentence stating the finding more strongly than the results section, or occasional causal verbs in prose while the formal claims stay associational. Or the sample's boundedness is real but acknowledged only in a limitations line while implications are written as if general. Or a plausible alternative explanation (social desirability, role interest, selection on success) is unaddressed but the affected claim is not the paper's central one. Or disconfirming cases are thin rather than absent — tension mentioned but not analysed. Or the logical connection between two sections is left implicit where a reader must supply the inference themselves. Or the limitations section is generic and does not name which specific conclusions its limitations qualify.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
- **what_to_look_for**: Whether the institutional and national context is specified enough that a reader outside this QA system knows what kind of universities, what regulatory regime, and what stage of QA maturity are in play. Whether QA-specific acronyms and framework names are expanded and glossed at first use. Whether the roles labelled "administrator" are defined in functionally transferable terms (what these people actually do) rather than by local job titles alone. Whether design choices are explained in general methodological vocabulary an organisational-studies or public-administration methodologist would recognise, so the study is appraisable from outside. Where the paper reaches into adjacent literatures — organisational culture, institutional theory, change management, implementation science — whether the borrowed constructs come with the methodological obligations they carry in their home field, or the vocabulary is imported and the design left behind. Whether implications addressed to adjacent audiences are bounded by what the data support, with transferability conditions stated so another setting can judge applicability.
- **what_triggers_block**: A high-priority dimension blocks only when the interdisciplinary claim is both substantial and unsubstantiated. Concretely: the paper's framing rests on an adjacent-field theory (institutional isomorphism, a named organisational culture model, an implementation framework) and asserts findings *about that theory* — confirming, extending, or refuting it — while the design collects no data capable of testing it. Or the paper is written so entirely inside one national QA system's vocabulary that an adjacent-field reader cannot determine what the study examined or to what it might transfer, making the contribution unassessable outside the immediate subfield. Or implications are addressed to a different discipline's practice on the basis of data from this one, with no bridging argument and no bounding conditions.
- **what_triggers_warn**: Context and terminology are mostly accessible but with gaps — acronyms unexpanded on first use, a national framework assumed familiar, "administrator" left as a local title without functional description. Or an adjacent-field construct is invoked as framing decoration without being operationalised in the design or returned to in the analysis. Or transferability is asserted generically ("these findings may inform other institutions") without stating the conditions under which transfer is reasonable — the thick description an outside reader would need to judge fit. Or the paper's implications for adjacent fields are plausible but stated without the bridging reasoning that would let a reader from that field evaluate them.

### D5: writing_and_structure
- **what_to_look_for**: Whether the manuscript has a locatable methods section or, in a short-form paper, a clearly demarcated equivalent, and whether methodological information is stated once in one place rather than scattered across introduction, results, and discussion. Whether reported quantities are internally consistent wherever they recur — participant counts, institution counts, interview counts, theme counts across abstract, methods, results, and any table. Whether tables and figures carrying methodological content (participant characteristics, coding framework, instrument items, analytic stages) are self-explanatory, correctly labelled, referenced in the text, and consistent with the prose. Whether the abstract's account of the method matches the body's. Whether reporting-standard conventions for the declared design are followed to the extent the format permits (COREQ/SRQR-type elements for qualitative work, APA 7.0 statistical formatting for any quantitative component), and whether an ethics statement occupies its conventional position. Whether the 2,249-word length is handled as a deliberate short-form structure or reads as a truncated full-length paper with sections missing. Whether methodological citations are present where procedures are named.
- **what_triggers_block**: Structural failure that makes methodological content unrecoverable rather than merely inconvenient — no methods content locatable anywhere in the manuscript in any section, so the reader cannot assemble what was done even by reading the whole paper. Or numbers describing the study conflict irreconcilably across sections (abstract, methods, results, tables each stating a different sample) with no way to determine which is correct. Or figures/tables that carry the study's methodological or empirical substance are absent, unlabelled, or so mislabelled that their content cannot be read. I hold a high bar here: this is the contract's lowest-priority dimension, and presentation problems that leave the method recoverable do not reach block.
- **what_triggers_warn**: Methodological content is present but poorly placed — method described inside the results or introduction, or split across sections so the reader must reassemble it. Or minor numerical inconsistency between abstract and body that is resolvable by inspection. Or tables that require the prose to be interpretable, missing units, unclear column definitions, or are not referenced in the text. Or the abstract's method description omits or misstates an element the body specifies. Or reporting-standard elements conventional for the declared design are absent (no positionality statement in an interpretive study, no ethics statement in its expected location, statistical notation not APA-conformant). Or the short format has produced compression that removes signposting — no visible structure marking where question, method, findings, and interpretation begin and end.

### Summary Assessment

The manuscript declares a QUAL-dominant mixed-methods design and reports a defensible qualitative core — three institutions, senior QA administrators, semi-structured interviews of 45–70 minutes, thematic analysis. That core is worth publishing in some form. What the manuscript cannot currently support is the reporting built on top of it.

Three methodological problems are of a different order from ordinary underspecification. First, the participant count is not stable: the Abstract reports fourteen interviewees, §3.2 reports twelve (n=12), and no reading of the manuscript reconciles them, so the denominator of every qualitative claim is unknown. Second, §4.5 discloses that dissenting participants were excluded from the analysis "for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure," in the same paragraph that claims the study "captur[ed] the full range of administrator views." That is analytic selection presented as comprehensiveness — a rigor failure, not a reporting slip, and it invalidates the balance claim rather than qualifying it. Third, §4.4's inferential claim (p<.05, n=9 vs n=11) arrives with no test name, no test statistic, no df, no effect size, no CI, no assumption check, and no instrument description; §3.4's "subgroup comparisons were examined where role categories permitted" indicates undisclosed multiplicity. That claim is not weak — it is not evaluable as reported.

Integration, the load-bearing requirement of a mixed-methods label, is absent: §4.4 states the survey "corroborated the qualitative picture" and then, one sentence later, that it contradicted what the interviews had led the authors to anticipate. Both cannot be true of the same result.

### Strengths

1. **Design–question fit at the qualitative core is sound.** The research question in §1 ("How do senior university administrators experience and enact QA implementation…") is an interpretive question, and semi-structured interviewing with thematic analysis is the appropriate instrument for it. The choice is not defended in §3.1, but it is correct, and the question is answerable by the data the authors collected.

2. **Purposeful multi-site sampling with a defensible contrast structure.** §3.2's three-site frame — large public research, mid-sized public teaching-focused, private — gives the design an institutional-type contrast rather than a convenience cluster, and the role frame (quality directors, associate deans, registry heads) targets the hinge position the study is theorising. The logic is implicit rather than stated, but the underlying selection is principled.

3. **Data collection parameters are partially specified and realistic.** §3.3 reports mode (in person or video call), duration range (45–70 minutes), and that interviews were audio-recorded and transcribed. Many manuscripts at this length omit all three. Duration and mode are the two parameters that most constrain what interview data can bear, and they are present.

4. **Analytic iteration is described in the right shape.** §3.4's account of coding "moving between transcripts and an emerging set of themes until a stable structure was reached" describes recursive rather than one-pass coding. The description is far too thin to be reproducible, but it points at the correct procedure rather than a mislabelled one.

5. **An ethics record exists and a disconfirming survey result was reported rather than suppressed.** §3.5 states institutional ethical approval and informed consent. And §4.4, despite its framing problem, does report that staff sentiment ran contrary to what the interviews had led the authors to expect — reporting an unwelcome result is a genuine integrity signal, and the fix is to relabel it, not to remove it.

### Weaknesses

1. **The participant count contradicts itself, and no reading of the manuscript resolves it.** The Abstract states "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth"; §3.2 states "Twelve senior administrators (n=12)." Every qualitative claim in §4 has an unknown denominator, which directly disables the frequency claims the Findings section makes ("recurred across every interview," §4.2; "several administrators," §4.3). *Why it matters:* a reader cannot assess whether a theme "recurring across every interview" spans 12 or 14 accounts, and cannot check the sample against the three-site frame. *Improvement:* state the true N once, in §3.2, and add a participants table cross-tabulating role × institution type × years in QA role, with the Abstract regenerated from that table rather than written independently.

2. **§4.5 discloses analytic exclusion of dissenting cases while claiming full-range coverage in the same paragraph.** The text reads: "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views… A minority of participants expressed more skeptical or dissenting views about the value of the entire QA enterprise; these were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure that organized our analysis." *Why it matters:* this is not a length constraint operating on already-analysed data — the stated criterion is fit to the theme structure, which means cases were removed *because* they disconfirmed it. Negative-case analysis is the specific procedure that makes thematic findings dependable; discarding negative cases and then asserting full-range representation inverts it. The three themes are, on this account, partly an artifact of the exclusion. *Improvement:* reinstate the dissenting accounts as a fourth theme or as an explicit negative-case section, report how many participants held these views and at which sites, and delete the "full range" and "balanced representation" sentences outright — they cannot be made true by softening. If the authors instead defend an exclusion, the criterion must be stated a priori and applied before theme construction, which is not what the manuscript describes.

3. **The quantitative strand is not evaluable as reported, and its subgroup arithmetic is unaccounted for.** §3.2 reports n=48; §4.4 compares n=9 against n=11, leaving 28 respondents with no stated disposition — no per-site distribution, no response rate, no sampling frame, no missing-data account. The significance claim carries no test name, no test statistic, no df, no effect size, no confidence interval, and no assumption check, and §3.4's "subgroup comparisons were examined where role categories permitted" signals an undisclosed number of comparisons with no correction. Separately, "M=3.9, SD=0.6" is uninterpretable: the instrument, its provenance, its scale range, its item count, and any reliability evidence are absent throughout, so a reader cannot tell whether 3.9 sits on a 5-point or 7-point scale, or whether it aggregates one item or twelve. *Why it matters:* an inferential claim at 9-vs-11 that reaches p<.05 without a reported test is not a weak finding, it is an unverifiable one, and it is doing argumentative work in §4.4 ("institutional type as a possible moderator"). *Improvement:* either (a) report the instrument (source, items, response scale, α or equivalent), the full per-site respondent distribution summing to 48, the response rate, the named test with statistic/df/effect size/CI, the assumption checks, and the total number of comparisons run with the correction applied; or (b) demote the survey to explicitly labelled descriptive context, delete the significance claim and the moderator inference, and re-present the paper as a qualitative study. Option (b) is the more honest route at this sample size and would cost the paper very little.

4. **The mixed-methods claim is not earned: the two strands are adjacent, and §4.4 asserts corroboration and disconfirmation of the same result.** §4.4 opens "The survey corroborated the qualitative picture," then states two sentences later that "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture." A result cannot both confirm and disconfirm the same expectation. *Why it matters:* integration is the defining criterion of a mixed-methods design; without a stated integration point, priority, timing, and meta-inference procedure (§3.1 gives only "the survey served to contextualize"), what the manuscript has is two studies printed consecutively. The retrofitted "corroborated" framing reads as coherence imposed after the fact rather than derived from the data. *Improvement:* name the design in Creswell/Plano Clark or Fetters terms (this appears to be convergent-parallel with qualitative priority, not explanatory sequential), state where and how the strands were integrated (a joint display would do it), and rewrite §4.4 to report the divergence as divergence — a level-of-institution discordance between administrators and staff is a more interesting finding than manufactured corroboration.

5. **§4.1's epistemological claim about the protocol contradicts §3.1 and is not supported by any reported procedure.** §4.1 states "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol, giving us confidence that the pattern was not an artifact of any single institution's local circumstances," while §3.1 describes the protocol as *semi*-structured. "Emerged" (inductive) and "structured protocol" (deductive) name opposite analytic commitments, and neither is evidenced: §3.4 names no analytic tradition, reports no coder count, no codebook, no inter-coder or negotiated-consensus procedure, no audit trail, no member checking, no reflexivity or positionality statement, and no basis for the "stable structure" (saturation is implied but never assessed). *Why it matters:* the cross-institution non-artifactuality claim in §4.1 is a dependability claim, and dependability is exactly what the unreported procedure would have established. *Improvement:* delete the "structured protocol" sentence; name the analytic tradition and follow its reporting conventions; report the number of coders and how disagreement was handled; add a positionality statement (acutely needed if any author works in QA administration); append the interview protocol; and either evidence the saturation judgment or drop the "stable structure" language in favour of a bounded claim.

### Detailed Comments

#### Research Questions & Hypotheses

The RQ in §1 is clear, single, and interpretive: how administrators experience and enact QA implementation, and how their accounts illuminate quality-culture formation. No hypotheses are stated, which is appropriate for a QUAL-dominant design. Two problems attach at the RQ level. First, the survey strand has no research question of its own — it appears in §3.1 as contextualisation, then in §4.4 as an inferential test of institutional type as a moderator, a question never posed. Second, the RQ asks about experience and enactment, but §5's second finding answers a different question (what "universities across the sector" do), and §6 answers a third (what QA processes are "more likely to foster" internalised commitment — a prospective effectiveness claim). The design supports the first question only.

#### Research Design

§3.1 declares "a mixed-methods design combining semi-structured interviews with a supplementary staff survey," with the qualitative component primary. The label is declared but the design is not specified: no design typology, no timing (concurrent or sequential — undeterminable from the text), no stated integration point, and no meta-inference procedure. The qualitative core is best read as a three-site comparative case study, but the manuscript never says so, and §3.2's institutional-type contrast is therefore never activated as a design feature until §4.4 tries to use it inferentially on survey data instead. Internal/external validity trade-offs are not discussed. A reader can determine roughly what was done; a reader cannot determine what design governs the inference.

#### Sampling Strategy

Unit of analysis, recruitment route ("through institutional QA offices"), site types, and role types are given. Missing: the sampling logic by name (purposive? theoretical? census of available QA staff?), the selection criteria, the number of participants per site, any experience threshold, and any account of who declined. Recruitment via institutional QA offices is a gatekeeper route with a predictable selection direction — offices are likelier to nominate cooperative, QA-invested staff — and this is unaddressed anywhere in the manuscript. Combined with the §4.5 exclusion of dissenters, the sample is filtered twice toward the same pole, once at recruitment and once at analysis. For the survey, no sampling frame, no recruitment route, no response rate, and no per-site distribution are reported at all. Twelve (or fourteen) administrators is a defensible qualitative N; it is the *description* of how they were selected, not the number, that fails here.

#### Data Collection

Interview mode, duration, recording, and transcription are reported (§3.3) — adequate for the format. Not reported: the interview protocol itself (neither appended nor summarised, so the domains covered are unknown), whether it was piloted, the language of collection and any translation procedure, the setting, who conducted the interviews, and whether transcripts were returned to participants. For the survey: the instrument is entirely undescribed — no source or provenance, no item count, no response scale or range, no reliability evidence, no indication whether "institutional quality culture" was measured by one item or a multi-item scale, and no operational definition of the construct being scored. "M=3.9, SD=0.6" cannot be read without at least the scale range. Administration mode ("online") is the only instrument detail present.

#### Analysis Methods

§3.4 gives two sentences for the qualitative analysis and one clause for the quantitative. Qualitative: "analyzed thematically" names no tradition (Braun & Clarke reflexive TA, codebook TA, Boyatzis, and qualitative content analysis carry different and partly incompatible obligations); no coder count; no codebook or coding frame; no inter-coder agreement or negotiated consensus; no software; no audit trail; no member checking; no peer debriefing; no negative-case analysis (§4.5 confirms the opposite occurred); no reflexivity or positionality. "Until a stable structure was reached" gestures at saturation without operationalising it or reporting evidence. Quantitative: "summarized descriptively, and subgroup comparisons were examined where role categories permitted" — the analysis of record in §4.4 compares *institutions*, not role categories, so the methods section does not describe the analysis the findings report. No test is named anywhere. Statistical assumptions are neither tested nor mentioned. No effect size, no CI, no power consideration, no correction for multiple comparisons despite the plural "comparisons," and no missing-data statement despite 28 of 48 respondents being unaccounted for in the only reported comparison. APA 7.0 formatting is not met (`M=3.9` should be *M* = 3.9, italicised with spaces; `p<.05` should be *p* < .05 with the exact value reported).

**Statistical reporting completeness: Unacceptable.** Effect size: absent. CI: absent. Power: absent. Assumption testing: absent. Missing data: absent. APA format: non-conformant. Red flags: undisclosed multiplicity (§3.4 plural comparisons, one reported); a subgroup contrast reported at p<.05 on n=9 vs n=11 with the test unnamed; and an analysis unit (institution) that differs from the one pre-described in the methods (role category). These constitute a selective-reporting pattern, not a set of independent omissions.

#### Results Presentation

Findings are presented as three themes with illustrative quotations. Frequency language is used without any counting: "recurred repeatedly," "recurred across every interview" (§4.2), "several administrators" (§4.3), "consistently described" (§4.2) — none anchored to a count, and none checkable against a participant N that the manuscript states two different ways. No participant table, no theme–participant matrix, no per-site breakdown of who said what, and no figures or tables of any kind. For a three-site comparative design, the absence of any cross-site display means the reader cannot see whether the themes are distributed across sites or concentrated in one — which is exactly the claim §4.1 makes without evidence. Non-confirming results are not merely under-reported; §4.5 states they were removed. The one genuinely disconfirming quantitative result is present but relabelled as corroboration.

#### Reproducibility

Not replicable as written. #### Methodological Fallacies Detected

## Review Body

The qualitative core of this study is legitimate and the question is worth answering. Senior QA administrators occupying the hinge between external requirement and internal practice are an under-documented population, and interviews of 45–70 minutes across three contrasting institutional types are a reasonable instrument for reaching them. My assessment is not that the study was not worth doing; it is that the manuscript currently reports it in a way that cannot be appraised, and makes claims the design cannot carry.

Start with what a reader cannot determine. The number of interviewees is stated two ways — fourteen in the Abstract, twelve in §3.2 — so the denominator behind "recurred across every interview" (§4.2) and "several administrators" (§4.3) is unknown. The interview protocol is neither appended nor summarised, so the domains covered are unknown. The analytic tradition is unnamed, the coder count unreported, disagreement handling unmentioned, and the audit trail, member checking, peer debriefing, and reflexivity statement all absent, so how the three themes were derived from transcripts is unknown. The survey instrument is undescribed in every particular that would make M=3.9 legible: source, item count, response scale, range, reliability. Of 48 respondents, only 20 are ever accounted for. None of these individually would be unusual in a short-form paper; together they mean the study is not appraisable, let alone replicable, and that is the standard D1 measures.

Then there is what the manuscript reports about its own conduct. Section 4.5 states that dissenting participants "were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure that organized our analysis," and in the preceding sentence that the study "achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views." The stated exclusion criterion is not length; it is non-conformity with the theme structure. Cases were removed because they disconfirmed the finding, and the finding was then reported as comprehensive. Negative-case analysis is the procedure that makes thematic claims dependable; running it in reverse and then claiming full-range coverage is not a reporting slip that softer wording would repair. The three themes are, to an unknown degree, a product of the exclusion, and §6's "first comprehensive account" inherits the problem directly. This finding is reachable from more than one direction — an ethics reviewer will reach the same paragraph by asking what the excluded participants consented to — and its arriving twice should be read as convergence, not duplication.

The quantitative strand compounds rather than offsets this. Section 4.4 reports a statistically significant difference at p<.05 between n=9 and n=11 with no test named, no statistic, no degrees of freedom, no effect size, no confidence interval, and no assumption check, and §3.4 signals that multiple subgroup comparisons were run — on role categories, while §4.4 reports an institution-type comparison the methods section never describes. One comparison reported from an undisclosed set, on a grouping the analysis plan did not specify, with no correction, at a sample size where legitimate significance is hard to reach: that pattern has a name, and the appropriate response is not to argue about the p-value but to record that the claim is not evaluable as reported. The moderator inference built on it in §4.4 ("institutional type as a possible moderator") should not stand in any version of this manuscript that does not supply the full reporting.

On the mixed-methods label: §3.1 gives priority and sequence in outline, but names no design type, no integration point, and no meta-inference procedure, and §4.4 states that the survey "corroborated the qualitative picture" one sentence before stating that the interviews "had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture." Corroboration and disconfirmation cannot both be true of one result. What the data appear to show is a level-of-institution discordance — administrators experiencing ritualism more acutely than staff at large — and that is a genuinely interesting result which the paper has relabelled into agreement. Reporting the divergent result at all is to the authors' credit; framing it as corroboration is not.

The coherence failures follow from all of this. Section 5 moves from three institutions in one unnamed system to "universities across the sector," "administrators everywhere," and "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture." Section 6 adds "the first comprehensive account" and a forward-looking effectiveness claim — that processes designed for negotiated character "are more likely to foster the internalized commitment" — from cross-sectional retrospective self-report with no comparison condition, no temporal ordering, and no outcome data. The sample was filtered twice toward the same pole, first by recruitment through institutional QA offices and again by the §4.5 exclusion, and neither filter is acknowledged. There is no limitations section anywhere, so nothing in the manuscript constrains any of these claims. Note also that §3.5's "fully anonymized… so that no individual could be identified" is contradicted by §4.1's attribution to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region"; the participant-protection consequences belong to another seat, but from mine it means the data-handling procedure the methods section describes is not the procedure the findings section evidences.

The route forward is not complicated, though it is substantial. Fix the participant count and publish a participants table. Reinstate the dissenting accounts and delete the balance claim — it cannot be rescued by qualification. Either report the survey to full standard or demote it to labelled descriptive context and drop the significance and moderator claims; at n=48 with 28 unaccounted for, demotion costs the paper very little and buys back its credibility. Name the analytic tradition, report the coding procedure and coder count, add positionality, append the protocol. Restate the findings at the scope three institutions support, and add a limitations section that names which specific conclusions each limitation constrains. Do that and there is a sound, publishable qualitative study here. As submitted, the manuscript's central claims are larger than its design, and two of them are contradicted by its own text.

No instruction-injection or reviewer-directed content was present in the manuscript; it was treated throughout as data under review.

3. **W3 — "Quality culture" is never made into a construct that could be confirmed or disconfirmed, yet it is scored.** Section 1 supplies a nominal definition attributed to Brennan & Osei (2020) — "the shared values, practices, and commitments through which an institution takes collective ownership of its own standards" — so the paper is not definitionally silent, and I want to be exact about that. Decoupling is not a neutral verb in this literature; it carries specific structural commitments in organizational sociology, and the finding in §4.1 — elaborate documentation produced for an external audience and disconnected from the technical core — is that tradition's founding observation. The manuscript neither names nor cites it, and uses "decouple" loosely, as a synonym for "drift apart." For a paper on building institutional quality culture, the load-bearing implicit premises are predictable in kind — that administrators' accounts are a valid proxy for institutional reality, that quality culture is a coherent property an institution can be said to possess and build, that QA implementation is a change process amenable to managerial intention, that compliance behaviour and cultural commitment lie on one continuum. I ask whether the argument would still stand if a reader from an adjacent field rejected one of these. I do not perform formal fallacy detection or section-to-section contradiction checking — those belong to the Devil's Advocate — and where my finding overlaps theirs, I report independently and let the synthesizer resolve it.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority).** This is my home dimension. The contract asks whether framing, definitions, and implications are accessible to adjacent-field readers, and whether interdisciplinary claims are substantiated. Two distinct tests live inside that sentence and I will run both. The accessibility test: can a reader from organizational behaviour, public administration, or implementation science follow the framing without insider QA vocabulary, and are field-specific terms and acronyms defined at first use rather than assumed? The substantiation test, which is the stricter one: when the paper reaches into another discipline — organizational culture theory, institutional theory, change management, sensemaking — does it carry that discipline's actual construct with its scope conditions and known contestations, or does it use a borrowed label decoratively? A paper can score well on accessibility and still fail substantiation, and the reverse. I also read this dimension for cross-cultural and cross-sectoral transfer: findings about administrators in one national QA regime do not automatically travel to another, and unmarked generalization is a substantiation failure, not merely a limitation to note.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority).** The contract asks about organization, clarity of exposition, figure and table quality, and venue conventions. My angle is the reader who is not from QA studies: structure and exposition are relevant to me insofar as they determine whether an adjacent-field reader can locate the argument, see what evidence supports what claim, and tell the empirical findings apart from the authors' interpretation and recommendations. At 2,249 words the paper is very short for an empirical study in this field, so my structural concern is proportionality — whether a compressed format has collapsed the distinctions a reader needs (method vs. findings, participant voice vs. author inference, evidence vs. implication) rather than whether the prose is polished. I hold this dimension to a lower bar than the others, consistent with its normal priority, and I do not police venue-specific formatting conventions I cannot verify.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
- **what_to_look_for**: Whether the design is stated at all and matches the question about administrator perspectives (qualitative interviews, survey, case study, mixed); who the administrators are, how many, from what institution types, and how they were selected; whether the analytic procedure connecting raw accounts to reported themes is described; whether the paper distinguishes self-reported perception from institutional practice; whether any reflexivity or positionality statement appears where the authors are themselves QA insiders; whether ethics/consent handling is mentioned for human participants; whether the reproducibility affordances an adjacent-field reader would expect (instrument, protocol, coding scheme, data availability) exist even in summary form; whether the word budget has crowded out method reporting entirely.
- **what_triggers_block**: The paper draws empirical conclusions about institutional quality culture from participant data while giving no recoverable account of who was studied or how the data were analyzed — no sample description AND no analytic procedure — so the inference from evidence to claim cannot be reconstructed by any reader; or the design is structurally incapable of supporting a claim the paper actually makes (e.g., causal or effectiveness claims about QA implementation drawn from one round of self-report, or claims about institutional outcomes evidenced only by administrator opinion), and this mismatch is asserted rather than acknowledged.
- **what_triggers_warn**: Design and sample are identifiable but thin in ways that limit outside assessment — sample size or composition given without selection rationale, analysis named ("thematic analysis") without procedure, no reflexivity where insider positionality is likely to shape findings, ethics/consent unmentioned, or no data/instrument availability statement; or the perception-vs-practice boundary is blurred in places but the paper's central claim does not depend on the conflation.

### D2: domain_accuracy
- **what_to_look_for**: Whether core QA terms (quality culture, quality assurance, internal/external QA, accreditation, enhancement vs. accountability, institutional effectiveness) are used consistently with their established meaning in the QA literature; whether the paper's characterization of prior QA scholarship is recognizable rather than strawmanned; whether constructs imported from adjacent fields (organizational culture, institutional isomorphism, change management, sensemaking, professional learning communities) are used in the sense their home literature established; whether national/system-level QA arrangements are described accurately if named; whether empirical claims attributed to prior work are stated at the strength that work supports; whether any acronym or framework is invoked without a referent.
- **what_triggers_block**: A load-bearing claim rests on a factually wrong statement about the domain — a QA construct, framework, or regulatory arrangement described in a way its source literature would not accept, or prior work characterized as finding something it did not — such that removing the error removes the paper's basis for its conclusion; or a construct borrowed from another discipline is redefined to mean something incompatible with its established sense and that redefinition carries the argument.
- **what_triggers_warn**: Terminology drifts across the paper (the same term doing two jobs, e.g. quality culture as both cause and outcome) without the drift being marked; or prior-work characterizations are imprecise, over-general, or stated at higher confidence than the cited base supports, without a mandatory claim depending on the imprecision; or a borrowed adjacent-field construct is used loosely but recognizably.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
- **what_to_look_for**: Whether the central thesis is stated explicitly enough to be tested; the chain from participant accounts → interpreted themes → institutional claims → recommendations, and whether each link is warranted or merely asserted; explicit premises and whether they survive an outsider's challenge; implicit premises the argument needs but never states (administrator accounts as proxy for institutional reality; quality culture as a buildable, coherent institutional property; QA implementation as a manageable change process; compliance and commitment on a single continuum); paradigmatic commitments (managerialist framing, linear causality from policy to culture, rational-actor administrators) and whether the paper shows awareness of them; whether counter-evidence, disconfirming cases, or alternative interpretations of the same accounts are entertained; whether conclusions and recommendations stay inside what the evidence licenses.
- **what_triggers_block**: The central thesis depends on an unexamined premise that, if an adjacent-field reader declines to grant it, collapses the paper's conclusion — and the paper neither states nor defends that premise anywhere; or recommendations/conclusions assert a level of certainty (causal, prescriptive, generalizable) that no part of the presented evidence can carry, so the argument's conclusion is disconnected from its evidence rather than merely over-reaching at the margin.
- **what_triggers_warn**: Load-bearing implicit assumptions are present and unexamined but the argument survives their being questioned in weakened form; or a plausible alternative interpretation of the findings (e.g. administrator accounts reflecting institutional impression management or role-based framing rather than culture) goes unaddressed; or conclusions overreach in specific passages while the main claim stays inside the evidence.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
- **what_to_look_for**: Whether QA-specific vocabulary and acronyms are defined at first use for a non-QA reader; whether the framing states why the problem matters beyond the QA community (to organizational studies, public administration, implementation science, professions research); whether borrowed theory arrives with its scope conditions, boundary assumptions, and known contestations or only as a citation-decorated label; whether the paper engages the adjacent literatures its own topic sits on top of — organizational culture and climate research, institutional theory and decoupling, implementation science, street-level bureaucracy — at least enough to position itself; whether stakeholders beyond administrators (faculty, students, QA staff, external agencies) appear as affected parties; whether the national/sectoral context of the findings is named and transfer limits stated; whether implications are written so a practitioner or policymaker outside the field could act on them.
- **what_triggers_block**: An interdisciplinary claim is made and left unsubstantiated in a way that carries the paper — a theory or construct from another discipline is invoked as the paper's framework or as warrant for its conclusion while being used in a sense that discipline would reject, or with no engagement with that discipline's evidence at all; or the paper generalizes its findings to contexts (other national QA systems, other institution types, other sectors) with no basis, and that generalization is what the contribution claim rests on; or framing and definitions are so wholly internal to QA insiders that an adjacent-field reader cannot determine what is being claimed.
- **what_triggers_warn**: The paper is readable across fields but its framing stays inside the QA conversation — adjacent literatures that bear directly on quality culture and implementation go unengaged; or borrowed constructs are used correctly but thinly, without scope conditions; or stakeholder coverage is administrator-only with no acknowledgement that other affected voices exist; or context of findings is identifiable but transfer limits are unstated; or undefined field-specific terms and acronyms appear but do not block comprehension of the main claim.

### D5: writing_and_structure
- **what_to_look_for**: Whether the paper has recoverable sections (problem/context, method, findings, discussion, implications) or at least a discernible equivalent progression; whether findings are separable from the authors' interpretation and from recommendations; whether any figures or tables have self-contained captions and whether the text's reading of them follows from what they display; whether the argument is locatable — abstract or opening states the claim; whether the ~2,249-word budget has been allocated coherently or has collapsed necessary distinctions; whether citation and reference formatting is internally consistent; whether hedging is calibrated to what the study can show.
- **what_triggers_block**: The organization prevents an adjacent-field reader from determining what was done, what was found, and what is being claimed — sections absent or so conflated that evidence, interpretation, and recommendation are indistinguishable throughout; or a figure/table is central to a claim and its caption or the text's reading of it contradicts what the artifact actually shows.
- **what_triggers_warn**: Structure is followable but uneven — interpretation bleeding into the findings section, thin or missing method/limitations exposition, an underweighted section that leaves a claim unsupported in exposition; or figures/tables have non-self-contained captions; or citation formatting is inconsistent; or the compressed length has produced telegraphic passages where the reader must infer the connective argument, without any single claim becoming unrecoverable.

### Summary Assessment

The underlying study is worth doing. Administrators occupying the hinge between an external QA regime and internal academic practice are a genuinely under-heard population, and the three themes are recognizable and plausible. My concerns are not about whether the topic deserves publication but about three things the manuscript does that an outsider notices immediately.

First, §3.5 promises that "no individual could be identified in reported findings," and §4.1 then attributes the manuscript's most career-damaging quotation ("It's theater, and everyone knows it's theater") to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" — a role that, given a three-institution design containing exactly one private university, belongs to one determinable person. That is an anonymity claim the manuscript itself breaks on the same read.

Second, the paper's headline finding — organizations producing elaborate ceremonial documentation that is decoupled from the technical work it purports to govern — is the foundational observation of neo-institutional organizational sociology. The manuscript reaches it without naming, citing, or engaging that tradition, and presents it as fresh discovery. This costs the paper analytic power it could simply pick up.

Third, the operative recommendation in §5 (favour "processes that maximize genuine participation") is aligned to Delacroix (2018) as *recommending* consultation, while §2 and the reference annotation both state Delacroix argues against exactly that. Whichever reading is correct, the paper's practical guidance currently rests on an internal contradiction, and a practitioner acting on it may be doing the thing the cited source warns produces more ritual.

### Strengths (4 items)

1. **A genuinely under-heard population, correctly located.** The framing of administrators as occupying a structural hinge rather than as either enforcers or victims is the manuscript's real contribution, and it is the kind of position that organizational sociology recognizes immediately (boundary-spanning, brokerage, street-level discretion) but that QA scholarship rarely centres in participants' own words. This is worth publishing.

2. **The identity-work theme resists the easy reading.** §4.3's observation that administrators "held the tension open, treating the gap itself as the space in which their professional identity was located" is analytically better than the compliance-versus-resistance binary the topic usually attracts. An outsider reads this as the paper's most original sentence.

3. **The survey's disconfirmation is more interesting than the paper lets it be.** That staff sentiment was moderately positive where administrators described ritualism is a finding about *stratified perception* — who in an organization can see the ceremony as ceremony. §5's closing paragraph gestures at this. It deserves to be the point, not a "note of complication."

4. **The manuscript takes ethics seriously in principle.** §3.5 states approval, consent, and anonymization explicitly rather than omitting the topic. My finding below is that the reporting practice contradicts the stated commitment — but the commitment is stated, which makes the fix tractable and locates the problem in the Findings section rather than in the study's design.

### Weaknesses (5 items)

1. **W1 — A real participant is identifiable, against the manuscript's own anonymity claim (must fix before any publication).** §3.5 states that "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified in reported findings." §4.1 then attributes a highly critical quotation to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region." §3.2 establishes that the sample comprises three institutions, exactly one of them private. The role is singular within an institution, the institution is determinable from the description, and the quotation is career-relevant: it accuses the speaker's own institution's QA practice of being theatre. The same section identifies "the associate dean for quality at the public research university," and §4.2 "one registry head" (weaker, but still a singular role). *Why this matters from my seat:* an anonymity promise is what the consent was given against. If a participant consented on the understanding that no individual could be identified, then publishing a determinable attribution changes the terms after the fact, and the person carrying the consequence is not the author. It also does not distinguish anonymity from confidentiality — the manuscript claims the former while practising something closer to pseudonymized role-attribution, which for a unique senior role is not de-identification at all. *Specific suggestions:* (a) strip institution-type-plus-role compounds throughout the Findings, attributing quotations to broad role bands (e.g. "a senior quality post-holder") with no institutional locator; (b) where the argument genuinely needs the institution type, use the type without the role, or the role without the type, never both; (c) re-check that the aggregate of scattered descriptors does not reconstruct an identity across sections; (d) state in §3.5 what was actually done (confidentiality via role-band pseudonymization) rather than claiming full anonymization; (e) confirm with the ethics committee whether the approval covered attributed quotation of a unique post-holder, and — given the quotation's content — consider returning to that participant for a review-of-attribution before publication. I recognize that role-plus-institution attribution is common practice in this field and that authors do it to preserve analytic texture; from an ethics-committee seat, common practice does not make a three-site design safe.

2. **W2 — The central finding reinvents neo-institutional theory without naming it.** "Elaborate documentation exercises whose connection to actual teaching quality they found tenuous" (§4.1) is ceremonial conformity and decoupling: the observation that organizations facing institutional pressure adopt formal structures for legitimacy while buffering their technical core from them. This is not an obscure corner of my field; it is its founding move, and it has forty years of subsequent work on when decoupling is loose, when it tightens under monitoring, and when it converts into "means-ends decoupling" where practice is coupled to policy but neither is coupled to outcomes. *Why this matters:* as written, §5's first finding can only "confirm long-standing concerns," which is the weakest possible contribution. With the tradition named, the same data does more work — the paper's actual novelty becomes that decoupling is *inhabited*, that the administrators who produce the ceremony can name it as ceremony and still invest identity in it, which is a mechanism the classical account does not supply. *Specific suggestion:* add a short framing paragraph in §2 positioning the study against decoupling scholarship, then reframe §5's first finding as extending rather than confirming it. I cannot tell from the text whether the omission is unawareness or a deliberate choice to stay inside the QA conversation; either way the paper loses by it.

3. **W3 — The practical recommendation is not safely actionable as written.** §5 aligns the paper's recommendation to favour maximal participation with "Delacroix (2018), who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture." §2 of the same manuscript says Delacroix "argues *against* treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of a healthy quality culture," and the reference-list annotation agrees with §2. I am not in a position to say what Delacroix actually argues — I am reporting only what this manuscript says about its own source, and it says two incompatible things. *Why this matters practically:* a quality office reading §5 would add consultation rounds. If the §2 reading is right, adding consultation rounds to an institution that already experiences QA as theatre produces one more documented artifact — the exact failure mode the paper's own leading theme describes. The recommendation, unrepaired, risks instructing practitioners to intensify the pathology. *Specific suggestion:* resolve the direction, and if §2 is correct, rewrite the recommendation to distinguish consultation-as-evidence (which the paper's own data suggests is co-optable) from conditions under which participation is consequential — did anything change as a result, do participants see the trace, is there a mechanism by which the consultation binds a decision. That distinction is testable and implementable; "processes that maximize genuine participation" is not, because "genuine" is doing all the work and is not operationalized anywhere.

4. **W4 — The excluded dissenters are the participants who most needed the record.** §4.5 states in one sentence that the study "achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views," and in the next that participants with "more skeptical or dissenting views about the value of the entire QA enterprise" were "excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure." Reviewer 1 will address what this does to the analytic claim; my concern is different and additive. These people gave interview time on the understanding their perspective would be represented; the perspectives removed are precisely the ones a QA-critical administrator has no other safe channel to voice, and inside a three-site system they may be the most professionally exposed. *Why this matters:* the exclusion sentence and the balance sentence cannot both survive being read by an excluded participant — and in a study of three institutions, excluded participants are likely readers. *Specific suggestions:* delete the "full range" claim (it is not what the paper did); report the dissenting accounts, even briefly, as a fourth theme or an explicit negative case — disconfirming cases strengthen a thematic account rather than untidying it; and if space is genuinely binding, say which views were set aside and why, so a reader knows the shape of what is missing rather than being told nothing is.

5. **W5 — Stakeholders beyond administrators are absent, including from the recommendations that will land on them.** The design centres administrators, which is legitimate and is the paper's point. But §5 and §6 issue design prescriptions for QA processes without any evidence from, or acknowledgement of, the people those processes are done to: academics (present only as literature, in §2), students (invoked rhetorically in §4.3's "whether students get a fair deal" but never consulted or represented), professional and administrative staff below the senior tier (the survey's 48 respondents supply a mean, not a voice), and the external agencies whose behaviour the whole apparatus responds to. *Why this matters:* the Conclusion's claim that quality culture is "an ongoing negotiation among administrators, academics, and external bodies" names three parties and evidences one. A negotiation account requires at least the shape of the counterparties' positions, or it is a single party's report of a negotiation. *Specific suggestion:* either narrow the claim to what one party's accounts license ("how administrators construe the negotiation they are in") or state the missing-parties limitation explicitly and mark the negotiation framing as a hypothesis the design cannot test. Also add students to the limitations discussion — the ultimate stakeholder of a quality regime is nowhere in the study.

### Detailed Comments

#### Assumption Audit

**Explicit assumptions.** Two are stated and both are defensible on their face. (a) That quality culture is the right object of study, and that it can be "built" or "cultivated" (§1, §2, §6). (b) That administrators' accounts illuminate quality-culture formation (§1). The second is the load-bearing one and it is stated carefully — "how their accounts illuminate" is appropriately modest. The problem is that §5 and §6 do not hold that modesty: §5's second finding moves from three institutions to "universities across the sector" and "administrators everywhere," and §6 claims "the first comprehensive account." Those are not the assumptions the Introduction committed to. From outside the field this reads as two papers stapled together — a careful interpretive study and a confident generalizing one.

**Implicit assumptions.** Four, none stated, all consequential:

- *Administrator accounts are a valid proxy for institutional reality.* The manuscript treats what administrators say about ritualism as evidence that QA *is* ritual, rather than as evidence about how administrators construe their work. An organizational sociologist would immediately raise impression management: a quality director telling an academic researcher that the portfolios are theatre is performing a particular professional identity — the clear-eyed insider who is not fooled by their own paperwork — and that performance is itself interesting data, but it is not the same as a description of institutional practice. §4.3 comes close to seeing this ("this vocational framing appeared to function as a coping resource") and then does not apply the same lens to §4.1. *Suggestion:* treat the theatre talk as identity work too, not just as reportage.
- *Compliance behaviour and cultural commitment lie on one continuum.* The paper's implicit model runs from ritual compliance at one end to internalized commitment at the other, and improvement means travelling toward the far end. My field's evidence does not support a single dimension: decoupling can be tight or loose independently of whether staff believe in the goal, and organizations routinely exhibit high belief with low coupling and the reverse. If compliance and commitment are two axes rather than one, the paper's central puzzle ("genuine improvement or merely the appearance of it," §1) has a third and fourth quadrant it never visits.
- *Quality culture is a coherent property an institution can be said to possess in the singular.* The survey scores it as one number per institution (M=3.9). The interviews suggest administrators and staff at large experience it differently (§5, final paragraph). Those two statements are in tension: if perception is stratified by position, an institution-level score is an average across incompatible standpoints, not a measurement of a shared property. The paper notices the divergence and does not notice what it does to the construct.
- *QA implementation is a change process amenable to managerial intention.* §6's prescription that processes "designed with this negotiated character in mind… are more likely to foster internalized commitment" assumes that negotiation can be designed for from above. If quality culture is genuinely negotiated among parties with unequal power, the party doing the designing is one of the negotiators, and designing the negotiation is a move within it, not a solution to it. This is not fatal, but it is unexamined.

**Paradigmatic assumptions.** The manuscript is interpretive in method and managerialist in its implications, and does not notice the seam. Its explanatory frame is essentially voluntarist — culture forms because the right people cultivate it in the right way — with almost no attention to structural constraint: resource asymmetry between the three institution types, the coercive character of accreditation deadlines, labour conditions of the staff producing the portfolios, or the market position that makes a private university's compliance calculus different from a public research university's. Institutional theory's alternative is unavailable to the paper because the tradition is unnamed: that decoupling may be an *equilibrium* rather than a pathology — a rational institutional response to conflicting demands, stable and reproducing, not a failure awaiting a better process. That reading would change the recommendations substantially, and the paper never has to confront it. There is also an unexamined normative commitment: that internalized commitment to QA is the good outcome. A reader from occupational sociology might ask whether administrators' scepticism is a defect to be cultivated away or an accurate professional judgement about a governance apparatus, and whether "creating room for meaning-making" is empowerment or a more sophisticated form of consent-manufacture. I raise this as a question the paper should be able to answer, not as a claim that it is wrong.

#### Cross-Disciplinary Connections

**Parallel research.** My own field has studied this exact object under different names for decades: ceremonial conformity and decoupling in organizational sociology; means-ends decoupling in later institutional work; audit-society and accountability scholarship in public administration, which describes precisely the "season of the year given over to feeding the machine" as a rituals-of-verification effect; street-level bureaucracy in policy implementation, whose central finding — that frontline discretion is where policy is actually made — maps directly onto §4.2's distributed-leadership theme; and institutional-work / institutional-logics scholarship, which is what §4.3's identity theme is an instance of. Implementation science offers a fourth angle: fidelity-versus-adaptation, the question of whether local modification of a mandated process is drift or necessary translation, which is the same question §4.1 poses without the vocabulary. *Why this helps:* each of these bodies of work supplies mechanisms and boundary conditions the manuscript currently has to assert.

**Borrowing opportunities.** Four concepts would each earn their keep. *Decoupling (policy–practice, and means–ends)* gives §4.1 a mechanism and a literature instead of a confirmation. *Institutional logics* explains why administrators experience the tension as irreducible — they sit at the intersection of a professional-academic logic and a managerial-accountability logic, and identity work is what one does at an intersection, which is exactly §4.3's finding restated with a causal story. *Boundary spanning / brokerage* names the hinge position that §2's gap claim and Silva and Tan's intermediary framing both reach for, and comes with an established finding that brokers experience role conflict and buffer it through identity strategies — which is, again, the paper's own result. *Ceremonial versus substantive adoption as an equilibrium* supplies the counter-hypothesis the paper needs to rule out. Adopting these would also solve the manuscript's contribution problem: it would no longer need the "first comprehensive account" claim, because a precise theoretical contribution is more defensible than a priority claim.

**Methodological borrowing.** Three suggestions, offered with the caveat that Reviewer 1 owns methods. First, my field would want the *practice* alongside the account: documents (a self-evaluation report, an action plan, a panel response) read against what administrators say they are for. That is the standard way to test a decoupling claim without relying on informants' self-reports, and it is cheap. Second, if quality culture is stratified by position, a design that samples vertically within one institution — quality director, faculty QA lead, department head, teaching staff, student representative — buys more than three institutions at one tier. Third, negative-case analysis is the specific remedy for what §4.5 discloses: the dissenting participants are not a space problem, they are the analytic resource that would let the paper say why the three themes hold where they hold. I would also observe, as an ethics chair, that a design with vertical sampling inside identifiable institutions raises the identifiability stakes rather than lowering them, so W1's remedy would need to be built into the protocol from the start.

#### Practical Impact

**Real-world application.** What a quality office can actually do with this manuscript today is thinner than the Discussion implies. The one operative prescription — "processes that maximize genuine participation should be favored" (§5) — is unimplementable as stated because "genuine" is unspecified and, per W3, is anchored to a source the manuscript elsewhere says argues the opposite. §6's "create room for meaning-making" is not a design instruction; a director asked to implement it has no way to know whether they have done it, and no way to tell it apart from holding more meetings. What *does* survive and is worth stating plainly: the finding that persuasion and coalition-building, not documentation, consume most QA administrators' effort (§4.2) is directly usable — it argues for staffing and workload models that recognize relational work, and against the assumption that a quality office is a documentation function. That is a concrete, implementable implication the paper undersells. Similarly, the stratified-perception finding (§5) implies that institutions should stop measuring quality culture with a single instrument administered at one tier, which is an actionable measurement recommendation.

**Implementation feasibility.** Barriers the paper does not discuss: participation costs staff time in institutions that are usually not resourced for it, and the three institution types in the sample almost certainly differ in that capacity — the private university and the public research university are not equally able to absorb consultation load, and the paper's own §4.4 finding that institutional type may moderate quality culture hints at exactly this without following it. Accreditation deadlines are externally fixed, so "cultivating" is being recommended into a calendar the institution does not control (§4.1's participants say so directly). And unintended consequences deserve a paragraph: participation mechanisms that are visibly consequence-free are worse than no consultation, because they teach staff that voice is decorative — which is, if §2's reading of Delacroix is correct, precisely the warning the manuscript inverts.

**Stakeholders.** Covered in W5. The one I want to underline is students. A quality regime exists nominally for them; in this manuscript they appear once, as a rhetorical warrant inside an administrator's self-description (§4.3). A paper prescribing QA process design without any student-side consideration should say so as a limitation.

#### Broader Implications

**Ethical dimensions.** Three, in descending order of severity. (1) The identifiability failure in §4.1 against the §3.5 anonymity claim (W1) — this is the one that should hold publication, because the harm is to a determinable person, is career-relevant, and cannot be repaired by revising the argument. It also raises a question the manuscript should answer explicitly: whether the ethical approval described in §3.5 contemplated attributed quotation of a unique senior post-holder, and whether the consent language used the word anonymity, in which case the reported practice does not match what participants agreed to. The manuscript should also state whether transcripts or quotations were returned to participants for approval; in insider-elite interviewing on a career-sensitive topic, that is where an anonymity risk of this kind is normally caught. (2) The removal of dissenting participants' contributions from the record while claiming full-range representation (W4) — a consent-adjacent problem, since the participants gave time on a representation premise the paper does not honour. (3) A quieter one: this is research on employees, conducted through their employers' QA offices (§3.2 — participants were "recruited through institutional QA offices"). Recruitment through the office a participant works in carries a coercion risk on voluntariness that the manuscript does not address, and it interacts with W1: if the employer can infer who spoke, gatekeeper recruitment plus role-attributed criticism is a combination an ethics committee would want to see justified.

**Social impact.** The finding that senior administrators privately regard QA as theatre while institutional-level metrics register moderate positivity (§4.4, §5) is a politically loaded result, and the paper does not consider how it can be used. Read one way, it supports lightening a compliance burden; read another, it is evidence that institutions cannot be trusted to self-report and warrants tighter external monitoring — which the decoupling literature predicts would produce more ceremony, not less. Since the manuscript withholds its national system, readers cannot judge which regulatory environment the finding would land in, and the authors cannot control the use. I would add a short paragraph on this. There is also an equity dimension the paper is positioned to see and doesn't: compliance load is not distributed equally across institution types, and if the well-resourced research university can staff a quality office while a teaching-focused or private institution cannot, then a uniform QA regime is a regressive tax on the institutions serving less-advantaged students. §4.4's institutional-type contrast is a doorway to that argument.

**Future directions.** Four, from where I sit. First, a document-and-account design that tests decoupling directly rather than inferring it from informants. Section 4.1 then hands the manuscript's most damaging sentence — "It's theater, and everyone knows it's theater" — to the quality director of the largest private university in the region, in a study of three institutions of which exactly one is private. That is one determinable person, in a singular senior role, on record criticizing their own employer's core compliance function. The associate dean for quality at the public research university is identified in the same section, and a registry head in the next. None of this is repairable by revising an argument, which is why my recommendation is that publication be held rather than merely revised: the remedy runs through the Findings section's attribution practice, and quite possibly through the ethics committee and back to the participant. I note also that participants were recruited through the QA offices they work in (§3.2), so the party best placed to identify the speaker is the employer. I recognize that role-plus-institution attribution is ordinary practice in this literature and that authors do it to keep analytic texture; from an ethics-committee seat, ordinary practice does not make a three-site design safe, and the manuscript's own anonymity sentence is what makes this a contradiction rather than a judgement call. No contract dimension in this panel owns research ethics, so this finding appears in none of my five scores. The synthesizer should not read that silence as a measure of its weight.

The second thing an outsider sees is a paper rediscovering my own field's founding observation without knowing it. Organizations under institutional pressure adopt elaborate formal structures for legitimacy while buffering their technical core from them; the documentation is produced for the panel, not for the teaching. That is ceremonial conformity and decoupling, forty years deep, with subsequent work on when decoupling loosens, when monitoring tightens it, and when it becomes means-ends decoupling in which practice tracks policy and neither tracks outcomes. The manuscript reaches this and presents it as fresh, which forces §5 into the weakest available formulation: the finding "confirms long-standing concerns." It could do much more. The genuinely new thing here is that the decoupling is *inhabited* — the people manufacturing the ceremony can name it as ceremony and still locate a vocation inside it. §4.3 says this well and does not know it is saying something the classical account lacks. Naming the tradition costs a paragraph in §2 and converts a confirmation into a contribution. It also hands the paper the counter-hypothesis it needs to rule out, which is that decoupling may be a stable equilibrium rather than a pathology awaiting better process design — a reading that would change every recommendation in §5.

The third thing is what a practitioner would do on Monday. §5's operative advice is to favour "processes that maximize genuine participation," aligned to Delacroix (2018) as recommending consultation as the central mechanism. Section 2 of the same manuscript, and the reference-list annotation, both say Delacroix argues against exactly that. I am not in a position to say what Delacroix argues; I report only that this manuscript says two incompatible things about its own source, and that the practical consequence is not symmetrical. If §2 is right, then a quality office following §5 will add consultation rounds to an institution that already experiences QA as theatre, and will produce one more documented artifact — the failure mode the paper's own leading theme describes. §6's "create room for meaning-making" is not implementable either; a director cannot tell whether they have done it. What *is* usable, and undersold, is §4.2: administrators spend most of their effort on persuasion and coalition-building rather than documentation. That is a staffing and workload finding a practitioner can act on, and it argues directly against treating a quality office as a documentation function. §5's stratified-perception observation is similarly actionable — stop measuring institutional quality culture with one instrument at one tier — and is the most publishable unexploited result in the manuscript.

Underneath these sit four unstated premises. That administrators' accounts of ritualism describe institutional reality rather than how administrators construe their work — the paper applies the constructionist lens to §4.3's vocational talk and then declines to apply it to §4.1's theatre talk, where impression management by a clear-eyed insider is at least as good an explanation. That compliance and commitment lie on one continuum, when my field's evidence has them as two axes with populated off-diagonal quadrants the paper never visits. That quality culture is a singular institutional property, which the survey's one-number-per-institution scoring assumes and §5's own divergence finding undermines. And that the negotiation can be designed for from above, when the party designing it is one of the negotiators. None of these is fatal; all are load-bearing and none is named. The paradigmatic reading is voluntarist throughout — culture forms because the right people cultivate it — with structural constraint (resource asymmetry across the three institution types, externally fixed accreditation calendars, the labour producing the portfolios) almost entirely absent, and with an unexamined normative commitment that internalized belief in QA is the good outcome. An occupational sociologist would ask whether administrator scepticism is a defect to cultivate away or an accurate professional judgement, and whether "room for meaning-making" is empowerment or better-engineered consent. The paper should be able to answer that; I do not claim it answers wrongly.

Two further absences matter from outside. §4.5 tells the reader in consecutive sentences that the study captured the full range of administrator views and that dissenting participants were excluded because they fell outside the three-theme structure. Reviewer 1 will say what that does to the analytic claim; my point is different. Those participants gave their time on a representation premise, the views removed are the ones a QA-sceptical administrator has no other safe channel for, and in a three-site system they are plausible readers of this paragraph. Delete the full-range claim, report the dissent as a negative case — which strengthens a thematic account rather than untidying it — and the problem dissolves. And the Conclusion names three negotiating parties while evidencing one. Academics appear only as literature, students once as a rhetorical warrant inside an administrator's self-description, lower-tier staff only as a mean, external agencies not at all. Either narrow the claim to how administrators construe the negotiation they are in, or mark the negotiation framing as a hypothesis this design cannot test.

I want to be clear about what I am not saying. The study is worth doing and the population is genuinely under-heard; the hinge position is the right place to stand, and §4.3 is the best sentence in the manuscript. The prose is good. That last point deserves a caution for the panel: the fluency is doing concealment work here, in that several of the least defensible sentences are among the most graceful. On my five contract dimensions I score three mandatory warns and a high-priority warn that sat immediately below my block line, which places my seat's decision at F2. My substantive recommendation is narrower and firmer than that machinery expresses: the §4.1 attribution must be fixed before this appears anywhere, and no amount of revision to the argument will fix it.

### D1: methodology_rigor

### D2: domain_accuracy

- `what_to_look_for` — Directional balance of the cited evidence base: whether the critical strand of QA scholarship (ritualism, compliance culture, gaming, performativity, decoupling between formal QA systems and actual practice) appears at all, or whether every citation runs toward "QA implementation builds culture"; whether cited work is characterized accurately or flattened into support (a study reporting mixed or null findings cited as if it reported positive ones); construct discipline across "quality culture," "quality assurance," "quality management," "quality enhancement," and "institutional culture," and whether a claim earned about one is transferred to another; whether national/system-specific QA arrangements are described correctly and whether findings from one regulatory regime are asserted as general; whether any empirical figure or prior result attributed to a source is stated correctly; and whether contradicting evidence, where acknowledged, is genuinely engaged or named-and-dismissed.
- `what_triggers_block` — A load-bearing domain claim is wrong or a source is materially misrepresented: prior work cited for a conclusion it does not support; a domain construct used in a sense that inverts its accepted meaning in a way the argument depends on; a factual error about QA mechanisms or regulatory arrangements that the argument rests on; or systematic one-directional citation on a question the field openly contests, where the omitted counter-evidence would change the paper's conclusion rather than merely complicate it. ## Devil's Advocate Review

### Strengths (for fairness)

The manuscript asks a real question, holds a genuine tension at its center (Delacroix's participation-ritual objection against Ferreira & Lund's cultivation thesis), and §4.3's reading of vocational identity as a *coping resource* that holds the compliance–meaning gap open rather than resolving it is a genuinely interesting analytic move — the one place where the interview material extends rather than confirms the literature review.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

If I held the opposite view, I would refute this paper without leaving its own pages, because it refutes itself four times.

The thesis is that quality culture is "an ongoing negotiation among administrators, academics, and external bodies" (§6), evidenced by three themes drawn from administrator accounts. But §4.5 discloses that "a minority of participants expressed more skeptical or dissenting views about the value of the entire QA enterprise; these were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure." The three-theme structure is the finding. Cases were removed *because* they did not fit it. That is not a reporting economy; it is the finding constructing itself, and it is disclosed in the same paragraph that claims the study "captured the full range of administrator views." Both sentences cannot be true. Once the exclusion is known, no reader can distinguish "three themes recurred" from "three themes survived."

The negotiation thesis then fails a second, independent test: it is unfalsifiable as stated. The paper never specifies what is negotiated, by whom, under what constraint, or what observation would count against it. Ritual compliance, distributed leadership, and identity work are all read *as* negotiation; so, presumably, would their absence be. A thesis that no possible interview transcript could contradict is not supported by these transcripts — it is merely compatible with them.

And a more parsimonious rival account is available in the paper's own data. Aoki (2019) is cited in §2 for exactly it: anticipation of external review reorganizes internal priorities. Administrators recruited *through institutional QA offices* (§3.2), whose professional role is to make QA legible, describe QA as theater they nonetheless work to redeem. Occupational self-justification under audit pressure predicts every reported theme — including the identity-work theme, which is what self-justification looks like when it is sincere. The paper never tests this rival, never mentions it, and cites the source that supplies it for something else.

Then the recommendation. §5's operative guidance — "processes that maximize genuine participation should be favored" — is aligned with "Delacroix (2018), who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture." §2 of this same manuscript states that Delacroix "argues *against* treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of a healthy quality culture," and the reference-list annotation agrees. The paper's central practical claim is warranted by the inversion of its own cited authority. Restore Delacroix and the recommendation becomes the thing Delacroix warns about: one more consultation artifact for the panel.

Four failures, four grounds, all internal. The paper does not need an external referee to fall over; it needs a reader who cross-checks §2 against §5, and §4.5 against itself.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. **Occupational self-justification under audit pressure** (the strongest rival; see C5). QA officeholders recruited through QA offices have a professional stake in QA being meaningful. This single mechanism generates all three themes without any appeal to "negotiation," and it is more parsimonious because it needs no unobserved cultural process. The paper cites the source that supplies it (Aoki, 2019) for a different purpose.
2. **Neo-institutional decoupling.** The ritual-compliance finding — elaborate ceremonial documentation running parallel to, and disconnected from, technical work — is a textbook instance of ceremonial conformity. Presented here as a fresh empirical discovery, it is the oldest prediction in organizational theory. If the tradition were engaged, the finding would be a confirmation with a known boundary condition rather than a novelty; the paper's contribution claim depends on not noticing this.
3. **Selection at the institutional level.** Three institutions willing to grant QA-office-mediated research access are plausibly institutions with functioning, self-confident QA offices. Site self-selection is an alternative account of why every site produced the same reconciling narrative, and it is never raised.
4. **Level effects rather than negotiation.** §4.4's own result — staff sentiment moderately positive where administrators report ritualism, with a *significant* between-institution difference — is at least as consistent with role-position and institution-type effects as with a shared sector-wide negotiation. The paper notes the divergence as "worth pursuing in future work" (§5) after having already generalized past it (§5, second finding).
5. **Interview-context effects.** Administrators speaking to researchers about QA, recruited by their own offices, in a 45–70 minute recorded interview, have an audience. "It's theater" may be performance for the interviewer as readily as disclosure to them. No reflexivity note anywhere addresses what the interview situation itself produced.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

- Academics and teaching staff — the population on whom QA is said to act, and whose "distrust-laden" experience the paper cites (Montero, 2021) but never samples; the *whole* claim that QA leaves "nothing behind that a teacher would recognize" is administrator testimony about teachers.
- Students — named as the moral warrant for administrators' vocational identity ("whether students get a fair deal," §4.3) and absent from the design.
- External reviewers / accreditation panel members — one of the three named parties to the "negotiation" thesis, never heard from.
- The dissenting administrators — present in the data, excluded from the record (§4.5); the only stakeholder group the study actually reached and then removed.
- Mid-level survey respondents — reduced to a single scalar (M=3.9) with no open response, no site breakdown for 28 of 48, and no voice in the analysis.

### Unexamined Premise

**That what administrators say about culture is evidence of what the culture is.** The manuscript's entire inferential chain runs from twelve (or fourteen) interviews to claims about institutional quality culture — an organizational-level property — without ever marking the level shift. "Perspectives" appears in the title and "culture" in the conclusion, and no sentence in the paper does the work of connecting them. This premise is upstream of every dimension I examined: it is what allows §5 to move from accounts to "how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture," and it is why the paper feels no need for any non-testimonial evidence — no observation of a QA cycle, no document analysis of the portfolios everyone describes, no academic or student data point. The study is not weakly triangulated; it is a study of administrator discourse presented as a study of institutional culture. A bounded version of this paper — "how QA administrators narrate their work" — would be defensible on this evidence and would lose nothing but the overreach.

It is more parsimonious than negotiation and fits the transcripts at least as well. Absent any limitations section, any reflexivity note, and any non-testimonial evidence — no document analysis of the portfolios everyone describes, no observation of a review cycle, no academic or student voice — the paper has nothing to set against it.

Two further internal breaks compound the picture rather than standing alone. §4.4 calls the survey corroborative and then reports that it violated the expectation the interviews generated; corroboration and disconfirmation are asserted of one result, and the honest version — a level divergence between administrators and staff — is the more interesting finding the paper declines to claim. And §3.5's guarantee that "no individual could be identified" is contradicted two pages later by "the quality director of the largest private university in the region," a unique role at the single private site, attached to the most career-relevant quotation in the manuscript. I flag that as an argument-level self-contradiction; its consequence for a real participant belongs to the seat that owns participant harm, and I do not want the reader of this report to mistake my framing for the full weight of it.

None of my findings rests on a claim about what this subfield should do. I have not asked for reproducibility artifacts, a coding frame, an audit trail, or a member-checking protocol as grounds for any block — those would be norm-dependent and I could not ground them from inside this document. Every block above rests on the manuscript contradicting itself or drawing a conclusion its own reported evidence does not reach. I note this because an adversarial seat is precisely where an ungrounded field norm gets smuggled in as severity, and I have deliberately kept D4 at warn for the same reason: the interdisciplinary fault here is an unnamed borrowing, not an unearned one.

What survives: the §4.3 reading of vocational identity as a resource for holding the compliance–meaning tension open, rather than resolving it, is a real analytic contribution and is untouched by everything above. A bounded paper — how QA administrators narrate their work, in one named system, including the dissenters — would be defensible on this evidence and would lose only the overreach.
