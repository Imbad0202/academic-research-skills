### Reviewer Configuration Card #2

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 1 — Methodology
**Identity Description**: Professor of research methodology in a school of education, specializing in mixed-methods design integration (joint displays, meta-inference quality, and the Fetters–Curry–Creswell integration typology), who also teaches survey construction and psychometrics and serves as the standing methods referee for two education journals; her published work includes a critique of "mixed-methods in name only" studies in which the quantitative and qualitative strands never actually meet.
**Review Focus**:

Assess power, the multiple-comparisons exposure implied by "subgroup comparisons were examined where role categories permitted" (§3.4), and whether "institutional type as a possible moderator" is a defensible inference or a chance finding.

Discordance between strands is a legitimate and interesting mixed-methods result; mislabeling it as corroboration is a reporting failure, and which of these it is determines whether the fix is a rewrite or a reanalysis.
**Possible blind spots**: May apply quantitative standards disproportionately to a study that is explicitly QUAL-dominant and recommend deleting the survey when the field-level contribution lies in the interviews; may not evaluate whether the QA literature is accurately represented; may treat participant identifiability as an ethics-committee matter outside her remit. The synthesizer should ensure her rigor critique does not crowd out the question of whether the qualitative material is genuinely valuable.

## Review Strategy Recommendations

**On the paper's defining characteristic — polish/rigor inversion.** This manuscript writes with pre-submission confidence and first-draft evidentiary discipline. Every reviewer should be explicitly warned that surface fluency is an unreliable signal here, and required to anchor each finding to a quoted line and section number rather than to an overall impression. The register for feedback should be developmental — there is real qualitative material worth saving and the authors are clearly competent writers — but the register affects wording only; the recommendation must stay evidence-based against the criteria.

**On the internal-contradiction cluster.** Four contradictions sit inside the manuscript's own text, requiring no external knowledge to detect: 14 vs. 12 participants (Abstract vs. §3.2); "corroborated" vs. the divergence reported two sentences later (§4.4); "balanced representation… the full range" vs. dissenting views "excluded" (§4.3); and "fully anonymized… no individual could be identified" (§3.5) vs. the identifying attributions in §4.1. A fifth, the Delacroix reversal, is detectable by comparing §5 against §2 of the same paper. These are internal-consistency failures, not matters of judgment, and any reviewer who misses all of them has not read closely enough. Expect duplicate detection — the participant discrepancy in particular will surface from EIC and R1 independently — and instruct the synthesizer to merge rather than double-count, while treating independent convergence as a confidence signal rather than noise.

**On the central tension the synthesizer must adjudicate: R1 versus R2.** R1 will likely conclude that the evidence cannot support the claims and press toward major revision or rejection on rigor. R2 will likely find the topic worthwhile and the themes plausible, and lean toward revise-and-resubmit on the strength of field relevance. The disagreement is not really about quality — it is about *which repair is required*. Two mutually exclusive paths exist: (a) scale the claims down to a three-institution comparative account, drop or fully document the survey, and publish modestly; or (b) keep the ambition and collect substantially more data, which means this is not the paper. The synthesizer must force this choice rather than splitting the difference, because a revision that trims some claims while keeping others will satisfy neither reviewer.

**On the secondary tension: R1 versus R3 on the survey.** R1 will want the quantitative strand documented and reanalyzed; R3 will likely argue it should be removed entirely as an underpowered distraction from a strong qualitative study. Both are defensible. The synthesizer should note that removing the survey also removes the manuscript's only counter-evidence to its own thesis (the moderately positive staff sentiment), which is arguably its most honest moment — deletion is not cost-free.

**On complementarity by design.** Coverage is partitioned so that each defect class has exactly one owner: reporting and inference defects → R1; literature representation and construct definition → R2; theoretical borrowing, fieldwork ethics, and practitioner usability → R3; claim calibration, journal fit, and integrity escalation → EIC. Two adjacencies are intentional and should not be collapsed: EIC and R2 both touch the "first comprehensive account" claim (EIC as a scope-of-claim editorial matter, R2 as a positioning-against-predecessors matter), and R2 and EIC both touch the Delacroix reversal (R2 owns the substantive finding, EIC decides whether it escalates).

**On the reference-verification flag.** All twelve references resolve to DOIs under the `10.5555/` prefix, sequentially numbered `1042001`–`1042012`. That prefix is a reserved test/example range, and sequential numbering across twelve different publishers is not how DOIs are assigned. This warrants verification before any conclusion is drawn — reviewers should flag it as a check-required item and must not assert fabrication on this basis alone. Relatedly, the working annotation left inside the Delacroix entry is an unremoved editing artifact; it happens to document the very argument the Discussion reverses, which makes it evidence rather than merely a formatting slip.

**On anonymization and transferability.** The national system is unnamed. Reviewers should not penalize the anonymization decision itself, but should require enough institutional and regulatory context for readers to judge transferability — currently a reader cannot tell what QA regime these administrators are responding to, which limits what the findings can be taken to mean. Note the irony worth raising in review: the paper anonymizes the country while identifying individual postholders, which is precisely backwards.

**On what to protect.** Amid substantial criticism, the panel should not lose the two things worth keeping: the §4.3 finding that administrators hold the compliance/meaning tension open as the site of professional identity, and the §4.4 divergence between administrator and staff perceptions. Both are genuinely interesting. A review that returns only defects will read as demolition and will not help the authors find the paper that is actually here.

**On the Devil's Advocate brief.** The strongest available attack is not any single defect but the aggregate: if the sector-level generalization is removed, the novelty claim retracted, the misrepresented citation corrected, the excluded dissenters reinstated, and the survey either documented or dropped, what remains is a competent three-institution study confirming that QA is experienced as ritual — a finding the manuscript's own §2 attributes to at least four prior sources. The Devil's Advocate should press whether the residual contribution justifies publication anywhere in the top three recommended journals, and should be given the §4.3 identity-work finding as the one line of defense the authors could legitimately mount.

## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory).** From the editor's chair, this dimension asks whether the study's design and its evidentiary machinery would survive the scrutiny of the field's own referees before I ever send it to them. For an empirical piece on administrator perspectives in higher education, that means the manuscript must make its design legible: who was studied, how they were selected, how their accounts were captured, and how those accounts were turned into the claims the paper makes. I am not the seat that audits coding schemes or statistical models line by line — that is the methodology reviewer's work — but I am the seat that decides whether enough of the method is on the page for anyone to audit at all. A manuscript of roughly 2,249 words is short for an empirical qualitative study, so my editorial question is whether the compression has eaten the methods reporting or merely the prose. Reproducibility affordances here mean instrument transparency, sampling logic, and a stated analytic route from raw administrator talk to reported findings.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory).** This dimension asks whether the paper is fluent in its own field. Internal quality assurance and quality culture are terms with contested but well-documented lineages in higher education studies — the quality-culture literature carries specific conceptual commitments about the relationship between structural/managerial elements and cultural/psychological ones, and internal QA has a standing vocabulary tied to institutional accreditation regimes. As editor I check whether the manuscript uses these terms as the field uses them, whether it represents the prior scholarship it invokes correctly rather than as a decorative citation wall, and whether its empirical claims about how quality assurance actually operates in universities are consistent with what is known. Misdescribing a framework, attributing a claim to the wrong source, or treating a contested construct as settled are all failures here, and they matter to me because they are the errors that most reliably embarrass a journal after publication.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory).** This is the dimension closest to my native seat. It asks whether the paper holds together as a single argument: whether the research question stated up front is the question the data address, whether the findings reported are the findings the conclusion rests on, and whether the strength of the conclusion is proportionate to the strength of the evidence. The characteristic failure in administrator-perspective work is over-reach — a modest set of interview accounts becoming a general claim about how quality culture forms in universities, or descriptive perceptions being narrated as causal mechanisms. I read title → abstract → introduction → findings → conclusion as a chain and look for the link that breaks. Internal inconsistency, unsupported inferential leaps, and conclusions that quietly answer a different question than the one posed all live here.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority).** My readership question. Higher education studies is a genuinely mixed audience — organisational scholars, policy researchers, accreditation practitioners, institutional administrators — and a paper on quality culture sits at the junction of organisational culture theory, public management, and QA practice. This dimension asks whether the manuscript is written so that a reader from an adjacent field can enter it: are jurisdiction-specific QA arrangements explained rather than assumed, are the theoretical borrowings from organisational culture made explicit rather than gestured at, and are the implications drawn in terms that travel beyond one national system? Where the paper claims interdisciplinary reach, I check that the claim is earned rather than asserted. Priority is high but not mandatory, which I read as: a failure here damages the paper's value to my readers without necessarily invalidating it.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority).** The craft dimension: does the manuscript's organisation serve its argument, is the exposition clear at first reading, do tables and figures carry their own weight and get interpreted in the text, and does the paper meet the conventions a journal in this field expects of a research article. At 2,249 words the structural question is unusually live — that length is closer to a research note or short communication than a full empirical article, so I assess both whether the internal proportions are sensible and whether the paper is presenting itself as an article type its length can sustain. Normal priority means craft problems are real feedback but do not on their own determine the fate of the manuscript; they change the amount of work a revision requires.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
- `what_to_look_for` — An explicit statement of design (qualitative interview study, survey, case study, mixed); the sampling frame and how administrators were selected, with institution count and role types; sample size and its stated adequacy; the data collection instrument and whether it is described, appended, or at least characterised; the analytic procedure that converts administrator accounts into the paper's themes or findings; any trustworthiness/validity affordances the field expects (member checking, intercoder agreement, audit trail, positionality, saturation reasoning); ethics/consent handling for human participants; and whether reported results are traceable to described procedures. I also scan for the mismatch case: a methods section that names a technique but reports outputs that technique cannot produce.
- `what_triggers_block` — The manuscript reports empirical findings about administrators while leaving the reader unable to reconstruct where those findings came from: no identifiable design or method section at all; participants asserted with no sampling, count, or selection basis; findings/themes presented with no stated analytic route from data to claim; or a described procedure that is internally impossible or contradicted by the results it supposedly produced. Also blocking: human-participant research with no indication of consent or ethical clearance where the venue and field require it. A single blocking pattern is enough — this is the mandatory dimension whose absence makes every downstream claim unverifiable.
- `what_triggers_warn` — Method is present and reconstructable but thin or under-specified: design named but instrument not described; sample size given without adequacy reasoning; analysis named ("thematic analysis") with no procedural detail on how themes were derived or checked; no trustworthiness affordance offered where the field routinely expects one; ethics mentioned only in passing; or reporting that is adequate for the claims made about the sample but too thin to support the transferability the paper implies. Warn is also where I land if the compression to ~2,249 words has plausibly displaced methods reporting that the study itself likely performed.

### D2: domain_accuracy
- `what_to_look_for` — How "quality culture" and "internal quality assurance" are defined and whose conceptualisation is invoked; whether the structural/managerial versus cultural/psychological distinction that the quality-culture literature turns on is handled or flattened; whether cited prior work is characterised in terms its authors would recognise; correct and consistent use of QA terminology (external QA, internal QA, accreditation, audit, self-evaluation, standards frameworks) without conflating levels or regimes; accuracy of any factual claims about national QA systems, agencies, or policy instruments named; whether the literature engaged is the literature that governs this question or a substitutable generic set; and whether contested constructs are presented as contested.
- `what_triggers_block` — A factual error or misrepresentation load-bearing for the paper's contribution: a named framework or theorist's position described incorrectly in a way the argument then builds on; QA constructs conflated such that the paper's central claim is about a different object than it thinks (e.g., treating external accreditation compliance and internal quality culture as the same phenomenon while claiming to study the latter); demonstrably wrong statements about a QA system or regulatory arrangement used as premises; or citations attributed to sources that do not support the claim made. Block requires that removing the error would change what the paper concludes.
- `what_triggers_warn` — Domain handling is broadly sound but loose: key constructs used without definition or with a definition that drifts across the paper; the quality-culture literature invoked at citation level without engaging what it actually argues; terminology used imprecisely in places without corrupting the central claim; conspicuous omission of a strand of prior work the field would expect on this exact question; or contested positions asserted as consensus. Also warn: claims about "universities" in general that are really claims about one system, left unqualified.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
- `what_to_look_for` — Whether a research question or purpose statement is stated and whether it is the same question the conclusion answers; the chain from title → abstract → introduction → findings → discussion → conclusion, checked link by link for drift; whether every substantive claim in the discussion can be traced to something actually reported in the findings; the calibration between evidence strength and claim strength, especially the perceptual-to-causal slide (administrators reporting a belief becoming the paper's claim about what causes quality culture); whether limitations are acknowledged where the argument needs them and whether the conclusion respects them or ignores them once stated; and whether alternative explanations for the reported pattern are engaged or silently excluded.
- `what_triggers_block` — The argument's spine is broken: the conclusion asserts something the reported findings do not support and could not support given the design (causal or effectiveness claims from perception data; generalisation to a population never sampled); the paper answers a materially different question than the one it poses without acknowledging the shift; or two central claims are mutually inconsistent and the paper depends on both. Block is for the case where an editor cannot let the sentence stand as written because the paper's headline contribution rests on it.
- `what_triggers_warn` — The argument holds but leaks: over-reaching qualifiers ("demonstrates", "establishes") where the evidence supports "suggests"; a discussion that travels further than the findings without breaking from them; limitations stated in a dedicated paragraph but not honoured in the conclusion's phrasing; plausible rival explanations left unaddressed; or a research question stated so broadly that the findings cannot fully discharge it. Also warn: the abstract promising more than the body delivers, where the body's own claims remain defensible.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
- `what_to_look_for` — Whether jurisdiction- and system-specific QA arrangements, agency names, and policy instruments are explained on first use for a reader outside that system; whether theoretical borrowings from organisational culture, institutional theory, or public management are made explicit and used, rather than named and dropped; whether the implications section speaks to anyone beyond QA specialists in one national context — institutional leadership, policy, adjacent organisational-studies readers; whether interdisciplinary claims the paper actually makes are substantiated with the relevant literature from the discipline being borrowed from; and whether the framing gives an adjacent-field reader a reason to care before it gives them detail.
- `what_triggers_block` — The paper is unreadable outside its own niche or its interdisciplinary claim is unsupported: sustained reliance on unexplained system-specific acronyms, agency names, or regulatory categories such that an adjacent-field reader cannot follow the argument; or an explicit interdisciplinary/theoretical claim (e.g., that the study extends organisational culture theory) asserted with no engagement of that discipline's literature or evidence. High-priority block; under F3 this alone drives major revision, so I reserve it for the case where the barrier is structural rather than cosmetic.
- `what_triggers_warn` — Accessible with friction: some terms explained and others assumed; theoretical framing gestured at but under-developed; implications drawn only for QA practitioners in the study's own system when the topic clearly generalises; findings whose transferability to other institutional contexts is neither claimed nor discussed; or a framing section that assumes the reader already agrees the problem matters. Warn is the expected resting place for a competent single-system paper that has not done the work of translation.

### D5: writing_and_structure
- `what_to_look_for` — Whether the section architecture matches the paper's type and does its job; proportionality across sections (introduction and literature not crowding out findings, findings not reduced to a list); whether tables and figures — if present — are self-contained, correctly labelled, referenced in text, and interpreted rather than merely displayed; clarity of exposition at first reading, including whether participant quotations are contextualised and attributed to role types; internal consistency of terminology and numbers between abstract, body, and any tables; adherence to research-article conventions (structured abstract, keywords, ethics/funding statements, consistent referencing); and whether ~2,249 words is being asked to carry more article than it can, or is honestly framed as a short-form contribution.
- `what_triggers_block` — Presentation defeats comprehension or credibility: the organisation is disordered enough that a reader cannot locate the study's question, evidence, or conclusion; findings and interpretation are fused so that reported data cannot be separated from the author's reading of them; tables/figures are uninterpretable, unlabelled, or contradict the text's numbers; or the manuscript is so far from the article conventions of the field that it cannot be sent to referees as submitted. Normal priority means this fires no failure condition on its own, but I score it honestly — an editor's desk-level "not sendable" judgement belongs on the record.
- `what_triggers_warn` — Craft problems that cost the reader without stopping them: uneven section proportions, especially a findings section thinner than its framing; quotations presented without enough context to be interpretable; repetition between literature review and discussion; missing or perfunctory venue-convention elements (keywords, ethics or funding statement, inconsistent citation formatting); a table that duplicates rather than extends the text; or prose that requires re-reading to extract the claim. This is where I expect most of a compressed manuscript's issues to land.

### Summary Assessment

The manuscript reports a QUAL-dominant mixed-methods study of senior administrators' experience of QA implementation across three universities in one unnamed national system, supported by a 48-respondent staff survey. The question is a real one and well posed: administrators occupy the hinge between external requirement and internal practice, and their own accounts are under-collected. The interview material is more than serviceable, and one passage in §4.3 — administrators holding the compliance/meaning tension open rather than resolving it, and locating professional identity in that gap — is a genuine contribution that the manuscript itself does not recognise as its best asset.

Against that, the paper is not currently in a state I can send to referees with a clean conscience. Its confident register is doing work its evidence cannot support: three sites become "universities across the sector" and "administrators everywhere" (§5), and twelve interviews become "the first comprehensive account" (§6) two pages after §2 names three predecessors working the same seam. More seriously, §5 attributes to Delacroix (2018) the exact position §2 of this same manuscript — and the reference entry itself — records him as arguing against. Four further contradictions sit inside the text and require no outside knowledge to see. The prose is at pre-submission polish; the evidence and internal consistency are at first-draft state.

### Strengths

1. **The research question is well posed and genuinely under-served**: §1 states it plainly — how senior administrators experience and enact QA implementation, and what their accounts show about quality-culture formation — and §2 earns it by identifying the hinge position as the gap. This is a question my readership wants answered.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §1 ¶3 — "How do senior university administrators experience and enact QA implementation, and how do their accounts illuminate the formation of institutional quality culture?"`

2. **§4.3 contains a real analytic finding**: the observation that administrators do not resolve the compliance/meaning tension but hold it open, treating the gap as where their professional identity lives, is more than a restatement of QA ritualism. It is the one place the paper says something the existing literature has not already said.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §4.3 — "Rather than resolving the tension between compliance and meaning, they held it open, treating the gap itself as the space in which their professional identity was located."`

3. **The survey divergence is the manuscript's most honest moment**: staff sentiment running moderately positive where administrators describe ritualism is counter-evidence to the paper's own thesis, and the authors report it rather than bury it. Framed as divergence rather than corroboration, it is publishable in its own right.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §4.4 — "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture; the survey suggests that the felt tension is more acute at the administrative level than among staff at large."`

4. **The literature review is competently organised and, where it is not later contradicted, accurate**: the three-strand structure (governance technology / lived experience / quality culture) is the right map of this field, and its rendering of Delacroix's actual argument is correct.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §2 ¶2 — "Delacroix (2018), by contrast, cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual, and argues against treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of a healthy quality culture."`

5. **The exposition is accessible to adjacent-field readers**: no acronym wall, no unexplained regulatory categories, no assumption that the reader already inhabits the QA field. An organisational sociologist or a public-management scholar can read this without a glossary.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §1–§2 (section-level) — e.g. "the machinery of QA — review cycles, evidence portfolios, action plans"`

### Detailed Comments

#### Journal Fit

Topically this is close to ideal for *Quality in Higher Education*: internal QA implementation, quality-culture formation at institutional level, qualitative and modest-n institutional work, and a reference list that already includes one of our own articles (Iversen, 2021). Our readership does not require generalisation to find a three-institution study useful, which means the single correction the manuscript most needs — deleting the sector claim — costs it nothing here that it would cost at a journal demanding scale. The fit problem is not topic but register: the paper is written for a venue that rewards sweeping claims, and it is submitting to one that does not need them. Two caveats on fit. First, the national system is unnamed. I do not object to the anonymisation as such, but a reader currently cannot tell what QA regime these administrators are responding to — periodic institutional audit, programme accreditation, ESG-aligned or not — and without that, transferability cannot be judged at all. Enough regulatory and institutional context must be restored for readers to place the findings. Second, at ~2,600 words of body text this is not currently a research article by our conventions; it is either an unfinished one or an unlabelled short communication.

#### Originality

Strip the sector generalisation and the "first comprehensive" claim, and ask what remains. Answer: a three-institution study confirming that QA is experienced as ritual — a finding the manuscript's own §2 attributes to at least four prior sources — plus a distributed-leadership theme that Pettersen (2022) has already staked, plus an identity theme that Rahman (2020) has already staked. That residue is thin, and I want to be direct with the authors that it would not, on its own, carry an article in this journal. What saves it is §4.3's final move: administrators holding the compliance/meaning tension open rather than resolving it, and locating professional identity in the unresolved gap. That is not in Pettersen or Rahman, it is not in Silva & Tan, and it converts a familiar finding into a new one. The paper currently buries it in the last third of a theme section and does not carry it into the conclusion. If there is a publishable article here, it is the one whose contribution claim is that finding — narrow, grounded, defensible — rather than the sector-level account currently on offer. The survey divergence is a genuine second contribution and is similarly under-claimed.

#### Significance

Conditional and currently unrealisable. If the claims held, the significance would be moderate: a corrective to the managerial cascade narrative, useful to institutional leadership and QA practitioners. But the claims that would carry significance are exactly the ones the design cannot support, and the claims the design supports are locally scoped. The most consequential thing in the manuscript for practice is the collision documented in W11 — that the participatory remedies the field keeps recommending are, on this study's own evidence, being absorbed into the ritual they were meant to cure. That is a finding with teeth for anyone designing a QA cycle, and the paper has it in hand and recommends the opposite. Correcting the Delacroix attribution is therefore not housekeeping; it is the route to the paper's actual practical significance.

#### Structural Coherence

The chain from title to conclusion breaks in at least four places, all detectable without leaving the manuscript. The Abstract reports fourteen participants and §3.2 reports twelve. §4.3 claims the full range of views and discloses the exclusion of dissenters. §4.4 announces corroboration and reports divergence. §3.5 promises that no individual can be identified and §4.1 identifies two by role and institution. A fifth break appears between §2 and §5 in the handling of Delacroix. Individually each is correctable; collectively they mean the manuscript's assertions cannot be taken at face value anywhere, because the paper has demonstrated five times that its own statements about itself are unreliable. That is the finding I most want the authors to absorb. The IMRaD architecture itself is sound and the sections do their jobs; the incoherence is in the content, not the container.

#### Title & Abstract

The title is accurate, informative, and appropriately scoped — notably more modest than the paper it introduces, which is the right direction for the error to run. The abstract is within conventional length and readable. It has three problems: the participant count contradicts the Methods; "Analysis surfaced three dominant themes" inherits the §4.3 exclusion without disclosing it; and "M=3.9, SD=0.6" is reported with no scale, so the number conveys nothing to a reader. The abstract's final two sentences are, unusually, better calibrated than §5 and §6 — "we argue" and "carry implications" are the right verbs, and the body should be brought up to the abstract's standard rather than the reverse.

#### Conclusion

§6 answers a larger question than §1 asked. The introduction asks how administrators experience and enact QA implementation and what that illuminates about quality-culture formation; the conclusion delivers a claim about what quality culture *is* institution-generally, prefaced by a first-in-field assertion. The negotiated-accomplishment formulation is defensible as an interpretation of these twelve (or fourteen) accounts and indefensible as a general account of the sector. The forward-looking sentence — that future research should test these dynamics across a wider range of institutional types and national systems — is correct, and quietly concedes the limitation that §5 spent a paragraph denying. Bring the conclusion into line with that sentence and most of D3's problems resolve.

### Questions for Authors

1. How many administrators were interviewed, and which corpus was analysed — fourteen or twelve? Please reconcile the Abstract with §3.2 and confirm that the reported themes derive from the corpus you name.
2. §2 records Delacroix (2018) as arguing *against* treating consultation as sufficient; §5 states he recommends it as the central mechanism. Which reading do you intend, and — given that your §4.1 data appear to support his actual position — what happens to your third discussion point once the attribution is corrected?
3. On what basis do twelve administrators in three institutions of one national system license the claims that "universities across the sector" treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment and that "administrators everywhere" face the same tension? If none, will you rescope the paper to a three-institution account?
4. §6 claims the first comprehensive account of administrator experience. How does that claim stand against Pettersen (2022), Rahman (2020), and Silva & Tan (2021), which your own §2 identifies on this exact object? What systematic search of prior accounts supports "first"?
5. Did the excluded dissenting participants consent to being interviewed for a study that would not report their views? What would the three themes look like if those accounts were reinstated?
6. §3.5 states no individual could be identified, while §4.1 attributes critical quotations to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and "the associate dean for quality at the public research university." Did participants consent to attribution at this granularity, and were the quotations returned to them for approval in this form?
7. For the survey: what instrument was used, what were the items and the response scale, what was the response rate and sampling frame, what test produced the p<.05 in §4.4, and how many comparisons were examined under §3.4's "where role categories permitted"?
8. All twelve references carry DOIs under the reserved `10.5555` prefix in sequential order. Please supply resolvable DOIs or full bibliographic records so the editorial office can verify the sources. I am asking as a routine verification step and am not asserting anything further.
9. Which QA regime are these administrators working under? Enough institutional and regulatory context is needed for readers to judge transferability, and it can be supplied without naming the country.

- "M=3.9, SD=0.6" appears in the Abstract and §4.4 without a scale range; state it in both places.

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** This is my primary jurisdiction. The contract asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical (or, for qualitative work, analytic) reporting, and reproducibility affordances clear the ordinary peer-review bar of the field. Read through a methodology lens for higher education studies on internal quality assurance, this means: the paper must state what kind of study it is, and that design must be capable of answering the question it poses. For administrator-perspective work the plausible designs are interview-based qualitative study, survey, document analysis, case study, or some mixture; each carries its own obligations. A qualitative design owes me a sampling logic (purposive, theoretical, maximum-variation), a described data-collection procedure, an analysis method that is named rather than gestured at, and some trustworthiness apparatus (coding reliability, member checking, audit trail, reflexivity). A survey design owes me instrument provenance, reliability and validity evidence, response rate, and analysis appropriate to the measurement level. Reproducibility means a reader could re-run the study: instruments, protocols, coding frames, and ethics approval are described or archived. At 2249 words this paper is short for an empirical study, so a real risk is that the Method section is compressed to the point of non-reconstructability; that compression is itself a methodological finding, not an excuse.

If the paper measures or codes for something other than what it names, that is a construct-validity defect, and construct validity is mine. I will confine my D2 judgement to that joint — definition-to-operationalisation fidelity, and correct use of methodological terminology — and will not score the paper's engagement with the substantive QA literature.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** For me this is the inference chain, not the rhetoric: does what the data can support actually reach the conclusions drawn? ## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
- `dimension_id`: D1
- `what_to_look_for`: An explicit statement of research design and paradigm; a research question that is answerable by that design. Participant/site description: how many administrators, from how many institutions, in what roles, in what national or system context, and how selected — with a stated sampling rationale rather than an implied convenience sample. Data-collection detail: instrument or protocol described (interview guide structure, survey items or their source), mode, duration, timeframe, language, and whether instruments are appended or archived. Named analysis procedure: for qualitative work, which tradition (thematic analysis, grounded theory, framework analysis) and how codes were derived, plus coder count and any agreement/consensus procedure; for quantitative work, which tests, on which variables, at which measurement level, with assumption checks, effect sizes, and confidence intervals. Trustworthiness or validity apparatus: member checking, triangulation across data sources, audit trail, negative-case analysis, reflexivity on the researcher's own position relative to the administrators studied (particularly salient if the researcher is an insider to the QA system). Saturation or power justification for the sample size actually used. Ethics: approval body, consent procedure, confidentiality handling for administrators identifiable by role and institution. Reproducibility affordances: availability of the interview guide, codebook, questionnaire, or data, or an explicit statement of why they cannot be shared.
- `what_triggers_block`: Any one of the following. (a) No identifiable method description at all — the paper reports findings about administrator perspectives without stating how those perspectives were obtained. (b) The design cannot in principle answer the stated research question — e.g. the question is about how quality culture develops or what causes it, and the data are a single cross-sectional snapshot with no longitudinal, comparative, or process-tracing element, and this mismatch goes unacknowledged. (c) The analysis procedure is unnamed and unreconstructable: findings appear as themes or percentages with no account of how raw data became them. (d) Reported quantitative results are internally impossible or unverifiable — Ns that do not reconcile, percentages that cannot arise from the stated sample, statistics reported without the test, denominator, or degrees of freedom needed to interpret them. (e) Human-subjects research on identifiable institutional actors with no ethics approval and no consent statement anywhere in the paper.
- `what_triggers_warn`: Any one of the following, where none of the block triggers fire. (a) Design and analysis are named but under-specified — the tradition is stated yet the coding process, coder count, or code-to-theme derivation is left implicit. (b) Sampling is described but unjustified: participants are listed with no rationale, no saturation claim, and no acknowledgement of the selection bias inherent in recruiting administrators who agreed to discuss their own QA implementation. (c) No trustworthiness or validity apparatus reported, and no reflexivity statement, for a design that conventionally requires one. (d) Instruments referenced but not reproduced, appended, or archived, so the study cannot be replicated. (e) Quantitative reporting is incomplete but interpretable — significance without effect sizes, no confidence intervals, no assumption testing, no missing-data account. (f) Ethics approval mentioned only in passing without body, protocol, or consent procedure. (g) The Method section is so compressed relative to the paper's claims that a competent reader could not re-run the study, even though nothing stated is wrong.

### D2: domain_accuracy
- `dimension_id`: D2
- `what_to_look_for`: Explicit definitions of the core constructs — quality culture, internal quality assurance, quality assurance implementation — and whether those definitions are then honoured by what is actually measured, asked, or coded. Whether the paper distinguishes constructs that the field distinguishes (culture vs. compliance; internal QA vs. external accreditation response; institutional-level structures vs. individual-level attitudes) or collapses them. Whether methodological terminology is used with its standard meaning: "case study", "grounded theory", "mixed methods", "generalisability", "validity", "saturation", "triangulation". Whether any framework or model borrowed to structure the instrument or coding frame is applied as its source specifies rather than renamed and reshaped. Correct labelling of the analysis unit — individual administrator, institution, or system — and consistency of that unit across instrument, analysis, and reporting.

### Summary Assessment

### Detailed Comments

#### Research Questions & Hypotheses

The question in §1 is two-part: (i) how senior administrators experience and enact QA implementation, and (ii) how their accounts "illuminate the formation of institutional quality culture." Part (i) is well matched to interviews and is the part the study can answer. Part (ii) is a formation question, and nothing in the design observes formation: no time dimension, no retrospective process reconstruction methodology, no comparison of institutions at different QA maturities. The paper never notices the gap, and with no limitations section there is no place where it could have been conceded. No hypotheses are stated for the survey, which is consistent with the declared supplementary role — but §4.4's significance test is then reported as though a directional expectation had existed ("with the teaching-focused institution scoring higher"), and §3.4 pre-specified a different grouping variable than the one reported.

#### Research Design

A mixed-methods design is asserted; a mixed-methods design is not specified. Priority is stated (QUAL-dominant), but typology, timing, and integration point — the other three decisions that constitute a mixed-methods design — are all absent. The three-site structure implies a comparative case design, which would be a defensible choice for this question, but no comparative analysis is performed in the primary strand; the sites function only as a recruitment pool. The internal/external validity trade-off is never discussed, which matters because §5 then claims the external validity of a sector-wide account while the design purchased none.

#### Sampling Strategy

Role-based selection is sensible and the roles chosen are the right ones. Everything around that is missing: no purposive or maximum-variation rationale, no site-selection criteria beyond type labels, no per-site participant distribution, no non-participation record, no saturation procedure, and no acknowledgement that QA offices nominating participants to discuss their own QA implementation is a directional selection mechanism. For the survey there is no frame and no response rate, so 48 is a count of respondents, not a sample. At the reconciled interview N — whether twelve or fourteen — the size itself is unremarkable for this design; it is the absent justification, not the number, that is the problem.

#### Data Collection

The interview strand reports mode, duration and recording, which is the minimum and is delivered. Not delivered: the interview guide or even its domains, the language of interviewing, the fieldwork period, who conducted the interviews, and whether transcripts were returned to participants. The survey strand reports only that it was "administered online and captured respondents' perceptions of institutional quality culture along with basic role and tenure information" — no items, no response format, no anchors, no timing relative to the interviews (which also forecloses any judgment about sequential design logic).

#### Analysis Methods

The qualitative analysis is one sentence long and names no tradition; see W7. For the quantitative strand:

**Step 4a — Statistical reporting adequacy: Unacceptable.**

| Item | Status |
|---|---|
| Effect sizes | Absent for the only test reported |
| Confidence intervals | Absent throughout |
| Statistical power | No a priori analysis; no Type II discussion; observed power for the implied effect ≈50% |
| Assumption testing | None reported (normality, variance homogeneity, treatment of a Likert-type composite as interval) |
| Missing data | Not mentioned; no completion or attrition figures |
| APA compliance | Test unnamed; no statistic; no df; no exact p; *M*/*SD* not italicized; one-decimal precision; scale range never given |
| Descriptive completeness | No per-site or per-role Ns, Ms, SDs; subgroup Ns (9+11) do not reconcile to 48 |

**Red flags**: (1) analysis-plan/report mismatch — §3.4 pre-states role-based subgroup comparisons, §4.4 reports an institution-based one; (2) undisclosed comparison count with no correction, and only the significant contrast surfaced; (3) a mean reported without its scale, making the headline descriptive uninterpretable; (4) significance achieved at n=9 vs n=11, which given SD≈0.6 requires d≈0.94 and therefore carries substantial magnitude-inflation risk; (5) "moderator" language over a design containing no interaction test.

**Recommendations**: report the full descriptive table with scale range; name the test with statistic, df and exact p; add an effect size with interval; report every comparison run; state assumption checks or adopt a rank-based test; label the contrast exploratory and hypothesis-generating.

On the question of whether the survey should simply be deleted: I do not recommend that, and I want to be explicit about it because it is the reflex my seat is prone to. The survey is the study's only piece of evidence that runs *against* its own thesis, and that is analytically the most valuable thing in the quantitative strand. The choice is document it properly or demote it to an explicitly labelled contextual descriptive with no inferential claim attached — not remove the disconfirming datum.

#### Results Presentation

Findings and interpretation are interleaved throughout, so the evidential basis of specific claims is hard to isolate: §4.1 embeds a warrant claim ("giving us confidence that the pattern was not an artifact"), §4.3 embeds a functional interpretation ("appeared to function as a coping resource"), §4.4 embeds an inference ("points to institutional type as a possible moderator"). Completeness fails in two directions: qualitatively, dissenting accounts are removed (W4); quantitatively, only the significant comparison appears (W10). Quotation is used well — the quotations are vivid and apt — but they are illustrative selections from an unknown corpus, presented without counts, so a reader cannot judge typicality. The vague quantifiers ("consistently," "repeatedly," "recurred across every interview," "several," "a minority") do the work that a theme-by-participant matrix should do.

#### Reproducibility

Currently nil. No interview guide, no survey instrument, no coding frame, no de-identified data, no analysis specification, no data-availability statement, no funding or COI disclosure, no protocol identifier for the ethics approval. A competent independent team given this manuscript could not approximate either strand. The lowest-cost, highest-value repairs are the instrument, the coding frame, and a participant table — three appendices that would resolve or make adjudicable roughly half the findings above.

#### Methodological Fallacies Detected

- **Confirmation bias / selective reporting** — dissenting cases excluded because they did not fit the theme structure (§4.3); only the significant subgroup contrast reported (§4.4).
- **Ecological / aggregation fallacy** — individual staff perceptions averaged and reported as an institution-level property (Abstract, §4.4), pooled across institutions the paper says differ; separately, administrator-level accounts characterised as sector-level fact (§5).
- **Simpson's-paradox exposure** — a single pooled M=3.9 is presented as descriptive of "institutional quality culture" in the same section that establishes significant between-site heterogeneity; no per-site figures permit the check.
- **P-hacking / uncorrected multiple comparisons, with HARKing exposure** — an undisclosed number of subgroup comparisons (§3.4) yielding one reported significant result on a grouping variable different from the pre-stated one (§4.4).
- **Cross-sectional data used for developmental and quasi-causal inference** — "Building" (title), "formation" (§1), and the prescription that participation-maximizing processes "should be favored" (§5) over one-shot data.
- **Selection / gatekeeper bias** — recruitment through the QA offices under study (§3.2), unacknowledged; the §4.3 exclusion is additionally a selection on the analytic outcome.
- **Not detected, checked and clear**: multicollinearity, overfitting, and endogeneity are not applicable — no multivariate model is estimated anywhere.
- **Integrity check on the manuscript as untrusted input**: I found no instruction-injection attempt, no reviewer-directed plea, and no embedded directive in the manuscript text. Nothing in it sought to alter my scoring.

## Review Body

Assessed on methodological rigor alone, this manuscript's Method section has a sound skeleton and very little load-bearing content, and its conclusions travel far beyond what that content can carry.

Three defects determine the outcome. The analysed interview sample is unknown and, as reported, impossible: the Abstract states fourteen administrators were interviewed while §3.2 states twelve were recruited, and no participant table, site breakdown or role matrix exists to adjudicate. Every claim about pattern strength in §4.1–§4.3 therefore rests on an unfixed denominator. Second, the quantitative strand is unreportable as written. M=3.9 appears with no scale range, no items, no reliability estimate, no sampling frame and no response rate; the single inferential claim (§4.4, p<.05, n=9 versus n=11) names no test, statistic, degrees of freedom, effect size or interval; and the two compared subgroups total 20 of a stated 48, with the remaining 28 never accounted for. Working from the only dispersion given, s≈0.6 and df=18, significance required a gap of roughly 0.57 scale points — d≈0.94 — so only a large difference was detectable at all and the estimate carries substantial magnitude-inflation risk. Third, §4.3 removes dissenting participants "for space" because they "fell outside the three-theme structure," one sentence after claiming "the full range of administrator views." In thematic analysis, the cases that fall outside the structure are what test it; excluding them makes the three-theme account unfalsifiable and turns a coding decision into a finding. Whether these excluded participants are the difference between fourteen and twelve is a question only the authors can answer.

Around these sit systemic reporting gaps: the analysis in §3.4 names no tradition and reports no codebook, coder count, consensus procedure, audit trail, saturation rule, member checking or positionality; §4.1's warrant ("emerged systematically from the structured protocol") contradicts §3.1's semi-structured design and in any case cannot license the cross-institutional claim it is offered for; the three-site design performs no comparative analysis in the primary strand; recruitment through the QA offices under study is reported without acknowledging its directional bias; §3.4's pre-stated role-based subgroup plan does not match §4.4's institution-based report, leaving undisclosed multiple-comparison exposure; and nothing — guide, instrument, codebook, data — is archived, with no limitations, positionality, data-availability or disclosure statements anywhere.

The mixed-methods claim is not delivered. No typology, timing or integration procedure is specified, no joint display or meta-inference appears, and §4.4 labels as corroboration a divergence it reports two sentences later and §5 re-describes as complication. That divergence is the study's most interesting quantitative moment and its only evidence against its own thesis, which is why my recommendation is to document the survey properly or demote it to a clearly labelled contextual descriptive — not to delete it.

What is worth protecting: the interview material is substantive, the role-based sampling frame is the right one for the question, and §4.3's observation that administrators hold the compliance/meaning tension open rather than resolving it is a genuine analytic claim that could be tested against the corpus once the negative cases and the coding frame are restored. Scaled to a three-institution comparative account with a documented instrument, a reconstructable analysis, reinstated dissenters, and the sector-level and priority claims withdrawn, this can become a sound paper. The revision is a large one, and two of its steps — reconciling the sample and producing the instrument — depend on records I cannot see; if those records do not exist, the paper is not repairable at revision.

It also has identifiable canonical work on quality culture (the European University Association's quality culture project line, Harvey and Green's conceptions-of-quality typology, Harvey and Stensaker on quality culture typologies, Ehlers on structural versus enabling quality-culture dimensions, Newton on academic staff responses to quality systems) and a live contemporary debate about ritualisation, decoupling, and compliance fatigue. Terminological slippage matters here in a way it would not in another field: conflating quality assurance with quality culture, or treating "quality culture" as a synonym for staff attitudes, is a substantive domain error and not a wording preference.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** I read coherence as a domain question: does the chain from stated problem, through framework, through evidence, to conclusion hold together under the standards a quality assurance scholar would apply? The characteristic failure in this literature is the causal leap from administrator self-report to institutional reality — treating what senior staff say about their quality system as evidence that the quality culture exists, or that the system caused an improvement. A second characteristic failure is the framework that appears in the theory section and then plays no role in analysis or in the discussion's return to theory. A third is conclusions that quietly exceed the evidence base, typically recommendations addressed to institutions or regulators that the study's data cannot support. I look for internal consistency between what the paper claims to have found and what it then asserts.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** Reviewer 3 owns cross-disciplinary impact; my narrower obligation is to check that where this paper borrows from adjacent fields, it borrows correctly. Quality culture work routinely imports organisational culture theory (Schein's levels, competing values framework), institutional theory (isomorphism, decoupling, legitimacy), and change or implementation theory. My question is whether those imports are represented accurately at their source, whether the paper is aware of the boundary conditions under which an organisational-culture construct transfers to a university — a professional bureaucracy with weak hierarchical control over academic work — and whether interdisciplinary claims are backed rather than gestured at. I also check that domain-specific vocabulary is defined for a reader from an adjacent field, since undefined quality assurance jargon is an accessibility failure and a precision failure at once.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** From the domain seat this is about whether the exposition lets a field reader locate and verify the substantive claims. I check whether key constructs are defined at first use with the sense the field uses, whether citation practice is precise enough to trace a claim to its source, whether the structure serves the argument rather than an abstract template, and whether the length is used on the study's actual contribution rather than on generic background. At 2,249 words, allocation is itself a structural judgement: a long generic preamble about the importance of quality assurance, at the cost of context, evidence, or limitations, is a structural defect with domain consequences. This is a normal-priority dimension and I will score it accordingly — problems here rarely block, but they can obscure whether a D2 problem exists.

### Summary Assessment

This manuscript is built on a defensible vantage point — administrators as the hinge between external requirement and internal practice — and it contains at least one observation of real theoretical interest (§4.3: administrators hold the compliance/meaning tension *open* and locate their professional identity in the gap). That material deserves to be published somewhere.

It cannot be published as written, for reasons that sit squarely in my seat. First, the Discussion's third and only design-facing claim is warranted by reversing a cited author: §5 has Delacroix (2018) *recommending* stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism of quality culture, while §2 of this same manuscript, and the annotation left inside the reference entry, both record that Delacroix argues the opposite. Second, §6 claims "the first comprehensive account" of a question §2 has just credited to three named predecessors. Third, the paper's central construct is never defined and changes referent mid-paper: "quality culture" is an institutional property in §1 and a 48-person perceptual mean in §4.4. A claim about quality culture that has no stable referent cannot be confirmed or refuted, which is the sharpest thing I can say about a paper whose thesis is about quality culture.

The literature base compounds this: twelve sources, none earlier than 2018, none regulatory, none from the definitional debate, and none on academic staff responses — which is how a finding this literature has reported for over two decades arrives here framed as new.

### Strengths

1. **The non-resolution finding is a genuine contribution.** §4.3 reports that administrators do not resolve the compliance/meaning tension but hold it open, "treating the gap itself as the space in which their professional identity was located." The field's dominant framings are resolution-oriented — either internalization or resistance/decoupling. Sustained non-resolution as the *site* of professional identity is a third position and is not, to my knowledge, established in the QA literature. This is the paper's best asset and it is currently buried in a conventional three-theme structure.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §4.3 ¶1 — "Rather than resolving the tension between compliance and meaning, they held it open, treating the gap itself as the space in which their professional identity was located."` | **Confidence**: 4 — fifteen years reading this literature; I cannot exclude a parallel formulation in adjacent identity-work scholarship I do not track.

An inversion of that default is substantively interesting, and it survives into the text even though it embarrasses the paper's framing. ### Detailed Comments

#### Literature Review

- **Coverage**: Materially incomplete for the claim being made. Twelve sources, all 2018–2022, all secondary scholarship, no primary policy or regulatory instrument, no definitional foundation, no work on academic staff responses. The strand structure of §2 (governance technology / lived experience / quality culture) is a fair map of the *recent* literature and an inaccurate map of the field, because the third strand is called "still emergent" when it has a twenty-year institutional and definitional history. See W4.
- **Integration quality**: Above average for a paper of this length, and better than the coverage. §2 is genuinely synthetic rather than enumerative — Nakamura and Delacroix are put into productive opposition rather than listed, and the documentation sub-literature (Iversen, Okonkwo) is characterised by its mechanism rather than its topic. The authors can read a literature; they have read too little of it.
- **Research gap argument**: Persuasive at §2 ¶4 and untenable at §6 ¶1. These are two different gap claims. The first — that Silva & Tan's intermediary position is conceptual and ungrounded in administrators' words — is precise and survives scrutiny. The second — first comprehensive account — is refuted by the manuscript's own preceding page. See W2.

#### Theoretical Framework

- **Appropriateness**: There is no framework to assess. "Quality culture" is the paper's organising construct, but it functions as a topic label rather than a framework: it generates no propositions, constrains no interpretations, and is not used to decide what would count as evidence. The three themes are inductive categories, which is a legitimate choice, but the paper never says so and never returns the themes to any theory.
- **Application depth**: Superficial by the paper's own structure. §1 gestures at a definition, §2 discusses the concept, §4 reports themes, §5 makes claims about the concept — and no step in that chain is constrained by the previous one. The clearest symptom is the §4.4/§5 slide from institutional property to perceptual mean, which a framework with stated boundary conditions would have blocked. See W3.
- **Alternative frameworks**: Ehlers' structural/enabling decomposition would give the paper exactly what it lacks — a way to say which observations bear on culture and at what level — and would accommodate the administrator/staff divergence in §4.4 as a structural finding rather than an anomaly. Harvey & Stensaker's typology is the other obvious candidate and would let the three sites be positioned rather than merged. Both are field-internal and neither requires new data.

#### Academic Argument Quality

- **Factual accuracy**: One demonstrable misrepresentation of a cited source (W1), one demonstrably false positioning claim (W2), and one domain misstatement about the maturity of the quality-culture literature (W4a). All three are checkable from within the manuscript or against literature I can name.
- **Argument logic**: Three unsupported moves, in increasing order of seriousness: from three institutions to "the sector as a whole" and "administrators everywhere" (§5 ¶2, contradicted by §4.4); from a 48-respondent sentiment mean to a statement about institutional quality culture (§4.4→§5 ¶4); and from findings that document participation being captured to a recommendation that participation be maximised (§5 ¶3, §6). The last of these is the paper arguing against itself without noticing.
- **Terminology precision**: The paper is disciplined about *quality assurance* — it consistently means the machinery of review, documentation, and self-evaluation, which is correct field usage. It is undisciplined about *quality culture*, which takes at least three senses across the paper: collective institutional ownership (§1), the outcome of negotiation among administrators, academics, and external bodies (§5, §6), and an average of staff perception (§4.4). The QA/quality-culture distinction itself is held; the quality-culture/staff-sentiment distinction is not.

#### Contribution to the Field

- **Incremental contribution**: Real but smaller than claimed, and located somewhere other than where the paper puts it. The three-theme structure restates existing categories in a new sample. The contribution is §4.3's non-resolution finding — administrators sustaining the compliance/meaning tension as the site of professional identity, rather than resolving it in either direction — plus §4.4's level-divergence between administrator and staff perception. Both are currently subordinate to the theme structure.
- **Positioning**: §2 positions the study correctly against Silva & Tan, Pettersen, and Rahman. §6 abandons that positioning entirely. These cannot both stand.
- **Overclaiming**: Present and structural rather than decorative. "First comprehensive account" (§6), "universities across the sector" and "administrators everywhere" (§5 ¶2), and "captured the full range of administrator views" (§4.3, in a paragraph disclosing an exclusion) are three separate overclaims, each contradicted by material inside this manuscript. A revision that trims the adjectives will not fix this; the claims have to be re-scoped to what three institutions in one system can support.

#### Missing Key References

The field has treated this construct's under-specification as a standing problem since Harvey and Stensaker, and Ehlers built a decomposition specifically so that the label could support measurement. Without that work done, the paper's central claim — that culture is negotiated rather than managed — is not wrong so much as unevaluable. The paper I would want to read scales the claims to three institutions in one characterised system, corrects Delacroix, defines its construct, reinstates the dissenters, and puts the non-resolution finding at the centre. That paper is reachable from the data already collected, which is why I land on major revision rather than rejection within the action the contract's fired conditions permit — and why I would move to rejection if the sector-level and first-account claims are defended rather than withdrawn.

### Detailed Comments

#### Assumption Audit

- **Explicit assumptions**: The paper states its central premise clearly — quality culture is "an ongoing negotiation among administrators, academics, and external bodies" rather than a management output (§1, §6). That premise is defensible and I have no quarrel with it. The problem is that it is asserted as a finding while only one of the three named parties was studied.

- **Implicit assumptions**: Three go unstated and unexamined. (a) That administrator self-report indexes institutional reality — the accounts are treated as windows onto what the institution *is*, never as accounts produced for a researcher by postholders whose professional role is to defend institutional quality. (b) That naming a theme is explaining it — "ritual compliance" is offered as an analytic result when it is a redescription of what participants said. (c) That participation is the antidote to ritualism, which §4.1's data specifically undercut. Each is consequential; none is surfaced.

- **Paradigmatic assumptions**: The manuscript is written from a managerial-directional paradigm in which culture is a thing institutions build and alignment is the natural good end state — visible in "Building" (title), "cultivated rather than mandated" (§5), and the design recommendations. From institutional theory the more productive framing is the reverse: decoupling is not a failure of culture-building but a stable equilibrium that serves the organization, and the interesting question is what it buys. The paper never registers that it has chosen a paradigm. Notably, its own §4.3 finding fits the alternative better than the one it adopted.

#### Cross-Disciplinary Connections

- **Parallel research**: Organizational sociology has studied this exact phenomenon under different names for four decades — ceremonial conformity and the buffering of technical cores from formal structure; the later literature on when decoupling is means-ends rather than policy-practice; audit studies on how measurement regimes reorganize the organizations they measure. The finding that QA is experienced as theater is, in that literature, the expected result rather than the discovery, which is directly relevant to §6's novelty claim.

- **Borrowing opportunities**: Three, in order of payoff for this manuscript. Decoupling as a *mechanism* rather than a label, which would let §4.1 explain rather than describe. Identity work as coping under stigmatized or contested occupational conditions, which is the literature §4.3 needs and which already contains the "hold the tension open" move the authors appear to have rediscovered. The culture/climate distinction, which resolves W8 cleanly. Sensemaking is a fourth candidate given the manuscript's own language of "meaning-making," but I would not add it on top of the others — one properly used framework beats three named ones.

- **Methodological borrowing**: The design that would answer the paper's question is not more interviews but observation of the QA cycle itself — sitting in the meetings where portfolios are assembled and panels are prepared. Decoupling is much easier to see than to be told about, and self-report is the weakest instrument for it. Failing that, interviewing the same administrators at two points in a review cycle would separate a stable professional orientation from a seasonal one, which matters because §4.1's own "seasons and cycles" language suggests the phenomenon is time-structured. Both are within reach at this scale.

#### Practical Impact

- **Real-world application**: A quality director could take one thing from this paper as written — that persuasion work matters more than paperwork (§4.2) — and that is a real, if familiar, insight. Everything else is either unactionable (W5) or unsafe to act on (the participation recommendation, W3). There is a risk of an unintended consequence here worth stating plainly: an institution that reads §5 and responds by adding consultation events will, on the paper's own evidence and its cited authority's argument, add ritual rather than culture.

- **Implementation feasibility**: The recommendations presume conditions the manuscript never checks — a quality office with discretion over process design, workload slack for meaning-making, and institutional stability across a review cycle. Institutions under enrolment or funding pressure, or those where QA is externally templated, have none of these. Since the paper claims sector-wide reach, the absence of any feasibility discussion is a substantive gap rather than a stylistic one.

- **Stakeholders**: Covered in W6. I would add that external quality agencies are treated throughout as an environmental condition rather than as an actor with interests, which is odd in a paper whose thesis is that quality culture is a negotiation *among* parties including "external bodies" (§6).

#### Broader Implications

- **Ethical dimensions**: W1 is the material issue and I regard it as requiring action before publication rather than in revision. Two second-order points. The exclusion of dissenters (W7) has an ethical face as well as a methodological one — those participants gave time on the understanding their views would be represented. And the paper carries no funding, competing-interest, or data-availability statement, which for research conducted inside institutions the researchers may be employed by is not a formality.

- **Social impact**: Muted, but real in one direction. Research that tells universities quality culture is buildable through better process design supplies a vocabulary for expanding QA activity, and the people who absorb that expansion are typically the academics and support staff this study did not sample. A paper whose own evidence shows the machinery producing "nothing behind that a teacher would recognize as helping them teach" (§4.1) should say something about who pays for the next round of it.

- **Future directions**: The highest-value follow-up is not wider sampling, despite what §6 says. It is the comparison the current data almost support: administrators who hold the tension open versus those who resolve it by exit or by full cynicism — the group §4.3 excluded. Second is the level-divergence in §4.4, which is a question about whose perception counts as the institution's culture, and is answerable with a design that samples both levels properly.

## Review Body

I read this manuscript as an outsider to higher education quality assurance, from institutional theory. What I see is a paper with one genuinely valuable finding, a set of borrowed labels standing in for the theory that would explain it, and a claim structure that has outrun the study by a wide margin.

The valuable thing is in §4.3. Administrators do not resolve the tension between compliance and meaning; they hold it open and locate their professional identity in the gap. In identity-work scholarship, which mostly studies how people repair threatened identities, an account where the unresolved contradiction *is* the identity site is a distinct and publishable move. It is currently the third of three themes, given one paragraph, and immediately followed by the disclosure that dissenting participants were dropped. That ordering is the paper's central self-inflicted wound.

What sits on top of it is a vocabulary problem. "Ritual compliance," "distributed leadership," and "identity work" all have homes outside this field, and the manuscript imports the words without the machinery — the reference list contains no source from any of the three literatures. The consequence is not pedantic. As used here, ritual compliance names how administrators *feel* about documentation. In its source literature it names an arrangement in which ceremonial conformity buys legitimacy while the technical core is buffered, and it comes with an account of what keeps the arrangement stable even when everyone can see it. The manuscript's own best line — "It's theater, and everyone knows it's theater" — poses exactly the question that account answers, and the paper does not ask it. Taking one construct seriously, properly, would convert three descriptions into one explanation, and would cost fewer words than the sector-level claims that need deleting anyway.

Two things require action rather than revision. The first is participant protection. §3.5 promises that no individual could be identified; §4.1 then attributes employer-critical quotations to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and "the associate dean for quality at the public research university." Inside a three-institution study in one national system, those are names. This is the internal-confidentiality failure mode — protection against strangers, none against the colleagues and employers who can actually place the speaker — and it is the category of error organizational research on identifiable postholders most often makes. The remedy is straightforward (generic attributions, an explicit account in §3.5 of what was done against internal identification), but whether the existing consent covers publication in the current form is a question for the authors and the editor, not something a revision letter can assume away. I note the inversion: the manuscript conceals the country, which readers need in order to judge transfer, and exposes the individuals, whom it promised to protect.

The second is §5's third discussion point. The paper recommends maximizing participation and grounds that recommendation in Delacroix (2018) — an author its own §2 reports as arguing against treating consultation as sufficient, on the grounds that participatory processes get co-opted into compliance performance. Reviewer 2 owns whether the source was misread. My concern is what happens once it is corrected: Delacroix's actual argument is the standard institutional-theory prediction, and §4.1's data confirm it independently. The manuscript had theory and evidence pointing the same way and recommended the opposite, because it never noticed it was recommending the thing its own findings show being captured. This is why the recommendation is also unusable in practice: an institution that responds by scheduling more consultation will, on this paper's evidence, manufacture more ritual. The recoverable version is in §4.2, where administrators distinguish persuasion and coalition work from paperwork — "Half my job is convincing people this matters. The forms are the easy part." That is a differently-shaped recommendation the data actually support.

On claim calibration: §5 asserts that "universities across the sector" and "administrators everywhere" share this tension, and §6 offers "the first comprehensive account," on twelve gatekeeper-recruited administrators in three institutions in a system the paper will not name. From institutional theory this is not merely an overreach but a self-defeating one — whether QA decouples varies with regulatory regime, status position, and funding dependence, and universalizing the finding erases the variation that would make it worth knowing. The same paragraph in §2 that sets up the contribution names three predecessors on quality officers, professional identity, and administrators-as-intermediaries.

Two omissions are structural rather than presentational. Faculty, students, support staff, and external agencies — three of them named in the paper's own definition of quality culture as a negotiation — are not sampled, yet institution-wide conclusions are drawn. And the dissenting administrators excluded from §4.3 are the most diagnostic cases the study had: they are where one finds out whether the meaning-recovery reported here is a real orientation or a professional script performed for a researcher. Their removal leaves the paper's own strongest rival explanation untested while the text claims "the full range of administrator views."

The paper this could be is a three-institution comparative study of how administrators inhabit an unresolved institutional contradiction, theorized with the decoupling and identity-work literatures it currently only gestures at. That paper is smaller than the one submitted and considerably more interesting.

I note my outsider status where it bears on weight: I am not positioned to say what counts as a novel contribution in higher education QA scholarship, and Reviewer 2 should govern there; I have not assessed the survey inference, which is Reviewer 1's; and the participant-count discrepancy and the DOI-prefix anomaly are flagged for the editor's integrity lane rather than adjudicated here.

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** The contract asks whether design, data handling, reporting, and reproducibility affordances clear the field's peer-review bar; my adversarial reading of that is narrower than the Methodology Reviewer's, because design critique is R1's seat, not mine. What I own here is the logical dependency between the method and the conclusion: whether the study's chosen instrument can, even in principle, deliver the inferential weight the paper will later place on it. For a 2,249-word study of administrator perspectives on quality assurance implementation, the likely instrument is a small-N interview or survey design at one or a few institutions, and the adversarial question is whether that instrument licenses claims about *quality-culture formation* — a construct that is longitudinal, organisational, and contested — or only about what administrators say about it at one moment. I will also treat unreported procedural facts (who was sampled, who declined, who coded, whether respondents were the same people who own the QA processes being evaluated) as attack surface, since each is a route by which a favourable finding could be an artefact rather than a result. Severity here is bounded by Challenge Dimension 9: reproducibility and data-release demands in qualitative higher-education research must be grounded in that field's actual accepted-practice boundary, not in a generic open-data norm imported from quantitative or physical science.

**D2 — domain_accuracy.** The contract asks whether claims align with current domain evidence and whether prior work is represented correctly. Adversarially, my interest is not literature *coverage* — that is R2's seat — but literature *selection* and *representation* as evidence of a predetermined conclusion. Internal quality assurance and quality culture form a field with a genuinely contested empirical record: the compliance-versus-improvement tension, the "audit culture" critique, and the well-documented finding that formal QA systems can coexist with weak or performative culture. A paper positioned to argue that institutional quality culture is being built has a standing incentive to cite the enabling literature and omit the sceptical literature. I will also treat terminological drift as a domain-accuracy defect rather than a wording issue: "quality culture" carries specific and mutually incompatible definitions across the European and Anglophone traditions, and a paper that quietly slides between them can manufacture agreement with prior work that does not exist.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** This is my home dimension: internal consistency of the core thesis, evidence-to-claim fit, and fallacies that undermine the central argument. The title's structure — "Building Institutional Quality Culture" from "Administrator Perspectives" — announces the paper's principal vulnerability before I have read a word of it, because a claim about culture-building is a claim about organisational outcome while administrator perspectives are self-reports from the actors responsible for that outcome. My job is to construct the strongest rival account of whatever pattern the paper reports: that administrators describe successful culture-building because describing it is part of their role, because the interviewer's framing invited it, or because the institutions that agreed to participate are the ones with something to show. I will hunt for hidden bridging premises (that stated commitment indexes enacted practice; that QA structures cause cultural change rather than co-occurring with it), for circular definitions in which quality culture is operationalised by the presence of the QA activities it is meant to explain, and for the standard slide from participants' accounts to the authors' causal voice in the discussion.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** The contract asks whether framing, definitions, and implications are accessible to adjacent-field readers and whether interdisciplinary claims are substantiated. My adversarial angle is the second clause. Quality-culture research routinely borrows from organisational-culture theory, institutional theory, and change management, and the borrowing is often decorative — a citation to Schein or to isomorphism used as a label rather than as a commitment that generates testable expectations. I will ask whether each imported construct does any inferential work, and whether the paper is answerable to the parent discipline's evidence standards once it has invoked them. The accessibility question I read as a substance question too: if the paper's key terms cannot be stated in a way an adjacent-field reader could falsify, the vagueness is protecting the argument rather than serving the reader.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** Organisation, exposition, table and figure quality, venue conventions. This is the dimension where a Devil's Advocate is most likely to overreach, so I read it strictly: presentation problems earn a place in my report only when the presentation *is* the argument's load-bearing surface. A 2,249-word manuscript is short for an empirical qualitative study, and the adversarial question is what got compressed out — method, negative cases, limitations, or the evidence for the central claim — and whether the resulting structure lets an unsupported claim pass as established because no section is allocated to testing it. Missing or unusable evidence displays fall here as well when the paper's claims cannot be checked against them. Ordinary prose infelicities do not, and per the severity table they cannot be inflated by my adversarial register.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
- `what_to_look_for` — Whether the paper states its design, sampling frame, participant count and roles, recruitment route, refusal or non-response, data-collection instrument, and analytic procedure; whether the unit of analysis matches the unit of the claim (individual administrator accounts versus institutional culture); whether the informants are the owners of the processes being appraised, creating a self-evaluation dependency; whether any negative, deviant, or disconfirming case is reported, or whether every account converges; whether member-checking, second coding, or any comparable credibility check is described; whether any triangulation against non-testimonial evidence (documents, QA records, outcome data) exists to corroborate self-reports; whether temporal design supports a "building" claim or is single-timepoint; whether reflexivity about the authors' own institutional position is stated where the authors are themselves QA actors. I will also fix, before scoring, the field's actual reporting boundary for qualitative higher-education studies — participant description, sampling logic, and analytic procedure are standard expectations; raw-transcript release is not — so that no severity of mine rests on an ungrounded norm.
- `what_triggers_block` — The paper places load-bearing inferential weight on an instrument that cannot carry it and does not disclose enough for a reader to see this: specifically, either (a) core methodological facts are absent to the point that the results are unevaluable — no stated participant count, roles, or sites, or no described analytic procedure — so the reported pattern cannot be distinguished from the authors' summary of their own impressions; or (b) the design is single-timepoint self-report from process owners and the paper nonetheless asserts culture change, causal effect, or effectiveness in its own voice without treating self-report as a limitation. A block requires that the gap be decision-determinative, that its anchor be typed, and — if I invoke a reporting expectation — that I name the field's accepted-practice boundary and why this paper crosses it.
- `what_triggers_warn` — Method is disclosed and broadly appropriate, but a specific procedural gap gives a rival explanation room that the paper does not close: convenience or single-site sampling used to speak about "universities" generally; no disconfirming case in a uniformly positive dataset; coding by a single author with no credibility check; no triangulation of self-report against any documentary or outcome evidence; unreported recruitment or refusal; or an unstated author-institution relationship. Also warn where the design's limits are acknowledged in a limitations paragraph but the abstract, conclusion, or implications continue to speak as if they had been overcome.

### D2: domain_accuracy
- `what_to_look_for` — Whether the sceptical strand of the internal-QA literature is present at all: audit culture and performativity critiques, compliance-versus-enhancement findings, evidence that formal QA systems coexist with weak or ritualised culture, and studies reporting failed or stalled culture-building. Whether the citation set is directionally balanced or reads as supporting-evidence-only. Whether cited sources are represented as they actually argue, or are recruited for a claim they qualify or contradict — including the common case of citing a critical study for its descriptive framework while ignoring its critical conclusion. Whether "quality culture" is defined and held stable, or drifts between traditions (the European quality-culture formulation with its structural/managerial and cultural/psychological components, versus looser Anglophone usages) in ways that change what the evidence would have to show. Whether domain terms of art — internal quality assurance, external quality assurance, accreditation, enhancement, quality management — are used with their accepted referents rather than interchangeably. Whether findings asserted as established in the field actually are, or are single-study results promoted to consensus.
- `what_triggers_block` — Selection or representation of the evidence base is distorted enough that the paper's positioning is not merely thin but misleading: the paper asserts a domain consensus that does not exist, or attributes to cited work a position it does not hold, and the central argument depends on that misrepresentation; or the sceptical/contradicting strand of the literature is systematically absent while the paper's contribution is framed as resolving precisely the question that strand contests, so the reader is given no way to see the counter-case. Requires a typed anchor identifying the specific claim and the specific source or the specific documented omission — an `absence` anchor here must name the surfaces I checked (introduction, framework, discussion, reference list).
- `what_triggers_warn` — A directional tilt in the evidence base that weakens credibility without collapsing the argument: a citation set noticeably skewed toward enabling findings; one or two relevant contradicting studies unaddressed; a key construct defined loosely or shifting between sections without changing the substantive conclusion; a domain term used imprecisely in a non-central claim; a contested finding stated flatly without acknowledging the contest. I will not warn on coverage breadth alone — sparse-but-fair citation is R2's concern, not a domain-accuracy defect of mine — and where my objection reduces to "the field should cite more", I will down-rate rather than dress it as inaccuracy.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
- `what_to_look_for` — The exact chain from the paper's evidence to its central claim, written out step by step, with each unstated bridging premise named: notably that self-reported commitment indexes enacted practice, that administrator accounts represent institutional reality, that QA structures cause cultural change rather than co-occur with it, and that reported activity constitutes culture. Whether the construct is operationalised circularly — quality culture measured by the presence of the QA implementation that is supposed to produce it. Whether the paper's voice shifts between reporting what participants said and asserting what is the case, and where that shift happens (abstract, findings, discussion, conclusion). Whether the strongest rival explanations are addressed: social desirability and role-based self-presentation, selection of cooperative or high-performing institutions, framing effects from the interview protocol, decoupling of formal structure from practice, and the possibility that observed convergence reflects shared sector vocabulary rather than shared culture. Whether internal contradictions exist between the abstract's claim, the findings, and the limitations. Whether any result reported as challenging the authors' expectations appears at all, since a wholly confirmatory paper is the signature of a predetermined conclusion. And whether the paper's inferential scope stays inside its sampled sites, or generalises to universities as a class.
- `what_triggers_block` — The main conclusion does not follow from the presented evidence even taking the evidence at face value. Concretely: the paper claims institutional quality culture is being built, or that QA implementation produced cultural change, on the basis of administrator self-reports with no triangulation and no engagement with the decoupling counter-explanation; or a rival account — social desirability, participating-institution selection, structure/practice decoupling — is at least as parsimonious as the paper's and fits the reported data at least as well, and goes unaddressed; or the central construct is defined circularly so the finding is true by construction; or an outright internal contradiction sits between what the findings section reports and what the abstract or conclusion asserts. Any of these is a Foundation Collapse, Logic Chain Break, or Stronger Counter-Narrative in this agent's CRITICAL taxonomy and blocks D3.
- `what_triggers_warn` — The inferential chain holds in outline but has a repairable break: a hidden premise that is defensible yet unstated; causal or quasi-causal verbs ("fosters", "leads to", "drives") applied to associational self-report evidence; a rival explanation named in the limitations but not weighed against the paper's own account; overgeneralisation from the sampled sites to universities in general in the abstract or implications while the findings stay properly bounded; or unevenly hedged claims where the same finding is cautious in one section and assertive in another. Per the Surface-Form Parity gate, I will judge each of these on substance and run the opposite-style counterfactual before committing — a fluent, technically-worded claim gets no credit for its polish, and a plainly-worded concern of mine gets no discount for its plainness.

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
- `what_to_look_for` — Whether imported constructs from organisational-culture theory, institutional theory, or change management do inferential work or serve as decoration: does each borrowed framework generate an expectation the data could have violated, or is it named once and abandoned? Whether the paper is answerable to the parent discipline's standards once it invokes them — if it claims culture in the organisational-culture sense, whether it engages anything beyond espoused values; if it invokes isomorphism or decoupling, whether it accepts the prediction that formal adoption can be ceremonial. Whether key terms are stated so an adjacent-field reader (organisational studies, public administration, education policy) could evaluate or falsify them, rather than resting on higher-education-sector shorthand. Whether implications addressed to policy or management audiences are supported by the study's actual evidence base or exceed it. Whether stakeholder positions structurally absent from an administrator-only design — faculty, students, quality officers, external agencies — are acknowledged as a boundary on the claim; I name the absence and stop there, since elaborating those voices is R3's seat.
- `what_triggers_block` — A load-bearing interdisciplinary claim is unsubstantiated in a way that voids the contribution for readers outside the immediate subfield: the paper's central construct is asserted in the organisational-culture register while the evidence supports only espoused-values talk, and the theoretical borrowing is what licenses the paper's headline claim; or the paper invokes a theoretical framework whose own predictions contradict its conclusion (for example decoupling or ceremonial-adoption theory) and neither reconciles nor acknowledges this. D4 is high-priority, not mandatory, so a block here fires F3 rather than F1 — which is exactly why I will reserve it for a substantiation failure that is genuinely load-bearing and not merely a thin theory section.
- `what_triggers_warn` — Cross-disciplinary framing is present but weakly cashed out: frameworks cited as labels without doing analytic work; definitions comprehensible only inside the higher-education QA community with no bridge for adjacent readers; implications for policy or management stated at a level of generality the evidence does not reach; or a structural stakeholder absence unacknowledged, leaving the reader to infer a whole-institution scope that the administrator-only design cannot support.

### D5: writing_and_structure
- `what_to_look_for` — Whether the manuscript's architecture allocates space to the things its argument needs: a method section sufficient to evaluate the design, a findings section separable from interpretation, an explicit limitations treatment, and a conclusion that does not exceed the findings. What 2,249 words appear to have cost — specifically whether method, participant description, negative cases, or limitations are the compressed casualties, and whether that compression is doing argumentative work by leaving the central claim untested rather than merely making the paper brief. Whether tables or figures presenting participant characteristics, coding structure, or supporting quotations exist and are readable and self-consistent, and whether claims in the text can actually be checked against them; whether quotation is used as evidence with attribution to participant roles or as illustration only. Whether the abstract accurately represents the body. Whether venue-facing basics — structural conventions, section completeness, internal cross-reference integrity — hold.
- `what_triggers_block` — Presentation failure that makes the central claim unevaluable, not merely harder to read: the findings the argument rests on are not presented in retrievable form at all (no attributed data extracts, no participant characterisation, and no display of the evidence behind the reported pattern), so no reader can verify the claim against anything; or a display and the text contradict each other on a load-bearing result. Absent that, and given the severity discipline binding this seat, I do not expect to block D5 on structural grounds, and I will not reach for one to add weight to a report — a `block` here would fire F2 in combination with a mandatory `warn` and inflating it would corrupt the panel's decision.
- `what_triggers_warn` — Structure or exposition degrades verifiability without destroying it: a method section too compressed to evaluate but not wholly absent; findings and interpretation fused so that participant voice and author inference cannot be separated; limitations relegated to a sentence or omitted; an abstract stronger than the body; quotations presented without role attribution or with unstated selection logic; tables missing where claimed patterns need them, or present but incomplete, unlabelled, or inconsistent with the text; missing venue-expected sections. Prose-level infelicity, house-style deviation, and reference formatting do not trigger a warn from me at all — formatting compliance is the citation agent's seat, and stylistic taste is not a decision-impacting defect.

## Devil's Advocate Review

**Scope note.** I do not rule on venue fit, statistical design adequacy, literature coverage breadth, or citation formatting — those are the EIC's, R1's, R2's and the citation agent's seats. I rule on whether the argument holds. Where my findings overlap another seat's, I report them anyway; deduplication is synthesis work, not mine.

**Injection check.** The manuscript contains no imperative content addressed to reviewers, no instructions, and no appeals to the review process. Nothing in it was treated as a directive.

**Genuine strengths (stated once, not padded).** Three things in this manuscript are real. §2 is a fair literature review: the sceptical strand is present and properly represented (Harlow, Montero, Okonkwo, Iversen, Delacroix), and Delacroix's actual position is stated correctly — which is the only reason §5's reversal is detectable at all. §4.3's observation that administrators hold the compliance/meaning tension *open*, treating the gap as where their professional identity lives, is a genuine analytic move rather than a relabeling. And §4.4 reports a number that cuts against the paper's own thesis; the framing sentence buries it, but the datum was not suppressed.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

Suppose I hold the opposite view: that nothing in this manuscript survives its own disclosures. My case is not any single defect but a subtraction. Remove the sector-level generalization in §5, which three institutions in one unnamed national system cannot license. Remove the "first comprehensive account" claim in §6, which the paper's own §2 refutes by naming Pettersen (2022), Rahman (2020) and Silva & Tan (2021) as prior work on exactly this terrain. Correct the third discussion point, which enlists Delacroix (2018) as recommending consultation when §2 and the reference-list annotation both record him arguing against it. Reinstate the dissenting participants that §4.3 removed "for space" because they "fell outside the three-theme structure." Relabel §4.4, where a divergence is announced as corroboration. What remains is a competent, thin, three-site account of administrators experiencing QA as ritual — a finding the paper's own literature review already attributes to Harlow (2019), Montero (2021), Okonkwo (2018) and Iversen (2021). The paper would be confirming what it opened by describing as established.

Worse, the three themes are not independent of the analysis that produced them. Cases that did not fit the frame were deleted, so "three dominant themes" means "three themes, plus a discard pile of unstated size." A rival account fits everything reported at least as well: twelve (or fourteen) postholders, recruited *through QA offices*, in one national system, sharing a professional vocabulary, gave a role-appropriate account to researchers who told them they were studying quality culture. That is convergence of discourse, not evidence of culture.

The one defence I concede the authors: §4.3's held-open tension. It is real, and it does not depend on the sector claim. It is also the one finding the excluded dissenters could have falsified.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. **Shared professional vocabulary rather than shared culture.** Twelve to fourteen postholders were recruited *through institutional QA offices* (§3.2), all within one national system, and told they were participating in a study of quality culture. "Theater," "feeding the machine," "you can't do quality *to* a department" — these are the idioms of a professional community that reads the same literature and attends the same meetings. Convergence of vocabulary across such a sample is expected under the null hypothesis of no shared institutional culture at all. This rival is more parsimonious than the paper's, fits §4.1–§4.3 exactly as well, and is never raised.
2. **Role-scripted self-presentation.** §4.3's theme — administrators as "custodians of student interests against a tide of box-ticking" — is the single most flattering account a QA postholder could give of their own work, delivered to researchers studying whether QA is meaningful. The paper reads it as a finding about identity. Social desirability and role performance predict the identical data. With no interview protocol, no positionality statement, and no triangulation against documents or outcomes, nothing in the manuscript distinguishes the two.
3. **Decoupling as equilibrium, not as failure.** Delacroix's actual position, plus §4.1's evidence, supports the reading that ritualization is the *stable state* of an audit apparatus and that participation initiatives are simply the next thing to be documented. Under this account, §5's design recommendation is not a finding but a restatement of the aspiration the data show failing.
4. **Selection of cooperative institutions.** Access was granted by QA offices at three institutions. Institutions whose QA offices decline are absent by construction, and no refusals or non-participation are reported at either the institutional or individual level.
5. **The survey divergence has a simpler reading the paper never considers.** Staff sentiment may be moderately positive because the QA ritual is cheap for people who do not run it and expensive only for those who do. That reading — QA as a localized administrative burden rather than a sector-wide crisis of meaning — undercuts the paper's register entirely and fits §4.4 at least as well as "different levels experience culture differently." It is also indistinguishable, on the evidence given (M5), from an artifact of an undocumented instrument.
6. **The unexamined path: less machinery rather than better machinery.** The paper considers only how to redesign QA processes. It never asks whether its own §4.1 evidence implies reducing the documentation apparatus. That option is not rejected; it is not raised.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

*Named only. Elaborating what these voices would say is R3's seat.*

- **Academic teaching staff** — the subjects of QA and the reference point for §4.1's central claim ("nothing behind that a teacher would recognize as helping them teach"), evaluated entirely through administrators' reports of them.
- **Students** — invoked as the moral warrant for the identity-work theme ("whether students get a fair deal," §4.3) and never consulted.
- **External QA agency and accreditation panel staff** — one half of the "negotiation among administrators, academics, and external bodies" that §6 claims to have characterized.
- **The dissenting administrators of §4.3** — a stakeholder absence created by the authors' analytic decision rather than by the sampling design, and the only group whose testimony could have falsified the paper's thesis.
- **The 28 survey respondents** outside the two compared subgroups (§4.4), whose institution and responses are never accounted for.

### Unexamined Premise

Beneath all eight challenge dimensions sits an assumption the paper never states and never tests: that "quality culture" names a property institutions possess in degrees, which better-designed QA can increase. The paper's own evidence supports a competing premise at least as well — that "quality culture" is a *discourse* the QA profession has acquired, and that what the study documents is administrators' fluency in it. Under the paper's premise, the convergence across twelve accounts is evidence of a shared institutional condition. Under the rival premise, the same convergence is evidence that these twelve people have the same job, read the same sources, and were recruited through the same offices in the same national system. Every finding in §4 is compatible with both, and the manuscript contains nothing that could discriminate between them — which is exactly what one would expect of a construct that is never operationalized (M4).

A second-order consequence: if the rival premise holds, §6's closing recommendation is not merely unsupported, it is category-confused — one cannot design a process to produce a vocabulary its participants already speak.

### Observations (Non-Defects)

- **A prior of mine was disconfirmed and should be recorded as such.** I entered expecting a citation set skewed toward enabling findings with the sceptical strand suppressed. That is not what this manuscript does — §2 gives the critical literature (Harlow, Montero, Okonkwo, Iversen, Delacroix) fair and accurate space. The confirmation bias in this paper is in its *data*, not its bibliography: the dissenters were removed from the interview corpus, not from the reference list. The panel should not carry an unexamined cherry-picking assumption forward from the paper's topic.
- **§4.3's held-open tension is the paper that is actually here.** The observation that administrators locate their professional identity in the unresolved gap between compliance and meaning does not require the sector claim, the novelty claim, the survey, or the Delacroix citation. It survives every subtraction in my counter-argument above. It is currently one sentence at the end of a subsection.
- **The §4.4 divergence is the manuscript's most honest moment and its framing sentence destroys it.** Whatever the panel decides about the survey strand, note that removing it also removes the only evidence in the paper that runs against the authors' thesis. Deletion is not cost-free.
- **Polish is not a quality signal in this manuscript.** The prose is confident and clean while the evidence layer contains at least six internal contradictions detectable without any external knowledge (Abstract vs §3.2; §3.1 vs §4.1; §4.3 vs itself; §4.4 vs itself; §3.5 vs §4.1; §2 vs §5). I flag this because the Surface-Form Parity gate cuts both ways here: none of my findings above earn their severity from fluent phrasing, and none of the paper's claims earn credibility from theirs.

## Review Body

This manuscript is not weak in the ordinary way that a small qualitative study is weak. It is a paper whose evidence layer contradicts itself at six separate points that require no external knowledge to detect, and whose most consequential contradiction is an admission: §4.3 states that the participants who disagreed with the paper's thesis were removed from the analysis because they did not fit the theme structure, one sentence after claiming the study captured "the full range of administrator views." Everything downstream inherits that. When §4.1 says a theme was "most pervasive," when §4.2 says a pattern "recurred across every interview," when §5 says the findings "demonstrate" something about universities across the sector — each of these is a statement about a corpus from which the disconfirming cases were deleted, of unstated size, drawn from a participant pool the manuscript numbers as fourteen in one place and twelve in another.

The second structural failure is that the paper's practical contribution rests on a source that argues against it. §2 states Delacroix's position accurately; the reference-list annotation states it accurately again; §5 then cites him as recommending the opposite and builds the paper's design implication on the inversion. I am not in a position to say whether this is a slip or a construction, and it does not matter to the decision: the recommendation that survives is one whose most relevant cited authority predicts its capture, and whose capture the paper's own §4.1 data document. Correcting the citation does not rescue the recommendation — it removes the recommendation's only support and supplies its refutation.

Third, the two strands do not do what the paper says they do. §4.4 announces corroboration and then reports divergence, and §5 quietly concedes the divergence again. The mixed-methods claim in the Abstract therefore describes a relationship between strands that the Findings section does not contain.

Against all of this, the defence I would mount in the authors' place is narrow and real. §4.3's observation that administrators hold the compliance/meaning tension open rather than resolving it — treating the gap itself as the site of professional identity — is an analytic move, not a relabeling, and it is the one claim in the manuscript that survives deleting the sector generalization, the novelty claim, the Delacroix citation, and the survey. That is the paper worth recovering. It is also the claim most directly exposed by C1, because the excluded dissenters are by definition the administrators who did *not* hold the tension open, and their removal is what makes the pattern look universal.

On calibration, three things about my own report. I entered Phase 1 expecting a one-sided citation set and did not find one; §2 represents the sceptical literature fairly, and I have recorded the disconfirmed prior rather than quietly retiring it. I declined to block D4 and D5 despite genuine defects in both, because their substance is already carried by the mandatory dimensions and inflating them would add decision weight without adding evidence. And the DOI-prefix pattern across the reference list, which is the most alarming-looking thing in the manuscript, I have banded MINOR and explicitly refused to characterize as fabrication: I cannot verify a single entry from this seat, and a check-required flag is what the evidence supports.

Two dimension scores in this report rest partly on field expectations, and I have named the boundary in each case rather than asserting one from my own prior. Where I invoke reporting standards, they are participant description, reflexivity, limitations, and inferential-statistics reporting — not data release. I have asked for no transcripts, no dataset, and no artifact this field does not routinely expect, and the block on D1 does not depend on the norms at all: a manuscript that states two different participant counts and claims full anonymization while identifying two unique postholders fails on its own terms under any reporting regime.

### Decision Rationale

Five seats, scoring against triggers fixed before they saw the manuscript, returned block on all three mandatory dimensions between them, and F1 fired from every card independently. That is not a marginal outcome and the decision is not a close call. What distinguishes this manuscript from an ordinarily weak small-*n* study is the character of its defects: six contradictions internal to roughly 2,600 words of body text, each detectable without leaving the page — Abstract against §3.2, §3.1 against §4.1, §4.3 against itself, §4.4 against itself, §3.5 against §4.1, §2 against §5. The EIC's formulation is the one the panel converged on independently: the prose is at pre-submission polish while the evidence layer is at first-draft state, and that inversion is itself the finding. Fluent writing normally signals authors who have finished thinking; here it conceals a corpus of unknown size, a pruned theme structure, a reversed source, and an anonymisation promise the Findings break.

Reject is available under the fired action and three seats reached for it conditionally. It is not the right decision, for a reason all five seats state in their own words: two findings survive every subtraction. §4.3's observation that administrators hold the compliance/meaning tension open and locate professional identity in the unresolved gap is, on R2's and R3's independent readings, absent from the cited QA literature and distinct from resolution-oriented identity-work scholarship. §4.4's administrator/staff divergence inverts the field's default picture and is the paper's only counter-evidence to its own thesis, reported rather than buried. Both are reachable from data already collected, which is what separates a major revision from a different study.

Major Revision is therefore granted, gated on the four conditions above, because the work required is identifiable and bounded — not because the present draft is close. Two of its steps depend on records the panel cannot see: reconciling the interview corpus, and producing a survey instrument. If those records do not exist, the band's other branch applies. The confidentiality contradiction is not part of that calculus; it concerns living participants and must be settled before any version of this work is published.
