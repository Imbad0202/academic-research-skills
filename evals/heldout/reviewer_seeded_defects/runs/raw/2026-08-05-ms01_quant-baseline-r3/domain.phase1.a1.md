## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** This dimension asks whether the study was actually built and reported in a way that a peer reviewer in this field would accept: a design capable of supporting the questions asked, transparent handling of the underlying data, statistical reporting that is complete rather than selective, and enough procedural detail that someone else could attempt to repeat the work. Reading it from a domain-accuracy standpoint, my concern is that methodological choices must be the ones the field's established literature treats as appropriate for the kind of question being asked; a design that is internally tidy but inconsistent with how the domain measures the construct is still a substantive problem. Ownership sits with the methodology reviewer, so I will not score it, but I note the overlap because domain conventions and methodological adequacy frequently constrain each other.

**D2 — domain_accuracy.** This is my assigned dimension. It requires that every substantive claim the manuscript makes be consistent with the current state of evidence in its field, that the prior literature it invokes be characterised as that literature actually stands rather than as a convenient foil, and that field-specific vocabulary, constructs, measures, instruments, and reported quantities be used correctly and consistently. Concretely I read this as four obligations: no assertion that contradicts well-established findings without acknowledging and engaging the contradiction; no citation that is attributed a result, direction of effect, population, or scope it does not have; no term of art used in a sense that differs from its accepted definition in the field; and no numerical or definitional error in how domain results are reported, restated, or carried forward into the discussion. Overclaiming beyond what the evidence base supports, and silently redefining a construct mid-argument, both fall inside this dimension even when the underlying analysis is competently executed.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** This dimension concerns whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether the stated evidence actually bears on the claims it is offered for, whether intermediate steps follow, and whether reasoning defects serious enough to undermine the main argument are present. From my vantage point this is adjacent to but distinct from domain accuracy, because a paper can reason validly from a mischaracterised premise, or reason invalidly from accurate domain facts. It is owned by the devil's-advocate reviewer with the methodology reviewer also eligible, so I will not assign it a score.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** Here the question is whether readers from neighbouring fields can follow the framing, whether definitions are supplied rather than assumed, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplinary boundaries is actually backed by evidence from the discipline it reaches into. The domain-accuracy connection is that borrowed constructs are a common site of misuse, since a term can be imported with a meaning it does not carry in its home literature. The dimension is owned by the perspective reviewer, so it is outside my scoring remit.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** This covers manuscript organisation, clarity of exposition, the quality and legibility of figures and tables, and conformity to the target venue's conventions of presentation. It is a presentation-quality judgement rather than a substantive one, though I recognise that unclear exposition can mask or manufacture apparent factual errors. The editor-in-chief role owns it and I will not score it.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution.** This asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it advances something original and significant enough to matter to that venue's readership. Judgements of originality depend on an accurate reading of what the prior literature already established, which is where it touches my dimension, but the fit-and-contribution verdict itself is reserved to the editor-in-chief role and I will not assign it a score.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether substantive claims are consistent with current field evidence; whether cited prior work is represented with its actual findings, direction of effect, population, and scope; whether field-specific terminology and constructs are used with their accepted definitions and held stable throughout; and whether reported domain quantities, measures, and derived statements are internally consistent and correctly carried into interpretation.
what_triggers_block: A central conclusion rests on a claim that contradicts well-established domain evidence without acknowledgement, or on a cited source materially misrepresented in a way that changes what the manuscript is entitled to assert, or on a core construct used in a sense the field does not recognise.
what_triggers_warn: Localised domain problems that do not carry the main conclusion, such as imprecise or inconsistent use of a secondary term, an overstated characterisation of the literature that a citation only partly supports, an outdated reference where a superseding result exists, or an interpretive overreach beyond what the reported evidence covers.
what_triggers_fatal: Fabricated, non-existent, or wholly unsupportable domain content, such as references to sources that do not exist or report nothing resembling the attributed result, invented instruments or measures, or reported domain values that are mutually contradictory to the point that no coherent factual account of the study can be reconstructed.
