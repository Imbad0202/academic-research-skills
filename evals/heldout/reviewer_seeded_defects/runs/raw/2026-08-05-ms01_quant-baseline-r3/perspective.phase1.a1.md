## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** This dimension asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical reporting, and reproducibility supports would survive scrutiny by specialists in the paper's own field. Read from my angle as the cross-disciplinary reader, the same requirement has a second face: a method is only rigorous in the interdisciplinary sense if a reader trained in an adjacent field can tell *what was done* well enough to judge it — sampling frame, unit of analysis, what the outcome variable actually measures, and what inference the design licenses. I do not own this dimension and will not score it, but I note that opacity in methods is the most common reason an adjacent-field reader cannot evaluate a claim at all.

**D2 — domain_accuracy.** Here the contract requires that assertions match current evidence in the home discipline, that prior work is represented faithfully, and that domain-specific terms and reported results contain no factual errors. My cross-disciplinary counterpart concern is terminological: the same word can be a precise construct in one field and a loose metaphor in the next, so accuracy inside the field is a precondition for translatability outside it. Scoring this belongs to the domain reviewer; I flag only that undefined or field-shifted vocabulary is where accuracy and accessibility problems tend to co-occur.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** The contract wants the central thesis to hold together internally, with evidence that actually supports the stated claims and without fallacies that would collapse the main line of reasoning. Viewed through the interdisciplinary lens, coherence is what allows a non-specialist to follow the chain from data to conclusion without having to supply missing premises from field-specific tacit knowledge. Ownership sits with the devil's-advocate role and secondarily with methodology; I will not score it, though I recognise that an argument requiring insider assumptions to close its gaps reads as incoherent to outsiders even when insiders accept it.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** This is my dimension. The contract asks whether the framing, the definitions, and the stated implications are genuinely accessible to readers in adjacent fields, and whether any claim that reaches beyond the home discipline is actually substantiated rather than asserted. Two distinct failure modes live here and I will treat them separately. The first is accessibility: jargon deployed without definition, constructs named but never operationalised in plain terms, background assumed rather than supplied, and implications stated so narrowly that a neighbouring-field reader cannot see what would transfer. The second, and in my experience the more serious, is over-reach: broad claims addressed to other disciplines, other populations, other institutional contexts, or other policy arenas, made on the strength of evidence that only supports a much narrower statement. Substantiation is the operative word in the contract — an interdisciplinary claim is not satisfied by being interesting to another field; it must be backed by something in the paper. This dimension carries `high` priority rather than `mandatory`, so my worst available verdict is `block`, which under F4 drives a major-revision outcome.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** The contract covers manuscript organisation, clarity of exposition, quality of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's conventions. This overlaps my territory at the edges — poor signposting and uninformative exhibits raise the entry cost for outside readers — but the contract assigns it to the editor-in-chief role, and I will not score it. Where I find that structural choices specifically obstruct adjacent-field comprehension, that observation belongs inside my D4 reasoning, not in a separate verdict.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution.** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it offers something original and significant to that venue's readership. It is adjacent to but distinct from my own: breadth of appeal is not the same as fit, and a paper can be perfectly legible to neighbouring fields while contributing nothing new, or highly original while remaining hermetically sealed. The editor-in-chief owns this call. I will confine myself to whether stated interdisciplinary reach is real, and leave the significance judgement to the owning role.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether key constructs and technical terms are defined at first use in language an adjacent-field reader could act on; whether the framing states the problem, the setting, and the unit of analysis without assuming home-field tacit knowledge; whether stated implications for other fields, populations, institutions, or policy contexts are each traceable to specific evidence presented in the manuscript; and whether the scope of the discussion and conclusions matches the scope of what was actually studied.
what_triggers_block: Claims of relevance, transferability, or implication that extend materially beyond the evidence presented — for example generalising to other institutions, populations, settings, or disciplines without supporting data or an argued warrant — or core constructs left so undefined that an adjacent-field reader cannot determine what was measured and therefore cannot evaluate the central claim at all.
what_triggers_warn: Localised accessibility defects that raise the cost of entry without disabling evaluation, such as undefined jargon that context eventually resolves, background or setting detail that must be inferred, implications for adjacent fields gestured at but underdeveloped, or scope language that is loose in places while the substantive claims remain within the evidence.
