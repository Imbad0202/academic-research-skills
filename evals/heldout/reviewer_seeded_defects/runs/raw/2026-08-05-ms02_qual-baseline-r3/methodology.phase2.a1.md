## Review Body

This manuscript is presented as a QUAL-priority mixed-methods study of QA implementation across three universities, and the underlying question — how administrators, positioned between accreditor and academic, construct quality culture — is a worthwhile one that the literature review situates competently. My assessment concerns whether the reported evidentiary chain can carry what the paper asserts. It cannot, in its present form, and the failures are not peripheral: they sit on the primary strand's analysis, on the sample's basic arithmetic, on the survey instrument's existence as a documented object, and on the inferential claim that the Discussion carries forward.

The central methodological problem is traceability. A reader cannot reconstruct how twelve (or fourteen) transcripts became three themes. Section 3.4 devotes three sentences to the analysis of the study's primary data: no named analytic approach, no coder count, no agreement or consensus procedure, no saturation criterion, no audit trail, no reflexivity or positionality statement, and no codebook or protocol available anywhere. Because the three themes *are* the contribution, an undocumented route to them means the contribution itself is unverifiable. Section 4.3 then compounds this: the paper claims balanced representation capturing "the full range" of administrator views, and in the very next sentence discloses that dissenting participants were dropped because they fell outside the three-theme structure. That is negative-case suppression justified by the analytic frame those cases should have been used to test. The two sentences cannot both stand, and the second one voids the first.

The quantitative strand is doing decorative rather than analytic work, and it is under-reported to a degree that makes even the decoration uninterpretable. M=3.9 (SD=0.6) appears in the abstract without a scale range, item wording, item count, instrument provenance, or any reliability or validity evidence; a mean without its scale carries no information. The §4.4 significance claim is worse: no named test, no test statistic, no degrees of freedom, no effect size, no confidence interval, no assumption check, on groups of nine and eleven drawn from a convenience sample, with twenty-eight of the forty-eight respondents and one of the three institutions entirely absent from the comparison. "Points to institutional type as a possible moderator" is not licensed by that contrast under any reading. Nor are the strands integrated: there is no joint display, no stated integration procedure, and no meta-inference logic, and §4.4 asserts corroboration one sentence before conceding that the survey ran counter to what the interviews had led the authors to expect. Adjacency is not mixing.

I considered whether D1 warrants a fatal block and concluded it does not. Nothing in the manuscript establishes fabrication or an ethics breach that would invalidate the dataset irrespective of revision. The fourteen/twelve discrepancy is more plausibly a reporting error than an impossibility; the deductive-disclosure problem in §3.5/§4.1 is a serious and mandatory pre-publication fix rather than an irreversible one; the n=9/n=11 figures are arithmetically consistent with an n=48 frame. Every defect below is, in principle, addressable — though I note that a re-analysis honestly incorporating the excluded dissenting cases may not return the same three themes, which is precisely why the exclusion cannot be waved through.

On D3, my concern from the methods seat is the inference chain rather than rhetoric. The Discussion's second headline finding generalises from three purposively recruited institutions in one national system to "universities across the sector" and "administrators everywhere," and does so with the verb "demonstrate." No design element supports that move: there is no sampling frame beyond three sites, no claim to theoretical or statistical representativeness, and the paper's own §4.3 admission that dissenting views were removed cuts directly against any claim to have captured the range even within the three sites. A second coherence defect is internal to the manuscript: §2 characterises Delacroix (2018) as arguing that consultation is *not* sufficient evidence of quality culture, while §5 enlists the same source as recommending consultation as the central mechanism of internalisation, and the third practical recommendation rests on that reading. Whether §5 or §2 misreads the source externally is a domain-fidelity question I leave to the domain reviewer; the internal contradiction is visible without leaving the manuscript, and one of the two passages must go.

I have not assessed whether the three themes are substantively novel against the QA literature, nor the accuracy of the paper's characterisations of cited work beyond the internal inconsistency just noted. Those belong to the domain seat.

### S1: Design priority is stated explicitly rather than left implicit

The manuscript names which strand is primary and what function the second serves. Many mixed-methods submissions leave priority and sequencing to be inferred; stating it is the precondition for judging integration quality, even though the integration itself is absent here.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.1 "The qualitative component was primary; the survey served to contextualize the interview findings"

### S2: Deliberate site heterogeneity within a single system

Selecting one large public research university, one mid-sized public teaching-focused university, and one private university within a single national regulatory system is a defensible maximum-variation logic that holds the regulatory environment constant while varying institutional type. Had the analysis been reported properly, this design choice would have supported exactly the institutional-type contrast §4.4 attempts.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.2 "one large public research university, one mid-sized public teaching-focused university, and one private university"

### S3: Basic interview data-collection parameters are reported

Mode, duration range, recording, and transcription are stated. This is a minimum bar, but it is met, and it makes the interview corpus's basic properties assessable in a way the coding procedure is not.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.3 "lasting between 45 and 70 minutes, and were audio-recorded and transcribed"

### W1: The interview sample size is reported inconsistently and cannot be reconciled

The abstract reports fourteen administrators interviewed in depth; §3.2 reports twelve, with the figure reinforced by the parenthetical n=12. Nothing in the manuscript reconciles the two — there is no mention of attrition, withdrawn consent, excluded transcripts, or pilot interviews. A reader therefore cannot establish the denominator of the primary strand, which is the base against which every claim about theme prevalence ("across institutions," "recurred across every interview," "a minority of participants") must be read. The authors must state which figure is correct, explain the discrepancy, and confirm that no reported pattern changes under the corrected N.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract and §3.2, "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth" versus "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct textual comparison requiring no specialist judgement

### W2: The §4.4 significance claim is not reportable as written and cannot support the moderator inference

"A statistically significant difference (p<.05)" is reported with no named test, no test statistic, no degrees of freedom, no effect size, no confidence interval, and no assessment of the distributional assumptions the test would require, on groups of nine and eleven. At those sizes the choice between a parametric and a rank-based test is consequential and the normality assumption is untestable in practice, so the unnamed procedure is not a formality — it determines whether the p-value means anything. The claim is then escalated into "points to institutional type as a possible moderator," a construct-level inference drawn from a twenty-person contrast within a convenience sample with no randomisation, no covariate adjustment, and no account of clustering within institution. Reporting the missing statistics would make the test auditable but would not, on my reading, rescue the moderator claim; the design cannot license it. The authors should either report the analysis in full and confine the language to a descriptive difference within these two subsamples, or remove the claim.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "we found a statistically significant difference (p<.05) in perceived quality culture"
**Confidence**: 5 — standard inferential reporting requirements in educational measurement

### W3: The survey instrument is absent in every respect that would make M=3.9 interpretable

The mean and standard deviation are carried into the abstract as a substantive finding, yet the manuscript never states the response scale range, reproduces or summarises any item, gives the item count, indicates whether the instrument was original or adapted from a published measure, or reports any internal consistency, factor structure, or validity evidence. M=3.9 on a five-point scale and M=3.9 on a seven-point scale support opposite conclusions, and "moderately positive institutional quality culture" is an interpretation the paper cannot license without demonstrating that the items measure the construct named. This is not a one-line fix: it requires the authors to supply the instrument, its provenance, and reliability evidence, or to withdraw the construct-level interpretation.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.3 and §4.4 — expected the survey response scale range, item wording, item count, instrument provenance, and reliability or validity evidence; checked §3.1, §3.2, §3.3, §3.4, §4.4, Abstract, References
**Confidence**: 5 — measurement reporting standards are unambiguous on this point

### W4: The thematic analysis is undocumented, so the study's primary finding is untraceable

Section 3.4 states only that transcripts "were analyzed thematically" and that coding "proceeded iteratively... until a stable structure was reached." No analytic tradition is named — reflexive thematic analysis, codebook TA, framework analysis, and IPA impose different and partly incompatible obligations, and "stable structure" is not a saturation criterion. The manuscript does not say how many people coded, whether any form of agreement, consensus, or peer debriefing was used, how codes were aggregated into the three themes, whether an audit trail exists, or what the researchers' relationship to the three institutions was. Given that the qualitative strand is designated primary and the three themes constitute the paper's contribution, the absence of any procedural account means the central result rests on a procedure whose validity cannot be assessed at all. A full methods rewrite plus a codebook or coding-frame appendix is the minimum remedy.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.4 — expected a named analytic framework, coder count, coding agreement or consensus procedure, saturation criterion, audit trail, and reflexivity or positionality statement; checked §3.1, §3.4, §3.5, §4.1, §4.2, §4.3
**Confidence**: 5 — routine qualitative rigour criteria applied to a QUAL-priority design

### W5: Disconfirming cases were excluded for falling outside the analytic frame, while the paper claims full representation

Section 4.3 asserts balanced representation capturing the full range of administrator views, then states that participants with more skeptical or dissenting views about the value of the QA enterprise "were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure." Two things are wrong here and they compound. First, the two claims are mutually exclusive: a corpus from which dissenters have been removed does not capture the full range. Second, and more seriously, the stated exclusion criterion is the analytic structure itself — the cases that would have tested whether three themes adequately organise the data were removed *because* they did not fit those three themes. That is circular, and it means the three-theme structure has never been exposed to the evidence most capable of falsifying it. "For space" is not an adequate justification for dropping negative cases in a study whose entire warrant is interpretive. To the authors' credit the exclusion was disclosed rather than silently performed, which is what makes it assessable; but disclosure does not repair it. The dissenting accounts must be reintegrated and the theme structure re-derived, with the balance claim deleted regardless of what re-analysis yields.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3, "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views" and "these were excluded for space"
**Confidence**: 5 — negative-case analysis is a core trustworthiness criterion

### W6: The anonymisation claim is inaccurate; attributions permit deductive disclosure

Section 3.5 states that all data were fully anonymized so that no individual could be identified in reported findings. Section 4.1 then attributes one quotation to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and another to "the associate dean for quality at the public research university." In a three-institution design where §3.2 already discloses that exactly one site is private and exactly one is a public research university, role plus institution type plus a superlative descriptor identifies a specific post-holder to anyone with sector knowledge — and the quoted content ("It's theater, and everyone knows it's theater") is professionally sensitive. The ethics statement as written is therefore false, which is a distinct problem from the disclosure risk itself: participants consented on a representation the manuscript does not honour. The attributions must be reduced to non-identifying descriptors, the ethics statement corrected to describe what was actually done, and the authors should confirm whether participants reviewed their quotations as attributed.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 and §4.1, "fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified" and "the quality director of the largest private university in the region"
**Confidence**: 5 — deductive disclosure risk in small-site qualitative research is a standard review check

### W7: The two strands are adjacent, not mixed, and the paper contradicts itself about what the survey showed

Nothing in the manuscript constitutes integration in the mixed-methods sense: there is no joint display, no stated point of interface, no procedure for relating survey responses to interview accounts, and no meta-inference drawing on both strands. The survey and interview samples are different populations (mid-level staff versus senior administrators) with no linking design, which makes any inference across them a comparison of unlike things unless the authors argue otherwise — and they do not. The paper also states in consecutive sentences that the survey "corroborated the qualitative picture" and that the interview accounts "had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture." Those describe a disconfirmation, not a corroboration; the second sentence is the honest one, and the framing sentence should be rewritten to say so. Either the authors add a genuine integration procedure with a joint display, or the paper should stop describing itself as mixed methods.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4, "The survey corroborated the qualitative picture" and "the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture"
**Confidence**: 5 — routine integration-quality review for QUAL→quan designs

### W8: The Discussion generalises to the whole sector from a design that cannot support it

The second of the Discussion's three headline findings states that the results "demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment," invites the reader to treat the study as "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture," and asserts that "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension." The design is three purposively recruited sites in one national system, with participants routed through institutional QA offices — a recruitment channel that plausibly selects for administrators invested in QA. There is no representativeness claim, no theoretical-sampling argument, no cross-system comparison, and, per W5, not even a within-site claim to range that survives. "Demonstrate" is the wrong verb for any of it. This claim, uncorrected, is simply false as stated, and it is the specific inference on which I score D3 as blocked. The remedy is straightforward — scope the claim to the three institutions and offer sector-level applicability as a hypothesis, which the Conclusion's call for wider testing already implies.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "these findings demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment rather than a managerial output"
**Confidence**: 5 — inference-to-design matching is the core of my competence here

### W9: A protocol contradiction is used to underwrite an unearned confidence claim

Section 3.1 specifies semi-structured interviews; §4.1 states that the themes "emerged systematically from the structured protocol." These describe different instruments, and the difference matters, because a semi-structured protocol admits interviewer-driven variation that a structured one does not. The contradiction is then put to work: the sentence infers from protocol structure that the pattern "was not an artifact of any single institution's local circumstances." That inference does not follow. Protocol standardisation controls for interviewer variation, not for site effects; ruling out an institution-specific artifact would require reporting how themes distributed across the three sites, which the manuscript never does. Either the protocol description is wrong, or §4.1 is; and in either case the artifact claim needs per-site evidence or deletion.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.1 and §3.1, "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol, giving us confidence that the pattern was not an artifact" versus "semi-structured interviews"
**Confidence**: 4 — the contradiction is textual; the artifact inference is a standard qualitative-validity judgement

### W10: The survey sample is undocumented and its denominators do not add up

Forty-eight respondents are reported, yet the only analysis reported involves twenty of them, leaving twenty-eight unaccounted for with no explanation. There is no sampling frame for the survey (who was eligible, how they were reached), no response rate, no per-institution respondent counts, and no statement of why the private university is absent from the institutional-type comparison — an omission that matters doubly because "institutional type" is the very moderator the paper proposes. Without denominators and a response rate, non-response bias cannot be assessed, and the reader cannot tell whether n=9 and n=11 are complete institutional subsamples or arbitrary subsets. A per-institution breakdown reconciling to 48, plus a response rate and eligibility description, is required.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.2, §3.3 and §4.4 — expected a survey sampling frame, response rate, and per-institution respondent counts reconciling n=48 with the n=9 and n=11 subgroups; checked §3.2, §3.3, §4.4, Abstract
**Confidence**: 5 — basic survey reporting arithmetic

### W11: No reproducibility affordances are offered

The manuscript provides no data or materials availability statement of any kind. The interview protocol, the coding frame or codebook, and the survey instrument are all absent, and no repository, appendix, or availability-on-request statement is given. Transcripts themselves may reasonably be unshareable on confidentiality grounds, but the instruments and codebook are not, and their availability is what would allow a reader to judge — or a later researcher to extend — the analysis. Supplying them as appendices would also partly mitigate W3 and W4.

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: Methods and end matter — expected a data or materials availability statement covering the interview protocol, codebook, and survey instrument; checked §3.1 to §3.5, §5, §6, References
**Confidence**: 4 — venue-dependent expectation, but standard in the mixed-methods literature

### W12: The priority claim in the Conclusion is unsupported and sits badly with the paper's own disclosures

"The first comprehensive account" asserts both priority and comprehensiveness. Priority is not established anywhere — §2 itself cites Pettersen (2022), Rahman (2020), and Silva and Tan (2021) as working on the quality office and the intermediary role — and comprehensiveness is directly contradicted by §4.3's disclosure that a category of participant views was excluded. Twelve interviews at three sites in one system is a useful contribution; it is not a comprehensive account of anything. Rewording costs the paper nothing.

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §6 "This paper has offered the first comprehensive account of how university administrators experience and enact QA implementation"
**Confidence**: 4 — internal comparison against the manuscript's own literature review and disclosures

### W13: The manuscript characterises Delacroix (2018) in two incompatible ways, and a recommendation rests on the second

Section 2 presents Delacroix as cautioning that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual and as arguing against treating consultation as evidence of a healthy quality culture. Section 5 then aligns the paper's findings with Delacroix as recommending that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building quality culture, and builds the third practical recommendation on that reading. These are opposite positions attributed to one source within one manuscript, and the Discussion's design recommendation inherits whichever reading is wrong. I flag this as an internal-consistency defect visible without leaving the text; whether §2 or §5 misrepresents the source externally is a question for the domain reviewer, but one of the two passages must be corrected regardless, and if §2 is the accurate reading then the third recommendation loses its stated warrant entirely.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 and §5, "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual" versus "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture"
**Confidence**: 4 — internal textual contradiction is clear; external source fidelity deferred to the domain seat
