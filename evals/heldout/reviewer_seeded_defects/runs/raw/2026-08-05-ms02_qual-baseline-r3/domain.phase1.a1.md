## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data collection, analytic procedures, and reporting of results would survive scrutiny from a competent reviewer in the manuscript's own field, and whether enough procedural detail is disclosed that another researcher could in principle repeat the work. Read from a domain-accuracy vantage point, my interest in it is indirect: methodological choices constrain what claims the domain evidence can legitimately support, so I note where a design would make a domain claim unsupportable, but the ownership of the judgement sits with the methodology reviewer and I will not score it.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role, mine).** This is my dimension. It asks whether the substantive claims the manuscript makes are consistent with the current state of evidence in higher education policy and quality assurance; whether the literature and prior scholarship it invokes are represented faithfully rather than distorted, over-credited, or straw-manned; and whether field-specific vocabulary, frameworks, institutional actors, regulatory instruments, and reported findings are used correctly. Errors here include misdefined technical terms, misattributed positions, conflation of distinct concepts or distinct governance regimes, citation of superseded evidence as current, and assertions of fact about the field that are simply wrong. Because it is mandatory, a sufficiently severe defect on this dimension can drive the editorial outcome on its own.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, da/methodology roles).** This dimension covers whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together internally, whether the evidence actually adduced does the work the argument asks of it, and whether reasoning defects are severe enough to undermine the core claim. It overlaps my remit at the boundary where a coherence failure is really a domain-knowledge failure, but the dimension is owned by the devil's-advocate reviewer and is outside my eligible scope, so I will describe rather than score it.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, perspective role).** This dimension asks whether the framing, key definitions, and stated implications are intelligible and useful to readers in adjacent fields, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplinary boundaries is actually substantiated rather than gestured at. From a domain standpoint, the relevant tension is that accessible framing sometimes purchases clarity at the cost of precision; but the assessment belongs to the perspective reviewer and I do not score it.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, eic role).** This dimension covers organisation, clarity of exposition, quality and integration of figures and tables, and conformity to the conventions of the target venue. It is the editor-in-chief's to judge. I mention presentation problems only when the wording itself creates a substantive misstatement about the field; otherwise it is out of my scope and unscored.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, eic role).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it advances something original and consequential for that readership. Judging originality does draw on domain knowledge of what is already established, but the dimension is assigned to the editor-in-chief; I will supply domain context if asked and will not issue a score for it.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether field-specific terminology (quality assurance, quality culture, accreditation, evaluation, institutional research, governance and accountability constructs) is defined and used as the higher education policy literature uses it; whether cited prior work, frameworks, and regulatory or agency arrangements are described accurately and attributed to the right sources; whether stated empirical findings about administrators, institutions, or national QA systems match what the evidence base actually shows; and whether normative or causal claims about quality culture are hedged in proportion to the strength of existing domain evidence.
what_triggers_block: One or more central substantive claims rest on a demonstrable factual error, a misdefined core construct, or a material misrepresentation of prior work, such that a specialist reader would reject the manuscript's substantive contribution unless the error is corrected and the surrounding argument rebuilt.
what_triggers_warn: Localised domain imprecision that does not destabilise the central claim — for example loose or inconsistent use of a secondary term, conflation of related-but-distinct QA concepts in passing, an over-general characterisation of the literature, stale or thin sourcing for a background assertion, or an unhedged claim that overshoots the cited evidence.
what_triggers_fatal: Pervasive domain incompetence or unsalvageable misrepresentation — core constructs of the field used incorrectly throughout, findings or agency arrangements asserted that are contradicted by the established record, or prior work reported in a way no revision could repair without discarding the manuscript's substantive premise.
