## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data handling, analytic procedures, and reporting would survive a hostile methodologist's reading rather than a charitable one. An adversarial reading treats every unstated design choice as a potential confound: undisclosed sampling frames, unreported response or attrition rates, analytic decisions made after seeing the data, statistics reported without dispersion or uncertainty, and an absence of any affordance (instrument, coding scheme, protocol, data availability statement) that would let another investigator reproduce or contest the result. For qualitative or mixed designs, the same hostility applies to trustworthiness machinery — how informants were recruited, how coding was checked, how negative cases were handled. The bar is the field's peer-review norm, not an aspirational ideal, but "the field is loose about this" is not a defence when the specific inferential weight placed on a claim exceeds what the design can bear.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by the domain role).** Here the challenge is whether the manuscript's substantive claims are true to the current state of evidence in its field, whether prior work is represented as its authors would recognise it, and whether domain-specific vocabulary, frameworks, instruments, and reported results are used correctly. Adversarially, the failure modes are misattributed findings, citations that gesture at a literature without supporting the specific proposition attached to them, terminology used in a locally invented sense that collides with its established meaning, stale evidence presented as current, and straw-man characterisations of competing positions that make the paper's own contribution look larger than it is. A single decorative citation error is an irritant; a chain of claims resting on misread sources is a structural defect.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by me as devil's advocate).** This is the dimension I own and it is where I am expected to be least generous. The question is not whether the paper is persuasive but whether its central thesis survives being attacked at its joints. I test whether the stated research question, the evidence actually presented, and the conclusions drawn are the same object throughout, or whether the claim quietly inflates between abstract, results, and discussion. I look for load-bearing inferential leaps: correlational or perceptual evidence carrying causal conclusions, a sample or setting whose scope is silently generalised, definitions that shift between sections, conclusions that would follow equally well from the opposite data, unfalsifiable framings, circularity where the conclusion is embedded in the construct's definition, and disconfirming evidence that is acknowledged rhetorically but never allowed to bite. Internal contradiction between sections, and evidence that is presented but never actually connected to the claim it is meant to support, are the core targets.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by the perspective role).** This dimension interrogates whether a competent reader from an adjacent field can enter the paper without the author's tacit background. Adversarially: are constructs defined or merely named; are field-internal acronyms and instruments explained; are the stated implications for other disciplines actually substantiated by anything in the paper, or are they gestural claims of breadth appended to a narrow result? Overreach across disciplinary boundaries — asserting relevance to policy, practice, or neighbouring literatures without evidence connecting to them — counts against this dimension just as much as impenetrable in-group jargon does.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the EiC role).** This dimension covers organisation, expositional clarity, the quality and self-sufficiency of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's conventions. Read adversarially, the concern is whether structural problems are cosmetic or whether disorganisation is concealing substantive weakness: results that appear only in a discussion section, tables whose numbers cannot be reconciled with the text, missing methods detail dressed as concision, absent or uninformative captions, and section proportions that reveal the paper has less evidence than argument. It is the lowest-priority dimension, so it should not be inflated into a substantive objection, but it also should not be waved through when the writing is what makes the reasoning unauditable.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the EiC role).** This asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue at all and whether it adds something its readership does not already have. The adversarial test is the "so what, and who didn't already know this" test: does the paper restate established findings in new wording, is the increment over the closest existing work articulated and defended, is the scale of the contribution proportionate to the claim of novelty, and would this venue's readers be the right audience or is the fit asserted rather than demonstrated? Because this is a mandatory dimension, a paper that is competent but derivative, or solid but addressed to the wrong readership, fails here regardless of how well it performs elsewhere.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether the research question, the evidence actually presented, and the conclusions asserted in abstract, results, and discussion refer to the same claim at the same strength and scope; whether each load-bearing claim has evidence explicitly connected to it; whether key constructs hold a stable definition throughout; whether the inference type (descriptive, associational, causal, prescriptive) matches the evidence type; and whether disconfirming cases, rival explanations, and stated limitations are engaged substantively rather than named and abandoned.
what_triggers_block: A load-bearing inferential defect that the central thesis cannot survive — e.g. a causal or prescriptive conclusion resting on evidence that can only support description or association, a direct contradiction between two sections that cannot both be true, a conclusion whose scope substantially exceeds the population, setting, or timeframe the evidence covers, circular reasoning in which the conclusion is embedded in the definition of the construct, or a headline claim for which no supporting evidence is presented anywhere in the manuscript.
what_triggers_warn: Localised coherence defects that weaken but do not void the thesis — e.g. hedged claims in the results that become unhedged in the abstract or discussion, a construct whose definition drifts between sections without changing the main conclusion, a secondary or ancillary claim asserted without support, rival explanations acknowledged only in a limitations paragraph without being tested against the data, or evidence presented whose relevance to the claim is left for the reader to infer.
what_triggers_fatal: The argument is structurally unsalvageable within the paper's own evidence base — the central thesis is internally self-refuting or unfalsifiable as stated, or the stated conclusion is contradicted by the manuscript's own reported evidence, such that no revision short of a different study or a different thesis could repair it.
