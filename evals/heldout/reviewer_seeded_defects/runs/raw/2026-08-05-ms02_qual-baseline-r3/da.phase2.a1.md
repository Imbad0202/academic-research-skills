## Review Body

I read this manuscript looking for the joints at which its argument could be pulled apart, and the joints failed in more than one place. The central thesis — that quality culture is a negotiated accomplishment rather than a managerial output — is a defensible and even attractive claim, and there is enough interview material here to make it plausible. That is precisely why the coherence failures matter: they are not decoration on a weak idea, they are load-bearing damage to a workable one.

The most consequential defect is that the manuscript cannot keep its own evidence base fixed. The abstract reports fourteen interviewees; Methods reports twelve, twice, once in words and once in notation. A reader cannot determine what data exist. This is not a formatting slip in isolation, because §4.3 discloses that "a minority of participants expressed more skeptical or dissenting views" which "were excluded." Two administrators disappear between abstract and methods, and the paper independently admits removing dissenting participants. I do not assert that these are the same two people, and the manuscript gives no way to check — but that is the problem. The reader is left to reconcile a discrepant N against an admitted post-hoc exclusion of the participants least favourable to the paper's thesis, with no accounting of either.

The exclusion passage is self-refuting on its own terms and would be a serious finding even if the N matched. Within two consecutive sentences the paper claims it "achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views" and then states that dissenting views were removed because "they fell outside the three-theme structure that organized our analysis." Both cannot be true. Worse, the stated exclusion criterion is circular: the three-theme structure is presented in the abstract as an output of analysis ("Analysis surfaced three dominant themes") and here functions as the gatekeeping criterion for which data are analysed. A thematic structure that determines its own inputs cannot be disconfirmed by those inputs. This converts the paper's most emphatic empirical claim — the pervasiveness of ritual compliance — into an artefact of case selection. The authors will likely respond that space constrained them; the answer is that a negative case does not consume more space than a confirming quotation, and the paper prints five confirming quotations.

The Discussion then commits a scope violation the design cannot support. Twelve (or fourteen) administrators at three institutions in one national system are said to "demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment," to license "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture," and to establish that "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension." No sampling logic, theoretical saturation argument, or comparative case rationale is offered that would carry a purposive three-site qualitative study to universal quantification over a global population. The Conclusion's own final sentence concedes the point by calling for research "across a wider range of institutional types and national systems" — an admission that the generalisation just asserted has not been earned. The paper both makes and retracts the claim, and it is the unretracted version that appears in the discussion's second numbered finding.

The third discussion point inverts a source the paper itself correctly summarised sixty lines earlier. §2 reports that Delacroix (2018) "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual, and argues *against* treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of a healthy quality culture," a reading confirmed by the article's own title and by the annotation in the reference list. §5 then aligns the paper's design recommendation with "Delacroix (2018), who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture." The paper's only cited warrant for its practical recommendation is a source that argues the opposite of what it is said to argue — and the paper knows this, having stated the correct reading itself. This is not a citation blemish; the recommendation loses its support entirely, and the inversion suppresses the one literature-based objection that would most directly threaten the paper's participation-centred prescription.

There is also an ethics-adjacent contradiction that the coherence lens surfaces. §3.5 asserts that "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified in reported findings." §4.1 then attributes quotations to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and "the associate dean for quality at the public research university." With three institutions individuated by type and size, these are single-occupant roles. The anonymisation claim and the reporting practice cannot both stand, and the disclosure is not repaired by the assurance that precedes it.

Two further inferential problems deserve naming. First, the warrant offered for theme robustness — that themes "emerged systematically from the structured protocol, giving us confidence that the pattern was not an artifact" — is a non sequitur, and it contradicts §3.1's "semi-structured interviews." A common instrument administered across sites makes convergence *more* likely as an instrumentation artefact, not less; protocol uniformity is the wrong evidence for the conclusion drawn from it. Second, the survey strand is described as corroborating in one sentence and as disconfirming expectation in the next, and as "a note of complication" in §5. Corroboration and surprise are different epistemic relations and the paper never chooses. The subgroup contrast compounds this: a significance claim on n=9 versus n=11 with no named test, no group means, no effect size, twenty-eight of forty-eight respondents unaccounted for, and institutional type confounded with institution at one case per type. The claim that institutional type is "a possible moderator" cannot be distinguished from the claim that these two particular universities differ.

Finally, the novelty claim contradicts the paper's own literature review. §6 calls this "the first comprehensive account" of administrator experience of QA, while §2 credits Pettersen (2022) and Rahman (2020) with turning "attention toward the quality office itself," on distributed leadership and professional identity — the paper's own second and third themes — and credits Silva and Tan (2021) with the intermediary position. The differentiating move available to the authors (grounding in administrators' own words) is stated in §2 and then abandoned in favour of a priority claim the review has already refuted.

What would repair this: reconcile N and disclose every exclusion with reasons; reinstate and analyse the dissenting cases as negative cases, which would strengthen rather than weaken the negotiation thesis; rescope every generalisation to three institutions in one system; correct the Delacroix reading and rebuild the recommendation on evidence the paper actually has; remove or properly de-identify the role-plus-institution attributions; and either report the survey to a standard that supports inference or demote it explicitly to context. The core thesis survives all of this, which is why I score D3 as a repairable rather than fatal block.
