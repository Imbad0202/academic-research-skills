## Review Body

I read this manuscript citation by citation against the primary quality-assurance and quality-culture literature. My judgement on domain accuracy is that the manuscript cannot go forward in its present form, but that the defects are repairable — in one case the repair would actually make the paper more interesting than it currently is.

The decisive problem is the treatment of Delacroix (2018). Section 2 represents him correctly and with unusual care: he cautions that participatory rhetoric itself becomes a compliance ritual and argues against reading stakeholder consultation as evidence of a healthy quality culture. The reference-list annotation confirms this reading, and the article's title — "Against the participation ritual" — leaves no room for ambiguity. Section 5 then recruits him for the opposite position, claiming the findings "align with Delacroix (2018), who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism." This is not a typo or a slip of attribution; it is the load-bearing citation for the paper's third headline implication, and the implication as written has no support in the source it names.

Correcting it does more damage than the authors may expect. Strip Delacroix out and the third implication reduces to "if quality culture is cultivated rather than mandated, processes that maximize genuine participation should be favoured" — an assertion with no cited warrant and no evidence in Section 4. Worse, Delacroix's actual argument is a live challenge to this study that the study never answers. Section 4.2 reports administrators who spend most of their energy on persuasion and coalition-building; Section 4.1 reports the same administrators describing the whole enterprise as theatre. Delacroix's thesis predicts precisely that configuration: consultation and buy-in work become one more artifact to be documented for the panel. The manuscript has collected data that bear directly on whether its own participatory activity is internalization or performance, and it has not asked the question. Rebuilt honestly, Section 5 would engage Delacroix as an unresolved counterargument rather than a false ally. That is a genuine revision, not a citation fix.

The second domain problem is the literature the manuscript does not engage at all. There is no Harvey and Green on the multiple meanings of quality, no Harvey and Stensaker quality-culture typology, no Newton on ritual compliance and academic responses to review, no EUA Quality Culture Project, and no ESG 2015 or named national equivalent. This matters because Section 4.1 restates, as a finding, a claim this literature established roughly two decades ago. The manuscript's own interview material makes the debt audible: an administrator describes "a whole season of the year given over to feeding the machine," which is within a word of the title under which the ritual-compliance argument entered this field. Without the canon on the page, a reader cannot tell whether Section 4.1 is a contribution or a rediscovery, and Section 6's claim to "the first comprehensive account" is contradicted by three works the manuscript itself cites (Pettersen, 2022; Rahman, 2020; Silva & Tan, 2021).

Third, the core construct is never operationalized. "Quality culture" is glossed once in Section 1 as shared values, practices and commitments, and thereafter the text moves without marking boundaries between quality culture, quality assurance, compliance, and staff sentiment. The survey then reports a mean of 3.9 as "institutional quality culture" with no instrument described, no items, no dimensionality, and no account of how a perceptual average becomes a property of a culture. In a field where the definitional problem is the oldest live controversy, an undefined central construct measured by an undescribed instrument is a substantive defect, not a presentational one.

Fourth, Section 5 asserts what three institutions in one national system cannot support — that "universities across the sector" treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment and that "administrators everywhere" face the same tension. The Conclusion's closing sentence concedes exactly this point, calling for tests across wider institutional types and national systems. The Discussion overrides the Conclusion, and the domain reader is left with a sector-level factual claim the design cannot license.

Fifth, and connected: the national system is never named, and neither is its QA regime. Whether external review is voluntary or mandatory, programme-level or institution-level, and whether funding or registration consequences attach are not background details in this argument. The paper's own theoretical frame — steering at a distance, anticipation of review reorganizing internal priorities — makes regime design the operative variable. Comparative work in this area shows ritualism varying sharply with regime consequence; suppressing the regime makes the findings uninterpretable as evidence about how external requirement becomes internal meaning.

Two matters outside my dimension I flag without scoring, for the methodology seat: the Abstract reports fourteen interviewees while Section 3.2 reports twelve, and the anonymization claim in Section 3.5 is contradicted by identifying descriptors attached to quotations in Section 4.1 given a three-institution sample.

### S1: Section 2 states the participation-versus-performance tension accurately and non-trivially

The literature review's rendering of the central controversy in quality-culture scholarship is precise and correctly attributed. The paper sets Nakamura's trust-as-infrastructure position against Delacroix's scepticism and captures the latter's mechanism exactly — elaborate consultation coexisting with absent ownership of standards. This paragraph is the strongest domain writing in the manuscript and is the ground on which a corrected Discussion could be rebuilt.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 "an institution can hold elaborate consultations and still lack any genuine ownership of standards"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct comparison with the cited source's stated thesis and the manuscript's own annotation.

### S2: The identity-work theme is a real domain contribution

Section 4.3's move — administrators locating professional identity in the unresolved gap between compliance and meaning, rather than resolving it — extends Rahman (2020) on vocation among quality professionals rather than restating it. This is the one theme that is not already settled in the quality-culture canon, and it is the part of the paper worth defending.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "treating the gap itself as the space in which their professional identity was located"
**Confidence**: 4 — familiarity with the quality-professional identity literature; limited by the excluded-case problem noted at W7.

### S3: The governance-technology strand is rendered correctly

Section 2's account of steering at a distance is faithful to how the concept functions in higher education policy scholarship: influence operating through anticipation rather than intervention, reshaping staffing and documentation before any panel arrives. The concept is not stretched or misapplied.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 "the mere anticipation of external review reorganizes internal priorities, staffing, and documentation practices"
**Confidence**: 4 — comparative reading of the accountability-regime literature.

### W1: Delacroix (2018) is cited in §5 for the position his paper exists to refute

Section 2 and the reference annotation both state that Delacroix argues against treating stakeholder consultation as evidence of quality culture. Section 5 states that he "recommends" consultation as "the central mechanism" for building it, and rests the paper's third implication on that reversal. The manuscript therefore contradicts itself about a source within its own pages, and the only external warrant offered for its design recommendation points the other way. Correcting the attribution removes the support for the third implication entirely; engaging Delacroix's real argument would additionally require the paper to address whether its Section 4.2 persuasion work is internalization or documented performance — a question the data could speak to and the manuscript never poses.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "our findings align with Delacroix (2018), who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism" vs §2 "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual"
**Confidence**: 5 — the misattribution is verifiable against the manuscript's own §2 and reference annotation, without external sources.

### W2: No engagement with foundational quality-culture scholarship, leaving §4.1 undecidable between finding and rediscovery

The manuscript's third literature strand is described as "still emergent" and supported by two references. The quality-culture literature is not emergent; it has a definitional canon, an established typology, a body of work on ritual compliance and academic response, a major multi-year European project, and a standards framework that most national systems reference. None appears. Because Section 4.1's ritual-compliance theme is a restatement of a long-settled claim, the omission is not a completeness quibble: it determines whether the paper's most prominent finding is additive at all, and it prevents any reader from locating the study's contribution relative to what is known.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §2 Literature Review and the reference list — expected engagement with foundational quality-culture scholarship (Harvey and Green on the multiple meanings of quality, the Harvey and Stensaker quality-culture typology, Newton on ritual compliance, the EUA Quality Culture Project, ESG 2015 or a named national framework); checked §1, §2, §4.1, §5, §6, and all twelve reference entries
**Confidence**: 5 — comparative QA scholarship is my primary field and the omitted works are canonical.

### W3: The central construct is never operationalized, and a survey mean is read as a cultural property

"Quality culture" carries the paper's title, research question, and headline number, yet receives no operational definition. The text slides between quality culture, quality assurance, compliance, and staff sentiment without marking the boundaries, so the claim that ritualism "coexists with a broadly favorable staff sentiment" cannot be evaluated — it may be a finding about culture, about climate, or about respondent acquiescence. The survey is described only as capturing "perceptions of institutional quality culture"; no items, scale provenance, response format, or dimensionality is given, and a mean of 3.9 is then reported as the institutions' quality culture. In a field whose foundational dispute concerns what quality means, this is a substantive error rather than an omission of detail.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 and Abstract "Survey results indicated a moderately positive institutional quality culture (M=3.9, SD=0.6)"
**Confidence**: 5 — construct definition and QA instrumentation are within my direct expertise.

### W4: §5 asserts sector-wide and cross-national facts that three institutions in one system cannot support

The Discussion states that the findings "demonstrate" how universities across the sector behave and offers itself as "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture," with administrators "everywhere" facing the same tension. Comparative evidence on QA regimes shows administrator experience varying substantially with regime design and institutional type — indeed the manuscript's own Section 4.4 subgroup contrast points that way. The Conclusion then concedes the point the Discussion overrode, calling for tests across wider institutional types and national systems. As written, the paper makes a factual claim about the sector that its design forecloses and that its own closing paragraph withdraws.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "these findings demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment rather than a managerial output"
**Confidence**: 5 — scope-of-inference judgement in comparative QA research.

### W5: The national system and its QA regime are never identified, though regime design is the operative variable

Section 3.2 gives institutional types but never names the national system or describes its QA arrangements: whether external review is voluntary or mandatory, conducted at programme or institution level, and whether outcomes carry funding, registration, or reputational consequences. The manuscript's argument is precisely about how external requirement translates into internal meaning, and the QA literature it does not cite documents that ritualism, defensive compliance, and documentation load track regime consequence closely. Without this, a domain reader cannot tell whether the study describes QA in general or one regime's characteristic pathology, and cannot judge transferability to any other system.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.2 Participants and setting — expected identification of the national system and its QA regime design, including whether external review is voluntary or mandatory, programme-level or institution-level, and what consequences attach; checked §1, §2, §3.1 to §3.5, §4, §5, and §6
**Confidence**: 5 — comparative internal-QA studies across national systems are my area of published work.

### W6: The priority claim in §6 is contradicted by the manuscript's own reference list

The Conclusion opens by claiming the first comprehensive account of how administrators experience and enact QA implementation. Pettersen (2022) examines distributed leadership in the quality office, Rahman (2020) examines identity and vocation among quality professionals, and Silva and Tan (2021) treat administrators as quality intermediaries — all cited, all on this topic. Section 2 acknowledges Silva and Tan as conceptual rather than grounded, which would support a narrower and defensible claim about administrators' own accounts. Withdrawing the priority claim requires the paper to state positively what it adds relative to these three works and to the canon absent under W2, which is a rewriting of the contribution argument, not a deletion.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §6 "This paper has offered the first comprehensive account of how university administrators experience and enact QA implementation"
**Confidence**: 5 — verifiable against the manuscript's own citations.

### W7: §4.3 claims the full range of views while reporting that dissenting cases were removed

Two sentences apart, the manuscript claims balanced representation capturing the full range of administrator views, and then reports that participants sceptical of the entire QA enterprise were excluded because they fell outside the three-theme structure. The excluded views are exactly the ones that would test the paper's central substantive claim — that administrators do not simply dismiss QA but work to recover meaning from it. Removing the disconfirming cases and then asserting comprehensiveness misstates what the evidence shows about the population, and the recovered-meaning finding cannot be assessed until those accounts are reported.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "A minority of participants expressed more skeptical or dissenting views about the value of the entire QA enterprise; these were excluded for space"
**Confidence**: 4 — the contradiction is on the page; the size and content of the excluded set are not recoverable from the text.

### W8: The anonymization claim in §3.5 is defeated by identifying descriptors in §4.1

Section 3.5 states that all data were fully anonymized so that no individual could be identified. Section 4.1 then attributes quotations to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and "the associate dean for quality at the public research university." With three named institution types in one system, each of these descriptors identifies at most one person to any reader who knows the sector, including that person's colleagues — and the attributed statements are critical of their own institutions. This is a research-ethics defect as well as an internal contradiction, and repairing it requires re-editing the quotations and possibly revisiting consent.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified" against §4.1 "the quality director of the largest private university in the region"
**Confidence**: 4 — identifiability judgement is a matter of sector knowledge rather than certainty about this specific system.

### W9: "Emerged systematically from the structured protocol" misuses the analytic vocabulary and overstates what §3 describes

Section 3.3 describes semi-structured interviews and Section 3.4 describes iterative thematic coding. Section 4.1 then attributes the themes to a "structured protocol" from which they "emerged systematically," and treats this as grounds for confidence that the pattern is not an artifact of local circumstance. Emergence and structured instrumentation are opposed terms in this methodological vocabulary, and neither warrants the cross-institutional inference drawn from it. The sentence should be replaced with an accurate description of the coding procedure.

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.1 "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol"
**Confidence**: 4 — standard usage in qualitative higher education research.

### W10: Brennan and Osei (2020) is placed in the empirical strand despite being a conceptual synthesis

Section 2 groups Brennan and Osei with Montero as documenting how academics perceive QA as burdensome and distrust-laden. The reference list gives the work's subtitle as a conceptual synthesis, and Section 1 cites it for a definition rather than for evidence. A synthesis cannot document perceptions; the strand's empirical support therefore rests on Montero alone. This is a small slip, but in a review whose organizing claim is that the third strand is thin, misplacing a conceptual paper into the empirical strand flatters the coverage.

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 "documenting how academics often perceive it as burdensome, distrust-laden, and disconnected from their disciplinary values (Montero, 2021; Brennan & Osei, 2020)"
**Confidence**: 4 — inferred from the manuscript's own bibliographic description of the source.
