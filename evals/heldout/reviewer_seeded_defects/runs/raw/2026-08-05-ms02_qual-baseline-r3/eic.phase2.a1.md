### D5: writing_and_structure

score: block
trigger: "the reader cannot reliably determine what was done or what is being claimed"

The manuscript reads fluently and follows a conventional arc, so the deficiency is not stylistic. It is that two of the study's load-bearing empirical facts cannot be recovered from the text. The Abstract reports fourteen interviewees and §3.2 reports twelve, with no way to tell which is true. The survey's headline number, M=3.9, arrives with no stated construct, no instrument, no item count, no response-scale range or anchors, and no test named for the subgroup comparison; the manuscript contains no table or figure of any kind against which a reader could check it. A second limb of the same trigger is also met: a reference apparatus whose identifiers do not resolve leaves every literature-based claim untraceable. This is a normal-priority dimension, so no fatality classification attaches, but the presentational repair required here is substantive rather than copy-editorial.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution

score: block
block_class: fatal
trigger: "rests on a premise that removes any basis for original claims"

The subject matter sits squarely inside this journal's remit, and on topic alone the paper would merit review. The fatality is not about scope. Every one of the twelve references carries a DOI in the reserved `10.5555` test prefix, allocated as a single sequential block from `1042001` to `1042012`, and several of the named outlets and the named publisher do not correspond to indexed venues. That prefix is not a live registration space; no source cited here can be located from the information supplied. Because the manuscript's entire claim to originality is constructed as a gap in that literature — Pettersen, Rahman, Silva and Tan are the named foils against which the increment is asserted — an unverifiable literature base removes the premise on which any original claim rests. Compounding this, §5 attributes to Delacroix (2018) the exact position that §2 and the reference list's own annotation say Delacroix argues against, which is the signature of an apparatus not built from the sources it names. Verification against Crossref and Scopus is the first action required, and it is a pre-review integrity matter rather than a revision item; nothing in a revision cycle can make a non-existent source exist. Independently of that, the Conclusion's "first comprehensive account" claim is refuted by the manuscript's own Literature Review, and the one increment a specialist readership would actually gain — the interaction among ritual compliance, distributed leadership, and identity work — is gestured at once and never developed.

## Review Body

I read this first for desk-level signals and then for whether a quality-assurance readership would learn something it does not already know. On the second question the paper is closer than its framing suggests, but on the first it does not clear screening, and the order matters: no assessment of contribution is meaningful until the reference list is verified.

The screening problem is the reference apparatus. Twelve references, twelve DOIs, all in the `10.5555` reserved test range, numbered consecutively. That pattern is not a formatting lapse or an anonymisation artefact; masking real identifiers does not produce sequential test-prefix strings, and it does not invent journal titles. Some named outlets here are real — *Higher Education Quarterly*, *Quality in Higher Education*, *Teaching in Higher Education* — while others are not indexed anywhere I can locate, and all of them carry identifiers that resolve nowhere. The corroborating internal signal is the Delacroix reversal in §5. A paper written from its sources does not describe an author as recommending stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for quality culture three pages after correctly reporting that the same author argues against exactly that, and then repeat the correct reading in an annotation appended to the reference entry. I am not in a position to adjudicate intent, and I do not need to: the editorial action is verification before any substantive assessment proceeds, and if verification fails, this is an integrity referral, not a revision.

On contribution, the honest position is that the paper's stated novelty does not survive its own §2. The Literature Review tells the reader that Pettersen (2022) treats distributed leadership in the quality office and Rahman (2020) treats professional identity among quality professionals, and that Silva and Tan (2021) theorise the intermediary position. Two of the three findings themes then reproduce those two topics, and the third — ritual compliance — is credited in §2 to Iversen, Okonkwo, and Montero. The Conclusion's "first comprehensive account" is therefore not merely immodest; it is contradicted by the manuscript's own evidence of what is already known. What is genuinely unpublished here is the relation among the three: administrators who perceive ritualism, distribute the work through persuasion rather than documentation, and locate their professional identity precisely in the unresolved gap between the two. §4.3's closing sentence about holding the tension open is the paper's best moment and its only real increment. It is one sentence. Developed into an analysis of how the three mechanisms sustain one another, the paper would have a defensible contribution claim for this readership; as submitted, three parallel themes each individually documented elsewhere do not constitute one.

The construct is the other thing a specialist reader will press on. "Quality culture" is the title concept, the object of the survey, and the subject of the Conclusion's central claim, and it is never given a definition the study could test. Across the Abstract, §1, §2, and §4.4 it slides between shared values, internalisation of QA, favourable staff sentiment, and the absence of ritual compliance, and it is nowhere distinguished from quality assurance itself, from accreditation compliance, or from general institutional climate. §4.4 then reports an "institutional quality culture score" as though the construct were settled and operationalised. If the survey measures staff sentiment about QA processes, the paper should say so and drop the culture language from that strand; if it measures something more, the instrument needs to appear.

Two matters fall outside my dimensions, and I want to record them rather than leave them to inference, because they are the places where a journal-fit reading is most likely to under-weight real problems. First, §4.1 quotes participants by role plus institutional descriptor — "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" — inside a study of twelve administrators drawn from three institutions whose types are stated, after §3.5 promises that no individual could be identified. That is a live confidentiality failure against the consent terms, and I do not regard it as a presentational matter. Second, §4.4's significance claim rests on nine versus eleven respondents with no test named and no effect estimate, and §4.3 reports that dissenting participants were excluded from the analysis in the same paragraph that claims the full range of views was captured. Both belong to the methodology and argumentative-coherence seats, and I defer the technical adjudication to them, but I do not want my deference read as a judgement that they are minor. Nor do I read the fourteen-versus-twelve discrepancy as a copy-editing slip: which number is true determines whether two interviews are missing from the account or whether the Abstract overstates the evidence base, and neither reading is trivial.
