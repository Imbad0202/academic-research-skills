## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor.** This dimension asks whether the study's design and execution would survive scrutiny by trained methodologists in higher education policy research: whether the sampling and recruitment of administrator informants are described well enough to judge, whether data collection and coding procedures are auditable, whether any quantitative or qualitative claims are reported with the statistics or evidentiary detail their field expects, and whether enough material is disclosed that another team could repeat the work. From my seat as the reader who cares about travel across field boundaries, methodological rigour matters because an outside reader cannot calibrate trust in an unfamiliar method unless the procedure is stated plainly; but the dimension itself belongs to the methodology reviewer, and I do not score it.

**D2 — domain_accuracy.** This dimension covers factual fidelity to the quality assurance and higher education policy literature: are accreditation concepts, regulatory frameworks, and technical terms used the way the field uses them, is prior scholarship characterised as its authors would recognise, and are empirical results from cited studies reported without distortion. My cross-disciplinary interest here is adjacent but distinct — I care whether terms are *explained* for outsiders, while the domain reviewer owns whether they are *correct*. I do not score this dimension.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence.** This asks whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether stated evidence actually licenses the conclusions drawn, whether the chain from administrator perspectives to claims about institutional quality culture is unbroken, and whether reasoning errors — equivocation, causal overreach, circularity — undercut the core claim. It is owned by the devil's-advocate role with methodology as a second eligible reviewer; I do not score it, though incoherence often surfaces first as a passage that outsiders cannot follow.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance.** This is my dimension. It asks whether a competent reader from an adjacent field — organisational studies, public administration, management, sociology of professions, evaluation research — can enter this manuscript, understand what is being claimed, and see why it matters beyond the quality assurance subfield. Concretely: are field-specific constructs such as quality culture defined rather than assumed; is jargon and acronym use either unpacked or avoided; is the framing positioned against literatures the paper reaches toward rather than only inward; and where the manuscript asserts interdisciplinary reach or transferable implications, are those assertions actually supported by the study's design and evidence rather than gestured at in a closing paragraph. Unsupported borrowing from another discipline's theory counts against this dimension just as much as impenetrable insider prose does.

**D5 — writing_and_structure.** This dimension covers craft: whether sections are ordered so the argument accumulates, whether prose is clear at sentence level, whether tables and figures are legible and self-sufficient, and whether the manuscript observes the target venue's conventions on length, apparatus, and formatting. It overlaps my concerns — bad structure is one common cause of inaccessibility — but it is owned by the editor-in-chief role and I do not assign it a score.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution.** This asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and gives that venue's readership something original and consequential rather than a restatement of settled knowledge. Contribution and reach are related to my dimension but not identical: a paper can be legible to outsiders yet contribute little, or contribute substantially yet be written only for insiders. Ownership sits with the editor-in-chief role; I do not score it.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Explicit definitions of core constructs (quality culture, quality assurance, institutional implementation) at first use; expansion and glossing of field-specific acronyms and regulatory references; an introduction that states the problem in terms an organisational-studies or public-administration reader would recognise; framing that names the adjacent literatures it engages; implications sections whose transferability claims are tied to specific features of the study's design, sample, or findings rather than asserted; and evidence that any imported theory or method from another discipline is used in a way consistent with its home field.
what_triggers_block: Core constructs are never defined and the argument cannot be reconstructed without insider knowledge of a specific national accreditation regime, or the paper asserts substantial cross-field generalisability that its own single-context administrator data cannot support anywhere in the text.
what_triggers_warn: The paper is followable by an adjacent-field reader but leaves several acronyms or regulatory references unglossed, defines key constructs only implicitly, or offers transferable implications that are plausible yet not explicitly linked back to reported evidence.
