## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** Read from a domain-accuracy standpoint, this dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data handling, analytic choices, and statistical reporting would survive scrutiny by competent peers in the manuscript's own field, and whether enough procedural detail is disclosed that another team could in principle reproduce the work. It is not my scoring responsibility, but I understand it as the check that the machinery producing the numbers is sound, distinct from whether the numbers are described in field-correct language.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by me as the domain reviewer).** This is the dimension I own. It asks whether every substantive claim the manuscript makes is consistent with the current state of evidence in its field; whether prior work, competing findings, and established positions are represented faithfully rather than flattened, strawmanned, or mis-cited; and whether the manuscript uses field-specific terminology, constructs, instruments, and reported quantities correctly. A domain-accuracy failure includes asserting as settled something the field regards as contested, attributing to a cited source a finding it does not contain, conflating constructs that the field carefully separates, or misstating the direction, magnitude, or meaning of a previously published result. Because the dimension is mandatory, a sufficiently severe misrepresentation is not a stylistic quibble; it goes to whether the manuscript's factual base can be trusted at all.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the discourse-analysis reviewer).** This dimension concerns whether the paper's central thesis holds together internally: whether stated evidence actually bears on the claims it is offered for, whether conclusions stay within what the evidence licenses, and whether any inferential fallacy is load-bearing for the main argument. From my vantage point this differs from my own dimension in that a paper can reason validly from claims that are factually wrong about the domain, or reason invalidly from claims that are domain-accurate; I score the truth of the inputs, not the validity of the chain.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by the perspective reviewer).** This asks whether a reader from an adjacent discipline can follow the framing, whether key terms are defined rather than assumed, and whether any claim that reaches beyond the home field is actually supported rather than asserted. I note the boundary with my own dimension: importing a concept from a neighbouring literature in a way that misstates that literature is a domain-accuracy problem as well as a relevance problem, and I will flag the factual side of such a case if I see it.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension covers organisation, clarity of exposition, quality and legibility of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's conventions. It is presentational rather than substantive, and I do not score it. I will only care about it indirectly, in the case where prose or a table is so imprecise that a domain claim cannot be evaluated for accuracy at all.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it advances something original and consequential for that readership. I do not score it. The adjacency to my dimension is that a claim of novelty is itself a factual claim about the state of the literature; if novelty is asserted where prior work already established the result, I treat the misrepresentation of prior work as a D2 matter and leave the significance judgement to the owner.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether each substantive assertion is consistent with the field's current evidence base; whether cited prior work is characterised faithfully and actually supports the proposition attached to it; whether field-specific constructs, instruments, measures, units, and reported effects are named and used correctly; whether contested matters are presented as contested and settled matters are not needlessly relitigated; whether any quantity, definition, or previously published result is reproduced accurately.
what_triggers_block: One or more load-bearing domain claims are factually wrong or materially misrepresent a cited source, such that a central conclusion of the manuscript rests on an inaccurate account of the field's evidence and cannot stand as written without substantive correction.
what_triggers_warn: Domain inaccuracies are present but peripheral or repairable — imprecise use of a technical term, an overstated characterisation of prior work, a citation that only partially supports its claim, or an omitted contrary finding — none of which by itself invalidates the manuscript's central conclusion.
what_triggers_fatal: The manuscript's factual foundation is unsalvageable within revision: for example, fabricated, non-existent, or systematically mis-attributed sources; results or quantities that contradict the manuscript's own reported data or the field's established record in a way that cannot be corrected without abandoning the paper's claims; or a core construct so fundamentally misunderstood that every downstream domain claim is void.
