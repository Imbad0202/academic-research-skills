## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, data handling, statistical reporting, and reproducibility supports would survive scrutiny by competent peers in the paper's own field. From my cross-disciplinary vantage point, the relevant consequence is that a reader trained in an adjacent discipline must be able to tell what was actually done and under what conditions the reported numbers were produced; methodological opacity is not just an internal-validity problem but a barrier to anyone outside the immediate specialty who wants to evaluate or reuse the work. I do not score this dimension — it belongs to the methodology reviewer — but I note it because inaccessible method reporting and unrigorous method reporting often co-occur.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role).** This dimension covers whether the manuscript's claims are consistent with the current state of evidence in its home domain, whether prior work is characterised faithfully, and whether domain-specific terms and results are used correctly. My interest in it is adjacent: terminology that is used incorrectly inside the field is almost always used misleadingly outside it, and mischaracterised prior work is the main way an adjacent-field reader acquires a false picture of a literature they cannot independently audit. Scoring authority here rests with the domain reviewer.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, da/methodology roles).** This dimension asks whether the central thesis holds together, whether the evidence presented actually supports the claims made from it, and whether any reasoning defects are severe enough to undermine the argument. Read across disciplinary lines, coherence is what allows a non-specialist to follow the chain from data to conclusion without having to trust the author's authority; gaps in that chain are typically most visible to readers who cannot fill them in with tacit field knowledge. I do not assign a score here.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high, perspective role).** This is my dimension. It asks whether the framing, the definitions of key constructs, and the stated implications are intelligible and usable to readers in neighbouring fields, and whether any claim that reaches beyond the home discipline is actually substantiated rather than asserted. Two distinct failure modes matter: an accessibility failure, where the work may be sound but is written so that only insiders can extract its meaning, and an overreach failure, where the work speaks confidently to other fields on the strength of evidence that does not license those statements. A strong manuscript on this dimension defines its constructs operationally, states the boundary conditions under which its findings would or would not transfer, and scales the breadth of its implications to the breadth of its evidence.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal, eic role).** This dimension covers organisation, clarity of exposition, the quality and legibility of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's conventions. It overlaps with my concerns without being identical to them: prose can be clean and conventional while still assuming disciplinary background that an adjacent reader lacks, and prose can be untidy while remaining conceptually transparent. Scoring belongs to the editor-in-chief role.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, eic role).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it offers something original and significant to that venue's readership. From a cross-disciplinary standpoint, fit and reach are related but separable questions: a paper can be squarely in-scope for its venue while making no claim of interest beyond it, and that is a legitimate outcome rather than a defect I should penalise. The editor-in-chief scores this dimension.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Explicit operational definitions of key constructs and outcome measures rather than field-internal shorthand; an introduction that motivates the problem in terms an adjacent-field reader can recognise; stated scope and boundary conditions governing where the findings would and would not transfer; implications sections whose breadth is matched by the evidence actually presented; jargon, acronyms, and instruments glossed on first use; and any claim addressed to another discipline backed by a citation, a mechanism, or data from this study.
what_triggers_block: Central interdisciplinary or generalising claims are asserted with no supporting evidence, mechanism, or citation, or the paper's key constructs are left undefined to the point that an adjacent-field reader cannot determine what was measured or what the conclusions would mean outside the home specialty.
what_triggers_warn: The work is comprehensible in outline but leans on unglossed field-specific jargon, omits boundary conditions or scope limits on transferability, or extends its implications somewhat further than the presented evidence supports while remaining recoverable through targeted revision.
