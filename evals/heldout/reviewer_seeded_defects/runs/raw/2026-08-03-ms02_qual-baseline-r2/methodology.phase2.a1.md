## Dimension Scores

### D1: methodology_rigor

score: block
block_class: fatal
trigger: "the described procedure could not have produced the reported results"

The fatal binding rests on §4.3 (W1): the manuscript reports that dissenting participants were removed from the analysis *because* they fell outside the three-theme structure, and in the adjacent sentence claims the study "achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views." A coding procedure that discards disconfirming cases cannot have produced a full-range thematic account; the reported themes are an artifact of the exclusion rather than a finding about the data. This is not underspecification that added description could repair — the disconfirming material has been excised from the analysis that the Findings report, so the reported evidence would have to be regenerated, not documented. Two further disjuncts of the same fatal trigger are independently satisfied (N reported as 14 in the Abstract and 12 in §3.2; an inferential subgroup claim applied to clustered n=9 vs n=11 on an unanchored scale), and the §3.5 anonymization guarantee is falsified in §4.1. I treat the block itself as overdetermined and the fatal classification as resting on W1 alone.

### D3: argumentative_coherence

score: block
block_class: repairable
trigger: "causal, comparative, or generalised conclusions drawn from a design incapable of supporting them"

The inferential warrant fails at the level of scope, not of internal position. §5 escalates from three purposively selected institutions to "universities across the sector" and "the higher education sector as a whole," and §6 adds a priority claim ("the first comprehensive account"). A twelve-interview, three-site qualitative study with a 48-respondent convenience survey cannot license any of these. The Discussion also relies on a characterisation of Delacroix (2018) that directly contradicts the manuscript's own §2 characterisation of the same source, and §4.4 labels a divergent survey result "corroborat[ion]" in the same paragraph in which it reports that the survey ran counter to what the interviews had led the authors to anticipate. I score this repairable rather than fatal because a coherent, defensible local thesis — that administrators at these three sites negotiate quality culture between external requirement and internal meaning — survives once the sector-wide, priority, and corroboration claims are withdrawn. Nothing here is self-defeating in the sense my plan reserved for fatality on this dimension; it is overreach and misdescription of the authors' own evidence, both correctable by restatement. I do not rule on whether the Delacroix characterisation is a citation error as such; that verdict belongs to the domain seat.

## Review Body

This manuscript studies a genuinely under-examined actor — the administrator who sits between accreditor and department — and the question in §1 is worth asking. My assessment concerns whether the reported procedure can carry the claims, and on that test the manuscript does not currently reconcile with itself. The central problem is not that the study is small. Twelve well-conducted interviews at three sites can support a careful interpretive account. The problem is that the manuscript's own sentences contradict each other at three load-bearing points: how many people were interviewed, whose views the themes represent, and whether the survey agreed or disagreed with the interviews. Each contradiction is visible on the page, so a reader cannot resolve it by charitable inference; one of the two statements in each pair has to be withdrawn, and which one is withdrawn changes the findings.

The gravest of these is §4.3. Removing dissenting participants because they do not fit the three-theme structure inverts the logic of thematic analysis: in an inductive design, cases that resist the coding frame are the evidence that tests the frame. Once they are removed, the claim that the three themes capture "the full range of administrator views" is guaranteed by construction rather than established by analysis. The pervasiveness claim in §4.1 inherits the same defect. I would also observe that §3.2's recruitment "through institutional QA offices" — through the gatekeepers whose work is the object of study — is a plausible partial explanation for why dissent appeared only as a residual minority; the manuscript never names this selection pressure, so the reader is left to supply it.

The survey component cannot be evaluated at all as reported. §3.3 gives the mode of administration and nothing else: no items, no scale range or anchors, no reliability or validity evidence, no sampling frame, no invitation base, no response rate, no handling of the fact that respondents are nested within three institutions. Consequently the headline M=3.9 (SD=0.6) that appears in the Abstract is uninterpretable — 3.9 on a five-point scale and 3.9 on a seven-point scale are different findings, and one of them would not support "moderately positive." The subgroup claim in §4.4 goes further than the data can go by any route: no test is named, no effect size or interval is reported, no correction is applied, the comparison is between n=9 and n=11 respondents whose institutional membership is perfectly collinear with the grouping variable, and the third site is silently dropped. In a design where institution *is* the group, a between-institution p-value computed on individual respondents has no clear inferential referent. "While based on small numbers" understates the issue; the appropriate revision is to present the contrast descriptively or to drop it.

On integration, the manuscript declares "mixed-methods" in the Abstract and §3.1 but names no design typology, states no priority sequence beyond "the qualitative component was primary," specifies no point of interface, and provides no joint display. That would be a reporting gap on its own. What converts it into a substantive error is §4.4's framing: the survey diverged from what the interviews predicted, and the manuscript calls that corroboration. Divergence is an interesting and reportable mixed-methods result — it is the beginning of the level-of-institution argument the authors gesture at in §5's final paragraph — but it has to be labelled as divergence and then explained, not relabelled as agreement.

The qualitative analysis is reported in two sentences. "Coding proceeded iteratively... until a stable structure was reached" names no analytic tradition, no criterion for stability, no coder or team process, no reflexivity or positionality statement despite an interpretive design, and no trustworthiness check of any kind. §4.1's assertion that themes "emerged systematically from the structured protocol" then contradicts §3.1's declared semi-structured design and §3.4's inductive language, and the confidence that follows ("giving us confidence that the pattern was not an artifact") is asserted by procedural fiat rather than derived from anything described. Finally, §3.5's guarantee that "no individual could be identified" is falsified in the next section by an attribution to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" — a description that resolves to one institution and one post-holder. I treat this as a live confidentiality matter for the editor, not a wording preference, and it also raises the prior question of what the consent documents actually permitted.

I have not scored domain accuracy, cross-disciplinary relevance, writing and structure, or venue fit, all of which sit with other seats. Where my findings touch those areas — the internal inconsistency in the manuscript's two accounts of Delacroix, the priority claim in §6 — I confine myself to the coherence and warrant question and leave the substantive and editorial verdicts to their owners.

### S1: Answerable research question grounded in a stated gap

The literature review organises prior work into three defensible strands and identifies the intermediary administrator as the actor those strands leave under-examined; the resulting question is one that a qualitative interview design could in principle answer. This alignment between gap and design choice is a real methodological strength and is why I regard the study as recoverable in principle.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §1 "How do senior university administrators experience and enact QA implementation, and how do their accounts illuminate the formation of institutional quality culture?"
**Confidence**: 5 — routine assessment of design-to-question fit.

### S2: Defensible maximum-variation site selection

Selecting one research-intensive public, one teaching-focused public, and one private institution within a single national system is a coherent purposive logic that holds the regulatory regime constant while varying institutional type. It is the element of the sampling design that is both stated and justified by its structure.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.2 "one large public research university, one mid-sized public teaching-focused university, and one private university"
**Confidence**: 5 — standard purposive-sampling appraisal.

### S3: Adequate reporting of interview data-collection basics

Mode, duration range, recording, and transcription are all reported. This is the one procedural layer a reader can reconstruct, and it establishes that the interviews were of a length capable of generating the depth the Findings claim.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.3 "Interviews were conducted in person or by video call, lasting between 45 and 70 minutes, and were audio-recorded and transcribed"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct reading of reported procedure.

### W1: Disconfirming cases excluded from the analysis while full-range representation is claimed

§4.3 states that participants with more skeptical views "were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure," immediately after asserting that the study captured the full range of administrator views. Exclusion on the grounds of non-fit makes the three-theme structure unfalsifiable by the study's own data, and it means the pervasiveness of the ritual-compliance theme in §4.1 and the "held open tension" reading in §4.3 rest on a corpus from which counter-evidence was removed. Uncorrected, this invalidates the Findings as reported; restoring the negative cases requires re-analysis whose outcome could change the theme structure itself, which is why I do not regard it as fixable by added description.

**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views"; "these were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure"
**Confidence**: 5 — negative-case handling is core reflexive thematic analysis practice.

### W2: Survey instrument wholly unreported, leaving the Abstract's headline mean uninterpretable

No items, response-scale range, or anchors are given, so M=3.9 (SD=0.6) cannot be read as "moderately positive" or as anything else; nor is there reliability evidence, validity evidence, a sampling frame, an invitation base, or a response rate. The survey nonetheless appears in the Abstract as a reported result and in §5 as a source of substantive complication. This is remediable by supplying the instrument and administration details, which presumably exist, so the core qualitative contribution survives — but every survey-derived statement in the manuscript is currently unverifiable.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.3 and §4.4 survey reporting — expected item wording, response-scale range and anchors, reliability or validity evidence, sampling frame, and response rate; checked §3.1–§3.5, §4.4, the Abstract, and the reference list for an instrument citation or supplementary appendix
**Confidence**: 5 — instrument reporting is a minimum survey-methods standard.

### W3: Inferential subgroup claim without a named test, effect size, correction, or treatment of clustering

§4.4 reports a "statistically significant difference (p<.05)" between staff at two institutions on the basis of n=9 versus n=11, naming no test, reporting no effect size or confidence interval, applying no correction for the multiple comparisons implied by "subgroup comparisons were examined where role categories permitted," and ignoring that institution and group are perfectly confounded. The third site is dropped without comment. The inference cannot be sustained as stated; the moderator suggestion built on it in §4.4 must be withdrawn or re-expressed descriptively.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "we found a statistically significant difference (p<.05) in perceived quality culture, with the teaching-focused institution scoring higher"
**Confidence**: 5 — elementary inferential reporting requirements.

### W4: Interview sample size reported as both fourteen and twelve

The Abstract reports fourteen interviewees; §3.2 reports twelve. These cannot both be true, and the resolution is consequential: it determines whether two interviews exist that are absent from the participant description, and — read alongside §4.3 — whether the "excluded" dissenting participants are the missing two. Until the governing figure is stated, the size and composition of the evidence base are indeterminate.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract and §3.2 "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth"; "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 5 — direct arithmetic inconsistency on the page.

### W5: No mixed-methods design typology or integration point, and divergence reported as corroboration

The design is labelled mixed-methods but no typology, sequence, or point of interface is named, and no joint display is offered; integration is asserted rather than performed. More seriously, §4.4 opens by claiming the survey "corroborated the qualitative picture" and then reports that the interviews had led the authors to expect the opposite. Divergence is a legitimate and interesting finding here and would strengthen the level-of-institution argument the Discussion gestures toward; describing it as corroboration misreports the authors' own result.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.4 "The survey corroborated the qualitative picture"; "This is notable because the interview accounts had led us to anticipate a more skeptical picture"
**Confidence**: 5 — standard mixed-methods integration and inference-quality criteria.

### W6: Thematic analysis procedure under-reported to the point of non-reconstructability

§3.4 gives two sentences. No analytic tradition is named; no criterion is given for judging when the structure became "stable"; no coder or team process, intercoder work, member checking, or audit trail is described; and there is no reflexivity or positionality statement despite an interpretive design in which the authors' relationship to QA practice plausibly shapes interpretation. A reader cannot judge how the three themes were arrived at or by whom, and therefore cannot assess whether they are warranted by the transcripts.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §3.4 analysis reporting — expected a named analytic approach, reflexivity or positionality statement, stated criterion for thematic stability, coder or team process, and at least one trustworthiness check such as member checking or an audit trail; checked §3.1–§3.5, §4.1–§4.3, and the Abstract
**Confidence**: 5 — reflexive thematic analysis reporting standards.

### W7: Findings attribute themes to a "structured protocol," contradicting the declared design, and assert unearned confidence

§4.1 credits the themes to systematic emergence "from the structured protocol," which contradicts §3.1's semi-structured interviews and §3.4's iterative, inductive account. The clause that follows converts this misdescription into a validity claim, asserting confidence that the pattern was not a single-site artifact. Nothing in the reported procedure supports that inference: with no cross-site comparison, no case-level display, and no stated per-site distribution of the theme, cross-institutional pervasiveness is asserted rather than shown.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.1 and §4.1 "semi-structured interviews"; "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol, giving us confidence that the pattern was not an artifact"
**Confidence**: 5 — internal inconsistency between declared design and findings warrant.

### W8: Stated anonymization guarantee falsified by an identifying attribution

§3.5 guarantees that no individual could be identified. §4.1 then attributes a quotation to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region," which identifies the institution and, through it, the individual post-holder; a second attribution ("associate dean for quality at the public research university") narrows similarly. This is a confidentiality problem rather than a stylistic one, and it raises a prior question about what the consent documents permitted. Fixable by generic role descriptors, but the ethics statement as written is currently untrue of the manuscript.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.5 and §4.1 "all data were fully anonymized prior to analysis so that no individual could be identified"; "the quality director of the largest private university in the region"
**Confidence**: 4 — the breach is plain; the consent terms behind it are not visible to me.

### W9: Gatekeeper recruitment and absent sampling logic left unexamined

Participants were recruited through the institutional QA offices whose work is the object of study, which is a selection pressure toward administrators invested in QA. The manuscript neither names this nor discusses its consequences, and it offers no account of how many people were approached, how many declined, why twelve (or fourteen) was sufficient, or what saturation or information-power logic governed. Transferability claims and the near-absence of dissent in the Findings both depend on this unreported layer.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.2 "were recruited through institutional QA offices"
**Confidence**: 5 — routine appraisal of access and selection in gatekeeper-mediated recruitment.

### W10: No reproducibility affordances

There is no interview protocol or topic guide, no coding frame or codebook, no survey instrument in an appendix, and no data-availability or materials statement indicating the conditions under which transcripts or aggregate survey data could be examined. Nothing here changes the findings, but their independent scrutiny is foreclosed, and supplying these materials is the cheapest of the repairs this manuscript needs.

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: Methods and back matter — expected interview protocol, coding frame or codebook, and a data-availability or materials statement; checked §3.1–§3.5, §4, §6, the reference list, and any supplementary-materials note
**Confidence**: 5 — direct check of the manuscript's surfaces.

### W11: Sector-wide conclusions drawn from three purposively selected sites

§5 moves from three institutions to "universities across the sector," offers the study as "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture," and asserts that "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension." The design supports none of this: single national system, three sites, no sampling logic, no comparative analytic strategy, and — per W1 — a corpus from which non-conforming cases were removed. The local claim survives once the generalisation is withdrawn, so this requires substantial rewriting of §5 and §6 rather than new data, but the Discussion as written misstates the study's reach and the Abstract's "across three universities" does not license it either.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "these findings demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment rather than a managerial output"; "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension"
**Confidence**: 5 — scope-of-inference judgement within my competence.

### W12: Design recommendation rests on a characterisation of a source the manuscript elsewhere contradicts

§2 presents Delacroix (2018) as cautioning that participatory rhetoric becomes a compliance ritual and as arguing *against* treating consultation as sufficient evidence of quality culture. §5 then aligns the authors' recommendation with Delacroix "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture." Both readings cannot stand, and the third implication of the Discussion is built on the second. Whatever the source actually argues — a determination I leave to the domain seat — the manuscript's internal reasoning is broken at the point where its practical recommendation is generated.

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 and §5 "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual"; "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture"
**Confidence**: 4 — the internal contradiction is certain; the external accuracy question is not mine.

### W13: Unsupported priority claim in the Conclusion

§6 opens by describing the paper as "the first comprehensive account" of administrator experience of QA implementation, which is inconsistent with §2's own acknowledgement of Pettersen (2022) and Rahman (2020) on the quality office and of Silva and Tan (2021) on the intermediary position, and which no comprehensiveness criterion in the Methods could establish from twelve interviews. Deleting the claim costs the paper nothing.

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §6 "This paper has offered the first comprehensive account of how university administrators experience and enact QA implementation"
**Confidence**: 5 — internal inconsistency with the manuscript's own literature review.
