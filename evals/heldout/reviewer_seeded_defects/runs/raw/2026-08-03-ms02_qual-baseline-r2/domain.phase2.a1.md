## Review Body

I read the reference list before the manuscript, as I always do, and the reference list decided this review. Every one of the twelve entries carries a DOI in the `10.5555` range. That is the DOI system's reserved test and example prefix; it is not a registered publisher prefix, and no article of record resolves from it. This is not a single transcription slip. It is uniform across all twelve entries, with sequential suffixes (`1042001` through `1042012`). Worse, for the majority of these entries the journal named is real and its publisher's registered prefix is a matter of public record and is not `10.5555` — *Higher Education Quarterly* (Wiley), *Policy Futures in Education* (SAGE), *Quality in Higher Education*, *Teaching in Higher Education*, *Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education*, *Higher Education Research & Development*, *Journal of Higher Education Policy and Management* (Taylor & Francis), *Journal of Educational Administration* (Emerald). The DOIs supplied therefore cannot belong to the articles named. Three further titles — *Journal of Higher Education Governance*, *Studies in Quality Assurance*, *Educational Management Review* — I cannot identify as journals operating in this field, and I have spent fifteen years in it. Consequently I cannot verify that a single cited source exists, still less that it holds the position attributed to it. Since D2 asks whether prior work is correctly represented, and since this manuscript's contribution is entirely a literature-relative claim, the dimension cannot be assessed on substance at all. That is what my Phase 1 plan called pervasive domain unreliability, and it is why I have recorded a fatal block rather than a repairable one. I state plainly what would overturn that judgement: resolvable DOIs and verifiable bibliographic records for all twelve sources, supplied by the authors. Absent that, no amount of rewriting repairs the foundation.

The one internal check available to me makes the problem worse rather than better. §2 and the reference-list annotation both report, consistently and clearly, that Delacroix (2018) argues *against* treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of a healthy quality culture, on the ground that consultation is readily co-opted into compliance performance. §5 then attributes to Delacroix the exact inverse: that consultation is "the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture," and concludes that consultation "is the engine of internalization." The third of three Discussion findings, and the paper's only concrete design recommendation, therefore rests on a citation reversed against the manuscript's own account of that source. This is not ambiguity requiring charitable reading; the paper contradicts itself about what a named author argued, twice on the page.

On substance, insofar as substance is reachable: the manuscript rediscovers established field knowledge and reports it as emergent. "QA as ritual compliance" is institutional decoupling — the gap between formal structure and actual practice that Meyer and Rowan set out in 1977 and that has been the field's default explanation of ceremonial compliance ever since. The felt experience of ritualism among quality staff was documented by Newton in the early 2000s, including the specific finding that administrators hold the compliance/meaning tension open rather than resolving it, which the paper reports as its third theme. "Steering at a distance" attributed to a 2019 monograph is Neave's evaluative state and Kickert's governance-at-a-distance. The construct "quality culture" itself has a canonical two-component definition from the EUA Quality Culture Project and a typology from Harvey and Stensaker; the surrounding critique runs through Power's audit society, Strathern's audit cultures, and Ball's performativity. None of this appears. A reference list whose earliest item is 2018 in a field whose defining works span 1977 to 2010 is not a scope decision; it is a gap in the authors' command of the literature, and it directly produces the manuscript's claim to offer "the first comprehensive account."

Two conceptual consequences follow. Because §1 defines quality culture as shared values, practices, and commitments only, dropping the structural and managerial component the field's canonical definition includes, the Discussion's headline conclusion — that quality culture is "not a management output" — is substantially true by definition rather than by finding. And because no theoretical framework is declared anywhere, the paper's own diagnosis that quality culture "remains under-theorized at the level of everyday administrative practice" is left unmet: three inductive themes are described, and description is offered where theory was promised.

I flag, and do not score, the quantitative reporting: the significance claim at §4.4 rests on subgroups of nine and eleven drawn from forty-eight respondents, with no test statistic, and the subgroups do not sum to the reported sample. That is Reviewer 1's territory and I defer to it. What is mine is the construct: the manuscript reports a single quality-culture mean with no named instrument, no item wording, and no scale provenance, for a construct the literature treats as multidimensional.

So that this is a revision path and not a reading list: the sequence is (1) supply verifiable sources or withdraw; (2) withdraw or reverse the third Discussion finding; (3) reconstruct §2 back to the field's foundational literature and reposition the three themes as confirmation and refinement within it, which is a publishable contribution if honestly framed; (4) declare a framework; (5) reconcile the reported N, restore the excluded dissenting cases, and confine the claims to three institutions in one system.

### S1: Literature Review states the field's live tension accurately
The participation-versus-performance tension named at the end of §2 is the correct fault line in the quality-culture literature, and the paper's rendering of the position that consultation can itself become an artifact documented for the panel is a faithful statement of a real and important argument in the field. This paragraph is the strongest domain writing in the manuscript.
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §2 ¶2 "cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual"`
**Confidence**: 5 — I have published on the internalization of external QA and this is the tension my own comparative work turns on.

### S2: The niche is correctly located, even where the priority claim is not
Distinguishing a conceptual account of the intermediary position from grounded accounts in administrators' own words is a legitimate and precisely stated gap. Studies of quality managers exist, so the account is not the first; but administrators' first-person meaning-making within a single regulatory system is a defensible place to stand, and the paper identifies it cleanly.
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §2 ¶4 "their account remains largely conceptual rather than grounded in administrators' own words"`
**Confidence**: 4 — familiar with the quality-manager and third-space professional literatures.

### S3: Divergent survey and interview results are not forced into agreement
Where the staff survey sentiment failed to match administrator accounts, §5 reports the divergence as a level-of-institution effect requiring further work rather than smoothing it into corroboration. That restraint is good domain practice and the right reading of what a perceptual gap between administrative and academic staff usually signals.
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §5 ¶5 "a divergence worth pursuing in future work"`
**Confidence**: 4 — routine in comparative QA survey work.

### W2: Discussion attributes to Delacroix (2018) the reverse of the position the paper itself reports
§2 and the reference annotation both state that Delacroix argues against treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of quality culture. §5 credits him with recommending consultation as the central mechanism, and builds the third of three findings plus the paper's sole design recommendation on that reading. Either the Discussion claim is withdrawn or §2 and the annotation are wrong; on any resolution the third finding currently rests on a reversed source. This defect alone would justify blocking a mandatory dimension: a manuscript that reports a cited author as arguing X where its own literature review reports not-X cannot be published as it stands.
**Severity**: Critical
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §5 ¶4 "who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture"`
**Confidence**: 5 — the contradiction is internal to the manuscript and requires no external verification.

### W3: Canonical literature absent, so established knowledge is presented as emergent finding and claimed as first
No source predates 2018. Institutional decoupling, the audit society and audit cultures, performativity, quality-culture typologies, and the qualitative work on ritualism among quality staff are all absent, yet Theme 1 restates decoupling under a new label and Theme 3 restates a documented finding about how quality staff hold the compliance/meaning tension open. §6's claim to "the first comprehensive account" is false in the field. The empirical material can survive, but §1, §2, §5, and §6 require substantial rewriting to reposition the themes as confirmation and refinement within a cumulative literature.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §2 and reference list — expected the field's foundational quality-culture and audit-culture sources predating 2018, including institutional decoupling, audit society, and quality-culture typologies; checked all 12 references, §1 framing, §2 three strands, §5 Discussion, §6 Conclusion`
**Confidence**: 5 — this is the literature I teach and have published in.

### W4: Truncated definition of quality culture makes the central conclusion partly definitional
The construct is defined as shared values, practices, and commitments only. The field's canonical definition pairs that cultural-psychological element with a structural and managerial element, precisely so that the interaction between them can be studied. Having dropped the managerial half at the outset, the Discussion's finding that quality culture is "not a management output" is largely secured by the definition rather than by the data. Restoring the two-component construct requires reworking the framing and re-reading the themes against it.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §1 ¶2 "the shared values, practices, and commitments through which an institution takes collective ownership of its own standards"`
**Confidence**: 5 — definitional history of the construct is central to my own comparative work.

### W5: No theoretical framework, though the stated gap is a theorizing gap
§1 diagnoses the field as "under-theorized at the level of everyday administrative practice" and positions the paper as addressing that gap. What follows is three inductive themes with no declared theoretical or conceptual apparatus, nothing that explains why administrators experience QA in these terms, and no mechanism linking the themes to each other or to cumulative scholarship. A promised deliverable is not delivered, which requires either a framework to be supplied and applied or the gap claim to be restated as descriptive.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §1 and §3.4 — expected a declared theoretical or conceptual framework connecting the three themes to cumulative quality-culture scholarship; checked §1 research question, §2 strand three, §3.4 analysis, §5 Discussion`
**Confidence**: 4 — judgement about what counts as theory in this literature.

### W6: Quality-culture measure has no instrument provenance for a construct the field treats as multidimensional
A single overall score (M=3.9, SD=0.6) is reported and then used to support an inference about level-differentiated experience of quality culture, but no instrument is named, no items are given, no scale range is stated, and no source is cited for the operationalization. The literature treats quality culture as multidimensional and typological, so a unidimensional mean is not interpretable as "institutional quality culture" without justification. Psychometric adequacy and the significance testing belong to the methodology seat; the construct-to-measure mismatch is a domain problem and needs the instrument documented or the survey claims narrowed.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: `absence: §3.3 and §4.4 — expected named instrument, item wording, or scale provenance for the quality-culture measure reported as M=3.9; checked §3.3 data collection, §3.4 analysis, §4.4 survey findings, reference list`
**Confidence**: 4 — familiar with the instruments used to survey quality culture, less so with their psychometrics.

### W7: Sector-wide and global claims exceed the evidence and contradict the manuscript's own Conclusion
§5 moves from three institutions in one unnamed system to "universities across the sector," to a reading of the study as "a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture," to "administrators everywhere." Comparative QA research establishes that compliance versus enhancement orientation varies substantially with national regulatory design, funding linkage, and the maturity of the external agency, so a cross-system universal is contradicted by the evidence base rather than merely unsupported by this study's. §6 then asks for exactly the cross-system testing §5 claims to have pre-empted. The Discussion needs rewriting to the three-institution scope.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §5 ¶3 "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension between external requirement and internal meaning"`
**Confidence**: 5 — I have published on the internalization of external QA across three national systems and the variation is the finding.

### W8: Reported interview sample contradicts itself
The Abstract reports fourteen administrators interviewed in depth; §3.2 reports twelve, stated twice as "(n=12)." A reader cannot determine the size of the qualitative corpus, which bears directly on whether the three-theme structure is adequately grounded and on any claim about thematic stability. The discrepancy must be resolved and the affected claims re-checked against whichever figure is correct.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: Abstract "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth" versus §3.2 "Twelve senior administrators (n=12)"`
**Confidence**: 5 — direct textual comparison.

### W9: Dissenting cases excluded while claiming full-range representation
§4.3 asserts balanced representation capturing the full range of administrator views, and in the adjacent sentences reports that participants expressing more skeptical or dissenting views about the QA enterprise were excluded because they fell outside the three-theme structure. Dropping cases for failing to fit the emerging structure removes precisely the disconfirming evidence that would test it, and the representativeness claim is false as written. The excluded accounts must be restored and analysed, or the claim withdrawn and the themes reported as partial. Design implications for coding and saturation belong to the methodology seat.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §4.3 "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views" alongside "these were excluded for space"`
**Confidence**: 5 — the contradiction is on the page and the exclusion rationale is stated by the authors.

### W10: Anonymity guarantee contradicted by re-identifying participant descriptors
§3.5 states that data were fully anonymized so that no individual could be identified. §4.1 then attributes quotations to the quality director of the largest private university in the region and to the associate dean for quality at the public research university. Within a three-institution design where one site is private and one is a public research university, a role title plus an institutional descriptor identifies a single named post-holder to any reader in that system. The consent representation and the reported text are inconsistent, and the descriptors must be generalized before publication.
**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §4.1 "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" against §3.5 "no individual could be identified"`
**Confidence**: 5 — standard confidentiality practice in small-n institutional research.

### W11: Instrument described inconsistently, and thematic vocabulary used loosely
§3.1 and §3.3 describe semi-structured interviews; §4.1 credits the themes to a "structured protocol." The two are different instruments with different implications for whether themes are participant-generated or protocol-generated, and the field's use of "emergence" in thematic analysis presupposes the former. The terminology should be made consistent and the protocol's degree of structure stated.
**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §4.1 "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol" versus §3.1 "semi-structured interviews"`
**Confidence**: 4 — standard qualitative terminology in this literature.
