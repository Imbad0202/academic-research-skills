## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, methodology role).** This dimension asks whether the study's design and execution would survive scrutiny from a competent peer reviewer in the field: is the sampling and data-collection strategy defensible for the question asked, is the handling of the data described in enough detail to be traceable, are any quantitative or qualitative analytic claims reported with the appropriate statistics or coding evidence, and could another researcher in principle repeat or audit the work from what the manuscript supplies. From a domain-accuracy standpoint I note this is owned elsewhere; my concern with it is only that methodological weakness is not confused with domain-factual error, and vice versa.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, domain role — mine).** This is the dimension I own. It asks whether the substantive claims the manuscript makes are consistent with the current state of evidence in its field, whether the prior literature it invokes is described as that literature actually stands rather than as a convenient straw version, and whether field-specific vocabulary, frameworks, instruments, regulatory or institutional facts, and reported results are used correctly. Failures here include misdefined technical terms, attributing to a cited body of work a finding or position it does not hold, stating as settled something the field treats as contested, ignoring a well-established counter-evidence base that bears directly on the central claim, and internal factual inconsistency in reported domain particulars.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, devil's-advocate and methodology roles).** This dimension asks whether the manuscript's central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether the stated conclusions actually follow from the evidence presented rather than from an unstated leap, whether the paper avoids reasoning defects such as circularity, equivocation between two senses of a key construct, or generalising from a base too narrow to bear it, and whether internal tensions between sections are resolved rather than left standing. It is distinct from my dimension in that a paper can reason validly from domain-inaccurate premises, or reason invalidly from accurate ones.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, perspective role).** This dimension asks whether a well-trained reader from a neighbouring field could enter the paper and follow it: are constructs defined rather than assumed, is jargon either avoided or unpacked, are the stated implications legible outside the immediate specialist community, and where the manuscript reaches into another discipline's territory, is that reach backed by evidence and citation from that discipline rather than asserted. It is a high-priority but not mandatory dimension, so its worst case escalates the decision without forcing rejection.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, editor-in-chief role).** This dimension covers craft and presentation: whether the manuscript's organisation serves the argument, whether the exposition is clear at sentence and section level, whether figures and tables are legible, correctly labelled, and genuinely informative rather than decorative, and whether the submission observes the conventions of the target venue in structure, apparatus, and referencing. It carries normal priority, meaning problems here shape revision requests rather than acceptance in principle.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, editor-in-chief role).** This dimension asks whether this manuscript belongs in this outlet and whether it adds something: does the topic and treatment match the configured venue's scope and readership, is the contribution original relative to what already exists rather than a restatement or a thin increment, and is the significance proportionate to what the venue's readers expect. Although mandatory, it is judged from the editorial chair and not from my seat.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether substantive claims about quality assurance and higher education policy match the current evidence base; whether cited frameworks, standards bodies, accreditation or audit mechanisms, and technical constructs (e.g. quality culture, internal/external QA, compliance versus enhancement orientations) are defined and used as the field actually uses them; whether prior work is represented faithfully rather than flattened into a foil; whether reported findings, figures, institutional facts, and terminology are internally consistent and consistent with what is verifiable; and whether contested positions are presented as contested rather than as settled.
what_triggers_block: One or more clear, decision-relevant domain errors that undermine a load-bearing claim — for example a core construct defined incorrectly or used in a sense contrary to the field's established meaning, a cited source credited with a finding or position it does not hold, a substantive claim contradicted by well-established evidence that goes unaddressed, or a mutually contradictory pair of factual statements about the same domain particular.
what_triggers_warn: Domain imprecision that does not topple the central argument but would mislead a reader — loose or drifting use of technical terminology, a literature characterisation that is oversimplified or dated, an unhedged claim in an area the field treats as unresolved, a missing engagement with an obviously relevant strand of prior work, or terminology used inconsistently across sections.
what_triggers_fatal: Pervasive domain unreliability such that the manuscript's substantive foundation cannot be repaired by revision — for instance fabricated, non-existent, or systematically misattributed references, invented standards, bodies, instruments, or regulatory requirements, results that are internally impossible given the stated domain context, or a central premise that is flatly false in the field and on which the entire paper rests.
