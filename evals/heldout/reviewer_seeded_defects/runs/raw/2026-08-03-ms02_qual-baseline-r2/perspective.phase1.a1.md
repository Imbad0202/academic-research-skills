## Contract Paraphrase

**D1 — methodology_rigor (mandatory, owned by the methodology reviewer).** This dimension asks whether the study's design, sampling, data handling, analytic reporting, and reproducibility affordances would survive scrutiny from a competent peer reviewer in the paper's own field. From my vantage point as the cross-disciplinary reader, methodological rigour matters chiefly because adjacent-field readers cannot audit an opaque method: if the procedure is under-described, a reader from an adjoining discipline has no way to judge whether the evidence transfers to their setting. I do not score this dimension, and I will not substitute my own judgement for the methodology reviewer's; where I notice that methodological opacity blocks outside comprehension, I will record that as an accessibility observation rather than as a rigour verdict.

**D2 — domain_accuracy (mandatory, owned by the domain reviewer).** This dimension covers whether the manuscript's claims are consistent with the current state of evidence in its home field, whether prior work is characterised fairly, and whether field-specific terminology and reported results are factually correct. It is not mine to score. It does bear on my remit in one direction: terms that are used accurately but idiosyncratically within the home field can still mislead an outside reader, and conversely, loose usage aimed at broadening appeal can quietly become a domain error. I will flag terminological ambiguity as a relevance and accessibility matter and leave correctness adjudication to the domain owner.

**D3 — argumentative_coherence (mandatory, owned by the discourse-analysis reviewer, also open to methodology).** This concerns whether the central thesis holds together on its own terms: whether stated evidence actually supports the stated conclusions, whether inferential steps are licensed, and whether any fallacy or internal contradiction undermines the spine of the argument. I am not an eligible scorer here. The overlap with my role is that incoherence and inaccessibility are easy to confuse — an argument that an outside reader cannot follow may be badly explained rather than badly reasoned. I will be careful to attribute what I find to exposition or scope rather than to logic, and defer coherence verdicts to the owning role.

**D4 — cross_disciplinary_relevance (high priority, owned by me as the perspective reviewer).** This is my dimension. It asks whether the framing, the operative definitions, and the stated implications are genuinely usable by a reader trained in an adjacent field rather than only by insiders, and whether any claim the manuscript makes about reaching beyond its own discipline is actually substantiated rather than merely asserted. Concretely I read for: whether core constructs are defined at first use in language that does not presuppose the home literature; whether the motivating problem is stated in terms an outsider recognises as a problem; whether borrowed concepts, instruments, or theoretical vocabulary imported from other disciplines are used in a way those disciplines would accept; whether claimed generalisation across institutional types, national systems, sectors, or levels of analysis is backed by evidence in the manuscript rather than by rhetorical breadth; and whether the implications section tells adjacent-field readers what, if anything, changes for them. Boundary-setting matters as much as breadth: a manuscript that is honestly and explicitly narrow can score well here, whereas one that gestures at universal applicability without support cannot. Because this dimension is high priority rather than mandatory, a block from me routes through F4 to major revision rather than to rejection, so I must reserve "block" for defects that genuinely disable cross-field use rather than for stylistic parochialism.

**D5 — writing_and_structure (normal priority, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This dimension covers organisation, clarity of exposition, quality of figures and tables, and conformity to the venue's conventions. I do not score it. It abuts my remit closely enough that I must police the boundary: prose that is merely clumsy, sections that are ordered oddly, or a table that is poorly labelled are writing problems, whereas a definition that only an insider can decode, or an inference that silently assumes shared disciplinary priors, is a relevance problem. When my observations are really about craft rather than cross-field usability, I will route them to the review body as non-scoring notes for the owning role.

**D6 — venue_fit_and_contribution (mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief role).** This asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it offers an original and significant contribution for that venue's readership. I am not an eligible scorer. My dimension informs it without duplicating it: if a venue draws a mixed or multi-disciplinary readership, then cross-field accessibility is part of what makes the contribution land, and I will supply evidence about accessibility that the editor can weigh. But judgements about originality, significance, and scope match against the venue remain the editor's, and I will not smuggle a fit verdict into my own score.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether core constructs and any imported cross-field vocabulary are defined at first use in outsider-legible terms, whether the motivating problem and the stated implications are articulated so an adjacent-field reader can tell what transfers to their setting, and whether every claim of applicability beyond the home discipline, institution type, or national system is substantiated by evidence presented in the manuscript rather than asserted.
what_triggers_block: Central claims of interdisciplinary or cross-system reach are made but rest on no supporting evidence in the manuscript, or the operative constructs are left so undefined and insider-dependent that an adjacent-field reader cannot reconstruct what was studied or what the findings would mean for them.
what_triggers_warn: Cross-field framing is broadly workable but locally impaired — for example a handful of key terms are used without first-use definition, borrowed concepts are deployed loosely relative to their source discipline, or the implications section addresses only home-field readers while the framing had promised broader relevance.
