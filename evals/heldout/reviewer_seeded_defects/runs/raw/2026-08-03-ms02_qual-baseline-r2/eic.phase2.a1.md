The manuscript is readable, all conventional sections are present, the reference list is complete and consistently formatted (one entry aside), and the prose is generally clean at sentence level. That is why this is not a block: nothing here requires architectural reconstruction, and no figure or table is referenced-but-missing because the manuscript contains no figures or tables at all. What it does contain is a dense accumulation of craft failures that a competent copyedit and a modest reorganisation could fix: the study's headline sample size is stated two different ways, the design is described two different ways, the central construct is never defined or operationalised anywhere in the paper, the quantitative results are delivered entirely in running prose with no table, section 4.4 is a single paragraph carrying the entire quantitative contribution, and the reference list carries an editorial annotation that does not belong in an APA-style entry. ### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution

score: block
block_class: repairable
trigger: "the novelty claim is asserted without engagement with the closest existing work"

On topic, register, and method tradition this manuscript belongs in this journal — administrator experience of QA implementation is core remit, and the paper cites this journal's own back catalogue (Iversen, 2021) accurately. The problem is contribution. The three themes the paper offers as findings map one-to-one onto three works the paper itself cites: ritual compliance onto Okonkwo (2018), distributed leadership onto Pettersen (2022), identity work onto Rahman (2020). The paper never states what its version of these themes adds to theirs. Against that background, the Conclusion's claim to "the first comprehensive account" is not merely oversold; it is contradicted by the manuscript's own reference list. There is a real residual increment here — Silva and Tan (2021) is characterised, correctly, as conceptual rather than grounded in administrators' own words, and this study could supply that grounding — but the manuscript does not currently argue that increment, and the increment it does assert is one the literature has already delivered. Compounding this, the study's national system, regulatory model, and review cycle are never identified, which for an international comparative readership is a fit problem and not only a clarity problem: a reader cannot determine what "ritual compliance" is compliance with. Repairable rather than fatal, because the differentiating claim exists in the material and could be argued; but it requires substantive repositioning, not tightening.

## Review Body

I read this as the Associate Editor who would have to decide whether this manuscript earns space in a journal whose readers work inside QA systems and compare across them. My remit here is craft and fit; the inferential statistics, the coding procedure, and the internal logic of the argument belong to my methodology, domain, and discourse-analysis colleagues, and I defer to them on scoring. I flag several items in their territory anyway, because they bear directly on whether the manuscript can be published at all.

The paper has a genuine question and, in its literature review, genuine control of the field. The identification of administrators as occupants of a structurally uncomfortable hinge is well drawn, the Delacroix/Ferreira tension is set up with precision, and section 4.3 contains the one moment where this study is unmistakably its own: administrators holding the compliance/meaning gap open rather than resolving it, and locating professional identity in the gap. That is a publishable insight. If the authors rebuilt the paper around it, they would have a contribution.

They have not. The paper instead claims comprehensiveness, and the claim collapses on contact with its own bibliography. Worse, the same literature review that renders Delacroix correctly — as arguing *against* stakeholder consultation as evidence of quality culture — reappears in the Discussion with Delacroix recommending consultation as the central mechanism for building it. The reference list even carries an annotation restating the correct reading. So the authors knew, and the Discussion's third practical implication is nonetheless built on the inversion. In eight years of handling submissions on quality culture, this is the specific error pattern that most reliably signals a manuscript assembled from notes rather than read as a whole, and it is the kind of thing this journal's readers check.

The conceptual discipline problem is more consequential than it may look. "Quality culture" is the paper's title construct, its survey outcome, and its contribution claim, and it is never defined beyond the Brennan and Osei gloss, never operationalised, and never distinguished from external accreditation compliance, external review, or internal QA. The three themes are not three aspects of one object: theme 4.1 concerns externally driven documentation for a panel, theme 4.2 concerns internal governance and departmental ownership, theme 4.3 concerns professional self-understanding. Calling all three "QA" and presenting them as a single tripartite finding is what allows the paper to look more integrated than it is. Disaggregating the referents is not cosmetic work; it is the analytic work the paper has skipped.

Against my own inclination to treat the quantitative strand as secondary, I looked at it closely, and I do not think it can stand as reported. A significance claim (p<.05) is made from n=9 against n=11 with no test named, no group means, no degrees of freedom, no effect size, and no accounting for how 9 plus 11 relates to the 48 respondents; the scale range behind M=3.9 is never stated. My methodology colleague should determine whether the comparison is sustainable at all. From my seat the reporting is simply not evaluable, and it is presented in prose where a table is required.

Two further items I would not let past regardless of the other seats' verdicts. The paper states that all data were anonymised so that no individual could be identified, then attributes quotations to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region" and "the associate dean for quality at the public research university" — in a sample containing exactly one private and one research university, these are identifiers, not descriptors. And the paper claims balanced representation capturing the full range of administrator views two sentences before disclosing that dissenting participants were excluded because they did not fit the three-theme structure. Both are stated on the page, in the authors' own words, and both are remediable — but neither can survive into print.

What would have to change: resolve the sample size, name the system and its QA regime, define and disaggregate the construct, correct Delacroix, restore the excluded accounts to the analysis or drop the representativeness claim, strip the identifying descriptors, report the survey properly in a table, and replace the comprehensiveness claim with the narrower and defensible one the material actually supports.

### S1: Well-identified gap, precisely located in the literature

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 "Missing from this body of work is sustained attention to the administrators who occupy the hinge between external requirement and internal practice."
**Confidence**: 5 — eight years handling quality-culture submissions at this journal.

The gap statement is specific, is positioned against named prior work rather than against a generic "little is known", and identifies a population whose experience genuinely is under-documented in first-person terms. This is the strongest paragraph in the manuscript.

### S2: Literature review handles the participation-versus-performance tension with real control

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §2 "Delacroix (2018), by contrast, cautions that participatory rhetoric can itself become a compliance ritual"
**Confidence**: 5 — this is the standing debate in the quality-culture strand.

Setting Delacroix against Ferreira and Lund and Nakamura, and drawing out that consultation can itself become an artefact for the panel, frames a live and non-trivial question. Section 2 reads as though written by someone who knows the field.

### S3: The identity-work analysis is original and analytically restrained

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "they held it open, treating the gap itself as the space in which their professional identity was located"
**Confidence**: 4 — editorial judgement of novelty against this journal's recent output.

Declining to resolve the compliance/meaning tension, and instead treating the unresolved gap as the site of professional identity, goes beyond Rahman's (2020) account of vocation. This is the finding the paper should be built on.

### W1: The novelty claim is contradicted by the manuscript's own reference list

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §6 "This paper has offered the first comprehensive account of how university administrators experience and enact QA implementation."
**Confidence**: 5 — direct comparison against the works the paper itself cites.

The three themes correspond one-to-one to Okonkwo (2018) on ritual in self-evaluation, Pettersen (2022) on distributed leadership in the quality office, and Rahman (2020) on identity and vocation among quality professionals. The manuscript nowhere states what it adds to any of the three. A comprehensiveness claim advanced over cited work that covers the same three-part terrain requires either differentiation or withdrawal. The core thesis about negotiation could survive a rewritten and narrower claim, which is why this is Major and not Critical.

### W2: Delacroix (2018) is reversed in the Discussion, against the paper's own reading of him

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "our findings align with Delacroix (2018), who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism"
**Confidence**: 5 — the source's argument is stated correctly elsewhere in this manuscript.

Section 2 and the reference-list annotation both render Delacroix as arguing *against* treating consultation as evidence of quality culture. The Discussion's third implication — that consultation is "the engine of internalization" — therefore rests on an inverted citation and additionally claims alignment with a source that contradicts it. The practical recommendation must be rebuilt on sources that actually support it, or reframed as a disagreement with Delacroix.

### W3: The study's sample size is stated two different ways

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: Abstract and §3.2 "Fourteen administrators were interviewed in depth" and "Twelve senior administrators (n=12) with direct QA responsibilities"
**Confidence**: 5 — plain textual contradiction.

A reader cannot currently determine the evidentiary base of the qualitative strand, which is the paper's primary component. Beyond correcting the number, the authors must re-verify every count in the manuscript, since a discrepancy of this kind in the abstract undermines confidence in the reported figures generally.

### W4: The national system, regulatory model, and review cycle are never identified

**Severity**: Major

### W5: "Quality culture" is never operationalised, and "QA" is used with sliding referents

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §1 Introduction and §3.4 Analysis — expected an operational definition of quality culture distinguishing it from external accreditation compliance, external review, and internal quality assurance, together with the survey instrument items and scale range behind the reported mean; checked Abstract, §1, §2 Literature Review, §3.3, §3.4, §4.4, §5
**Confidence**: 5 — conceptual distinctions are foundational in this subfield.

Meanwhile the three themes attach "QA" to three different objects — externally driven documentation, internal governance, and professional self-understanding — without acknowledging the shift, which is what makes the tripartite finding look more unified than it is. Remedying this requires definitional work and re-presentation of the themes, not a sentence of clarification.

### W6: The representativeness claim is contradicted two sentences later by an exclusion disclosure

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "the study achieved balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views" and "these were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure"
**Confidence**: 5 — both statements appear in the same paragraph.

Excluding participants because they fell outside the organising structure means the structure was preserved by discarding the cases that would have tested it, and the claim to have captured the full range of views cannot stand alongside that disclosure. The remedy is to reincorporate the dissenting accounts into the analysis, or to drop the representativeness claim and report the exclusion as a limitation with its rationale. My methodology and discourse-analysis colleagues will have views on the analytic consequences; editorially, the contradiction cannot appear in print.

### W7: The anonymisation guarantee is contradicted by identifying attributions

**Severity**: Major

**Confidence**: 5 — the sample composition in §3.2 makes the descriptors uniquely identifying.

### W8: Findings from three institutions are generalised to the sector and to "administrators everywhere"

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §5 "administrators everywhere face the same fundamental tension between external requirement and internal meaning"
**Confidence**: 5 — the inferential reach is stated explicitly in the text.

The Discussion asserts that the study "demonstrate[s]" how universities across the sector behave and can be read as a general account of the sector as a whole, on the basis of interviews in three institutions within one unidentified system. This is the same overreach that drives the Conclusion's comprehensiveness claim, but it is a separate textual commitment and needs separate correction: the claims must be scaled to the design, which also makes the paper's stated limitation about wider institutional types coherent rather than contradictory.

### W9: Quantitative results are unevaluable as reported, and no table is provided

**Severity**: Major
**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §4.4 Survey findings — expected a table reporting group means, standard deviations, the test performed, degrees of freedom, and an effect size for the subgroup comparison, plus reconciliation of the subgroup counts with the 48 respondents; checked §3.3 Data collection, §3.4 Analysis, §4.4 Survey findings, and the whole manuscript for any figure or table callout
**Confidence**: 4 — familiar with reporting norms for QA survey work, not a statistician.

A p<.05 claim is made from 9 against 11 respondents with no named test, no group means, no degrees of freedom, no effect size, and no account of the remaining respondents; the scale range behind M=3.9 and SD=0.6 is never stated. The manuscript contains no tables or figures at all, so the entire quantitative strand arrives as prose assertion. Whether the comparison is defensible is for the methodology seat; editorially, it is not currently in a form a reader can assess, and the moderator claim built on it must be withdrawn or properly reported.

### W10: The design is described inconsistently, and the consistency claim in §4.1 does not follow

**Severity**: Minor
**Evidence Anchor**: text: §3.1 and §4.1 "We adopted a mixed-methods design combining semi-structured interviews with a supplementary staff survey" and "These themes emerged systematically from the structured protocol"
**Confidence**: 4 — reading the design description against the findings narrative.

Semi-structured interviews do not have a "structured protocol", and themes cannot both emerge inductively and be produced systematically by a protocol. The sentence is also used to license a confidence claim about cross-institutional pattern that does not follow from protocol uniformity. Minor rather than Major because the design itself is recoverable from §3.1 and the offending warrant can simply be struck; the coherence consequences belong to the discourse-analysis seat.

### W11: A reference-list entry carries an editorial annotation
