# Field Analysis Report

## Paper Basic Information

- **Title**: Building Institutional Quality Culture: Administrator Perspectives on Quality Assurance Implementation in Universities
- **Abstract length**: ~155 words
- **Full text length**: ~2,600 words of body text (Sections 1–6); ~3,100 words including abstract and reference list
- **Number of references**: 12 (all dated 2018–2022; no source older than 2018)

## Field Analysis

| Dimension | Analysis Result |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Primary Discipline** | Higher education studies — specifically quality assurance / internal QA and institutional governance |
| **Secondary Disciplines** | (1) Organizational sociology of professions (professional identity, distributed leadership, institutional decoupling); (2) Public administration / accountability and audit regimes; (3) Educational measurement (the survey component, weakly developed) |
| **Research Paradigm** | Mixed methods, qualitative-dominant (QUAL → quan supplementary). Underlying epistemology is interpretive, but the survey section shifts without warning to a post-positivist inferential register (p<.05 subgroup claim). The paradigm mismatch is unacknowledged. |
| **Methodology Type** | Multi-site semi-structured interview study (n=12 per Methods; n=14 per Abstract) with reflexive-style thematic analysis, plus a cross-sectional perceptual survey (n=48). Functionally an embedded/convergent design, but the paper names no design typology and cites no mixed-methods methodologist. |
| **Target Journal Tier** | **Aspiration Q1–Q2; current execution Q3-at-best.** Rationale: the topic, framing, and reference profile target mainstream field journals (*Quality in Higher Education*, *Higher Education Quarterly*). But the manuscript contains contradictions that a Q1/Q2 desk screen would catch immediately — sample size disagreement between abstract and methods (14 vs 12), a claim of "balanced representation" in the same paragraph that reports excluding dissenting participants, an inferential claim on n=9 vs n=11, no limitations section, no reflexivity, no instrument description, and a Discussion citation of Delacroix (2018) that reverses the argument the paper itself attributes to Delacroix in Section 2. As submitted, likely desk-rejected at Q1/Q2; a major revision could be competitive at Q2. |
| **Paper Maturity** | **Complete first full draft / early revised draft — not pre-submission.** Rationale: prose is polished and the structure is conventionally complete, which superficially reads as pre-submission. But the methodological apparatus expected of an empirical qualitative paper is absent (no limitations, no positionality, no trustworthiness procedures, no coding detail, no survey instrument), and multiple internal facts do not reconcile with each other. Surface polish is running ahead of substantive completion — a pattern that misleads authors into submitting early. |

**Language**: English throughout; register is appropriate for an international journal. Reviews should be written in English.

## Recommended Target Journals (Top 3)

1. **Quality in Higher Education** (Taylor & Francis) — The single best fit. It is the field's dedicated outlet for internal QA and quality-culture work, and the paper already cites Iversen (2021) from it. Its readership will, however, expect the national regulatory system to be named and the quality-culture literature genealogy (EUA Quality Culture Project lineage; Harvey & Stensaker; Newton) engaged directly.
2. **Higher Education Quarterly** (Wiley) — Publishes qualitative institutional-governance work of roughly this scope and length; Ferreira & Lund (2022) is cited from it. More tolerant of conceptual framing, but will press hard on theoretical contribution beyond restating known findings.
3. **Journal of Higher Education Policy and Management** (Taylor & Francis) — Explicitly oriented to administrators and managers, which matches the paper's chosen vantage point; Silva & Tan (2021) is cited from it. Most receptive to the "administrator as intermediary" angle, but that also means the reviewers there will know Silva & Tan well and will test the "first comprehensive account" claim against it.

*Fallback if the sample and survey cannot be strengthened*: **Tertiary Education and Management** or a regional QA journal, reframed honestly as an exploratory three-institution study with the survey removed or demoted to descriptive context only.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #1

**Role**: EIC
**Display role**: Journal-Fit Reviewer
**Identity Description**: Associate Editor of *Quality in Higher Education*, specializing in internal quality assurance and quality-culture development across European and Asian systems; served on a national agency's institutional-audit standards revision panel and has handled roughly 200 submissions on quality culture over eight years, most of which she rejected for recycling the same three findings.
**Review Focus**:
  1. **Fit and originality against the journal's own back catalogue** — whether the three themes (ritual compliance, distributed leadership, identity work) add anything to what the quality-culture literature already established, and whether the Conclusion's claim to offer "the first comprehensive account" survives contact with Pettersen (2022), Rahman (2020), and Silva & Tan (2021), all of which the paper itself cites as covering this terrain.
  2. **Interpretability of the QA context for an international readership** — the study reports "three universities in a single national system" without naming the system, the regulatory regime, the review cycle, or whether QA there is accreditation-based, audit-based, or standards-based. Without this, a QA reader cannot judge what "ritual compliance" is compliance *with*, and cross-national comparison is impossible.
  3. **Conceptual discipline in the use of "quality"** — whether QA, accreditation, external review, internal QA, and quality culture are held distinct or allowed to slide into one another; and whether "quality culture" is given any operational definition at all beyond the Brennan & Osei gloss.
**Will particularly care about**: Whether "quality culture" is doing analytic work or functioning as a fashionable label, and whether the paper conflates external accreditation compliance with internal quality assurance — a conflation this journal's readers treat as a competence signal.
**Possible blind spots**: Likely to under-scrutinize the inferential statistics (the n=9 vs n=11 significance claim) and may accept methodological thinness if the conceptual framing is fashionable. May also default to "this is a nice fit, fix the framing" without registering how many of the paper's factual claims contradict each other.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #2

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 1
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 1
**Identity Description**: Mixed-methods methodologist in a school of education, trained in Creswell/Plano Clark design typologies and Braun & Clarke reflexive thematic analysis, who also teaches introductory inferential statistics and routinely reviews for *Journal of Mixed Methods Research*. Known for demanding that every number in a manuscript reconcile with every other number.
**Review Focus**:
  1. **Internal factual consistency of the sample** — the Abstract reports "Fourteen administrators were interviewed"; §3.2 reports "Twelve senior administrators (n=12)". These cannot both be true. Which figure governs, and what else in the analysis depends on the answer.
  2. **Mixed-methods integration** — no design typology is named, no integration point or joint display is provided, and §4.4 and §5 describe the survey as having "corroborated the qualitative picture" while simultaneously reporting that it *contradicted* what the interviews led the authors to anticipate. Divergence is a legitimate and interesting mixed-methods finding; labelling divergence as corroboration is not.
  3. **The survey as an unreported instrument** — no items, no scale anchors (M=3.9 on what range?), no reliability or validity evidence, no sampling frame, no response rate, no clustering by institution. The subgroup claim ("statistically significant difference (p<.05)") names no test, reports no effect size or confidence interval, applies no correction, and rests on n=9 versus n=11 drawn from three clustered sites. Whether any inferential claim can survive here.
  4. **Thematic analysis rigour** — §3.4 describes coding in two sentences. There is no reflexivity or positionality statement, no account of how thematic stability was judged, no intercoder or team process, and no member checking. §4.1 states that themes "emerged systematically from the structured protocol," which is internally inconsistent with the semi-structured design declared in §3.1 and with the inductive language of §3.4.
  5. **Negative case handling** — §4.3 asserts "balanced representation of perspectives, capturing the full range of administrator views" and then states that dissenting participants "were excluded for space, as they fell outside the three-theme structure." Excluding disconfirming cases because they do not fit the coding frame inverts the logic of qualitative analysis; the two sentences cannot both stand.
  6. **Sampling and access** — participants were "recruited through institutional QA offices," i.e. through the gatekeepers whose work is the object of study. Selection bias needs to be named, not left implicit, and it may partly explain finding 5.
  7. **Ethics claim versus practice** — §3.5 states data were "fully anonymized... so that no individual could be identified," yet §4.1 attributes a quotation to "the quality director of the largest private university in the region," a description that identifies both institution and person. This is a live confidentiality problem, not a stylistic slip.
**Will particularly care about**: Whether the evidence base — 12 (or 14) interviews and an undescribed 48-person survey — can carry the inferential and generalizing weight the Discussion places on it, and whether the authors' confidence statements ("giving us confidence that the pattern was not an artifact") are backed by procedure or merely asserted.
**Possible blind spots**: Will likely miss the substantive mis-citation of Delacroix (2018) and the absent theoretical genealogy of quality culture, since neither is a design defect. May also under-weigh whether the topic matters at all to the field — pure design critique can be silent on significance.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #3

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 2
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 2
**Identity Description**: Professor of higher education policy specializing in comparative quality assurance and audit cultures; has published on the internalization of external QA across three national systems and knows the quality-culture literature back to the EUA Quality Culture Project and Newton's work on ritual compliance. Reads reference lists first.
**Review Focus**:
  1. **Mis-citation that reverses a source's argument** — §2 correctly reports that Delacroix (2018) "argues *against* treating stakeholder consultation as sufficient evidence of a healthy quality culture," and the reference-list annotation confirms this. Yet §5 states that "our findings align with Delacroix (2018), who recommends that institutions treat broad stakeholder consultation as the central mechanism for building a healthy quality culture," and concludes "Consultation... is the engine of internalization." The Discussion attributes to Delacroix the exact opposite of what the paper's own Literature Review and reference annotation say he argued. This must be resolved: either the Discussion claim is withdrawn or the Literature Review is wrong, and in either case the third of three Discussion findings currently rests on a reversed citation.
  2. **Missing canonical foundations** — no source predates 2018. The literature that defines this problem space is entirely absent: the EUA Quality Culture Project reports, Harvey & Stensaker on quality culture typologies, Ehlers, Newton on ritual and "feeling the squeeze," and — most consequentially — Meyer & Rowan's institutional decoupling, which is precisely the mechanism the paper rediscovers and names "ritual compliance," alongside Power's audit society, Strathern's audit cultures, and Ball's performativity. As it stands the paper presents established field knowledge as emergent findings.
  3. **Reference-set integrity** — all 12 DOIs share the `10.5555` prefix, which is a reserved test/example range rather than a registered publisher prefix, and several journal titles (*Journal of Higher Education Governance*, *Studies in Quality Assurance*, *Educational Management Review*) do not correspond to journals I can identify in this field. Every citation needs independent verification before this manuscript can be assessed on substance.
  4. **Theoretical framework** — none is declared. Three inductive themes are reported without any theory that would explain *why* administrators experience QA this way or connect the findings to a cumulative literature. The paper claims to address a gap in theorizing "at the level of everyday administrative practice" but then supplies description rather than theory.
  5. **Unwarranted generalization** — §5's second finding asserts that "these findings demonstrate that universities across the sector treat QA as a negotiated accomplishment" and that the study "can be read as a general account of how the higher education sector as a whole constructs quality culture," with "administrators everywhere" facing the same tension. Three institutions in one unnamed system cannot license a sector-wide or global claim, and this move sits awkwardly beside the Conclusion's own call to "test these dynamics across a wider range of institutional types and national systems."
**Will particularly care about**: Whether the paper's stated contribution is genuine or a rediscovery of well-established findings under new labels, and whether the sources it leans on say what it says they say.
**Possible blind spots**: May under-weight practitioner usability and will probably delegate the statistical problems to Reviewer 1. Risk of prescribing a reading list rather than a revision path.

### Reviewer Configuration Card #4

**Role**: Peer Reviewer 3
**Display role**: Peer Reviewer 3
**Identity Description**: Organizational sociologist of professional work with a second career as head of institutional review at a national QA agency; publishes on street-level bureaucracy, institutional work, and the labour of compliance, and has sat on both sides of the review table. Reads every QA paper asking "whose interests does this account serve, and what could a quality office actually do differently on Monday?"
**Review Focus**:
  1. **Power and the politics of the vantage point** — the paper centres administrators and then treats their self-descriptions largely at face value. Administrators are not only recipients of the audit regime; they are its local enforcement arm. §4.3's "custodians of student interests against a tide of box-ticking" is a self-legitimating account by actors with an interest in that account, and it is reported as a finding rather than analyzed as identity work in the sociological sense. Academics and students — the people on whom QA acts — have no voice in the data at all, yet the Conclusion pronounces on what QA processes should look like for them.
  2. **The unnamed frameworks that already explain these findings** — street-level bureaucracy (Lipsky) on discretion under conflicting mandates, institutional work and decoupling on how actors maintain legitimacy while buffering core practice, and the sociology of professional identity on "coping resources." §4.3's observation that administrators "held the tension open" is the paper's most interesting move and is currently undertheorized; these literatures would let it become a contribution rather than an anecdote.
  3. **Whose sample this is** — QA offices selected the participants, and dissenting participants were then excluded from the analysis (§4.3). Structurally, the institutions being studied curated the evidence about themselves, and the residual criticism was filtered out. This is a political fact about the study, not only a sampling technicality, and it deserves explicit treatment.
  4. **Practical implications are non-operational** — §5 and §6 recommend processes that "create room for meaning-making" and "maximize genuine participation." Neither tells a quality director what to stop doing, what it costs in staff time, what a regulator would have to change to permit it, or how to tell a meaningful process from a well-documented one. The paper diagnoses documentation burden without ever quantifying it.
  5. **Transferability beyond the resourced Anglophone case** — one private and two public institutions in one unnamed system, all apparently with functioning QA offices. Institutions without a dedicated quality office, systems where QA is tied to funding or licensure, and non-Anglophone documentation regimes would likely produce different dynamics. The scope of the claim should match the scope of the evidence.
**Will particularly care about**: Whether the paper's silences — academics, students, dissenting administrators, the regulator — reproduce a managerial view of quality while presenting itself as a critique of managerialism.
**Possible blind spots**: Tends toward scope creep (asking for a different, larger study rather than a fixable version of this one), and will not police statistics or citation accuracy. May over-read political significance into what are sometimes ordinary drafting errors.

## Review Strategy Recommendations

**Special characteristics requiring attention**

1. **Surface polish exceeds substantive completeness.** The prose is fluent and the structure conventional, which will tempt reviewers to write a light "minor revisions" review. The defect profile does not support that: at least three claims in the manuscript contradict other claims in the same manuscript, one Discussion finding rests on a citation that reverses its source, and the ethics statement is contradicted by the Findings. The register should be developmental — this is a fixable draft by an author who can write — but the verdict must follow the evidence, not the tone. Major revision at minimum.

2. **Treat the manuscript's self-assessments as claims to be checked, not as findings.** The paper repeatedly asserts its own rigour: "giving us confidence that the pattern was not an artifact," "achieved balanced representation," "capturing the full range," "corroborated the qualitative picture," "the first comprehensive account." In each case the surrounding text undercuts the assertion. Reviewers should verify each against the paper's own evidence rather than accepting the framing.

3. **Reconcilable-fact checklist to distribute, so nothing is dropped**: 14 vs 12 participants (Abstract vs §3.2); "fully anonymized" vs identification of the quality director of the largest private university in the region (§3.5 vs §4.1); "balanced representation / full range" vs exclusion of dissenting cases (§4.3); "themes emerged" vs "structured protocol" vs semi-structured design (§3.1, §3.4, §4.1); "corroborated" vs "we had anticipated a more skeptical picture" (§4.4, §5); Delacroix argues *against* consultation (§2, reference annotation) vs Delacroix "recommends" consultation as central mechanism (§5).

4. **Structural absences to name explicitly**: no limitations section, no positionality or reflexivity statement, no survey instrument, no theoretical framework, no named national system, no design typology.

**Complementarity and tension among reviewers**

- **Deliberate non-overlap**: R1 owns numbers, design and procedure; R2 owns literature, citation accuracy and theoretical positioning; R3 owns power, politics and practitioner utility; the Journal-Fit Reviewer owns fit, originality and conceptual discipline. The Delacroix mis-citation is assigned solely to R2 (it is a substantive misreading, not a formatting slip) and the survey statistics solely to R1, to prevent both from being reported twice or missed entirely.
- **Live tension — fix versus reframe**: R1 will push toward strengthening the survey and reporting the missing procedures. R3 will argue the survey is unsalvageable at n=48 across three clustered sites and should be cut, with the paper reframed as a purely qualitative study of intermediary work. These are genuinely different revision paths with different costs, and the synthesizer should present both rather than blending them into a vague "strengthen the methods."
- **Live tension — framing**: the Journal-Fit Reviewer will want the managerial "quality culture" framing sharpened for *Quality in Higher Education*'s readership; R3 will want that framing interrogated as complicit. Do not average these. The choice determines which of the three recommended journals the paper should target.
- **Coverage gap to watch**: no configured reviewer is a specialist in survey psychometrics. R1 will catch that the instrument is unreported and that the subgroup test is unsupportable, which is sufficient for this manuscript; if the authors respond by producing a fuller instrument, a psychometric specialist should be added at the next round.
