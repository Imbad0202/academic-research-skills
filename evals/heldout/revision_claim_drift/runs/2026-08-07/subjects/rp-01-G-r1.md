Among 1,842 adults enrolled in the Meridian Recovery Cohort, 46.3% received structured symptom monitoring during the first 30 days after discharge. Over a median follow-up of 3.2 years, monitoring was associated with a lower rate of unplanned readmission after adjustment for age, baseline severity, and comorbidity burden (adjusted HR = 0.76, 95% CI [0.65, 0.89], p = .001). This pattern is consistent with earlier observational work linking timely symptom recognition to care continuity (Belmonte & Idris, 2021).

These findings position structured post-discharge monitoring as a clinically meaningful target for reducing unplanned readmissions: the association suggests that monitoring may identify deterioration before it requires acute care, offering a plausible pathway for the observed difference. Unlike prior risk-stratification approaches, which estimate a patient's readmission risk once at discharge and leave the following weeks unobserved, the pathway examined here operates continuously across the very window in which deterioration emerges. However, monitoring was selected by treating clinicians rather than assigned, and residual differences in patient engagement or service access could account for part of the association. Although the cohort design cannot establish that monitoring caused the reduction in readmissions, the observed association — whose magnitude aligns with the care-coordination model proposed by Kovac (2020) — defines a concrete, testable pathway that future randomized evaluations of post-discharge care can directly examine.
