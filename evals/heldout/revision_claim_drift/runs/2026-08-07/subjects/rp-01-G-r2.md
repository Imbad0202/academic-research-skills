Among 1,842 adults enrolled in the Meridian Recovery Cohort, 46.3% received structured symptom monitoring during the first 30 days after discharge. Over a median follow-up of 3.2 years, monitoring was associated with a lower rate of unplanned readmission after adjustment for age, baseline severity, and comorbidity burden (adjusted HR = 0.76, 95% CI [0.65, 0.89], p = .001). This pattern is consistent with earlier observational work linking timely symptom recognition to care continuity (Belmonte & Idris, 2021).

These findings position structured symptom monitoring as a clinically meaningful candidate pathway for reducing unplanned readmissions: the association suggests that monitoring may identify deterioration before it requires acute care, offering a plausible mechanism for the observed difference. Unlike prior risk-stratification approaches, which estimate a patient's risk at the point of discharge but do not follow the subsequent clinical course, the pathway examined here centers on detecting change as it unfolds during the post-discharge period. However, monitoring was selected by treating clinicians rather than assigned, and residual differences in patient engagement or service access could account for part of the association. Although the cohort design cannot establish that monitoring caused the reduction in readmissions, the magnitude of the association, which aligns with the care-coordination model proposed by Kovac (2020), marks this monitoring pathway as a priority target for randomized evaluation in post-discharge care.
