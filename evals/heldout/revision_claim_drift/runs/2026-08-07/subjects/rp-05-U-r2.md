The fictional Canopy Basin Survey yielded 12,480 sensor-hours from 38 wetland plots. Two descriptive contrasts separated restored from unrestored plots. Mean nitrate concentration was lower in restored plots (3.216 mg/L) than in unrestored plots (4.783 mg/L), a difference of -1.567 mg/L (95% CI [-1.842, -1.291], p < .001). Dissolved oxygen above 6.250 mg/L was recorded more often in restored plots (62.47% of observations) than in unrestored plots (44.18%). Both contrasts were associated with restoration status and are consistent with the nutrient-retention process described by Halberg and Qureshi (2019).

In the mixed-effects model, two predictors were associated with nitrate concentration. Canopy cover predicted lower nitrate concentration (β = -0.384, SE = 0.071, 95% CI [-0.523, -0.245], p < .001), whereas water depth showed a smaller positive association (β = 0.117, SE = 0.048, p = .015). The restoration-by-canopy interaction was positive (0.092, 95% CI [0.014, 0.170]), suggesting that canopy effects may differ modestly across restoration conditions. This interpretation aligns with the hydrologic buffering framework proposed by Senaka (2023).
