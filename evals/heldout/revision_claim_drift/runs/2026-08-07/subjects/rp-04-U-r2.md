Among 247 participants in the fictional Affect and Routine Panel, 61.1% completed all four diary waves. Daily routine stability was associated with lower emotional exhaustion (r = -0.29, 95% CI [-0.40, -0.17], p < .001), and the adjusted association remained detectable after accounting for workload and sleep duration (b = -0.12, SE = 0.04, p = .004). This pattern is consistent with the resource-conservation account advanced by Nwosu and Talek (2021) and suggests that predictable routines may support short-term emotional regulation.

Three limitations apply. Reliance on self-report for both routine stability and exhaustion may have inflated their association through shared response tendencies; the six-week window does not establish temporal ordering, leaving open the possibility that exhaustion disrupts routines; and recruitment from a narrow set of metropolitan counseling networks may limit generalization to people with different work arrangements or service access—a relevant concern given that contextual variability has altered routine–well-being associations in prior diary research (Ferreira, 2018).
