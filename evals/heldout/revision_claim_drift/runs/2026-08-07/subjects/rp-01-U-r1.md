Among 1,842 adults enrolled in the Meridian Recovery Cohort, 46.3% received structured symptom monitoring during the first 30 days after discharge. Over a median follow-up of 3.2 years, monitoring was associated with a lower rate of unplanned readmission after adjustment for age, baseline severity, and comorbidity burden (adjusted HR = 0.76, 95% CI [0.65, 0.89], p = .001). This pattern is consistent with earlier observational work linking timely symptom recognition to care continuity (Belmonte & Idris, 2021).

These findings advance clinical care research by identifying a plausible and modifiable mechanism through which structured monitoring may reduce unplanned readmissions: the recognition of deterioration before it requires acute care. In contrast to prior risk-stratification approaches, which estimate who is likely to be readmitted but do not specify when or how to intervene, the monitoring pathway examined here targets the post-discharge interval in which deterioration first becomes detectable and potentially actionable. Nonetheless, monitoring was selected by treating clinicians rather than assigned, and residual differences in patient engagement or service access could account for part of the association. The cohort design therefore cannot establish that monitoring caused the reduction in readmissions, although the magnitude aligns with the care-coordination model proposed by Kovac (2020). Within these limits, the present findings position early symptom monitoring as a concrete, testable mechanism for improving care continuity and a priority target for experimental evaluation, shifting the emphasis from predicting readmission risk toward acting on it.
