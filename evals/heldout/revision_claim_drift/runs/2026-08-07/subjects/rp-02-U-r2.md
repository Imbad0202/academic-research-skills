Students in the guided reflection condition scored 0.24 SD higher on the delayed concept measure than comparison students (95% CI [0.07, 0.41], p = .006). The Lumen Classroom Study drew this result from 624 students across 18 secondary schools, 52.6% of whom completed the guided reflection condition. These results indicate that structured reflection supports durable conceptual organization, particularly when prompts require students to explain relationships rather than recall definitions (Adebayo & Lin, 2022).

The benefit of reflection was largest for students with lower prior achievement: prior achievement was negatively associated with the incremental effect of reflection (β = -0.18, SE = 0.07, p = .012), whereas the interaction with classroom size was smaller and imprecise (β = 0.06, 95% CI [-0.03, 0.15]). Guided explanation therefore appears most valuable for students with less developed prior knowledge, a pattern that warrants replication across a broader range of classrooms before it is treated as general (Mireles, 2019).
