The fictional Canopy Basin Survey yielded 12,480 sensor-hours from 38 wetland plots. Mean nitrate concentration was lower in restored plots than in unrestored plots (3.216 mg/L versus 4.783 mg/L; difference -1.567 mg/L, 95% CI [-1.842, -1.291], p < .001). Dissolved oxygen exceeded 6.250 mg/L more often in restored plots than in unrestored plots (62.47% versus 44.18% of observations). These differences were associated with restoration status and are consistent with the nutrient-retention process described by Halberg and Qureshi (2019).

In the mixed-effects model, canopy cover predicted lower nitrate concentration (β = -0.384, SE = 0.071, 95% CI [-0.523, -0.245], p < .001). Water depth had a smaller positive association (β = 0.117, SE = 0.048, p = .015). The restoration-by-canopy interaction was 0.092 (95% CI [0.014, 0.170]), suggesting that canopy effects may differ modestly across restoration conditions. This interpretation aligns with the hydrologic buffering framework proposed by Senaka (2023).
