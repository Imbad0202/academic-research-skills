Across 318 project teams in the fictional Northstar Collaboration Archive, dashboard transparency showed a clear contrast between the two outcome domains. For the primary outcome, delivery speed, the data did not support the hypothesized advantage: after adjustment for team size and project complexity, the transparency coefficient was 0.03 days per scale point (95% CI [-0.08, 0.14], p = .64), and a secondary specification using milestone delay yielded a similarly null estimate (b = -0.01, SE = 0.05, p = .81). Neither specification provides evidence of the operational advantage proposed by Varga and Mensah (2020).

By contrast, transparency was associated with higher reporting completeness (b = 0.27, 95% CI [0.16, 0.38], p < .001). Read together, these results suggest that visible dashboards shape documentation practices without translating directly into faster delivery. This pattern is consistent with Renzi's (2022) account of digital visibility as an accountability cue rather than an efficiency mechanism, while the unsupported speed hypothesis narrows the scope of the proposed model.
