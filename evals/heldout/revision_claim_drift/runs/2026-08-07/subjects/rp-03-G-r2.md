Data from 318 project teams using the fictional Northstar Collaboration Archive yielded a consistent pattern across both delivery outcomes. For the primary outcome, the results did not support the hypothesis that dashboard transparency predicts delivery speed: after adjustment for team size and project complexity, the transparency coefficient was 0.03 days per scale point (95% CI [-0.08, 0.14], p = .64). The secondary specification, using milestone delay, produced a similarly null estimate (b = -0.01, SE = 0.05, p = .81). Across both specifications, then, the data provided no evidence of the expected operational advantage (Varga & Mensah, 2020).

Where transparency showed no relation to how quickly teams delivered, it did relate to how they documented their work: transparency was associated with higher reporting completeness (b = 0.27, 95% CI [0.16, 0.38], p < .001). This contrast suggests that visible dashboards may shape documentation practices without translating directly into faster delivery. The pattern is consistent with Renzi's (2022) account of digital visibility as an accountability cue rather than an efficiency mechanism, although the unsupported speed hypothesis narrows the proposed model.
