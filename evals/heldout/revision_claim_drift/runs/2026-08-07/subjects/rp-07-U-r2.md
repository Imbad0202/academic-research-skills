The fictional Atlas Worklife Study included 412 managers from 57 firms, with a response rate of 68.7%. Coaching climate was associated with psychological safety (b = 0.34, SE = 0.08, p < .001), and psychological safety predicted retention intention after adjustment for tenure and team size (b = 0.29, 95% CI [0.15, 0.43], p < .001). These relationships are consistent with the relational-support model described by Basu and Engel (2020).

The estimated indirect effect of coaching climate on retention intention through psychological safety was b = 0.21 (95% CI [0.09, 0.28]). The direct effect remained positive (b = 0.22, SE = 0.07, p = .003), suggesting partial rather than complete mediation. This configuration aligns with Dobrev's (2022) proposition that supportive managerial practices may influence attachment through both interpersonal and instrumental pathways.
