Data from 318 project teams using the fictional Northstar Collaboration Archive did not support the primary hypothesis that dashboard transparency predicts delivery speed. After adjustment for team size and project complexity, the transparency coefficient was 0.03 days per scale point (95% CI [-0.08, 0.14], p = .64), and a secondary specification using milestone delay yielded a similarly null estimate (b = -0.01, SE = 0.05, p = .81). Across both specifications, then, we found no evidence of the operational advantage predicted by Varga and Mensah (2020).

By contrast, transparency was associated with higher reporting completeness (b = 0.27, 95% CI [0.16, 0.38], p < .001). Read together, the two results suggest that visible dashboards shape documentation practices without translating into faster delivery. This divergence is consistent with Renzi's (2022) account of digital visibility as an accountability cue rather than an efficiency mechanism, although the unsupported speed hypothesis narrows the scope of the proposed model.
