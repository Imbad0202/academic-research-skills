Among 1,842 adults enrolled in the Meridian Recovery Cohort, 46.3% received structured symptom monitoring during the first 30 days after discharge. Over a median follow-up of 3.2 years, monitoring was associated with a lower rate of unplanned readmission after adjustment for age, baseline severity, and comorbidity burden (adjusted HR = 0.76, 95% CI [0.65, 0.89], p = .001). This pattern is consistent with earlier observational work linking timely symptom recognition to care continuity (Belmonte & Idris, 2021).

These findings identify structured post-discharge symptom monitoring as a clinically significant and potentially actionable target for reducing unplanned readmissions: the 24% lower adjusted hazard suggests that monitoring may detect deterioration before it requires acute care, offering a plausible mechanistic pathway for the observed difference. In contrast to prior risk-stratification approaches, which estimate readmission risk at the single time point of discharge, the monitoring pathway examined here operates continuously across the post-discharge interval, targeting deterioration as it emerges rather than predicting it in advance. However, monitoring was selected by treating clinicians rather than assigned, and residual differences in patient engagement or service access could account for part of the association; the cohort design therefore cannot establish that monitoring caused the reduction in readmissions. Within these limits, the findings extend the care-coordination model proposed by Kovac (2020) by specifying early symptom detection as a concrete, testable mechanism, and they position post-discharge monitoring as a priority candidate for confirmation in randomized or quasi-experimental designs.
